# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Jesus:** The one the Gospels present as the promised Messiah and the Son of God.
- **The disciples:** Jesus's closest followers. They are hungry.
- **The Pharisees:** Religious leaders who were very strict about God's law.
- **A man with a withered hand:** A man whose hand is shriveled and can't be used.

**Setting**

- Grainfields in Galilee, then a synagogue. A synagogue is a meeting place where Jewish people gathered to pray and hear Scripture.

**When**

- About 2,000 years ago, early in Jesus's work in Galilee.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**God gave his people a day of rest.** The Sabbath was the seventh day, Saturday. No one was to work (Exodus 20:8-10).

**Over time, teachers made many rules.** They tried to define exactly what counted as "work." Picking grain counted.†

**Jesus had already surprised people.** He forgave a paralyzed man's sins and ate with tax collectors (2:5, 2:16).

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Hungry in the Grainfields

One Sabbath, Jesus was walking through the grainfields. {v: 2:23}

His disciples were hungry. As they walked, they picked heads of grain to eat. {v: 2:23}

The Pharisees saw them. **"Look, why are they doing what is unlawful on the Sabbath?"** (2:24). {v: 2:24}

To them, picking grain was harvesting, and harvesting was work. {v: 2:24}

### Scene 2: What David Did

Jesus reminded them of King David. {v: 2:25}

Long before, David and his men were hungry. David went into God's house and ate the holy bread that only priests were allowed to eat. He shared it with his men too. {v: 2:25, 2:26}

Then Jesus said, **"The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath"** (2:27). {v: 2:27}

God gave the day of rest as a gift for people. People weren't made to serve a rule. {v: 2:27}

**"Therefore, the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath"** (2:28). "Son of Man" is a name Jesus used for himself. {v: 2:28}

### Scene 3: A Man in the Synagogue

Another time, Jesus went into a synagogue. A man with a withered hand was there. {v: 3:1}

Some people watched Jesus closely. If he healed on the Sabbath, they could accuse him. {v: 3:2}

Jesus said to the man, **"Stand up among us"** (3:3). {v: 3:3}

Then Jesus asked everyone, **"Which is lawful on the Sabbath: to do good or to do evil, to save life or to destroy it?"** (3:4). {v: 3:4}

No one answered. {v: 3:4}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture the man standing in the middle of the room, holding his shriveled hand close to his chest. Every eye is on Jesus. The room is completely silent.

### Scene 4: "Stretch Out Your Hand"

Jesus looked around at them. He was angry, and he was sad, because their hearts were hard. {v: 3:5}

Then he said to the man, **"Stretch out your hand"** (3:5). {v: 3:5}

The man stretched it out. His hand was completely healed. {v: 3:5}

But the Pharisees went out and started making plans with Herod's supporters to kill Jesus. {v: 3:6}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Mark 2:23 to 3:6 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Why are they doing what is unlawful on the Sabbath?"** (2:24) | The Pharisees cared more about their rules than about hungry people. |
| **"The Sabbath was made for man"** (2:27) | God's day of rest is a gift for people. |
| **"Lord even of the Sabbath"** (2:28) | Jesus has God's authority over the day God made. |
| **"To do good or to do evil"** (3:4) | Doing good is never against God's law. |
| **"Anger and sorrow at their hardness of heart"** (3:5) | Jesus was upset that they didn't care about the man. |
| **"Stretch out your hand"** (3:5) | Jesus healed with just a word. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**God's law allowed hungry travelers to pick grain from a field by hand.** The question was only whether it could be done on the Sabbath (Deuteronomy 23:25).†

**The holy bread was called the "bread of the Presence."** Twelve loaves sat in the tabernacle before God. The story of David eating it is in 1 Samuel 21:1-6.†

**The Herodians supported King Herod's family.** Normally the Pharisees didn't get along with them. They joined together against Jesus.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does this mean God doesn't care about rest?** No. God made the day of rest as a gift. Jesus isn't getting rid of it. He is showing what it is for.

**Was Jesus wrong to be angry?** No. Jesus was angry because people cared more about rules than about a man who needed help. His anger came with sorrow, and it led him to heal.†

**Do Christians still keep the Sabbath on Saturday?** Most Christians gather on Sunday, the day Jesus rose from the dead. Christians believe Jesus himself is our true rest (Matthew 11:28).†

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Being religious means following lots of rules."*

**What's true:** God's commandments are good. They protect us and our neighbors.

**What the story is mainly about:** The Pharisees used rules to judge hungry people and to stop a man from being healed. Jesus said the day of rest was made for people. He is Lord of the Sabbath, and he came to do good and to save.

**Where Jesus is:** Standing up for hungry friends, and healing a man everyone else was ignoring.

## ✝️ Good News

The leaders watched to catch Jesus doing something wrong. Jesus did good anyway, even though it put him in danger.

**"The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath"** (2:27).

Jesus is Lord of the Sabbath. He gives rest. He heals. He came to save life, even at the cost of his own.

**Jesus is Lord of the Sabbath. He gave the day of rest as a gift, and he came to heal and to save.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Small Catechism, Luther explained the Third Commandment, "Remember the Sabbath day by keeping it holy." He said it means we should not despise preaching and God's word, but hold it sacred and gladly hear and learn it.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Mark 2:23 to 3:6 on the Ninth Sunday after the Epiphany (in years when it falls) and again in early summer (Lectionary 9).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The Sabbath was made for people. Jesus is Lord of the Sabbath, and he came to do good, to heal, and to save.**
