# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Jesus:** The one the Gospels present as the promised Messiah and the Son of God.
- **A leading Pharisee:** An important religious leader. He has invited Jesus to dinner.
- **The guests:** Pharisees and experts in God's law. They are watching Jesus closely.
- **A sick man:** A man whose body is swollen with fluid. The old word for this is "dropsy."

**Setting**

- The home of a leading Pharisee, at a Sabbath dinner.

**When**

- About 2,000 years ago, while Jesus was traveling toward Jerusalem.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Jesus often ate with people.** He ate with tax collectors and sinners (5:29-30). He also ate with Pharisees (7:36).

**Jesus had healed on the Sabbath before.** Some leaders were angry about it (13:14).

**Seats at a dinner showed who was important.** The places closest to the host were the places of honor.†

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Watched closely | 14:1-6 | Jesus heals a man at a Sabbath dinner |
| 2. Places of honor | 14:7-9 | Guests grab the best seats |
| 3. The last place | 14:10-11 | Jesus says to take the lowest seat |
| 4. Who to invite | 14:12-14 | Jesus tells the host to invite the poor |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Watched Closely

One Sabbath, Jesus went to eat at the home of a leading Pharisee. The guests were watching him closely. {v: 14:1}

Right in front of Jesus was a man whose body was swollen with a sickness called dropsy. {v: 14:2}

Jesus asked the experts in the law and the Pharisees, **"Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath or not?"** (14:3). {v: 14:3}

They didn't answer. {v: 14:4}

So Jesus took hold of the man, healed him, and sent him on his way. {v: 14:4}

Then Jesus asked them, if your son or your ox fell into a well on the Sabbath, wouldn't you pull him out right away? {v: 14:5}

They had no answer. {v: 14:6}

### Scene 2: Places of Honor

Jesus noticed how the guests were choosing the best seats. So he told them a parable. {v: 14:7}

When you are invited to a wedding feast, don't sit in the place of honor. {v: 14:8}

Someone more important might be coming. Then the host will say, "Give this man your seat." And you'll have to move to the last place, embarrassed in front of everyone. {v: 14:8, 14:9}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture the guests trying not to look as if they are hurrying to the best cushions. A few have already sat down near the host. Jesus watches it all.

### Scene 3: The Last Place

**"But when you are invited, go and sit in the last place, so that your host will come and tell you, 'Friend, move up to a better place.'"** (14:10). {v: 14:10}

Then you will be honored in front of everyone. {v: 14:10}

**"For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted"** (14:11). {v: 14:11}

To exalt means to lift up. To be humbled means to be brought low. {v: 14:11}

### Scene 4: Who to Invite

Then Jesus spoke to the host. {v: 14:12}

When you give a dinner, don't just invite friends, family, or rich neighbors who can invite you back. {v: 14:12}

**"But when you host a banquet, invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind, and you will be blessed"** (14:13-14). {v: 14:13, 14:14}

They can't pay you back. But God will, at the resurrection. {v: 14:14}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Luke 14:1-14 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Watching Him closely"** (14:1) | They were hoping to catch Jesus doing something wrong. |
| **"Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath or not?"** (14:3) | Jesus asked them the question they were thinking. |
| **"Go and sit in the last place"** (14:10) | Don't push yourself forward. |
| **"The one who humbles himself will be exalted"** (14:11) | God lifts up humble people. |
| **"Invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind"** (14:13) | Welcome people who can't pay you back. |
| **"You will be repaid at the resurrection"** (14:14) | God sees and rewards kindness. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Guests reclined on couches around a low table.** The seats nearest the host were the best ones.†

**Proverbs gives the same advice.** It says not to put yourself forward before a king. It is better to be told, "Come up here" (Proverbs 25:6-7).†

**Dinners were about honor.** People invited guests who could invite them back and raise their status.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is Jesus just giving tips for being honored?** It sounds like it at first. But Jesus is teaching about God's kingdom, where the humble are lifted up. God is the host who says, "Friend, move up."

**Can I never invite my friends to dinner?** Of course you can. Jesus is saying: don't only invite people who can pay you back. Make room for people who are left out.

**Does being humble mean thinking I'm worthless?** No. Being humble means not needing to be the most important. You are precious to God.

# Why It Matters

- At school :: It's tempting to want to be first. Jesus lifts up the humble.
- With friends :: Who is being left out? You could invite them.

- Online :: Wanting to be noticed is normal. You're already noticed by God.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"You have to push your way to the top."*

**What's true:** Working hard and using your gifts is good.

**What the story is mainly about:** In God's kingdom, the humble are lifted up. Jesus himself took the lowest place. He was born in a stable and died on a cross. God lifted him up.

**Where Jesus is:** At a dinner where everyone wanted the best seat, healing a sick man and making room for people left out.

## ✝️ Good News

Jesus took the lowest seat. He came down from heaven to serve us.

**"For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted"** (14:11).

God invites people who can't pay him back. That's all of us. He welcomes us to his table, and says, "Friend, come up here."

**Jesus took the lowest place for us. God welcomes everyone who can't pay him back, and lifts up the humble.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that we come to God like beggars, with empty hands. God fills them with his gifts. At the Lord's Supper, Christ welcomes sinners to his table.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads Luke 14:1 and 7-14 in late August or early September (Lectionary 22).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Take the lowest seat and invite people who can't pay you back. God lifts up the humble, and welcomes us to his table.**
