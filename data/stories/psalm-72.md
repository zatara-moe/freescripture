# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's for.** The heading says "Of Solomon" (72:1). It's a prayer for the king, probably prayed when a new king was crowned.

**What it is.** A prayer that the king will rule with justice, protect the poor, and bring peace, so that all nations will be blessed through him.

**When the church sings it.** On the Epiphany, when wise men from the east brought gifts to the newborn King, and in Advent.

**How to read it.** Notice what makes a good king: not power or wealth, but care for the poor.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: A King Who Brings Justice

**"Endow the king with Your justice, O God, and the son of the king with Your righteousness"** (72:1). {v: 72:1}

Endow means to give as a gift. {v: 72:1}

**"May he judge Your people with righteousness and Your afflicted with justice"** (72:2). {v: 72:2, 72:3}

**"May he vindicate the afflicted among the people; may he save the children of the needy"** (72:4). {v: 72:4, 72:5}

**"May he be like rain that falls on freshly cut grass"** (72:6). {v: 72:6, 72:7}

A good king refreshes his people, like rain on a field. {v: 72:6}

### Section 2: All Kings Will Bow

May his kingdom stretch **"from sea to sea"** (72:8). {v: 72:8, 72:9}

**"May the kings of Tarshish and distant shores bring tribute; may the kings of Sheba and Seba offer gifts"** (72:10). {v: 72:10}

**"May all kings bow down to him and all nations serve him"** (72:11). {v: 72:11}

### Section 3: He Will Save the Needy

Why should the nations honor him? {v: 72:12}

**"For he will deliver the needy who cry out and the afflicted who have no helper"** (72:12). {v: 72:12}

**"He will take pity on the poor and needy and save the lives of the oppressed"** (72:13). {v: 72:13}

**"For their blood is precious in his sight"** (72:14). {v: 72:14}

The lives of the poor matter to him. {v: 72:14}

May the land overflow with grain. **"In him may all nations be blessed"** (72:17). {v: 72:15, 72:16, 72:17}

**"Blessed be the LORD God, the God of Israel, who alone does marvelous deeds"** (72:18). {v: 72:18}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 72 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Endow the king with Your justice"** (72:1) | A good king needs God's justice. |
| **"Your afflicted with justice"** (72:2) | The king's job is to protect the weak. |
| **"Like rain that falls on freshly cut grass"** (72:6) | A good ruler brings new life. |
| **"May all kings bow down to him"** (72:11) | All nations will honor this king. |
| **"Their blood is precious in his sight"** (72:14) | The lives of the poor are precious. |
| **"In him may all nations be blessed"** (72:17) | God's promise to Abraham comes true through this king. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Tarshish** was far to the west, probably in Spain. **Sheba** was far to the south, in Arabia or Africa. The Queen of Sheba visited Solomon with gifts (1 Kings 10:1-2).

**Ancient kings** often used their power for themselves. This psalm describes a king who uses his power for the poor.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is this about Solomon or Jesus?** It was a prayer for Israel's kings. No king lived up to it fully. Christians see it fulfilled in Jesus, the King to whom wise men brought gifts.

**Does "lick the dust" mean the king hurts his enemies?** It's a picture of total defeat, a common way ancient poems described victory.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Power is about being served."*

**What's true:** Kings and leaders have authority.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The true king **"will take pity on the poor and needy and save the lives of the oppressed"** (72:13). Real power serves the weak.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the King of kings, who came not to be served but to serve (Mark 10:45). The wise men brought him gold.

## ✝️ Good News

There is a King who cares for you.

**"For he will deliver the needy who cry out and the afflicted who have no helper"** (72:12).

Jesus is that King. Your life is precious to him.

**Psalm 72 prays for a king who brings justice, defends the poor, and blesses all nations. Jesus is that King, and the nations bring him their gifts.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that rulers are God's servants. Their job is to protect the weak and keep peace, not to serve themselves.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 72 on the Epiphany of Our Lord in all three years, and on the Second Sunday of Advent in Year A.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 72 prays for a king who defends the poor, rescues the needy, and blesses all nations. Jesus is that King, and the wise men bowed before him with gifts.**
