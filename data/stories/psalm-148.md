# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**What it is.** A giant choir of praise. Psalm 148 calls everything God made to praise him: angels and stars, sea monsters and snow, mountains and trees, kings and children.

**Who wrote it.** It doesn't say. It's one of the five "Hallelujah psalms" that end the book of Psalms (146-150).

**When the church sings it.** On the First Sunday of Christmas.

**How to read it.** Picture each thing as it's named. Imagine hail praising God.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Praise From the Heavens | 148:1-6 | Angels, sun, moon, and stars |
| 2. Praise From the Earth | 148:7-12 | Sea creatures, weather, mountains, animals, and people |
| 3. His Name Alone | 148:13-14 | God is above all, and near his people |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Praise From the Heavens

**"Hallelujah! Praise the LORD from the heavens; praise Him in the highest places"** (148:1). {v: 148:1}

Hallelujah means "Praise the LORD!" {v: 148:1}

**"Praise Him, all His angels"** (148:2). {v: 148:2}

**"Praise Him, O sun and moon; praise Him, all you shining stars"** (148:3). {v: 148:3, 148:4}

Why should they? **"For He gave the command and they were created"** (148:5). {v: 148:5, 148:6}

### Section 2: Praise From the Earth

Now the psalm comes down to earth. {v: 148:7}

Sea creatures and ocean depths. Lightning, hail, snow, and wind. {v: 148:7, 148:8}

Mountains and hills, fruit trees and cedars. {v: 148:9}

Wild animals and cattle, creeping things and birds. {v: 148:10}

Kings and rulers, and every person. {v: 148:11}

**"Young men and maidens, old and young together"** (148:12). {v: 148:12}

### Section 3: His Name Alone

**"Let them praise the name of the LORD, for His name alone is exalted"** (148:13). {v: 148:13}

**"His splendor is above the earth and the heavens"** (148:13). {v: 148:13}

God is above everything, and yet he has made his people **"a people near to Him"** (148:14). {v: 148:14}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 148 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Hallelujah!"** (148:1) | Praise the LORD! |
| **"He gave the command and they were created"** (148:5) | God made everything with a word. |
| **"Powerful wind fulfilling His word"** (148:8) | Even the weather does what God says. |
| **"Old and young together"** (148:12) | Everyone is invited to praise. |
| **"His name alone is exalted"** (148:13) | Only God deserves this praise. |
| **"A people near to Him"** (148:14) | The great God is close to his people. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Other nations worshiped** the sun, moon, and stars as gods. Psalm 148 says they are creatures that praise the true God.

**Saint Francis of Assisi** wrote a famous hymn inspired by this psalm, "All Creatures of Our God and King."†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**How can snow praise God?** By being what God made it to be. Creation praises God just by existing and doing what he commands.

**Why sing this at Christmas?** At Christmas, the Creator became part of his creation. All creation has a reason to sing.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| All creation | Joyful | Everything praises the LORD |
| God's people | Close to God | They are a people near to him (148:14) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Nature is just an accident."*

**What's true:** Creation follows patterns and laws we can study.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"He gave the command and they were created"** (148:5). Everything was made by God, and everything is called to praise him.

**Where Jesus is:** All things were made through him (John 1:3). At Christmas, he became one of us.

## ✝️ Good News

The God who made the stars is near to you.

**"Let them praise the name of the LORD, for His name alone is exalted"** (148:13).

He rules over everything, and he calls his people "near to Him."

**All creation, from angels to sea creatures to children, is called to praise the LORD, because he made everything and keeps his people near.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Small Catechism, Luther says, "I believe that God has made me and all creatures." He saw every gift of creation as a reason to thank and praise God.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 148 on the First Sunday of Christmas in all three years, and on the Fifth Sunday of Easter in Year C.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Hallelujah! Everything God made, from the angels and stars to the snow, animals, and people, old and young together, is called to praise the LORD.**
