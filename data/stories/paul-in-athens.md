# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Paul:** A follower of Jesus who once attacked the church. Jesus met him on the road to Damascus. Now he travels from city to city, telling people about Jesus.
- **The philosophers:** Teachers from two famous schools of thought in Athens, the Epicureans and the Stoics. A philosopher is someone who studies big questions about life and the world.
- **The Areopagus:** A council of leaders in Athens. It met on or near a rocky hill with the same name.†
- **Dionysius and Damaris:** A member of the Areopagus and a woman from Athens. Both believed Paul's message.

**Setting**

- Athens, a famous old city in Greece, full of temples and statues. The synagogue, the busy marketplace, and then the meeting of the Areopagus.

**When**

- Almost 20 years after Jesus rose from the dead, around AD 50. Soon after Paul's time in Philippi.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Paul had to leave town twice.** In Thessalonica, some people believed Paul's message, and others turned against him and started a riot (17:4-5). Then in Berea, people from Thessalonica came to stir up the crowds again (17:13).

**So the believers sent Paul away, alone.** They took him to Athens. Silas and Timothy stayed behind in Berea for now (17:14-15).

**Athens was a city of ideas.** For hundreds of years, it had been famous for its teachers and thinkers.

**Here's what you know that almost nobody in this story knows:** The God they call "unknown" has already made himself known. Paul is about to tell them who he is.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: A City Full of Idols

Paul was waiting in Athens for Silas and Timothy. While he waited, he walked the city. {v: 17:16}

Everywhere he looked, he saw idols. An idol is a statue people worship as a god. **"He was deeply disturbed in his spirit to see that the city was full of idols"** (17:16). {v: 17:16}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> White marble statues line the streets and crowd the temple steps. People hurry past them to the busy market.

So Paul started talking. In the synagogue, he reasoned with Jewish people and with Gentiles who worshiped Israel's God. Gentiles are people who are not Jewish. {v: 17:17}

He also talked every day in the marketplace, with anyone who was there. {v: 17:17}

### Scene 2: "What Is This Babbler Saying?"

Some philosophers began to argue with Paul. Some of them were Epicureans and some were Stoics, two famous schools of thought. {v: 17:18}

Some of them said, **"What is this babbler trying to say?"** (17:18). A babbler is someone who picks up scraps of ideas and repeats them. It was an insult. {v: 17:18}

Others said Paul seemed to be preaching about foreign gods. They said this because Paul kept talking about Jesus and **"the resurrection"** (17:18). The resurrection means rising from the dead. {v: 17:18}

So they took Paul to a meeting of the Areopagus. They wanted to hear this new teaching. **"You are bringing some strange notions to our ears, and we want to know what they mean"** (17:20). {v: 17:19-20}

Acts explains that the people of Athens, and the visitors who lived there, spent their time doing little else than hearing and talking about the newest ideas. {v: 17:21}

### Scene 3: To an Unknown God

Paul stood up in front of the council. He didn't start by attacking them. He started with something he had noticed. **"Men of Athens, I see that in every way you are very religious"** (17:22). {v: 17:22}

While he walked around, Paul had looked at the things they worshiped. He even found an altar with these words carved on it: **"TO AN UNKNOWN GOD"** (17:23). {v: 17:23}

An altar is a stone table where people left gifts for a god. This one was for a god whose name they didn't know. {v: 17:23}

Then Paul said, **"Therefore what you worship as something unknown, I now proclaim to you"** (17:23). To proclaim means to announce. {v: 17:23}

### Scene 4: The God Who Made the World

Paul began with who God is. God made the world and everything in it. He is **"the Lord of heaven and earth"** (17:24). {v: 17:24}

So God doesn't live in temples that people build. Athens was full of beautiful temples. Paul said God doesn't live in any of them. {v: 17:24}

God doesn't need people to serve him, either, as if he were missing something. It's the other way around. **"He Himself gives everyone life and breath and everything else"** (17:25). {v: 17:25}

### Scene 5: Not Far from Any of Us

Paul went on. God made every nation from one man. He decided when and where people would live. {v: 17:26}

God did this so that people would seek him, and maybe reach out for him and find him. **"He is not far from each one of us"** (17:27). {v: 17:27}

Then Paul said, **"For in Him we live and move and have our being"** (17:28). And he quoted their own poets: **"We are His offspring"** (17:28). Offspring means children. {v: 17:28}

Paul used their own poets to make his point. If we are God's children, God can't be like a statue of gold, silver, or stone that people make with their hands. {v: 17:29}

### Scene 6: A Day and a Man

Now Paul came to the hard part. In the past, God had let people's not-knowing go by. But now God **"commands all people everywhere to repent"** (17:30). To repent means to turn around, and turn back to God. {v: 17:30}

Why now? God has set a day when he will judge the world with justice. He will do it through a man he has chosen. {v: 17:31}

And God has given everyone proof. He raised that man from the dead. {v: 17:31}

Paul didn't say the man's name in these verses. But remember, he had been talking about Jesus and the resurrection every day (17:17-18). {v: 17:31}

### Scene 7: Some Laughed, Some Believed

When the listeners heard about someone rising from the dead, some of them laughed at Paul. {v: 17:32}

Others said, **"We want to hear you again on this topic"** (17:32). {v: 17:32}

Then Paul left the meeting. {v: 17:33}

But some people went with him and believed. One was Dionysius, a member of the council. Another was a woman named Damaris. Others believed too. {v: 17:34}

Paul had come to Athens alone, sent away from two cities. He left with new brothers and sisters, even in Athens. {v: 17:34}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Acts 17:16-34 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Deeply disturbed in his spirit"** (17:16) | Paul was upset and troubled. God's people were never to make idols (Exodus 20:4). |
| **"God-fearing Gentiles"** (17:17) | People who were not Jewish but worshiped Israel's God. |
| **"This babbler"** (17:18) | An insult. It meant someone who repeats scraps of ideas he doesn't understand. |
| **"TO AN UNKNOWN GOD"** (17:23) | An altar for a god whose name the people didn't know. Paul used it as a door to talk about the true God. |
| **"Does not live in temples made by human hands"** (17:24) | God is bigger than any building. Solomon said the same when he built God's temple (1 Kings 8:27). |
| **"He is not far from each one of us"** (17:27) | God is close to every person, even people who don't know him yet. |
| **"We are His offspring"** (17:28) | A line from a Greek poet. Every person comes from God and owes God their life. |
| **"Repent"** (17:30) | Turn around. Change direction and turn back to God. |
| **"By raising Him from the dead"** (17:31) | The resurrection of Jesus is God's proof that Jesus is the one he has chosen. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Athens.** Athens was the most famous city of ancient Greece. It was known for its thinkers and its art. In Paul's time it was part of the Roman Empire. (Easton's Bible Dictionary, "Athens")

**Epicureans and Stoics.** Epicureans taught that the best life was a calm, pleasant one, free from pain and fear. Stoics taught that people should accept whatever happens and live by reason, without being ruled by feelings. (Easton's Bible Dictionary, "Epicureans" and "Stoics")†

**The Areopagus.** The name means "the hill of Ares," a Greek god of war. The Romans called him Mars, so the hill is also called Mars Hill. The council that met there took its name from the hill. (Easton's Bible Dictionary, "Areopagus")†

**The poets.** "We are His offspring" comes from a Greek poet named Aratus. Many scholars think "In Him we live and move and have our being" comes from an older poet, Epimenides.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Paul quoted poets who wrote about Greek gods. Was he agreeing with them?** No. He borrowed their words to point to the true God, the way you might quote a song someone loves to start a conversation. He didn't say their gods were real. He said the God they were reaching for was the one who made the world (17:24).

**Did Paul's speech fail, since some people laughed?** No. Some laughed, some wanted to hear more, and some believed (17:32-34). That's how it often goes when people hear about Jesus. Acts even names two of the believers.

**Why doesn't Paul say "Jesus" in his speech?** In these verses, Paul calls him **"the Man He has appointed"** (17:31). But Acts tells us Paul had been talking about Jesus all along (17:18). The speech in Acts may also be a summary of a longer talk.†

**"God overlooked the ignorance of earlier times" (17:30). Does that mean God didn't care?** No. It means God was patient. God held back his judgment, waiting for the time when he would send Jesus. Now that Jesus has come, God calls everyone to turn to him (17:30).

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"All religions are just different paths up the same mountain."*

**What's true:** Paul said God made people to seek him (17:27). He took the people of Athens seriously, and he started with what they cared about.

**What the story is mainly about:** Paul didn't tell the people of Athens they were already on the right path. He said they were worshiping what they didn't know, and he told them who God is. He called everyone to turn to God (17:30). The true God made the world, gives us life and breath, and raised Jesus from the dead (17:24-31).

**Where Jesus is:** He is the man God raised from the dead (17:31). We don't climb up to God. In Jesus, God came down to us.

## ✝️ Good News

The people of Athens had built an altar to a god they didn't know. They were reaching into the dark.

God has set a day to judge the world (17:31). Guessing right about God is not what makes anyone ready for that day.

But God doesn't wait for us to find him. **"He is not far from each one of us"** (17:27). He gives every one of us life and breath and everything else (17:25).

And he has shown us who he is. He raised Jesus from the dead (17:31). The unknown God has shown his face. We see him in Jesus.

**You don't have to find God in the dark. God has come near to you in Jesus.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the *Heidelberg Disputation* (1518), Luther said we can't know God truly by studying the world and reasoning our way up to him. We know God truly in Christ, and most of all in his suffering and his cross. Paul started with creation in Athens, but he didn't stop there. He ended with the man God raised from the dead.†

## 🕊️ From the Wider Church

Augustine, a church leader in North Africa about 1,600 years ago, began his book *Confessions* with a prayer: "You have made us for yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in you." It's the same idea Paul taught in Athens. God made us to seek him (17:27).†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow a reading plan called the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Acts 17:22-31 on the Sixth Sunday of Easter in Year A.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The God people reach for in the dark has come near, and he made himself known by raising Jesus from the dead.**
