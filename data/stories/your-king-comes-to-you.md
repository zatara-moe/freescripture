# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Zechariah was a prophet and priest among the people who came home from exile in Babylon, around 520 BC (1:1, Ezra 5:1).

**What was happening.** Jerusalem was small and poor. The temple was being rebuilt. The people had no king of their own. They wondered if God's promises to David's family were finished.

**What this passage is.** A short, joyful promise. A king is coming, but not the kind anyone expected.

**How to read it.** If you know the story of Palm Sunday, listen closely. This is the prophecy Jesus acted out.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Your King Comes | 9:9 | Humble and riding on a donkey |
| 2. Peace to the Nations | 9:10 | The weapons of war will be broken |
| 3. Prisoners of Hope | 9:11-12 | God will set his people free |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Your King Comes

**"Rejoice greatly, O Daughter of Zion! Shout in triumph, O Daughter of Jerusalem!"** (9:9). {v: 9:9}

"Daughter of Zion" means the people of Jerusalem. {v: 9:9}

**"See, your King comes to you, righteous and victorious, humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey"** (9:9). {v: 9:9}

Kings who came to conquer rode war horses. This king comes on a young donkey. He comes in peace. {v: 9:9}

### Section 2: Peace to the Nations

God will take away the chariots and war horses. **"The bow of war will be broken"** (9:10). {v: 9:10}

**"Then He will proclaim peace to the nations"** (9:10). {v: 9:10}

His kingdom will reach **"from sea to sea"** (9:10), all the way to the ends of the earth. {v: 9:10}

### Section 3: Prisoners of Hope

**"As for you, because of the blood of My covenant, I will release your prisoners from the waterless pit"** (9:11). {v: 9:11}

A waterless pit was a dry well used as a prison. Joseph was thrown into one (Genesis 37:24). {v: 9:11}

**"Return to your stronghold, O prisoners of hope; even today I declare that I will restore to you double"** (9:12). {v: 9:12}

Prisoners of hope are people held tight by God's promise, even in hard times. {v: 9:12}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> On Palm Sunday, Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a donkey. Matthew and John both quote Zechariah 9:9 (Matthew 21:5, John 12:15).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Zechariah 9:9-12 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Rejoice greatly"** (9:9) | The coming of this king is very good news. |
| **"Humble and riding on a donkey"** (9:9) | This king comes gently, not to make war. |
| **"The bow of war will be broken"** (9:10) | His kingdom brings an end to war. |
| **"Proclaim peace to the nations"** (9:10) | His peace is for everyone. |
| **"The blood of My covenant"** (9:11) | God keeps his promise to his people. |
| **"Prisoners of hope"** (9:12) | People held by God's promise. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Donkeys** were ordinary animals for everyday work and travel. King David's son Solomon rode David's mule to be crowned (1 Kings 1:33).

**War horses and chariots** were signs of military power.

**After the exile,** Judah was a small province of the Persian Empire, with no king.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did Zechariah know about Jesus?** Zechariah spoke of a coming king. The Gospel writers saw that Jesus fulfilled it when he rode into Jerusalem.

**Why does the king ride a donkey and a colt?** It's Hebrew poetry saying the same thing twice: a young donkey, the foal of a donkey.

**What does "restore to you double" mean?** God will give back more than they lost.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God shows up with power and wipes out his enemies."*

**What's true:** God is mighty, and his kingdom will win.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God's King comes **"humble and riding on a donkey"** (9:9). He brings peace, not war, and frees prisoners by his covenant blood.

**Where Jesus is:** Riding into Jerusalem on Palm Sunday, on his way to the cross, where his blood made the new covenant.

## ✝️ Good News

The King is coming to you.

**"See, your King comes to you, righteous and victorious, humble and riding on a donkey"** (9:9).

He comes gently, he brings peace, and he sets prisoners free.

**God's King comes humble, riding on a donkey. He brings peace to the nations and sets free the prisoners of hope.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved this verse's words "your King comes to you." He said the gospel is that Christ comes to us, to serve us. We don't climb up to him.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Zechariah 9:9-12 in early July (Lectionary 14), alongside Jesus's words, "Come to Me, all you who are weary." Many churches also hear it on Palm Sunday and the First Sunday of Advent.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Rejoice! Your King comes to you, humble and riding on a donkey. He brings peace to the nations and sets free the prisoners of hope.**
