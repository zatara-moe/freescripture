# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's for.** God's people in exile in Babylon, far from home. Some had settled in and made money. Others were poor and hungry for hope.†

**What comes before.** Chapter 53 tells of God's servant who suffers for the sins of others. Chapter 54 promises God's everlasting love.

**What this passage is.** An invitation to a free feast. It sounds like someone calling out in a marketplace, but everything is free.

**How to read it.** This is the end of a long section of Isaiah. It closes with joy: mountains singing and trees clapping.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Come, Buy Without Money | 55:1-2 | God invites the thirsty to a free feast |
| 2. Listen and Live | 55:3-5 | God promises an everlasting covenant |
| 3. Seek the LORD | 55:6-7 | God will freely pardon |
| 4. My Ways Are Higher | 55:8-9 | God's thoughts are beyond ours |
| 5. Rain and Snow | 55:10-11 | God's word does what he sends it to do |
| 6. Trees Clap Their Hands | 55:12-13 | All creation celebrates |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Come, Buy Without Money

**"Come, all you who are thirsty, come to the waters; and you without money, come, buy, and eat!"** (55:1). {v: 55:1}

**"Come, buy wine and milk without money and without cost!"** (55:1). {v: 55:1}

It sounds impossible. How do you buy without money? God's gifts are free. {v: 55:1}

**"Why spend money on that which is not bread, and your labor on that which does not satisfy?"** (55:2). {v: 55:2}

People were working hard for things that didn't fill them up. {v: 55:2}

### Section 2: Listen and Live

**"Incline your ear and come to Me; listen, so that your soul may live"** (55:3). To incline your ear means to lean in and listen. {v: 55:3}

God promises an everlasting covenant, the faithful love he promised King David. {v: 55:3}

Nations that don't know God will come running. {v: 55:5}

### Section 3: Seek the LORD

**"Seek the LORD while He may be found; call on Him while He is near"** (55:6). {v: 55:6}

Anyone who has gone the wrong way can turn around and come back to God. {v: 55:7}

**"For He will freely pardon"** (55:7). To pardon means to forgive. {v: 55:7}

### Section 4: My Ways Are Higher

**"For My thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways My ways,"** declares the LORD (55:8). {v: 55:8}

**"For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so My ways are higher than your ways and My thoughts than your thoughts"** (55:9). {v: 55:9}

God's forgiveness is bigger than we can imagine. People might not forgive so freely. God does. {v: 55:8, 55:9}

### Section 5: Rain and Snow

Rain and snow fall from the sky. They don't go back up until they have watered the earth and made things grow. {v: 55:10}

**"So My word that proceeds from My mouth will not return to Me empty, but it will accomplish what I please, and it will prosper where I send it"** (55:11). {v: 55:11}

This is a comparison. When God speaks, something happens. His word always does its work. {v: 55:11}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Jesus told a parable about a farmer sowing seed. The seed is the word of God (Luke 8:11).

### Section 6: Trees Clap Their Hands

**"You will indeed go out with joy and be led forth in peace"** (55:12). {v: 55:12}

**"The mountains and hills will burst into song before you, and all the trees of the field will clap their hands"** (55:12). {v: 55:12}

Instead of thorns, beautiful trees will grow. {v: 55:13}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 55 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Come, all you who are thirsty"** (55:1) | Everyone who needs God is invited. |
| **"Without money and without cost"** (55:1) | God's gifts can't be bought. They are free. |
| **"That which does not satisfy"** (55:2) | Many things we chase leave us empty. |
| **"He will freely pardon"** (55:7) | God forgives generously. |
| **"My ways are higher than your ways"** (55:9) | God's mercy is bigger than ours. |
| **"My word... will not return to Me empty"** (55:11) | God's word always does what he sends it to do. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Water sellers called out in markets.** In dry lands, people sold water in the streets. Isaiah's invitation sounds like their call, but free.†

**The "everlasting covenant" with David** was God's promise that David's family would rule forever (2 Samuel 7:16).

**"The trees of the field will clap their hands"** has become a well-known worship song.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does this mean money is bad?** No. Isaiah is saying that the most important things, like forgiveness and life with God, can't be bought.

**"Seek the LORD while He may be found." Will God stop being findable?** God is always near. But Isaiah is urging people not to wait. Today is the day to come.†

**Do trees really clap?** No. This is poetry. It means even creation celebrates when God saves his people.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The thirsty | Empty | They spent money on what doesn't satisfy (55:2) |
| The wicked | Lost | They had gone their own way (55:7) |
| God's people | Joyful | They go out with joy (55:12) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What are you thirsty for?** Tap one.

- Something that satisfies :: God invites you to the feast that fills.
- Forgiveness :: "He will freely pardon." Come back.
- I'm broke :: The feast is without money. It's free.
- God's word :: It never comes back empty. Keep listening.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"You get what you pay for."*

**What's true:** Many things in life cost money or work.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The most important thing can't be bought. God offers life, forgiveness, and joy for free. All you can do is come, receive, and eat.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus said, **"If anyone is thirsty, let him come to Me and drink"** (John 7:37). He gives living water and the bread of life, free.

## ✝️ Good News

God throws a feast and says, "Come, it's free."

**"Come, all you who are thirsty, come to the waters"** (55:1).

Jesus paid the full price, so we can come without money. His forgiveness is free, and his word always does its work.

**God invites the thirsty to come and receive for free. He freely forgives, and his word never returns empty.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved Isaiah 55:11. He said God's word does its work by itself. Our job is to preach it, and God's word will accomplish what he sends it to do.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Isaiah 55:1-9 on the Third Sunday in Lent in Year C. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 55:10-13 in July (Lectionary 15) and Isaiah 55:1-5 in early August (Lectionary 18). It is also read at the Easter Vigil.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**"Come, all you who are thirsty." God's gifts are free, his forgiveness is generous, and his word never returns empty.**
