# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Jacob:** Abraham's grandson, a twin. Years ago, he tricked his brother Esau and ran away.
- **A man:** A mysterious stranger who wrestles with Jacob all night. Jacob comes to believe he has met God.
- **Jacob's family:** His two wives, Leah and Rachel, two servant women, and eleven sons.

**Setting**

- The Jabbok River, a stream east of the Jordan River, at night.

**When**

- Long before Moses, about 3,800 years ago by traditional dating.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Jacob had cheated his brother.** He tricked their father Isaac into giving him the blessing meant for Esau. Esau wanted to kill him, so Jacob ran away (27:41-43).

**On the way, God met Jacob in a dream.** God promised, **"I am with you, and I will watch over you wherever you go"** (28:15).

**Twenty years later, Jacob was going home.** He had a big family and many animals. But Esau was coming to meet him with four hundred men (32:6).

**Jacob was terrified.** He prayed, **"Please deliver me from the hand of my brother Esau"** (32:11).

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Alone in the dark | 32:22-24 | Jacob sends everyone across the river |
| 2. Wrestling until dawn | 32:24-26 | A man wrestles Jacob and hurts his hip |
| 3. A new name | 32:27-29 | Jacob becomes Israel |
| 4. Limping into the sunrise | 32:30-32 | Jacob says he has seen God face to face |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Alone in the Dark

That night, Jacob got up. He sent his wives, his servants, his eleven sons, and everything he owned across the river. {v: 32:22, 32:23}

**"So Jacob was left all alone"** (32:24). {v: 32:24}

### Scene 2: Wrestling Until Dawn

Then a man came and wrestled with him until daybreak. {v: 32:24}

The man saw that he could not win. So he touched Jacob's hip and put it out of joint. {v: 32:25}

**"Let me go, for it is daybreak,"** the man said (32:26). {v: 32:26}

But Jacob held on. **"I will not let you go unless you bless me"** (32:26). {v: 32:26}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture two shapes struggling in the dark by the rushing water. Hours pass. Jacob is hurt and exhausted, but he won't let go. The sky starts to turn gray.

### Scene 3: A New Name

**"What is your name?"** the man asked (32:27). {v: 32:27}

**"Jacob,"** he replied (32:27). The name Jacob sounds like the Hebrew word for "heel." It came to mean someone who trips people up and cheats.† {v: 32:27}

Saying his name was like admitting who he had been. {v: 32:27}

**"Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel, because you have struggled with God and with men, and you have prevailed"** (32:28). To prevail means to hold on and win. {v: 32:28}

Jacob asked the man's name. The man didn't tell him. But he blessed Jacob there. {v: 32:29}

### Scene 4: Limping Into the Sunrise

Jacob named the place Peniel, which means "face of God." {v: 32:30}

**"Indeed, I have seen God face to face, and yet my life was spared"** (32:30). {v: 32:30}

The sun rose as Jacob went on. He was limping because of his hip. {v: 32:31}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Genesis 32:22-32 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Jacob was left all alone"** (32:24) | Jacob had to face God by himself. |
| **"A man wrestled with him until daybreak"** (32:24) | Jacob's whole life had been a struggle. Now he struggled with God. |
| **"I will not let you go unless you bless me"** (32:26) | Jacob knew he needed God's blessing more than anything. |
| **"What is your name?"** (32:27) | Jacob had to admit who he was. |
| **"Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel"** (32:28) | God gave him a new name and a new start. |
| **"I have seen God face to face"** (32:30) | Jacob met God and lived. |
| **"He was limping"** (32:31) | Jacob carried a mark of that night for the rest of his life. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Names showed who a person was.** Changing someone's name meant changing who they were. God renamed Abram and Sarai too (17:5, 17:15).†

**The name Israel became the name of the whole nation.** Jacob's twelve sons became the twelve tribes of Israel.

**The next morning, Jacob met Esau.** Esau ran to meet him and hugged him, and they both wept (33:4).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did Jacob really beat God in a wrestling match?** God could have ended it with one touch, and he did touch Jacob's hip. God let Jacob hold on. The "win" was that Jacob refused to let go until God blessed him.†

**Who was the man?** The Bible calls him "a man." Jacob said he saw God face to face. The prophet Hosea said Jacob struggled with an angel (Hosea 12:3-4). Some Christians believe it was the Son of God before he was born as Jesus.†

**Does God hurt people?** God touched Jacob's hip, and Jacob limped. But God also blessed him and gave him a new name. Jacob's limp reminded him that God had met him.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Real faith never struggles."*

**What's true:** Faith trusts God.

**What the story is mainly about:** Jacob struggled with God all night, and God blessed him. The whole nation was named after this struggle: Israel. Faith sometimes looks like holding on to God in the dark and refusing to let go.

**Where Jesus is:** Jacob held on and asked for a blessing. Jesus is the one who brings God's blessing to every nation (Galatians 3:14). In Gethsemane, Jesus struggled in prayer all night too, so that we could be blessed.†

## ✝️ Good News

Jacob had been a cheat. God didn't give up on him. He met him in the dark, and blessed him.

**"Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel"** (32:28).

God gives new names and new starts. In baptism, God names you as his own child. You can hold on to him, because he is holding on to you.

**God met Jacob in the dark, blessed him, and gave him a new name. He doesn't give up on his people.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther wrote about this story in his lectures on Genesis. He said that sometimes God seems to fight against us, and faith keeps holding on to his promise anyway, until the blessing comes.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads Genesis 32:22-31 in October (Lectionary 29), next to Jesus's parable of the widow who kept asking. In Year A, it is read in August (Lectionary 18).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Jacob wrestled with God all night and would not let go. God blessed him and gave him a new name, Israel.**
