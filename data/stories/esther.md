# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Esther:** A young Jewish woman in Persia. Her parents had died. Her Hebrew name was Hadassah.
- **Mordecai:** Esther's older cousin, from the tribe of Benjamin. He raised Esther as his own daughter.
- **King Xerxes:** The king of Persia. Older Bibles call him Ahasuerus.
- **Haman:** The king's highest official. The Bible calls him "the enemy of the Jews" (3:10).
- **Hegai:** The palace official in charge of the young women.

**Setting**

- Susa, one of the capital cities of the Persian Empire. The king's palace had marble pillars, gold couches, and courtyards hung with blue and white cloth (1:6). Outside was the king's gate, where officials sat.

**When**

- About 2,500 years ago, after the exile, when many Jewish people lived far from Jerusalem.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Many Jewish people lived far from home.** Years before, Babylon's army had taken people from Jerusalem away as captives (2:6). Then Persia took over Babylon's empire. Some Jewish people went home, and many stayed (Ezra 1:1-3).

**The king was Xerxes.** Older Bibles call him Ahasuerus (Easton's Bible Dictionary). He ruled 127 provinces, from India to Cush in Africa (1:1).

**The king had removed his queen.** Xerxes held a long feast. When he was merry with wine, he ordered Queen Vashti to come and show off her beauty to his guests. She refused. The Bible doesn't say why. The king's advisers feared other wives would follow her example, so Vashti was removed as queen (1:10-19).

**God's name never appears in the book of Esther.** Not once. The book never mentions prayer, and no prophet speaks. Still, many readers see God at work in it, hidden behind everything that happens.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Taken to the Palace

King Xerxes had no queen. So officials in every province gathered beautiful young women for the king's harem in Susa. The harem was the part of the palace where the king's women lived. The one who pleased the king would be queen. {v: 2:1, 2:2, 2:3, 2:4}

In Susa lived a Jewish man named Mordecai. He had raised his young cousin as his own daughter, because her father and mother had died. Her name was Hadassah, and she was also called Esther. {v: 2:5, 2:7}

The king's order went out. **"Esther was also taken to the palace"** (2:8). She had little choice. {v: 2:8}

Hegai, the official in charge of the women, was pleased with Esther. He gave her beauty treatments, special food, and seven maids. {v: 2:9}

Mordecai had told Esther not to tell anyone she was Jewish. So she kept it secret. Every day Mordecai walked outside the harem courtyard to learn how she was. {v: 2:10, 2:11}

### Scene 2: Queen Esther

After a year of beauty treatments, each young woman was taken to the king for one night. She did not go back unless the king called for her by name. {v: 2:12, 2:13, 2:14}

When Esther's turn came, she asked only for what Hegai advised. Everyone who saw her liked her. {v: 2:15, 2:16}

The king loved Esther more than all the other women. He put the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti. {v: 2:15, 2:16, 2:17}

Esther was queen now. But she still kept it secret that she was Jewish, as Mordecai had told her. {v: 2:20}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Lamps glow on marble pillars. Blue and white curtains stir in the breeze, and servants hurry across the tiled courtyard carrying trays of food.

### Scene 3: A Plot at the Gate

Mordecai often sat at the king's gate. Two of the king's guards, Bigthan and Teresh, grew angry. They made a plan to kill King Xerxes. {v: 2:19, 2:21}

Mordecai found out and told Queen Esther. She told the king, giving Mordecai the credit. The report proved true, and both men were hanged. {v: 2:22, 2:23}

The whole thing was written down in the king's record book. But Mordecai got no reward. {v: 2:23, 6:3}

### Scene 4: Mordecai Will Not Bow

Then the king promoted a man named Haman. The king made Haman more important than all the other princes. {v: 3:1}

The king commanded everyone at the gate to bow to Haman. **"But Mordecai would not bow down or pay homage"** (3:2). {v: 3:2}

Day after day the other servants warned him. Then they reported him to Haman. {v: 3:3, 3:4}

The Bible doesn't say why Mordecai would not bow. But remember: he had told them he was a Jew (3:4). {v: 3:4}

Haman was filled with rage. Punishing one man was not enough for him. He decided to destroy all of Mordecai's people, the Jews, in the whole kingdom. {v: 3:5, 3:6}

### Scene 5: The Order Goes Out

Haman had the Pur, or lot, cast to choose a day. It was a way of choosing by chance. The lot picked a day almost a year away, in the month of Adar. {v: 3:7}

Haman went to the king. Without naming the Jews, he spoke of a certain people scattered through the kingdom. **"Their laws are different from everyone else's, and they do not obey the king's laws"** (3:8). He said the king should not let them live. {v: 3:8}

Haman offered a huge amount of silver to pay for it. The king handed Haman his signet ring, the king's seal. "These people are given to you," the king said. {v: 3:9, 3:10, 3:11}

Letters went out to every province. The order was **"to destroy, kill, and annihilate all the Jews"** (3:13). It included young and old, women and children, all on one single day. {v: 3:12, 3:13, 3:14}

**"Then the king and Haman sat down to drink, but the city of Susa was in confusion"** (3:15). {v: 3:15}

### Scene 6: Mordecai Grieves

Mordecai tore his clothes and put on sackcloth and ashes. Sackcloth was rough cloth people wore when they grieved. He wailed loudly in the city. {v: 4:1, 4:2}

Jewish people in every province mourned and fasted. To fast means to go without food, often while praying or grieving. {v: 4:3}

Esther's servants told her about Mordecai. She was deeply upset. She sent him clothes, but he would not take them. {v: 4:4}

Through a palace official named Hathach, Mordecai sent Esther a copy of the order. He asked her to go to the king and beg for her people. {v: 4:5, 4:6, 4:7, 4:8, 4:9}

### Scene 7: "For Such a Time as This"

Esther answered with a law everyone knew. Anyone who went to the king without being called would be put to death. The only way to live was if the king held out his gold scepter. A scepter is a rod a king holds to show his power. {v: 4:10, 4:11}

And the king had not called for Esther in thirty days. Going to him could cost her life. {v: 4:11}

Mordecai answered that the palace would not keep her safe. If she stayed silent, help for the Jews would come **"from another place"** (4:14). But she and her family would die. {v: 4:12, 4:13, 4:14}

Then Mordecai asked, **"And who knows if perhaps you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"** (4:14). {v: 4:14}

Mordecai did not name God. But many readers believe his words point to God, working unseen. {v: 4:14}

### Scene 8: "If I Perish, I Perish"

Esther told Mordecai to gather all the Jews in Susa. They should fast for her for three days and nights. She and her servants would fast too. {v: 4:15, 4:16}

Then she would go to the king, even though it was against the law. **"And if I perish, I perish!"** (4:16). To perish means to die. {v: 4:16}

Mordecai went and did everything Esther told him. Before, Esther had obeyed Mordecai. Now Mordecai obeyed Esther. {v: 2:20, 4:17}

### Scene 9: The Gold Scepter

On the third day, Esther put on her royal robes and stood in the inner court, facing the throne. {v: 5:1}

The king saw Esther, and he was pleased with her. He held out the gold scepter. Esther came near and touched its tip. She would live. {v: 5:2}

"What is your request, Queen Esther?" the king asked. He offered her up to half the kingdom. This is an exaggeration. It meant, "Ask for anything." {v: 5:3}

Esther did not ask yet. She invited the king and Haman to a banquet. There she invited them to a second banquet the next day. The Bible doesn't say why she waited. {v: 5:4, 5:5, 5:6, 5:7, 5:8}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> A long hall gleams with gold. The throne faces the open doorway, and the only sound is the soft step of one woman's sandals.

### Scene 10: A Gallows for Mordecai

Haman left the banquet full of joy. Then he passed Mordecai at the gate, and Mordecai did not stand or tremble. Haman was filled with rage again. {v: 5:9}

At home, Haman bragged to his wife, Zeresh, and his friends about his wealth and honors. {v: 5:10, 5:11, 5:12}

Then Haman said, **"Yet none of this satisfies me as long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate"** (5:13). {v: 5:13}

His wife and friends told him to build a gallows about 80 feet high. A gallows is a tall frame for hanging a person. Then Haman could ask the king to hang Mordecai on it. Haman had it built. {v: 5:14}

### Scene 11: The King Cannot Sleep

**"That night sleep escaped the king"** (6:1). So he had the book of records read to him. {v: 6:1}

The reading came to the day Mordecai saved the king. "What honor has Mordecai received for this?" the king asked. "Nothing has been done for him," his servants said. {v: 6:2, 6:3}

At that moment, Haman walked in to ask the king to hang Mordecai. {v: 6:4, 6:5}

Here's what you know that almost nobody in this story knows: the king wanted to honor Mordecai. Haman wanted to kill him. {v: 6:3, 6:4}

The king asked Haman how to honor a man the king delighted in. Haman thought, **"Whom would the king be delighted to honor more than me?"** (6:6). So Haman described a great honor. The man would wear a royal robe and ride a royal horse in a parade through the city square. {v: 6:6, 6:7, 6:8, 6:9}

### Scene 12: Haman Honors Mordecai

"Hurry," the king told Haman. "Do all this for Mordecai the Jew." {v: 6:10}

So Haman dressed Mordecai in the robe. He led him on the horse through the city square. He shouted, **"This is what is done for the man whom the king is delighted to honor"** (6:11). {v: 6:11}

Afterward, Mordecai went back to the king's gate. {v: 6:12}

But Haman hurried home with his head covered. Covering the head was a sign of deep grief. His wife and advisers said his downfall had begun. Then the king's servants rushed Haman to Esther's second banquet. {v: 6:12, 6:13, 6:14}

### Scene 13: Esther Speaks

At the second banquet, the king asked again. "What is your request, Queen Esther?" {v: 7:1, 7:2}

Now Esther spoke. **"Grant me my life as my petition, and the lives of my people as my request"** (7:3). She said she and her people had been sold to be destroyed. {v: 7:3, 7:4}

"Who would do such a thing?" the king asked. {v: 7:5}

Esther answered, **"The adversary and enemy is this wicked man"** (7:6). It was Haman. {v: 7:6}

Haman stood in terror. {v: 7:6}

### Scene 14: Haman Falls

The king went out to the garden in fury. Haman stayed to beg Queen Esther for his life. {v: 7:7}

When the king came back, Haman had fallen onto the couch where Esther was. The king accused him of attacking the queen. {v: 7:8}

A servant said Haman had built a gallows for Mordecai, the man who saved the king. The king ordered Haman hanged on it. {v: 7:9}

**"So they hanged Haman on the gallows he had prepared for Mordecai"** (7:10). Then the king's anger calmed down. {v: 7:10}

### Scene 15: A New Letter

The king gave Haman's property to Esther. Esther told the king that Mordecai was her family. The king gave Mordecai his signet ring. Esther put Mordecai in charge of Haman's property. {v: 8:1, 8:2}

But Haman's order was still the law. Esther fell at the king's feet, crying. She begged him to stop Haman's evil plan. {v: 8:3}

The king held out the gold scepter again, and Esther stood up. She asked for a letter to cancel Haman's letters. **"How could I bear to see the destruction of my kindred?"** (8:6). {v: 8:4, 8:5, 8:6}

The king answered that a law sealed with his ring could not be canceled. But Esther and Mordecai could write a new one. {v: 8:7, 8:8}

So Mordecai wrote a new order in the king's name. It let the Jews in every city defend themselves against anyone who attacked them. Its words were as harsh as Haman's. {v: 8:9, 8:10, 8:11}

Mordecai came out in royal robes. Earlier, Susa had been in confusion. Now it shouted for joy. **"For the Jews it was a time of light and gladness, of joy and honor"** (8:16). {v: 3:15, 8:15, 8:16}

No one in this story spoke God's name. But the people who had fasted and wept were now feasting. {v: 4:3, 8:17}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Esther 2 to 8 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Esther did not reveal her people or her lineage"** (2:10) | Esther kept it secret that she was Jewish. Her lineage means her family line. |
| **"Haman son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews"** (3:10) | Haman is called an Agagite. Many readers connect this to Agag, an old enemy king of Israel (1 Samuel 15:8). |
| **"The Pur (that is, the lot)"** (3:7) | Haman chose the day by chance, like rolling dice. The holiday Purim is named after this word (9:26). |
| **"Sackcloth and ashes"** (4:1) | Rough cloth and ashes were signs of deep grief. Mordecai was mourning in public. |
| **"Relief and deliverance for the Jews will arise from another place"** (4:14) | Mordecai was sure the Jews would be rescued, even without Esther. Many readers believe these words quietly point to God. |
| **"For such a time as this"** (4:14) | Maybe Esther became queen for this very moment. Mordecai said "who knows." He asked it as a question, not a promise. |
| **"If I perish, I perish"** (4:16) | Esther knew she might die. She went anyway. |
| **"Even up to half the kingdom"** (5:3) | An exaggeration. It was a king's polite way to say "ask for anything." |
| **"A gallows fifty cubits high"** (5:14) | About 80 feet high. Some scholars think it was a tall wooden pole, not a gallows with a rope. |
| **"Sealed with the royal signet ring cannot be revoked"** (8:8) | A Persian law with the king's seal could not be canceled. So the king allowed a second law to answer the first. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Ahasuerus is almost certainly the king history calls Xerxes.** He ruled Persia about 486 to 465 B.C. (Easton's Bible Dictionary)

**Susa was where the Persian kings had a palace.** Older Bibles call it Shushan. Most of the book of Esther happens there. (Easton's Bible Dictionary)

**A cubit was the length of the forearm,** from the elbow to the fingertips. Easton's gives it as about 20 inches. So fifty cubits is about 80 feet. (Easton's Bible Dictionary)

**Mordecai came from the family of Kish** (2:5). King Saul, Israel's first king, was the son of a man named Kish (1 Samuel 9:1-2). Haman is called an Agagite. Long before, Saul fought Agag, king of the Amalekites (1 Samuel 15:8). Many readers see an old conflict coming back in this story.

**Purim is still celebrated today.** Each year, Jewish communities read the whole book of Esther aloud. They do this in the month of Adar, usually in February or March. When Haman's name is read, the people cry out to blot out his name (Smith's Bible Dictionary). Today many use noisemakers too.† People send food to friends and give gifts to the poor, as Esther 9:22 says.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Why is God never named?** It's okay to notice things like this. Careful readers have asked about it for centuries. The book never says "God" or "the LORD." But it is full of turns no one planned. A king could not sleep on the right night. A forgotten good deed was read aloud at the right moment. Haman built a gallows and was hanged on it. Many readers see God working there, hidden. Luther taught that God often works in hidden ways, even through weakness and suffering. The From Luther box below says more.

**Was Esther choosing to be in the harem?** The text says she "was taken" (2:8). The king's officials gathered young women from every province. Esther had little choice. The Bible tells this part quietly and without blame.

**Were Haman's words about the Jews true?** No. Haman was angry at one man who would not bow. Then he blamed a whole people (3:6). He said their laws were different and they were a danger to the king (3:8). Words like Haman's have been used against Jewish people for thousands of years. Hatred of Jewish people is still real today. **Hatred of Jewish people is sin.** Christians have been guilty of it too, including Martin Luther in his later writings. In 1994, the ELCA said about Luther's words, "we reject this violent invective." It also expressed its "deep and abiding sorrow" for the harm. It called antisemitism "a contradiction and an affront to the Gospel." [Read our note on Luther and the Jewish people](/about/#luther-and-the-jewish-people).

**What happened on the thirteenth of Adar?** The story continues in chapter 9, and it is hard to read. The enemies of the Jews had hoped to overpower them that day. Instead, the Jews overpowered those who hated them (9:1). The Bible says they killed many people, including Haman's ten sons. It gives the number in the provinces as 75,000 (9:16). Esther asked the king for a second day of fighting in Susa. She also asked for the bodies of Haman's sons to be hanged (9:13). The Bible says the Jews did not take their enemies' goods, even though the new order allowed it (9:10, 9:15, 9:16). The new order's words about "women and children" (8:11) echo Haman's first order (3:13). Scholars read that verse in different ways. Christians have struggled with this passage for a long time. We do not have to call every killing good. The Bible tells what happened. When a story like this frightens us, Christians look to Jesus, the clearest picture of who God is. Jesus taught, **"love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you"** (Matthew 5:44). If this story brings up big feelings, talk with a parent, pastor, or trusted adult.

**Is Esther a model for obeying a husband?** The book does not teach that. Chapter 1 says the king's advisers wanted every wife to obey her husband because Vashti refused (1:20-22). That was the advice of the king's court. The Bible reports it. It does not praise it. The Bible doesn't say why Vashti refused. But remember: she was ordered to show off her beauty to a room of drinking men (1:10-11).

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God put you here for such a time as this. Be brave like Esther."*

**What's true:** Esther was brave. She risked her life for her people. Courage is a gift from God, and it is a good gift. God does place people where they can help others.

**What the story is mainly about:** A people who could not save themselves were saved. Esther was afraid. She was not powerful on her own. She needed the king's scepter held out to her, and she asked others to fast for her. Mordecai said "who knows," not "God promises." The story shows rescue coming through a sleepless night and a forgotten record. It also shows Haman's plan ending the opposite way from what he intended. God is the main character of the book, even though God is never named.

**Where Jesus is:** Esther went to the king uncalled and said, "if I perish, I perish." She lived. Jesus went to the cross for us, and he did die. Then God raised him. Now Jesus is with God and speaks to God for us. Paul writes that Christ **"is at the right hand of God—and He is interceding for us"** (Romans 8:34). We do not have to wait for a king to hold out a scepter to us. Because of Jesus, God always welcomes us.

## ✝️ Good News

God's name is missing from the book of Esther, but God was not missing. A plan to destroy a whole people was turned around. Mourning became joy.

Maybe you feel afraid, or not enough, or unsure that God sees you. Esther did not have to be enough. God does not need you to be enough either. In Jesus, God is no longer hidden. We see who God is in Jesus on the cross and at the empty tomb. The Holy Spirit gives us faith to trust him, even when we cannot see him working.

**God works even when he is hidden. In Jesus, he shows us who he is and keeps us as his own.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Heidelberg Disputation (1518), Luther taught what he called the theology of the cross. He said God is not found mainly in power and glory. God shows himself in what looks weak and hidden, above all in Jesus's suffering on the cross. The book of Esther fits this. God's name is never spoken. Yet proud Haman falls, and the frightened Jews are rescued.

To be honest, Luther himself did not think highly of the book of Esther. In *The Bondage of the Will* (1525), he wrote about Esther. He said that in his judgment it should not be counted as part of the Bible. Remarks recorded in his *Table Talk* show that part of his dislike of Esther was tied to his wrong views about Jewish people. We follow the ELCA in rejecting those views. Luther still kept Esther among the Old Testament books of his German Bible. Lutherans read it as Scripture today.

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. Some churches read the Old Testament in order during Year B. They hear Esther 7:1-6, 7:9-10, and 9:20-22 around the end of September (Lectionary 26, also called Proper 21).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Even when his name is never spoken, God keeps his people and turns their mourning into joy.**
