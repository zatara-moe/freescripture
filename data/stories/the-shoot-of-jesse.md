# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Isaiah was a prophet in Jerusalem, about 740 to 700 BC (Isaiah 1:1).

**What was happening.** Assyria, a cruel empire, was cutting down nations like a forester cuts down trees. Just before this chapter, Isaiah says God will chop down the proud like a forest (10:33-34).†

**What this passage is.** A promise. After the trees are cut down, something small and green will grow. It is poetry.

**Who Jesse is.** Jesse was King David's father. Saying "Jesse" instead of "David" reminds us that David's family started out small, with a shepherd in Bethlehem.†

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A Shoot From a Stump | 11:1 | A new king will grow from David's family |
| 2. The Spirit Will Rest on Him | 11:2-3 | God's Spirit will fill him |
| 3. Fair to the Poor | 11:4-5 | He will judge with justice |
| 4. The Wolf and the Lamb | 11:6-9 | His kingdom will be full of peace |
| 5. A Banner for the Nations | 11:10 | All peoples will come to him |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: A Shoot From a Stump

**"Then a shoot will spring up from the stump of Jesse, and a Branch from his roots will bear fruit"** (11:1). {v: 11:1}

Picture a tree cut down, with only a stump left. It looks dead. Then a tiny green shoot pushes up. {v: 11:1}

This is a comparison. David's royal family would be cut down. But God would raise up a new King from it. {v: 11:1}

### Section 2: The Spirit Will Rest on Him

**"The Spirit of the LORD will rest on Him"** (11:2). {v: 11:2}

Isaiah describes the Spirit's gifts: wisdom and understanding, counsel and strength, knowledge and the fear of the LORD. {v: 11:2}

The "fear of the LORD" means deep respect and trust in God. This King will delight in it. {v: 11:3}

He won't judge by what he sees or hears on the surface. He will see the truth. {v: 11:3}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> When Jesus was baptized, the Holy Spirit came down on him like a dove (Matthew 3:16).

### Section 3: Fair to the Poor

**"But with righteousness He will judge the poor, and with equity He will decide for the lowly of the earth"** (11:4). {v: 11:4}

Equity means fairness. The weakest people will finally get a fair hearing. {v: 11:4}

His words will be strong enough to stop the wicked. Righteousness and faithfulness will be like the belt he wears. {v: 11:4, 11:5}

### Section 4: The Wolf and the Lamb

Then Isaiah paints a picture of peace. {v: 11:6}

**"The wolf will live with the lamb, and the leopard will lie down with the goat"** (11:6). {v: 11:6}

**"And a little child will lead them"** (11:6). {v: 11:6}

The cow and the bear will eat together. The lion will eat straw like an ox. A baby will play safely by a snake's hole. {v: 11:7, 11:8}

**"They will neither harm nor destroy on all My holy mountain, for the earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the sea is full of water"** (11:9). {v: 11:9}

### Section 5: A Banner for the Nations

**"On that day the Root of Jesse will stand as a banner for the peoples"** (11:10). {v: 11:10}

A banner is a flag on a pole that people can see from far away and gather around. {v: 11:10}

People from every nation will come looking for him. {v: 11:10}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Jesus calls himself **"the Root and the Offspring of David"** (Revelation 22:16).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 11:1-10 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"A shoot will spring up from the stump of Jesse"** (11:1) | New life from what looked dead. |
| **"The Spirit of the LORD will rest on Him"** (11:2) | This King is filled with God's Spirit. |
| **"With righteousness He will judge the poor"** (11:4) | He will be fair to people others ignore. |
| **"The wolf will live with the lamb"** (11:6) | Enemies will live in peace. |
| **"A little child will lead them"** (11:6) | In his kingdom, power isn't about being big. |
| **"The earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD"** (11:9) | Everyone will know God. |
| **"A banner for the peoples"** (11:10) | People of every nation will come to him. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**David's family lost the throne.** About 150 years after Isaiah, Babylon conquered Jerusalem. No descendant of David ruled as king after that.†

**The "Jesse tree" is an Advent tradition.** Families hang ornaments for Bible stories, from creation to Jesus, on a branch.†

**Matthew's Gospel begins with Jesus's family line through Jesse and David** (Matthew 1:5-6).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Will lions really eat straw?** Isaiah is using pictures to describe a world with no more harm. We don't know exactly what the new creation will look like. We know it will be full of peace.†

**Does this mean animals in heaven?** The Bible promises a new creation (Revelation 21:1). Isaiah's picture includes animals. Beyond that, the Bible doesn't say much.†

**Was Isaiah talking about Jesus?** The New Testament says yes. Paul quotes verse 10 about Jesus bringing hope to all nations (Romans 15:12).

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"When something is gone, it's gone forever."*

**What's true:** Real losses happen. David's family really did lose its throne.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God can bring new life from a dead stump. He did it with David's family by sending Jesus. He brings new life where things look finished.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus is the shoot from Jesse's stump, filled with the Spirit, fair to the poor, and the banner for every nation.

## ✝️ Good News

The tree was cut down. But God made something new grow.

**"Then a shoot will spring up from the stump of Jesse"** (11:1).

Jesus is that shoot. He brings justice for the poor and peace for the world. One day, nothing will harm or destroy anymore.

**From a dead stump, God raised up Jesus, the King full of the Spirit. His kingdom brings justice and peace.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther noted that Isaiah says the King comes from Jesse, a humble shepherd, not from David the great king. He said God likes to work from small and lowly beginnings.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 11:1-10 on the Second Sunday of Advent.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**From the stump of Jesse, God will raise a King full of his Spirit. In his kingdom, the wolf will live with the lamb.**
