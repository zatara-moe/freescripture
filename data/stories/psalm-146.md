# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**What it is.** One of the five "Hallelujah Psalms" that close the book of Psalms (146-150). Each begins and ends with Hallelujah — "Praise the LORD."

**What it's about.** Do not put your trust in princes. Instead, trust the God of Jacob — the one who made everything, keeps faith forever, feeds the hungry, frees prisoners, and loves the righteous.

**When the church sings it.** On Sundays in Ordinary Time, especially when the readings touch on what God's kingdom looks like or what leaders are for.

**How to read it.** Notice what God does. The list is specific: justice for the oppressed, food for the hungry, sight for the blind, support for the widow. These are not abstract — they are what God is known for.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Hallelujah

**"Hallelujah! Praise the LORD, O my soul"** (146:1). {v: 146:1}

**"I will praise the LORD all my life; I will sing praises to my God while I have my being"** (146:2). {v: 146:2}

All my life. While I have breath. Praise is not occasional — it is what the whole life is for. {v: 146:2}

### Section 2: Not Princes

**"Put not your trust in princes, in mortal man, who cannot save"** (146:3). {v: 146:3}

**"When his spirit departs, he returns to the ground; on that very day his plans perish"** (146:4). {v: 146:4}

Every human leader, however great, dies. And when they die, their plans die with them. {v: 146:4}

This is not cynicism. It is clarity about where real help comes from. {v: 146:4}

### Section 3: The God of Jacob

**"Blessed is he whose help is the God of Jacob, whose hope is in the LORD his God,"** (146:5) **"the Maker of heaven and earth, the sea, and everything in them. He remains faithful forever"** (146:6). {v: 146:5, 146:6}

God made everything and does not run out of faithfulness. {v: 146:5, 146:6}

**"He executes justice for the oppressed and gives food to the hungry. The LORD sets the prisoners free,"** (146:7) **"the LORD opens the eyes of the blind, the LORD lifts those who are weighed down, the LORD loves the righteous"** (146:8). {v: 146:7, 146:8}

**"The LORD protects foreigners; He sustains the fatherless and the widow, but the ways of the wicked He frustrates"** (146:9). {v: 146:9}

Each line names someone vulnerable — the oppressed, the hungry, the prisoner, the blind, the bowed down, the foreigner, the orphan, the widow. The LORD is for them. {v: 146:9}

**"The LORD reigns forever, your God, O Zion, for all generations. Hallelujah!"** (146:10). {v: 146:10}

Princes die. The LORD reigns forever. {v: 146:10}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 146 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Put not your trust in princes"** (146:3) | Human power is real but limited. |
| **"On that very day his plans perish"** (146:4) | No human plan outlasts death. |
| **"The God of Jacob"** (146:5) | Jacob was a struggling, ordinary man — God is his God. |
| **"He remains faithful forever"** (146:6) | God's commitment does not expire. |
| **"The LORD sets the prisoners free"** (146:7) | God is on the side of those who cannot free themselves. |
| **"The LORD reigns forever"** (146:10) | Every human reign ends; His does not. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"Foreigners, fatherless and widow"** — these three groups were the most vulnerable in ancient society, with no land or tribe to protect them. The law of Israel repeatedly required care for them (Deuteronomy 10:18).

**"Princes"** (146:3) meant kings, rulers, nobles — the powerful. The psalm is saying: even the most powerful human being cannot save you the way God can.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is this saying we should never vote, trust leaders, or obey governments?** No. It is saying not to *trust* in them as your ultimate hope. Leaders are limited and mortal. God is not.

**Does the LORD always free prisoners and feed the hungry right now?** The psalm is describing what God is known for — the pattern of his character. In Jesus, this list becomes a mission statement (Luke 4:18-19).

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Put your hope in the right leaders — they can fix everything."*

**What's true:** Leaders matter; their decisions affect lives.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"Put not your trust in princes, in mortal man, who cannot save"** (146:3). Only God saves. Leaders are stewards, not saviors. The LORD is the one who reigns forever.

**Where Jesus is:** When John's disciples asked if Jesus was the one to come, he answered by listing what he was doing: blind see, lame walk, lepers cleansed, deaf hear, dead raised, poor hear good news (Matthew 11:4-5). He was quoting the work of Psalm 146's God. He is that God.

## ✝️ Good News

The LORD lifts those who are weighed down.

**"He remains faithful forever"** (146:6).

Princes die and their plans perish. The LORD reigns — for all generations, including yours.

**Psalm 146 begins and ends with Hallelujah. In between: do not trust princes — they die. Trust the God who made everything, remains faithful forever, and lifts the weighed down. The LORD reigns forever.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said the First Commandment — "You shall have no other gods before me" — means no person, power, or thing should take the place of God as our ultimate trust and hope.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 146 on the Twenty-Second Sunday in Ordinary Time in Year C, the Twenty-Sixth Sunday in Ordinary Time in Year B, and on other Sundays throughout the year.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Hallelujah! Do not trust princes — they die. Trust the God of Jacob, who made everything, remains faithful forever, feeds the hungry, frees prisoners, and lifts the weighed down. The LORD reigns forever.**
