# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Zephaniah was a prophet in Jerusalem around 630 BC, during the reign of King Josiah (Zephaniah 1:1).

**What comes before.** Most of Zephaniah's short book is a hard warning about "the day of the LORD," a day of judgment on sin (1:14-15).

**What this passage is.** The ending of the book, and a complete turnaround. Judgment gives way to singing. God himself sings.

**How to read it.** Read it out loud. It's a song of joy.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Sing for Joy | 3:14-15 | God has taken away the punishment |
| 2. Do Not Fear | 3:16-17 | God is among you and sings over you |
| 3. I Will Gather You | 3:18-20 | God brings his scattered people home |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Sing for Joy

**"Sing for joy, O Daughter of Zion; shout aloud, O Israel! Be glad and rejoice with all your heart"** (3:14). {v: 3:14}

The Daughter of Zion means the people of Jerusalem. {v: 3:14}

Why rejoice? **"The LORD has taken away your punishment; He has turned back your enemy"** (3:15). {v: 3:15}

**"Israel's King, the LORD, is among you; no longer will you fear any harm"** (3:15). {v: 3:15}

### Section 2: Do Not Fear

**"Do not fear, O Zion; do not let your hands fall limp"** (3:16). Hands fall limp when we're scared or give up. {v: 3:16}

**"The LORD your God is among you; He is mighty to save"** (3:17). {v: 3:17}

Then comes something amazing. {v: 3:17}

**"He will rejoice over you with gladness; He will quiet you with His love; He will rejoice over you with singing"** (3:17). {v: 3:17}

Usually God's people sing to God. Here, God sings over his people. Like a parent singing over a child at bedtime, quieting them with love. {v: 3:17}

### Section 3: I Will Gather You

God will gather people who grieve. {v: 3:18}

**"I will save the lame and gather the scattered"** (3:19). {v: 3:19}

People who were ashamed will be praised. {v: 3:19}

**"At that time I will bring you in; yes, at that time I will gather you"** (3:20). {v: 3:20}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Zephaniah 3:14-20 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The LORD has taken away your punishment"** (3:15) | God has dealt with their sin. |
| **"Israel's King, the LORD, is among you"** (3:15) | God lives with his people. |
| **"He is mighty to save"** (3:17) | God is strong enough to rescue. |
| **"He will quiet you with His love"** (3:17) | God calms his people's fears. |
| **"He will rejoice over you with singing"** (3:17) | God delights in his people. |
| **"I will save the lame and gather the scattered"** (3:19) | God brings home the weak and the lost. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**King Josiah led a great reform.** He tore down idols and brought back the Passover (2 Kings 23).†

**"The day of the LORD"** was a day God would act to judge and to save. Zephaniah's book is full of it.

**Gaudete Sunday.** The Third Sunday of Advent is sometimes called Gaudete, Latin for "rejoice," because its readings are full of joy.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God really sing?** This is a picture of God's joy over his people. The Bible describes God with feelings: joy, love, sorrow.

**Did their troubles all end?** Not right away. Zephaniah looks ahead to what God will do. Christians see it beginning in Jesus and completed when he comes again.†

**Is God happy with me?** In Jesus, God delights in you. He doesn't just put up with you. He rejoices over you.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| God's people | Joyful | Their punishment was taken away (3:14-15) |
| God's people | Afraid | Their hands had fallen limp (3:16) |
| God | Delighted | He rejoices over his people with singing (3:17) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God is always disappointed in me."*

**What's true:** God takes sin seriously. Zephaniah warned about it.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God took the punishment away. Now he is among his people, mighty to save, and he rejoices over them with singing. God delights in the people he saves.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus told of a shepherd who carries a lost sheep home rejoicing, and of joy in heaven over one sinner who repents (Luke 15:5-7).

## ✝️ Good News

After all the warnings, God sings.

**"The LORD your God is among you; He is mighty to save. He will rejoice over you with gladness; He will quiet you with His love; He will rejoice over you with singing"** (3:17).

Jesus took our punishment away. Now God is with us, and he delights in us.

**God has taken away our punishment. He is among us, mighty to save, and he rejoices over his people with singing.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that the gospel is a joyful sound. Because Christ has taken away our sin, God's people can sing and rejoice.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads Zephaniah 3:14-20 on the Third Sunday of Advent.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Sing for joy! God has taken away our punishment. He is among us, mighty to save, and he rejoices over us with singing.**
