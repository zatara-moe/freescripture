# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Hannah:** A woman who longs for a child. She has none.
- **Elkanah:** Hannah's husband. He loves her very much.
- **Peninnah:** Elkanah's other wife. She has children, and she is cruel to Hannah.
- **Eli:** The old priest at the house of the LORD in Shiloh.
- **Samuel:** Hannah's son, born in answer to her prayer.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- Ramah, Hannah's hometown, and Shiloh, where God's house (the tabernacle) stood.

**When**

- More than 1,000 years before Jesus, before Israel had a king.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Israel had no king yet.** Leaders called judges led the people. It was a time when many people did whatever they wanted (Judges 21:25).

**God's house was at Shiloh.** Families traveled there every year to worship and offer sacrifices.

**Having children mattered a great deal.** A woman with no children was often pitied or looked down on.†

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Year after year | 1:1-8 | Hannah is taunted and weeps |
| 2. A silent prayer | 1:9-18 | Hannah pours out her heart to God |
| 3. "I asked the LORD for him" | 1:19-28 | Samuel is born and given to God |
| 4. Hannah's song | 2:1-11 | Hannah praises God |
| 5. A little robe every year | 2:18-21, 26 | Samuel grows up at God's house |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Year After Year

Elkanah had two wives, Hannah and Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had none. {v: 1:1, 1:2}

Every year the family went to Shiloh to worship the LORD. {v: 1:3}

Elkanah loved Hannah, and gave her a double portion of the meal. {v: 1:4, 1:5}

But Peninnah teased Hannah cruelly about having no children. It happened year after year, until Hannah cried and wouldn't eat. {v: 1:6, 1:7}

Elkanah asked, **"Hannah, why are you crying?"** He asked, **"Am I not better to you than ten sons?"** (1:8). {v: 1:8}

### Scene 2: A Silent Prayer

After the meal, Hannah got up and went to the house of the LORD. Eli the priest was sitting by the doorway. {v: 1:9}

**"In her bitter distress, Hannah prayed to the LORD and wept with many tears"** (1:10). {v: 1:10}

She made a promise. If God would give her a son, she would give him back to the LORD for his whole life. {v: 1:11}

She was praying in her heart. Her lips moved, but no sound came out. {v: 1:12, 1:13}

Eli thought she was drunk! **"Put away your wine!"** he said (1:14). {v: 1:14}

**"No, my lord,"** Hannah replied. **"I have poured out my soul before the LORD"** (1:15). {v: 1:15}

**"Go in peace,"** Eli replied, **"and may the God of Israel grant the petition you have asked of Him"** (1:17). A petition is a request. {v: 1:17}

Hannah went on her way and began eating again, **"and her face was no longer downcast"** (1:18). Downcast means sad. {v: 1:18}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture Hannah walking out of God's house. Her eyes are still red from crying. But she sits down with her family and picks up some bread.

### Scene 3: "I Asked the LORD for Him"

The LORD remembered Hannah. In time, she had a son. {v: 1:19, 1:20}

She named him Samuel, **"Because I have asked for him from the LORD"** (1:20). {v: 1:20}

When Samuel was old enough to stop nursing, Hannah brought him to Shiloh. {v: 1:24}

She said to Eli, **"I am the woman who stood here beside you praying to the LORD"** (1:26). {v: 1:26}

**"I prayed for this boy, and since the LORD has granted me what I asked of Him, I now dedicate the boy to the LORD"** (1:27-28). {v: 1:27, 1:28}

### Scene 4: Hannah's Song

Then Hannah prayed a song of praise. {v: 2:1}

**"My heart rejoices in the LORD"** (2:1). {v: 2:1}

**"There is no one holy like the LORD. Indeed, there is no one besides You! And there is no Rock like our God"** (2:2). {v: 2:2}

She sang that God turns things upside down. The strong are made weak. The weak are made strong. The hungry are filled. {v: 2:4, 2:5}

**"He raises the poor from the dust and lifts the needy from the ash heap"** (2:8). {v: 2:8}

### Scene 5: A Little Robe Every Year

Samuel stayed at Shiloh and served the LORD with Eli. {v: 2:11, 2:18}

**"Each year his mother would make him a little robe and bring it to him"** (2:19). {v: 2:19}

Eli blessed Hannah and Elkanah. God gave Hannah three more sons and two daughters. {v: 2:20, 2:21}

**"And the boy Samuel continued to grow in stature and in favor with the LORD and with man"** (2:26). {v: 2:26}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Samuel 1:1 to 2:26 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Year after year"** (1:7) | Hannah's sadness went on for a long time. |
| **"Wept with many tears"** (1:10) | Hannah was honest with God about her pain. |
| **"I have poured out my soul before the LORD"** (1:15) | Prayer can be crying out everything to God. |
| **"Because I have asked for him from the LORD"** (1:20) | Samuel's name reminded Hannah that God heard her. |
| **"There is no Rock like our God"** (2:2) | God is strong and steady, someone to lean on. |
| **"He raises the poor from the dust"** (2:8) | God lifts up people who are low. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Having two wives happened in those days.** The Bible often shows how much pain it caused, as in this story.†

**Children were often nursed until age two or three.** That's when Hannah brought Samuel to Shiloh.†

**Mary's song sounds like Hannah's.** When Mary was expecting Jesus, she sang that God **"has filled the hungry with good things"** (Luke 1:53). Her song echoes Hannah's.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did Hannah give her son away?** She gave him to serve God, as she had promised. She visited him every year and made him a new robe. Samuel became one of Israel's greatest prophets.

**If I pray hard enough, will I get what I ask for?** God heard Hannah, and he hears you. Sometimes his answer is different from what we ask. Hannah's prayer is a picture of trusting God with our deepest longings.

**Why was Hannah teased?** People sometimes treat others cruelly over things they can't control. If you or someone you love is longing for a child, God sees that sorrow.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hannah | Heartbroken | She had no children and was teased (1:7) |
| Eli | Mistaken | He thought she was drunk (1:14) |
| Hannah | Peaceful | She trusted God with her prayer (1:18) |
| Hannah | Joyful | God gave her a son (2:1) |

# Why It Matters

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"You have to pray with the right words."*

**What's true:** God gives us words to pray, like the Psalms and the Lord's Prayer.

**What the story is mainly about:** Hannah didn't have perfect words. She cried, and her lips moved without sound. Even the priest misunderstood her. But God heard. Prayer is pouring out your heart to God.

**Where Jesus is:** Hannah sang about God lifting up the lowly. Many years later, Mary sang a song much like hers, because God was sending Jesus, who lifts up the lowly forever.

## ✝️ Good News

Hannah's sorrow went on for years. God remembered her.

**"There is no one holy like the LORD... And there is no Rock like our God"** (2:2).

God hears people who weep. He raises the poor from the dust. In Jesus, he came down to lift us up.

**God heard Hannah's tearful prayer. He lifts up the lowly, and there is no Rock like our God.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that God looks at the low and humble. In his explanation of Mary's song, he said God's way is to lift up people who are nothing in the world's eyes.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads 1 Samuel 1:4-20 in November (Lectionary 33), with Hannah's song in place of a psalm. In Year C, it reads 1 Samuel 2:18-20 and 26 on the First Sunday of Christmas, next to the boy Jesus in the temple.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Hannah poured out her heart to God, and he gave her a son, Samuel. God hears people who weep, and lifts up the lowly.**
