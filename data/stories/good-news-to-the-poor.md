# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's for.** These words speak to God's people after they came home from exile in Babylon. Jerusalem was still in ruins, and people were poor and grieving.†

**Who is speaking.** Someone filled with God's Spirit speaks in the first person: "The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on Me." The speaker isn't named.†

**What this passage is.** A job description for God's chosen messenger, and a song of joy.

**Why it matters.** Jesus read these words out loud in his hometown synagogue. Then he said they were coming true (Luke 4:21).

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Spirit Is on Me | 61:1-3 | A messenger brings good news to the hurting |
| 2. Rebuilding the Ruins | 61:4-7 | Ruined places will be restored |
| 3. God Loves Justice | 61:8-9 | God makes an everlasting covenant |
| 4. Clothed in Salvation | 61:10-11 | The people sing for joy |
| 5. A New Name | 62:1-3 | God will not rest until Jerusalem shines |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Spirit Is on Me

**"The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on Me, because the LORD has anointed Me to preach good news to the poor"** (61:1). {v: 61:1}

To anoint means to pour oil on someone to show God has chosen them. {v: 61:1}

He is sent **"to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives and freedom to the prisoners"** (61:1). {v: 61:1}

To bind up means to bandage a wound. {v: 61:1}

He will comfort everyone who mourns. He will give them **"a crown of beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, and a garment of praise for a spirit of despair"** (61:3). {v: 61:2, 61:3}

People in grief put ashes on their heads. God will replace the ashes with a crown. {v: 61:3}

They will be called **"oaks of righteousness, the planting of the LORD"** (61:3). Oaks are strong trees with deep roots. {v: 61:3}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> In Nazareth, Jesus read these words, rolled up the scroll, and said, **"Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing"** (Luke 4:21).

### Section 2: Rebuilding the Ruins

The people will rebuild cities that have been ruined for generations. {v: 61:4}

Instead of shame, they will receive a double portion. Instead of being humiliated, they will rejoice. {v: 61:7}

They will be called **"the priests of the LORD"** (61:6). {v: 61:6}

### Section 3: God Loves Justice

**"For I, the LORD, love justice"** (61:8). {v: 61:8}

God hates robbery and wrong. He will make an everlasting covenant with his people. A covenant is a promise that binds two sides together. {v: 61:8}

Everyone will see that they are a people the LORD has blessed. {v: 61:9}

### Section 4: Clothed in Salvation

Then the speaker sings for joy. {v: 61:10}

**"I will rejoice greatly in the LORD, my soul will exult in my God; for He has clothed me with garments of salvation and wrapped me in a robe of righteousness"** (61:10). {v: 61:10}

To exult means to celebrate with great joy. {v: 61:10}

It's like a bride and groom dressed for their wedding. {v: 61:10}

Like seeds growing in a garden, God will make righteousness and praise spring up before all nations. {v: 61:11}

### Section 5: A New Name

**"For Zion's sake I will not keep silent, and for Jerusalem's sake I will not keep still, until her righteousness shines like a bright light"** (62:1). {v: 62:1}

Jerusalem will get a new name from God himself. {v: 62:2}

**"You will be a crown of glory in the hand of the LORD"** (62:3). {v: 62:3}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 61:1 to 62:3 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on Me"** (61:1) | God's chosen messenger is filled with his Spirit. |
| **"To preach good news to the poor"** (61:1) | God's good news is for people with nothing. |
| **"To bind up the brokenhearted"** (61:1) | God cares for people who are hurting inside. |
| **"A crown of beauty for ashes"** (61:3) | God turns grief into joy. |
| **"I, the LORD, love justice"** (61:8) | God cares about what is fair and right. |
| **"Garments of salvation"** (61:10) | God dresses his people in his own goodness. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"The year of the LORD's favor" may echo the Year of Jubilee.** Every fiftieth year, God's law said, debts were canceled and land was returned (Leviticus 25:10).†

**Ashes were a sign of grief.** People in mourning sat in ashes or put them on their heads.†

**Mary's song echoes this hope.** Mary sang that God lifts up the humble and fills the hungry (Luke 1:52-53).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Who is "Me"?** Isaiah doesn't name him. Jesus read these words and said they were fulfilled in him (Luke 4:21). Christians believe Jesus is the anointed one.

**Does this mean prisoners should all go free?** The promise is about freedom from everything that binds people: sin, death, and injustice. God cares about people in prison, too (Matthew 25:36).†

**Are we really dressed in salvation?** This is a comparison. God covers us with his goodness, like a beautiful robe, so we can stand before him.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The poor | Hopeless | They had nothing (61:1) |
| The mourners | Grieving | Their city was in ruins (61:2-3) |
| God's people | Joyful | God clothed them in salvation (61:10) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God helps those who help themselves."*

**What's true:** God gives us work to do and strength to do it.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God's good news is for the poor, the brokenhearted, the captives, and the grieving, the people who can't help themselves. God helps them first.

**Where Jesus is:** In a synagogue in Nazareth, reading these words and saying, "Today this is fulfilled."

## ✝️ Good News

God sent his anointed one with good news for the poor.

**"He has sent Me to bind up the brokenhearted"** (61:1).

Jesus is that anointed one. He binds up broken hearts, sets captives free, and clothes us in his own righteousness.

**God's chosen one brings good news to the poor and joy for ashes. Jesus said, "Today this is fulfilled."**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that our own goodness is like dirty rags, but Christ covers us with his own righteousness, like a robe. Isaiah 61:10 was one of the verses he loved for this.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Isaiah 61:1-4 and 8-11 on the Third Sunday of Advent, and Isaiah 61:10 to 62:3 on the First Sunday of Christmas.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The Spirit of the Lord is on his anointed one, to bring good news to the poor and a crown of beauty for ashes. Jesus said, "Today this is fulfilled."**
