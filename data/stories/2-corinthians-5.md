# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul, writing to the church in Corinth around AD 55.

**What came before.** In chapter 4, Paul said we have God's treasure in jars of clay. Our outer self is wasting away, but our inner self is renewed day by day (4:7, 4:16).

**What this chapter is.** Paul looks ahead to what happens when our bodies wear out, and then to the heart of the good news: in Christ, God is making peace with the world. Anyone in Christ is a new creation.

**How to read it.** Watch for the word "reconcile." It means bringing enemies back together as friends.

## 🗺️ Map of the Letter

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A House From God | 5:1-5 | Our earthly tent will be replaced |
| 2. We Walk by Faith | 5:6-10 | Confident, whether here or with the Lord |
| 3. Christ's Love Compels Us | 5:11-15 | One died for all |
| 4. A New Creation | 5:16-17 | The old has gone, the new has come |
| 5. Ambassadors for Christ | 5:18-21 | Be reconciled to God |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: A House From God

Paul compares our bodies to a tent, something temporary that wears out. {v: 5:1}

**"Now we know that if the earthly tent we live in is dismantled, we have a building from God, an eternal house in heaven, not built by human hands"** (5:1). {v: 5:1}

In this tent we groan. We long for the new body God has ready. {v: 5:2, 5:3, 5:4}

God gave us the Holy Spirit as a promise of what is coming. {v: 5:5}

### Section 2: We Walk by Faith

**"Therefore we are always confident"** (5:6). {v: 5:6}

**"For we walk by faith, not by sight"** (5:7). {v: 5:7}

Paul would rather be **"away from the body and at home with the Lord"** (5:8). {v: 5:8}

Whether here or there, he wants to please the Lord. One day everyone will stand before Christ. {v: 5:9, 5:10}

### Section 3: Christ's Love Compels Us

Paul works hard to persuade people about Jesus. {v: 5:11, 5:12, 5:13}

**"For Christ's love compels us, because we are convinced that One died for all, therefore all died"** (5:14). {v: 5:14}

Compels means pushes or drives. {v: 5:14}

**"And He died for all, that those who live should no longer live for themselves, but for Him who died for them and was raised again"** (5:15). {v: 5:15}

### Section 4: A New Creation

Paul used to judge Jesus by outward things. Not anymore. And he doesn't judge anyone else that way either. {v: 5:16}

**"Therefore if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away. Behold, the new has come!"** (5:17). {v: 5:17}

### Section 5: Ambassadors for Christ

**"All this is from God, who reconciled us to Himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation"** (5:18). {v: 5:18}

God was making peace with the world in Christ, **"not counting men's trespasses against them"** (5:19). {v: 5:19}

**"Therefore we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making His appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ: Be reconciled to God"** (5:20). {v: 5:20}

An ambassador speaks for a king in another land. {v: 5:20}

**"God made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that in Him we might become the righteousness of God"** (5:21). {v: 5:21}

Jesus took our sin. We receive his righteousness. {v: 5:21}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

2 Corinthians 5 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The earthly tent we live in"** (5:1) | Our bodies are temporary. |
| **"An eternal house in heaven"** (5:1) | God has something lasting ready for us. |
| **"We walk by faith, not by sight"** (5:7) | We trust God's promises, even when we can't see them. |
| **"Christ's love compels us"** (5:14) | Jesus's love moves us to act. |
| **"A new creation"** (5:17) | In Christ, God makes us new. |
| **"Ambassadors for Christ"** (5:20) | We speak for Jesus in the world. |
| **"To be sin on our behalf"** (5:21) | Jesus took our sin as if it were his. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Paul was a tentmaker** by trade (Acts 18:3). He knew tents well.

**Ambassadors** in the Roman world carried messages from the emperor to other peoples, speaking with his authority.†

**Corinth's church** had been in conflict with Paul. His words about reconciliation spoke to them, too.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did Jesus become a sinner?** No. He "knew no sin." God put our sin on him, and he carried it on the cross. This is called the "great exchange."

**Does "new creation" mean I'll never struggle again?** God has made you new in Christ. The old life still pulls at us, but it no longer defines us.

**Should I be afraid of the judgment seat of Christ?** The one on that seat is the one who died for you. Paul still says "we are always confident" (5:6).

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"People don't really change."*

**What's true:** Real change is hard, and we can't make ourselves new.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God does what we can't. **"If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away. Behold, the new has come!"** (5:17).

**Where Jesus is:** He died for all, took our sin, and made peace between us and God.

## ✝️ Good News

God didn't wait for us to come back. He came to us.

**"God made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that in Him we might become the righteousness of God"** (5:21).

Jesus took your sin and gave you his righteousness. In him you are a new creation.

**In Christ, God reconciled the world to himself, not counting our sins against us. Jesus took our sin and gave us his righteousness. Anyone in Christ is a new creation.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther called this the "happy exchange." Like a marriage, Christ takes what is ours, our sin and death, and gives us what is his, his righteousness and life.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads 2 Corinthians 5:16-21 on the Fourth Sunday in Lent, alongside the Prodigal Son. In Year B, it reads 2 Corinthians 5:6-17 in June (Lectionary 11). 2 Corinthians 5:20b to 6:10 is read on Ash Wednesday.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**We walk by faith, not by sight. In Christ, God reconciled the world to himself, and anyone in Christ is a new creation.**
