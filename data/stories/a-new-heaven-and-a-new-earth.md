# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** John, exiled on the island of Patmos because he preached about Jesus (1:9). Christians have long said this is John the apostle.†

**What this book is.** Revelation is a vision full of pictures and symbols, written to seven churches facing hard times (1:4, 1:11). It shows that, whatever happens, Jesus wins.

**What this passage is.** The ending of the whole Bible's story. The Bible began in a garden with a tree of life (Genesis 2:9). It ends in a city with a river and a tree of life, where God lives with his people forever.

**How to read it.** Don't try to draw a map. These are pictures. Ask what each one says about God and his people.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |

| 1. All Things New | 21:1-4 | God will live with his people and wipe away every tear |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: All Things New

**"Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and earth had passed away, and the sea was no more"** (21:1). {v: 21:1}

In the Bible, the sea often stands for chaos and danger. Now it's gone. {v: 21:1}

John sees the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down from God, beautiful as a bride on her wedding day. {v: 21:2}

**"Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man, and He will dwell with them"** (21:3). {v: 21:3}

**"He will wipe away every tear from their eyes"** (21:4). {v: 21:4}

**"There will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the former things have passed away"** (21:4). {v: 21:4}

### Section 2: It Is Done

The One seated on the throne says, **"Behold, I make all things new"** (21:5). {v: 21:5}

**"It is done! I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End"** (21:6). {v: 21:6}

Alpha and Omega are the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet. God is the A to Z of everything. {v: 21:6}

**"To the thirsty I will give freely from the spring of the water of life"** (21:6). {v: 21:6}

Those who overcome will inherit everything. Those who reject God shut themselves out. {v: 21:7, 21:8}

### Section 3: The Holy City

An angel carries John to a high mountain to show him the city. {v: 21:9, 21:10}

It shines with God's glory, clear as crystal. {v: 21:11}

It has twelve gates named for the twelve tribes of Israel, and twelve foundations named for the twelve apostles. God's people from both Testaments are there. {v: 21:12, 21:13, 21:14}

The city is a perfect cube, huge beyond measure. {v: 21:15, 21:16, 21:17}

Its walls are made of jewels. **"And the twelve gates were twelve pearls"** (21:21). The street is pure gold, clear as glass. {v: 21:18, 21:19, 21:20, 21:21}

### Section 4: No Temple, No Night

**"But I saw no temple in the city, because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple"** (21:22). {v: 21:22}

A temple was the place to meet God. In the new city, God is everywhere. {v: 21:22}

**"The glory of God illuminates the city, and the Lamb is its lamp"** (21:23). {v: 21:23}

The Lamb is Jesus. {v: 21:23}

People from every nation walk in its light. Its gates are never shut. {v: 21:24, 21:25, 21:26}

Everyone whose name is written in the Lamb's Book of Life is there. {v: 21:27}

### Section 5: The River and the Tree

The angel shows John **"a river of the water of life, as clear as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb"** (22:1). {v: 22:1}

On both sides of the river grows the tree of life, with fruit every month. {v: 22:2}

**"And the leaves of the tree are for the healing of the nations"** (22:2). {v: 22:2}

**"No longer will there be any curse"** (22:3). {v: 22:3}

**"They will see His face"** (22:4). {v: 22:4}

No more night. God himself will be their light, and they will reign with him forever. {v: 22:5}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Revelation 21:1 to 22:5 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"A new heaven and a new earth"** (21:1) | God will renew his whole creation. |
| **"The dwelling place of God is with man"** (21:3) | God will live with his people forever. |
| **"He will wipe away every tear"** (21:4) | All sorrow will end. |
| **"I make all things new"** (21:5) | God doesn't throw creation away. He renews it. |
| **"The Alpha and the Omega"** (21:6) | God is the beginning and the end of everything. |
| **"The Lamb is its lamp"** (21:23) | Jesus is the light of the new world. |
| **"The healing of the nations"** (22:2) | Every wound between peoples will be healed. |
| **"They will see His face"** (22:4) | We will be face to face with God. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Patmos** is a small rocky island off the coast of what is now Turkey. The Romans sent prisoners there.†

**The seven churches** that received Revelation faced pressure to worship the emperor (2:13).†

**The tree of life** first appears in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 2:9, 3:22-24). After the fall, people were kept from it. Here it is open again.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is the city really made of gold and jewels?** These are pictures. John uses the most beautiful things he knows to describe something even better.

**Will we float around in the clouds forever?** No. Revelation pictures a new earth, with a city, a river, and trees. God's people live there in real, resurrected bodies.

**What about people who aren't there?** Revelation is honest that some reject God (21:8). But the gates are never shut, and the water of life is free to anyone who is thirsty (21:6, 22:17).

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"When we die, we become angels and live on clouds."*

**What's true:** Believers who die are with the Lord (Philippians 1:23).

**What the passage is mainly about:** Something even better at the end: a new heaven and a new earth, where God lives with his people. **"Behold, I make all things new"** (21:5). We stay human, raised and renewed.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the Lamb, the light of the city, sharing God's throne.

## ✝️ Good News

The story doesn't end in sadness.

**"He will wipe away every tear from their eyes"** (21:4).

God will live with his people. No more death, mourning, crying, or pain. And we will see his face.

**God will make all things new. He will live with his people, wipe away every tear, and end death forever. We will see his face.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther longed for "the dear Last Day," when Christ would come and make all things new. He saw it not as a day of fear but of rescue for God's people.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads Revelation 21:1-6 on the Fifth Sunday of Easter and Revelation 21:10, 22 to 22:5 on the Sixth. Revelation 21:1-6a is also read on All Saints Sunday in Year B. It's often read at funerals.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God will make all things new. In the new heaven and new earth, he will live with his people, wipe away every tear, and be their light forever.**
