# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** David (145:1). It's the last psalm in the book with his name on it.

**What it is.** A song of praise to God the King. Like Psalm 25, each verse starts with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet.

**What it's about.** God's greatness and his kindness. He is King of an everlasting kingdom, and he lifts up everyone who falls.

**How to read it.** Notice how often David says "all": all who fall, all who are bowed down, all who call on him.

**"Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and Your dominion endures through all generations"** (145:13). {v: 145:13}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 145 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"My God and King"** (145:1) | God is the true King. |
| **"His greatness is unsearchable"** (145:3) | God is greater than we can ever understand. |
| **"One generation will commend Your works to the next"** (145:4) | Faith is passed down. |
| **"The LORD is good to all"** (145:9) | God's kindness reaches everyone. |
| **"Lifts up all who are bowed down"** (145:14) | God helps people weighed down by trouble. |
| **"You open Your hand"** (145:16) | Every meal is God's gift. |
| **"Near to all who call on Him"** (145:18) | God is close when we pray. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Kings in the ancient world** were often cruel and distant. David describes a King who is kind, near, and lifts up the fallen.

**Jewish tradition** says that whoever prays this psalm three times a day has a share in the world to come. It's prayed often in Jewish worship.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God give everyone everything they want?** God "satisfies the desire of every living thing" by giving food and life. He knows what we truly need.

**If God is good to all, why do people go hungry?** God provides, and he calls his people to share. Hunger is often a sign that people haven't shared what God gave.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God is too big to care about little things like dinner."*

**What's true:** God is greater than we can understand.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The same psalm that says **"His greatness is unsearchable"** (145:3) also says **"You open Your hand and satisfy the desire of every living thing"** (145:16). The great King feeds the birds and you.

**Where Jesus is:** He fed the five thousand, lifted up the fallen, and taught us to pray, "Give us this day our daily bread."

## ✝️ Good News

The King is near.

**"The LORD is near to all who call on Him, to all who call out to Him in truth"** (145:18).

He lifts up the fallen and opens his hand to feed his creatures.

**The LORD is a great and everlasting King, and he is gracious and compassionate. He lifts up all who fall, feeds every living thing, and is near to all who call on him.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Small Catechism, Luther teaches families to pray before meals: "The eyes of all look to You, O Lord, and You give them their food in due season. You open Your hand and satisfy the desire of every living thing."†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 145 on several summer Sundays, including the feeding of the five thousand in Years A and B.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The LORD is a great and everlasting King, gracious and compassionate. He lifts up all who fall, opens his hand to feed every living thing, and is near to all who call on him.**
