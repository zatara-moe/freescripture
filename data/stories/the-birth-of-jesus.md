# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Mary:** A young woman from the town of Nazareth. She is engaged to Joseph. The Bible does not give her age.
- **Joseph:** Mary's husband-to-be. He is from the family of King David.
- **Jesus:** Mary's baby. The one the Gospels present as the promised Messiah and the Son of God.
- **The shepherds:** Ordinary workers who watched sheep in the fields at night.
- **The angel of the Lord:** A messenger from God.
- **Caesar Augustus:** The emperor of Rome, the most powerful ruler in the world at that time.

**Setting**

- Bethlehem, a small town in Judea, about 6 miles from Jerusalem. Fields with sheep lie close by.

**When**

- About 2,000 years ago.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**An angel visited Mary.** He told her she would have a baby, God's own Son, and name him Jesus (Luke 1:30-33).

**God had promised a king from David's family.** The angel said Jesus would be that king (Luke 1:32).

**A prophet named the town.** Hundreds of years earlier, Micah said the promised ruler would come from Bethlehem (Micah 5:2).

**Here's what you know that almost nobody in this story knows:** The emperor's order is about to bring Mary and Joseph to Bethlehem, the very town the prophet named.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: An Order from Rome

Caesar Augustus, the emperor of Rome, gave an order. Everyone in the empire had to be counted. This count is called a census. {v: 2:1}

It was the first census while a man named Quirinius was governor of Syria. {v: 2:2}

So everyone went to their family's hometown to sign up. {v: 2:3}

### Scene 2: The Trip to Bethlehem

Joseph lived in Nazareth, a town in Galilee. But his family came from King David. So Joseph had to go to David's town, Bethlehem. {v: 2:4}

David, the shepherd boy who fought Goliath, grew up in Bethlehem. {v: 2:4}

Joseph went with Mary. They were engaged to be married. Mary was going to have a baby soon. {v: 2:5}

The trip from Nazareth to Bethlehem was about 90 miles. {v: 2:4}

### Scene 3: No Room

While they were in Bethlehem, the time came for the baby to be born. {v: 2:6}

Mary gave birth to her first child, a son. She wrapped him snugly in strips of cloth. This kept a newborn warm and safe. {v: 2:7}

Then she laid him in a manger. A manger is a box that holds food for animals. There was no room for them in the inn. {v: 2:7}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture a small, crowded room with a lamp burning low. Animals shuffle and breathe nearby.

### Scene 4: Shepherds at Night

Out in the fields near Bethlehem, shepherds were awake. They were watching their sheep through the night. {v: 2:8}

Shepherds were ordinary workers. They were not important people in town. {v: 2:8}

Suddenly an angel of the Lord stood in front of them. God's glory shone all around them. Glory means God's bright, shining greatness. {v: 2:9}

The shepherds were terrified. {v: 2:9}

### Scene 5: Good News

The angel said, **"Do not be afraid! For behold, I bring you good news of great joy that will be for all the people"** (2:10). Behold means look. {v: 2:10}

**"Today in the city of David a Savior has been born to you. He is Christ the Lord!"** (2:11). {v: 2:11}

A Savior is someone who rescues. Christ is the Greek word for Messiah, the king God promised to send. {v: 2:11}

The angel gave them a sign so they would know the baby. They would find him wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger. {v: 2:12}

Think about that sign. A king would usually be found in a palace. This king was lying in an animal's food box. {v: 2:12}

### Scene 6: The Sky Fills with Angels

Suddenly a huge crowd of angels appeared with the first angel. The Bible calls them "the heavenly host." Host means a great army. {v: 2:13}

The angels praised God. **"Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men on whom His favor rests!"** (2:14). {v: 2:14}

### Scene 7: The Shepherds Go

The angels went back into heaven. The shepherds said to each other, "Let's go to Bethlehem and see this." {v: 2:15}

They hurried off. They found Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger. {v: 2:16}

After they saw him, the shepherds told everyone what the angel had said about this child. {v: 2:17}

Everyone who heard them was amazed. {v: 2:18}

### Scene 8: Mary Remembers

Mary kept all these things close. She thought about them deeply in her heart. {v: 2:19}

The shepherds went back to their fields. They praised God for everything they had heard and seen. It was all just as the angel had told them. {v: 2:20}

Eight days later, the baby was circumcised, as God's law said. Circumcision was the sign that a Jewish boy belonged to God's people. Then he was named Jesus. The angel had given that name before he was born. The name Jesus means "the LORD saves." {v: 2:21}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Luke 2:1-21 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Good news of great joy that will be for all the people"** (2:10) | The good news is for everyone, starting with ordinary shepherds. |
| **"A Savior has been born to you"** (2:11) | God sent someone to rescue people. The angel says "to you," to the shepherds. |
| **"He is Christ the Lord"** (2:11) | Jesus is the promised king, and he is Lord. |
| **"Lying in a manger"** (2:12) | The king of everything came small and poor. |
| **"Peace to men on whom His favor rests"** (2:14) | God's favor brings peace. In the Beatitudes, "blessed" means the same thing: God's favor rests on someone. |
| **"Mary treasured up all these things"** (2:19) | Mary held on to what happened and kept thinking about it. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**A census was a count of people.** Rome counted people so it could collect taxes.

**Babies were wrapped tightly in cloth strips.** People believed it kept a newborn warm and safe. (Easton's Bible Dictionary)

**Bethlehem was called the city of David.** David grew up there, watching his father's sheep (1 Samuel 17:15).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was Jesus born in a stable?** The Bible mentions a manger, but it does not mention a stable or a barn. Many people picture a stable because mangers held animal food.

**Was there an inn with a mean innkeeper?** The Bible does not mention an innkeeper. The Greek word can also mean a guest room in a house.

**Were there three wise men?** Not in this story. Wise men come in Matthew 2, and the Bible does not say how many there were.

**Was Jesus born on December 25?** The Bible does not give a date. Christians chose that day to celebrate his birth.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The shepherds | Terrified | An angel appeared, and God's glory shone around them (2:9) |
| The shepherds | Eager | They hurried to see the baby (2:16) |

| Mary | Thoughtful | She treasured these things in her heart (2:19) |
| The shepherds | Joyful | Everything was just as the angel said (2:20) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**Which part of this story stays with you?** Tap one.

- "Do not be afraid!" :: These were the angel's first words. God did not come to scare people. God came to be with them, and with you.
- The shepherds on the night shift :: God told the news first to ordinary workers. You don't have to be important to be invited.
- A baby in a manger :: The king of everything came small and poor. No one is too small or too poor for God to come close.
- Mary, thinking it over :: It's okay to have questions and keep thinking. Mary did.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Christmas is a sweet story about a baby."*

**What's true:** It is tender. A mother wraps her newborn and lays him down to sleep.

**What the story is mainly about:** God came close. The promised king came as a poor baby. The first people to hear were ordinary shepherds working the night shift.

**Where Jesus is:** Right in the middle of the story. Another Gospel calls him Immanuel, which means "God with us" (Matthew 1:23).

## ✝️ Good News

The angel's first words were "Do not be afraid." God did not come to scare people. God came to be with them. **The good news of great joy is for all the people, and that includes you.**†

## 📚 From Luther

Martin Luther loved this story. In his Christmas sermons, he asked people to picture how poor and cold Mary and Joseph were. He also wrote a Christmas hymn. It is traditionally said to be for his own children (1534–35). English translation by Catherine Winkworth (1855):†

> From heaven above to earth I come
> To bear good news to every home;
> Glad tidings of great joy I bring,
> Whereof I now will say and sing.

Luther also said the Old Testament is like "the swaddling cloths and the manger in which Christ lies" (*Preface to the Old Testament*, 1523). He meant that the Old Testament points to Christ. It is also Israel's Scripture, which Jewish people still read and love today.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches read Luke 2:1-14 on Christmas Eve, often with verses 15-20. Luke 2:15-21 is read on January 1, when the church remembers the name of Jesus.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God came to us as a baby and told the good news first to ordinary people.**
