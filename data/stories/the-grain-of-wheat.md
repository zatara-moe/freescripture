# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Jesus:** The one the Gospels present as the promised Messiah and the Son of God. It is a few days before he dies.
- **Some Greeks:** Visitors who are not Jewish. They have come to worship at the feast.
- **Philip and Andrew:** Two of Jesus's twelve disciples.
- **The crowd:** People in Jerusalem for the Passover feast.

**Setting**

- Jerusalem, during the days before Passover.

**When**

- Holy Week, about 2,000 years ago, a few days before Jesus was crucified.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Jesus had raised Lazarus from the dead.** Many people believed in him (11:43-45).

**Jesus had ridden into Jerusalem on a donkey.** Crowds waved palm branches and shouted, "Hosanna!" (12:12-13).

**The leaders were worried.** **"Look how the whole world has gone after Him!"** they said (12:19).

**Here's what you know:** All through John's Gospel, Jesus has said, "My hour has not yet come." Now it has.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: "We Want to See Jesus"

Some Greeks had come to Jerusalem to worship at the feast. {v: 12:20}

They came to Philip and asked, **"Sir, we want to see Jesus"** (12:21). {v: 12:21}

Philip told Andrew. Then both of them went and told Jesus. {v: 12:22}

### Scene 2: A Grain of Wheat

Jesus answered, **"The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified"** (12:23). {v: 12:23}

"The hour" was the time of his death and rising. Glorified means shown in all his greatness. {v: 12:23}

Then he gave a picture. {v: 12:24}

**"Unless a kernel of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remains only a seed; but if it dies, it bears much fruit"** (12:24). {v: 12:24}

This is a comparison. Jesus is the seed. His death would bring life to many. {v: 12:24}

Jesus said whoever holds on tight to their life will lose it. Whoever follows him will be where he is. {v: 12:25, 12:26}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture Jesus holding a single grain of wheat in his palm. It is small and dry. He presses it into the dirt.

### Scene 3: A Voice From Heaven

**"Now My soul is troubled"** (12:27), Jesus said. {v: 12:27}

He knew what was coming. Should he ask the Father to save him from it? {v: 12:27}

**"No, it is for this purpose that I have come to this hour. Father, glorify Your name!"** (12:27-28). {v: 12:27, 12:28}

Then a voice came from heaven: **"I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again"** (12:28). {v: 12:28}

Some in the crowd said it was thunder. Others said an angel had spoken. {v: 12:29}

Jesus said the voice was for them, not for him. {v: 12:30}

### Scene 4: Lifted Up

**"Now the prince of this world will be cast out"** (12:31). The prince of this world is the devil. {v: 12:31}

**"And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw everyone to Myself"** (12:32). {v: 12:32}

John explains: Jesus said this to show how he would die. He would be lifted up on a cross. {v: 12:33}

The crowd was confused. Jesus told them the Light would be with them only a little longer. **"While you have the Light, believe in the Light"** (12:36). {v: 12:34, 12:35, 12:36}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

John 12:20-36 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Sir, we want to see Jesus"** (12:21) | People from outside Israel were coming to Jesus. |
| **"The hour has come"** (12:23) | The time of Jesus's death and rising had arrived. |
| **"Unless a kernel of wheat falls to the ground and dies"** (12:24) | Jesus's death would bring life to many. |
| **"Now My soul is troubled"** (12:27) | Jesus felt real fear about his death. |
| **"It is for this purpose that I have come"** (12:27) | Jesus chose to go to the cross. |
| **"Will draw everyone to Myself"** (12:32) | The cross is how Jesus brings people from every nation to himself. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Greeks often meant non-Jewish people.** Some of them worshiped Israel's God and came to the feasts.†

**Philip had a Greek name.** He was from Bethsaida, a town with many Greek-speaking people. That may be why the Greeks came to him.†

**Wheat was the main food.** Farmers knew a seed had to be buried before it could grow into a plant with many grains.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"Whoever hates his life." Should I hate my life?** No. This is a way of saying that following Jesus matters more than anything else. God loves your life. If you are having thoughts of not wanting to live, please tell a trusted adult right away.

**Was Jesus scared?** He said his soul was troubled. Jesus felt real feelings. He was afraid, and he went anyway, because he loved us.

**Did the Greeks get to see Jesus?** John doesn't say. But Jesus's answer means that through the cross, people from every nation would come to him.†

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |

| The Greeks | Eager | They wanted to see Jesus (12:21) |
| Jesus | Troubled | He knew he was going to die (12:27) |
| The crowd | Confused | They didn't understand what he meant (12:34) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What do you want from Jesus?** Tap one.

- To see him :: Like the Greeks. He draws everyone to himself, including you.
- Courage :: Jesus was troubled and went anyway. He can help you face hard things.
- New life :: A seed has to be planted to grow. Jesus brings life out of death.
- I'm troubled too :: Jesus said, "My soul is troubled." He knows how you feel.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Jesus's death was a tragedy that went wrong."*

**What's true:** Jesus's death was terrible, and it was unjust.

**What the story is mainly about:** Jesus knew it was coming and said, **"It is for this purpose that I have come to this hour"** (12:27). Like a seed planted in the ground, his death would bring a great harvest of life.

**Where Jesus is:** In Jerusalem, troubled and determined, on the way to the cross where he would be lifted up for everyone.

## ✝️ Good News

A seed has to be buried before it grows. Jesus was buried, and he rose.

**"And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw everyone to Myself"** (12:32).

The cross wasn't the end. It was how Jesus would gather people from every nation, including you.

**Jesus is the grain of wheat that died and bore much fruit. Lifted up on the cross, he draws everyone to himself.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that God shows himself most clearly in the cross, where we would least expect it. He called this the "theology of the cross": God's glory is hidden in Jesus's suffering for us.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads John 12:20-33 on the Fifth Sunday in Lent.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Like a grain of wheat, Jesus died to bring life to many. Lifted up on the cross, he draws everyone to himself.**
