# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**
- **David:** The youngest of Jesse's eight sons, from Bethlehem. A shepherd God chose to be king. The Bible calls him a "youth" but doesn't give his age.
- **Jesse:** David's father. Old, and too old to fight.
- **Eliab:** David's oldest brother. A soldier in Saul's army.
- **Saul:** Israel's first king. A head taller than anyone in Israel.
- **Abner:** The commander of Saul's army.
- **Goliath:** A giant warrior from the Philistine city of Gath. The Philistines' champion.
- **The two armies:** Israel on one side, the Philistines on the other. The Philistines were Israel's longtime enemies.

**Setting**
- The Valley of Elah, in southwest Israel. Two hills face each other, with a valley and a stream between them.

**When**
- About 1,000 years before Jesus.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Israel asked for a king.** For hundreds of years, God himself was Israel's king. But the people wanted a human king "to go out before us, and to fight our battles" (1 Samuel 8:20). God told Samuel, **"they have rejected Me as their king"** (1 Samuel 8:7). Still, God gave them Saul.

**Saul turned away from God.** He disobeyed God more than once. God said Saul would not stay king.

**God secretly chose someone else.** God sent the prophet Samuel to Jesse's house in Bethlehem. Samuel saw Eliab, tall and impressive, and thought he must be the one. God said no: **"man sees the outward appearance, but the LORD sees the heart"** (1 Samuel 16:7). **LORD** in capital letters is God's personal name in the original language. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect. God chose the youngest son, David. Samuel anointed him with oil while his brothers watched, and **"the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David from that day forward"** (1 Samuel 16:13).

**Here's what you know that almost nobody in this story knows:** the shepherd boy is Israel's future king.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: The Standoff

The Philistine army camped on one hill. Saul's army camped on the hill across from them. The Valley of Elah lay between them, with a stream running through the bottom.

This valley mattered. It led from Philistine land up into the hills where Israel lived. David's hometown, Bethlehem, was only about 15 miles away.† If the Philistines won here, the road into Israel's towns was open.

Neither army attacked. The Bible doesn't say why they waited. One likely reason: charging meant running downhill, crossing the stream, and fighting uphill against an enemy who was ready.

### Scene 2: The Giant's Challenge

Then a champion walked out from the Philistine camp. His name was Goliath, from the city of Gath.

He was enormous. He wore a bronze helmet and bronze armor that weighed about 125 pounds, as much as a grown adult. Bronze plates covered his legs. A bronze javelin hung across his back. The wooden shaft of his spear was as thick as a beam on a weaving loom. Its iron point alone weighed about 15 pounds. A soldier walked in front of him carrying his shield.

Goliath shouted across the valley. "Why are you lining up for battle? Choose one of your men and send him down to me. If he kills me, we'll be your servants. If I kill him, you'll be ours. I defy the ranks of Israel today! Send me a man to fight."

Sometimes wars were settled this way. Instead of thousands dying, each side sent one champion. The winner's army won everything.

Israel had a serious problem. The Philistines knew how to work iron. Israel had no blacksmiths at all. Israelites even had to go to the Philistines to sharpen their farm tools. Only Saul and his son Jonathan owned a sword or spear (1 Samuel 13:19–22). Most soldiers had no real weapons of war.

So Israel had one obvious choice: King Saul. He was the tallest man in Israel. He was the king the people had asked for, to fight their battles. He had one of the only swords in the army.

Saul didn't go. He and all his soldiers were terrified.

Goliath came out every morning and every evening. He did this for 40 days.

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Imagine the same booming voice rolling across the valley twice a day. After 40 days, everyone in camp knows the words by heart.

### Scene 3: David Arrives

David's three oldest brothers were in Saul's army. David was the youngest. He went back and forth from Saul's camp to take care of his father's sheep in Bethlehem.

Being a shepherd was lonely, dangerous work. Shepherds lived outdoors with the flock, found them water and grass, and guarded them from wild animals. Lions and bears lived in Israel back then (1 Samuel 17:34).

Soldiers often depended on food sent from home. So Jesse gave David a bag of roasted grain and ten loaves of bread for his brothers. He also sent ten portions of cheese for their commander. Jesse wanted news too. "Check on your brothers, and bring back something to show they're okay."

David got up early and left the sheep with another shepherd. He reached the camp just as the army was marching out and shouting the battle cry. He left the food with the supply keeper and ran to find his brothers.

While David was talking with them, Goliath stepped out and shouted his usual challenge. David heard it. He watched the Israelite soldiers run from Goliath in fear.

### Scene 4: The Reward and the Angry Brother

The soldiers told each other about a reward. King Saul had promised that whoever killed Goliath would get great riches. He would marry the king's daughter. His father's family would never pay taxes again.

David asked what would be done for the man who killed this Philistine. Then he asked a bigger question: **"Just who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"** (17:26). To David, Goliath wasn't only insulting soldiers. He was insulting God.

Eliab, David's oldest brother, heard him and got angry. "Why did you come down here? Who's watching those few sheep? I know how proud you are. You only came to watch the battle."

The Bible doesn't say exactly why Eliab reacted so harshly. But remember: Eliab was the tall, impressive oldest son that Samuel first thought God would choose. Instead, Eliab watched his youngest brother get anointed.

David said, "What did I do now? It was only a question." He turned to other soldiers and asked again. They gave him the same answer.

### Scene 5: David Volunteers

Someone told Saul what David was saying. Saul sent for him.

David said, "Don't let anyone lose heart because of this Philistine. I'll go and fight him."

Saul said, "You can't. You're only a boy. He has been a warrior since he was young."

David explained what shepherding had taught him. When a lion or a bear carried off a lamb, David went after it. He struck it and rescued the lamb from its mouth. When the animal turned on him, he grabbed it and killed it. "The LORD who rescued me from the lion and the bear will rescue me from this Philistine."

Saul said, "Go, and may the LORD be with you."

### Scene 6: Armor That Doesn't Fit

Saul dressed David in his own tunic, armor, and bronze helmet. David strapped on Saul's sword and tried to walk. He couldn't. He had never trained in armor.

Remember, Saul was a head taller than everyone else. His armor was made for him.

David took it all off. He picked up his shepherd's staff. He walked down to the stream and chose five stones, worn smooth by the water. He put them in his shepherd's bag. With his sling in his hand, he walked toward Goliath.

A sling was a real weapon, not a toy. It was a strip of woven material or leather with a pouch in the middle. A slinger whirled it, then let one end go. Skilled slingers could hit very small targets (Judges 20:16).

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Imagine thousands of soldiers watching from both hillsides. Down by the stream, one young shepherd kneels, choosing stones.

### Scene 7: The Face-Off

Goliath came closer and closer, with his shield bearer in front of him. When he saw David, he looked down on him. David was only a boy, ruddy and handsome.

"Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?" Goliath cursed David by his gods. "Come here, and I'll give your body to the birds and the wild animals."

David answered him:

**"You come against me with sword and spear and javelin, but I come against you in the name of the LORD of Hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied"** (17:45).

David said that today the whole world would know there is a God in Israel. Everyone there would know the LORD doesn't save with sword or spear. **"For the battle is the LORD's"** (17:47).

### Scene 8: One Stone

Goliath started forward to attack. David ran quickly toward him. He reached into his bag, took out a stone, and slung it.

The stone hit Goliath on the forehead and sank in. Goliath fell facedown on the ground.

David had no sword. He ran and stood over the giant. He pulled Goliath's own sword out of its sheath and killed him with it. Then he cut off his head.

Remember what David said: the LORD doesn't save with sword or spear. David won without a sword. The only sword in the fight belonged to Goliath.

### Scene 9: The Chase

When the Philistines saw their hero was dead, they turned and ran. Goliath had promised they would become Israel's servants if he lost. They didn't stay to keep that promise.

The men of Israel and Judah, who had been afraid for 40 days, charged forward with a shout. They chased the Philistines all the way to the gates of the Philistine cities of Gath and Ekron.

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Imagine a valley that had been silent with fear for 40 days. Now it fills with the roar of an army running downhill.

### Scene 10: "Whose Son Is This?"

As David had gone out to fight, Saul asked Abner, the commander of his army, "Whose son is this young man?" Abner didn't know.

After the battle, Abner brought David to Saul. David was still holding Goliath's head. Saul asked him, "Whose son are you, young man?"

David said, "I am the son of your servant Jesse of Bethlehem."

Saul still didn't know that the young man in front of him, holding Goliath's head, was the king God had already chosen. But you do.

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Samuel 17 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| "ruddy" | Having a reddish, healthy-looking face |
| "uncircumcised Philistine" | Circumcision was the sign of God's promise to Israel. David means this man is outside God's people and insults God |
| "the armies of the living God" | Israel's army belongs to the real God who is alive, unlike the Philistines' idols |
| "the LORD of Hosts" | The Lord of armies. God commands the armies of heaven |
| "Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?" | An insult. Goliath feels disrespected that Israel sent a young shepherd |
| "I will give your flesh to the birds of the air" | A threat before a fight. Both men say something like it |
| "the battle is the LORD's" | God wins this fight, not David's strength or weapons |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**How tall was Goliath?** The standard Hebrew text says six cubits and a span, about 9 feet 9 inches. The BSB follows this. The oldest Greek translation, a Dead Sea Scroll, and the historian Josephus say four cubits and a span, about 6 feet 9 inches. Either way, he was huge (BSB footnote).†

**Measurements:** Goliath's armor weighed 5,000 shekels, about 125 pounds. His spear point weighed 600 shekels, about 15 pounds (Easton's Bible Dictionary, "Shekel").†

**Anointing:** Pouring oil on someone's head showed that God had chosen them for a special job, like king or priest (Easton's Bible Dictionary, "Anoint").

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

In chapter 16, David already plays the harp for Saul and becomes his armor-bearer (1 Samuel 16:21). So why does Saul ask who he is? People give three explanations:
- **Saul is asking about David's family.** He promised the winner's family freedom from taxes, so he needs to know who David's father is. David answers with his father's name.
- **The story may combine two older tellings.** Many scholars think this chapter weaves together two accounts. The oldest Greek translation has a shorter version without verses 12–31 and 55–58.
- **The book arranges stories by topic,** not always in exact time order.

It's okay to notice things like this. Careful readers have asked about it for centuries.

# Why It Matters

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Be brave like David, and you'll defeat the giants in your life."*

**What's true:** God does give courage, and David's trust in God is a real example.

**What the story is mainly about:** God rescuing helpless people. David's courage came from the Spirit God gave him. We're less like David and more like the scared soldiers on the hill.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus is our champion, the one who fights the enemies we can't beat.

## ✝️ Good News

Israel asked for a king to fight their battles. When the giant came, that king was afraid. So God sent his own champion: the youngest son of a family in Bethlehem, a shepherd nobody expected. That's how God often works, through the small and overlooked.

David fought on behalf of the whole army. When David won, every soldier won, even the ones who ran away.

About 1,000 years later, another king was born in Bethlehem, from David's family: Jesus. He is the champion God sends for us. He fought sin, death, and the devil. He won through the cross and the empty tomb. His victory becomes ours, even when we're scared, even when we run.

**You don't have to be the hero. Jesus already is.**

## 📚 From Luther

**"A Mighty Fortress Is Our God"** by Martin Luther (1529), English translation by Frederick Hedge (1853):

> *Did we in our own strength confide,*
> *Our striving would be losing;*
> *Were not the right Man on our side,*
> *The Man of God's own choosing.*
> *Dost ask who that may be?*
> *Christ Jesus, it is He.*

**The Small Catechism** explains the Second Commandment: we should call on God's name in every trouble, pray, praise, and give thanks. That's what David does. He comes "in the name of the LORD of Hosts."

## 🕊️ From the Wider Church

Early church writers, including Augustine, saw David as a picture of Christ: an unexpected champion from Bethlehem who defeats the enemy of God's people.†

## 📅 In Church

ELCA congregations following the Revised Common Lectionary may hear this story in the summer of Year B, often alongside Mark 4:35–41, where Jesus calms a storm while his disciples are terrified.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**When God's people were helpless before the giant, God sent his own champion to fight for them. Jesus is that champion for us.**
