# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's for.** God's people after they came home from exile. They were rebuilding, worshiping, and fasting. But something was wrong.†

**What a fast is.** Fasting means going without food for a time, to pray and turn to God. God's people fasted on special days.

**What this passage is.** God answers people who complain that he isn't noticing their fasting. He tells them what kind of fast he really wants.

**How to read it.** Notice the big "if... then" promises in the second half.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cry Aloud | 58:1-2 | God's people look religious but miss the point |
| 2. "Why Don't You See?" | 58:3-5 | Their fasting is mixed with cruelty |
| 3. The Fast God Chooses | 58:6-7 | Set people free and feed the hungry |
| 4. Your Light Will Break Forth | 58:8-12 | God promises light, healing, and help |
| 5. The Sabbath a Delight | 58:13-14 | God's day of rest is a gift |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Cry Aloud

**"Cry aloud, do not hold back! Raise your voice like a ram's horn"** (58:1). A ram's horn was blown like a trumpet. {v: 58:1}

God tells his prophet to tell the people their sins. {v: 58:1}

They look like they love God. They come every day and seem eager to know his ways. {v: 58:2}

### Section 2: "Why Don't You See?"

The people complain: **"Why have we fasted, and You have not seen?"** (58:3). {v: 58:3}

God answers. On their fast days, they still do whatever they want. They mistreat their workers. They fight and argue. {v: 58:3, 58:4}

Bowing your head and sitting in ashes isn't what God is looking for. {v: 58:5}

### Section 3: The Fast God Chooses

**"Isn't this the fast that I have chosen: to break the chains of wickedness, to untie the cords of the yoke, to set the oppressed free and tear off every yoke?"** (58:6). {v: 58:6}

The oppressed are people being crushed by others. {v: 58:6}

**"Isn't it to share your bread with the hungry, to bring the poor and homeless into your home, to clothe the naked when you see him"** (58:7). {v: 58:7}

### Section 4: Your Light Will Break Forth

**"Then your light will break forth like the dawn, and your healing will come quickly"** (58:8). {v: 58:8}

**"Then you will call, and the LORD will answer; you will cry out, and He will say, 'Here I am.'"** (58:9). {v: 58:9}

If they stop blaming and gossiping, and give themselves to the hungry, their darkness will become like noon. {v: 58:9, 58:10}

**"You will be like a well-watered garden, like a spring whose waters never fail"** (58:11). {v: 58:11}

They will be called **"Repairer of the Breach"** (58:12). A breach is a hole in a wall. They will rebuild what was broken. {v: 58:12}

### Section 5: The Sabbath a Delight

God's holy day isn't a burden. **"If you call the Sabbath a delight"** (58:13). {v: 58:13}

Instead of doing whatever they please, they can rest in God. **"Then you will delight yourself in the LORD"** (58:14). {v: 58:14}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 58 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Why have we fasted, and You have not seen?"** (58:3) | They thought God owed them for their religion. |
| **"You oppress all your workers"** (58:3) | Their worship didn't match how they treated people. |
| **"Set the oppressed free"** (58:6) | God wants people freed from what crushes them. |
| **"Share your bread with the hungry"** (58:7) | Real worship includes caring for people in need. |
| **"He will say, 'Here I am.'"** (58:9) | God is near to people who care for others. |
| **"Repairer of the Breach"** (58:12) | God's people help rebuild what's broken. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Sackcloth and ashes** were signs of sorrow. People wore rough cloth and sat in ashes when they fasted or mourned.†

**Workers were paid daily.** Holding back a worker's pay hurt families who needed it to eat that night (Deuteronomy 24:15).†

**Jesus taught about fasting.** He said not to fast to be seen by others (Matthew 6:16-18).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is fasting bad?** No. Jesus fasted, and many Christians fast in Lent. God was warning about fasting while being cruel to others.

**Do I have to earn God's answer by helping people?** God answers because he is gracious. But God loves to be near people who share his care for the hungry and oppressed.†

**Should I bring homeless people into my home?** Talk with a trusted adult about how your family can help people in need safely. There are many ways to share bread and clothing.

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What would God's kind of "fast" look like for you?** Tap one.

- Share :: Share your food, clothes, or time with someone who needs it.
- Stand up :: Help someone who is being treated unfairly.
- Stop pointing :: God mentions "the pointing of the finger." Stop blaming.
- Rest :: God calls his day a delight. Take time to rest in him.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Going to church makes you a good person."*

**What's true:** Worship is good. God wants his people to gather.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God's people worshiped and fasted, but treated workers badly and fought. God wanted worship that shows up in kindness: setting people free, feeding the hungry, and sheltering the poor.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus said, **"Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers of Mine, you did for Me"** (Matthew 25:40).

## ✝️ Good News

God doesn't just want rituals. He wants his people to share his heart for the hungry and oppressed.

**"Then you will call, and the LORD will answer; you will cry out, and He will say, 'Here I am.'"** (58:9).

Jesus broke every chain. He fed the hungry and set people free. And he makes his people into repairers and restorers.

**God's chosen fast is to set people free and share bread with the hungry. Then his light breaks forth like the dawn.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said fasting can be good training for the body, but it never earns God's favor. True worship flows into love for our neighbor.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 58:1-9a on the Fifth Sunday after the Epiphany. In Year C, it reads Isaiah 58:9b-14 in August (Lectionary 21). It is also read on Ash Wednesday.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God doesn't want empty fasting. His chosen fast is to set the oppressed free and share bread with the hungry. Then your light will break forth like the dawn.**
