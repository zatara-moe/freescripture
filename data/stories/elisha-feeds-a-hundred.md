# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Elisha was a prophet in Israel, the successor of Elijah, known for healing and miracles (2 Kings 4).

**What was happening.** There was a famine in the land. A man came to Elisha with a small gift of food — twenty loaves of barley bread and some grain. It was nowhere near enough for a hundred hungry people.

**What this passage is.** A short miracle story. A servant doubts. A prophet trusts. Everyone eats.

**How to read it.** If this sounds familiar, it is. It is the same shape as a miracle Jesus would do centuries later.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Not Enough | 4:42-43 | A man brings bread; the servant sees only the shortage |
| 2. According to the Word | 4:43-44 | Elisha trusts God's word; everyone eats, with leftovers |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Not Enough

A man came from Baal-shalishah with a sack of twenty loaves of barley bread from the first ripe grain. **"Give it to the people to eat,"** said Elisha (4:42). {v: 4:42}

The man brought his firstfruits — his offering — to Elisha. Elisha's first instinct was to share it. {v: 4:42}

But his servant asked, **"How am I to set twenty loaves before a hundred men?"** (4:43). {v: 4:43}

The servant was not wrong. Twenty loaves for a hundred people is not enough. That is simple arithmetic. {v: 4:43}

### Section 2: According to the Word

**"Give it to the people to eat,"** said Elisha, **"for this is what the LORD says: 'They will eat and have some left over.'"** (4:43). {v: 4:43}

The word of the LORD overrules the arithmetic. God promises not just enough — but extra. {v: 4:43}

**"So he set it before them, and they ate and had some left over, according to the word of the LORD"** (4:44). {v: 4:44}

It happened exactly as God said. The phrase "according to the word of the LORD" is the point of the whole story. {v: 4:44}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Jesus fed five thousand people with five loaves and two fish, and had twelve baskets left over (John 6:1-13). The Gospel of John places it just before Jesus says, "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35). The same shape, the same promise, the same God.

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

2 Kings 4:42-44 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Give it to the people to eat"** (4:42) | Elisha gives away what is given to him. |
| **"How am I to set twenty loaves before a hundred men?"** (4:43) | The servant sees the gap between what they have and what they need. |
| **"They will eat and have some left over"** (4:43) | God's provision exceeds the need. |
| **"According to the word of the LORD"** (4:44) | The miracle is grounded in God's promise, not in Elisha's power. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Firstfruits** were the first produce of the harvest, traditionally brought as an offering to God (Leviticus 23:20). The man brought his offering to Elisha as God's representative.

**Barley bread** was common, everyday food — not luxury. This was simple provision, not a feast.

**Famines** were frequent in ancient Israel, and prophets like Elisha often traveled to communities in need.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did this really happen?** The story is told as historical fact. Its shape — too little, a word of trust, abundance — appears again and again in Scripture, which suggests it is pointing to something real about how God provides.

**Why does it matter that there were leftovers?** Leftovers signal abundance, not just enough. God's provision is generous. This pattern appears in every feeding miracle in the Bible.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Miracles like this don't happen anymore."*

**What's true:** This miracle happened once, at a specific time and place.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"They will eat and have some left over"** (4:43). God's word is reliable. What looks like not-enough becomes more than enough when God speaks.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus fed five thousand with five loaves and two fish, and gathered twelve baskets of leftovers (John 6:1-13). That same day, he said, "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35). Elisha's miracle was a preview.

## ✝️ Good News

Whatever you have is enough when God speaks.

**"They will eat and have some left over"** (4:43).

He provides. He multiplies. There is always more with him than the math suggests.

**Elisha had twenty loaves. God had a hundred hungry people. God's word made the difference — and there was food left over.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther pointed to feeding miracles as signs of the kingdom: where Christ is present, the hungry are fed. The physical feeding of bodies was, for Luther, a sign of the spiritual nourishment Christ gives to souls.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, 2 Kings 4:42-44 is read on the Seventeenth Sunday after Pentecost (Lectionary 17), alongside the account of Jesus feeding the five thousand in John 6. The same Sunday, the congregation hears Jesus say, "I am the bread of life."†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Twenty loaves. A hundred hungry people. God said they would eat and have some left over — and they did.**
