# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Samuel:** God's prophet. He anointed Saul as Israel's first king.
- **Saul:** Israel's king. He has turned away from God.
- **Jesse:** A man from Bethlehem with eight sons.
- **Eliab and his brothers:** Jesse's seven older sons. They are tall and strong.
- **David:** Jesse's youngest son. He is out taking care of the sheep.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- Bethlehem, a small town in the hills of Judah.

**When**

- About 3,000 years ago, while Saul was king.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Israel asked for a king.** They wanted to be like the other nations. God gave them Saul (8:5, 10:1).

**Saul looked like a king.** He was a head taller than everyone else (9:2).

**But Saul stopped obeying God.** So God rejected him as king (15:23). Samuel was heartbroken.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Fill Your Horn With Oil

The LORD said to Samuel, **"How long are you going to mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him as king over Israel?"** (16:1). {v: 16:1}

**"Fill your horn with oil and go. I am sending you to Jesse of Bethlehem, for I have selected from his sons a king for Myself"** (16:1). {v: 16:1}

Samuel was afraid. If Saul heard, he might kill him. {v: 16:2}

God told Samuel to take a young cow and go to offer a sacrifice. God would show him what to do. {v: 16:2, 16:3}

The leaders of Bethlehem trembled when Samuel came. **"Do you come in peace?"** they asked (16:4). {v: 16:4}

**"In peace,"** Samuel said (16:5). He invited Jesse and his sons to the sacrifice. {v: 16:5}

### Scene 2: Not This One

When they arrived, Samuel saw Eliab, Jesse's oldest son. He was tall and impressive. {v: 16:6}

Samuel thought, "Surely this is the one God has chosen!" {v: 16:6}

But the LORD said, **"Do not consider his appearance or height, for I have rejected him; the LORD does not see as man does. For man sees the outward appearance, but the LORD sees the heart"** (16:7). {v: 16:7}

Then Jesse brought his next son. Not this one. And the next. Not this one either. {v: 16:8, 16:9}

Seven sons passed in front of Samuel. **"The LORD has not chosen any of these"** (16:10). {v: 16:10}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture seven tall young men standing in a row, each one looking a little more confused. Their father glances at Samuel, then out at the hills.

### Scene 3: The Youngest

Samuel asked Jesse, **"Are these all the sons you have?"** (16:11). {v: 16:11}

**"There is still the youngest,"** Jesse replied, **"but he is tending the sheep"** (16:11). {v: 16:11}

Nobody had even thought to invite him. {v: 16:11}

**"Send for him,"** Samuel said. They wouldn't sit down to eat until he came (16:11). {v: 16:11}

So they brought David in from the fields. {v: 16:12}

The LORD said, **"Rise and anoint him, for he is the one"** (16:12). {v: 16:12}

Samuel poured the oil on David's head in front of his brothers. To anoint means to pour oil on someone to show God has chosen them. {v: 16:13}

From that day on, the Spirit of the LORD came powerfully on David. {v: 16:13}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Samuel 16:1-13 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"How long are you going to mourn for Saul"** (16:1) | God had a new plan, even in Samuel's sadness. |
| **"I have selected from his sons a king"** (16:1) | God chose the king. People didn't. |
| **"Do not consider his appearance or height"** (16:7) | Looks don't show what someone is really like. |
| **"The LORD sees the heart"** (16:7) | God knows who we really are on the inside. |
| **"There is still the youngest"** (16:11) | The one nobody counted was the one God chose. |
| **"The Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David"** (16:13) | God gave David his power to do what he called him to do. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Oil was kept in an animal horn.** Pouring oil on someone's head showed that God had chosen them for a special job, like priest or king.

**"Anointed one" in Hebrew is "Messiah."** In Greek, it is "Christ." Jesus is the Anointed One, the King God promised.†

**The youngest son had the least important job.** Watching sheep was often left to the youngest or to servants.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God not care how I look?** God made your body, and it is good. But he loves you for who you are, not for how you look.

**Did David become king right away?** No. Saul stayed king for years. David waited, served Saul, and fled from him before he finally became king.†

**Was David perfect?** No. David later did terrible wrongs. God's choice was about grace, not about David being perfect.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Samuel | Sad | He was mourning for Saul (16:1) |
| Samuel | Afraid | Saul might kill him (16:2) |
| The town leaders | Nervous | They trembled when Samuel came (16:4) |
| David's brothers | Surprised | Their youngest brother was chosen (16:13) |

# Why It Matters

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God only uses impressive people."*

**What's true:** God gives people many gifts, including strength and talent.

**What the story is mainly about:** Seven impressive brothers were passed over. The forgotten youngest, out with the sheep, was God's choice. God sees the heart, and he often chooses people the world overlooks.

**Where Jesus is:** About a thousand years later, another king was born in Bethlehem, from David's family. Jesus is the Good Shepherd and the Anointed King. He too was overlooked: he had **"no stately form or majesty to attract us"** (Isaiah 53:2).

## ✝️ Good News

Everyone looked at the tall older brothers. God looked at the shepherd boy.

**"For man sees the outward appearance, but the LORD sees the heart"** (16:7).

God sees you completely, and he loves you. In Jesus, the true Anointed King from Bethlehem, he chose you as his own.

**God chose the youngest shepherd boy to be king. He sees the heart, and he chooses people the world overlooks.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that God doesn't find people lovable and then love them. God's love creates what is lovable. He loves people who seem like nothing.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads 1 Samuel 16:1-13 on the Fourth Sunday in Lent, next to Jesus healing a man born blind.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God sent Samuel to anoint a new king, and chose David, the youngest shepherd boy. Man sees the outward appearance, but the LORD sees the heart.**
