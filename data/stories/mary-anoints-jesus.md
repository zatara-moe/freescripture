# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Jesus:** The one the Gospels present as the promised Messiah and the Son of God. It is less than a week before he dies.
- **Mary:** A friend of Jesus, sister of Martha and Lazarus.
- **Martha:** Mary's sister. She is serving the dinner.
- **Lazarus:** Their brother. Jesus raised him from the dead.
- **Judas Iscariot:** One of the twelve disciples. He will betray Jesus.

**Setting**

- A home in Bethany, a village about two miles from Jerusalem.

**When**

- Six days before Passover, about 2,000 years ago.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Jesus had raised Lazarus from the dead.** Lazarus had been in the tomb four days (11:39-44).

**After that, the leaders made plans to kill Jesus.** **"So from that day on they plotted to kill Him"** (11:53).

**Mary had sat at Jesus's feet before.** She listened to him teach (Luke 10:39). When Lazarus died, she fell at Jesus's feet and wept (11:32).

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A dinner for Jesus | 12:1-2 | Friends host a dinner in Bethany |
| 2. A pint of perfume | 12:3 | Mary pours perfume on Jesus's feet |
| 3. "Why this waste?" | 12:4-6 | Judas complains |
| 4. "Leave her alone" | 12:7-8 | Jesus defends Mary |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: A Dinner for Jesus

Six days before Passover, Jesus came to Bethany. {v: 12:1}

This was the hometown of Lazarus, whom Jesus had raised from the dead. {v: 12:1}

They gave a dinner for Jesus. Martha served. Lazarus was at the table with him. {v: 12:2}

### Scene 2: A Pint of Perfume

Then Mary took about a pint of very expensive perfume, made of pure nard. {v: 12:3}

She poured it on Jesus's feet and wiped his feet with her hair. {v: 12:3}

**"And the house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume"** (12:3). {v: 12:3}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture the room going quiet as the smell spreads, sweet and strong, into every corner. Mary kneels at Jesus's feet, her long hair falling down.

### Scene 3: "Why This Waste?"

But Judas Iscariot, the disciple who would betray Jesus, objected. {v: 12:4}

**"Why wasn't this perfume sold for three hundred denarii and the money given to the poor?"** (12:5). {v: 12:5}

Three hundred denarii was about a year's pay for a worker. {v: 12:5}

John tells us that Judas didn't really care about the poor. He kept the disciples' money bag, and he used to steal from it. {v: 12:6}

### Scene 4: "Leave Her Alone"

**"Leave her alone,"** Jesus replied (12:7). {v: 12:7}

**"She has kept this perfume in preparation for the day of My burial"** (12:7). {v: 12:7}

Back then, perfume and spices were put on a body when it was buried. Mary was getting Jesus ready for his death. {v: 12:7}

**"The poor you will always have with you, but you will not always have Me"** (12:8). {v: 12:8}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

John 12:1-8 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Pure nard"** (12:3) | Very expensive perfume from a plant that grows in faraway mountains. |
| **"Wiped them with her hair"** (12:3) | Mary showed humble, total love for Jesus. |
| **"The house was filled with the fragrance"** (12:3) | Her gift couldn't be hidden. |
| **"Why wasn't this perfume sold"** (12:5) | Judas complained that her gift was a waste. |
| **"For the day of My burial"** (12:7) | Jesus knew he would soon die. |
| **"You will not always have Me"** (12:8) | This was a special moment to honor Jesus. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Nard came from the Himalaya mountains.** It was carried a very long way, which made it very costly.†

**Guests reclined at the table.** They lay on cushions with their feet away from the table. That made it easy for Mary to reach Jesus's feet.†

**Matthew and Mark tell a similar story.** In their telling, a woman pours perfume on Jesus's head (Mark 14:3-9, Matthew 26:6-13).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does Jesus not care about the poor?** He cares deeply. Jesus is quoting God's law, which says there will always be poor people, so always be generous to them (Deuteronomy 15:11). He is saying this was a once-in-a-lifetime moment to honor him.†

**Was Mary wasting money?** Judas thought so. Jesus called it a beautiful thing. Love sometimes gives more than seems sensible.

**Is this the same Mary as Mary Magdalene?** No. This is Mary of Bethany, the sister of Martha and Lazarus.†

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Martha | Busy | She was serving the dinner (12:2) |
| Mary | Devoted | She poured out her most precious thing for Jesus (12:3) |
| Judas | Greedy | He wanted the money for himself (12:6) |
| Jesus | Grateful | He defended Mary's gift (12:7) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What would you give Jesus?** Tap one.

- My time :: Mary sat at his feet before. Time with Jesus is a gift.
- Something precious :: Mary gave her best. Jesus loved it.
- I feel judged :: Judas criticized Mary. Jesus said, "Leave her alone."
- I have little to give :: Jesus gave himself for you first. Love him back with what you have.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Spending money on worship is a waste."*

**What's true:** God wants us to care for the poor. Jesus did too.

**What the story is mainly about:** Mary's gift was love poured out for Jesus, right before he poured out his life. Jesus defended her. Real love for Jesus and care for the poor go together.

**Where Jesus is:** At a dinner with his friends, less than a week before his death, being honored by someone who loved him.

## ✝️ Good News

Mary poured out her perfume for Jesus. A few days later, Jesus poured out his life for Mary, and for us.

**"She has kept this perfume in preparation for the day of My burial"** (12:7).

Jesus knew where he was going. He went to the cross and the tomb, and he rose again. The fragrance of that love fills the whole world.

**Mary poured out her love on Jesus. Jesus poured out his life for us.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that good works flow out of faith, like fruit from a tree. Mary's gift wasn't to earn anything. It was the overflow of a heart that loved Jesus.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads John 12:1-8 on the Fifth Sunday in Lent. In all three years, John 12:1-11 is read on Monday of Holy Week.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Mary poured expensive perfume on Jesus's feet to prepare him for his burial. Her love was beautiful, and his love was greater.**
