# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Job:** A good man who loves God. He has a big family and great wealth.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.
- **Satan:** The name means "the accuser." He claims Job only loves God for what he gets.
- **Job's wife:** She suffers the same losses Job does.
- **Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar:** Three friends who come to comfort Job.

**Setting**

- The land of Uz, somewhere east of Israel. Also, the court of heaven.

**When**

- Long ago. The book doesn't say when.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Job is one of the oldest questions in the world.** Why do good people suffer?

**The book is a long poem in the middle.** Chapters 3 to 37 are speeches: Job crying out, and his friends arguing. This story walks through the beginning and the end.

**Here's what you know that Job never learns:** what happened in the court of heaven.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: A Good Man

In the land of Uz lived a man named Job. **"This man was blameless and upright, fearing God and shunning evil"** (1:1). {v: 1:1}

He had seven sons and three daughters, and huge flocks and herds. He was the greatest man in the East. {v: 1:2, 1:3}

He prayed for his children all the time. {v: 1:5}

### Scene 2: "Does Job Fear God for Nothing?"

One day, the angels came before the LORD, and Satan came too. {v: 1:6}

God said, **"Have you considered My servant Job?"** (1:8). No one on earth was like him. {v: 1:8}

Satan answered, **"Does Job fear God for nothing?"** (1:9). God protected him and blessed everything he did. {v: 1:9, 1:10}

Take it away, Satan said, and Job will curse you. {v: 1:11}

God allowed Satan to test Job, but not to harm Job himself. {v: 1:12}

### Scene 3: One Terrible Day

Then, in one day, messenger after messenger came running. {v: 1:13}

Raiders stole the oxen and donkeys. Fire killed the sheep. Raiders took the camels. Servants were killed. {v: 1:14, 1:15, 1:16, 1:17}

Then the worst news: a mighty wind knocked down the house where Job's children were eating. All ten of them died. {v: 1:18, 1:19}

Job tore his robe and fell to the ground. And he worshiped. {v: 1:20}

**"The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away. Blessed be the name of the LORD"** (1:21). {v: 1:21}

### Scene 4: Sitting in Ashes

Satan came again. "Hurt his body, and he'll curse you." God allowed it, but Job's life must be spared. {v: 2:4, 2:5, 2:6}

Job was covered with painful sores from head to foot. He sat in ashes, scraping himself with a piece of broken pottery. {v: 2:7, 2:8}

His wife said, "Curse God and die!" {v: 2:9}

Job answered, **"Should we accept from God only good and not adversity?"** (2:10). Adversity means hard times. {v: 2:10}

His three friends came. They barely recognized him. They wept and sat on the ground with him for seven days without saying a word. {v: 2:11, 2:12, 2:13}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture four men sitting in the dust under the open sky. Nobody speaks. Day turns to night, and night turns to day, seven times.

### Scene 5: "My Redeemer Lives"

Then the friends started talking. They said Job must have done something wrong to deserve this. Job said he hadn't. He cried out to God, asking why. {v: 19:23}

In the middle of his pain, Job said something amazing. {v: 19:25}

**"But I know that my Redeemer lives, and in the end He will stand upon the earth"** (19:25). A Redeemer is someone who rescues and sets things right. {v: 19:25}

**"Even after my skin has been destroyed, yet in my flesh I will see God"** (19:26). {v: 19:26}

### Scene 6: Out of the Whirlwind

At last, God answered Job, **"out of the whirlwind"** (38:1). A whirlwind is a powerful storm. {v: 38:1}

God didn't explain why Job suffered. Instead, he asked Job questions. {v: 38:2, 38:3}

**"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?"** (38:4). {v: 38:4}

God asked who set the measurements of the earth, and who told the sea, **"You may come this far, but no farther"** (38:11). {v: 38:5, 38:8, 38:11}

God showed Job how great and wise he is, far beyond anything Job could understand. {v: 38:4, 38:11}

### Scene 7: "Now My Eyes Have Seen You"

Job answered, **"I know that You can do all things"** (42:2). {v: 42:2}

**"My ears had heard of You, but now my eyes have seen You"** (42:5). {v: 42:5}

God told the three friends they had been wrong about him. Job had spoken honestly. Job prayed for his friends. {v: 42:7, 42:8, 42:9}

Then God restored Job. He gave him twice as much as before. He had ten more children. His family came and comforted him. {v: 42:10, 42:11, 42:12, 42:13}

Job lived a long life and saw his grandchildren and great-grandchildren. {v: 42:16, 42:17}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Job 1 to 2, 19:23-27, 38:1-11, and 42 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Does Job fear God for nothing?"** (1:9) | The question is whether Job loves God, or only God's gifts. |
| **"The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away"** (1:21) | Job trusted God even when he lost everything. |
| **"No one spoke a word to him"** (2:13) | The friends' silence was their best comfort. |
| **"I know that my Redeemer lives"** (19:25) | Job held on to hope beyond death. |
| **"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?"** (38:4) | God is far greater and wiser than we understand. |
| **"Now my eyes have seen You"** (42:5) | Meeting God was the answer Job needed. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Many people believed suffering was always punishment.** Job's friends thought so. The book of Job says they were wrong (42:7).†

**"I know that my Redeemer lives"** became a famous Easter hymn, and an aria in Handel's *Messiah*.

**James wrote about Job.** He said Job is an example of patient endurance, and that the Lord is full of compassion (James 5:11).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God make deals with Satan about people?** The opening scene is told in a way that helps us see the question: will Job love God for God's sake? It doesn't mean God is careless with people. The book is about trusting God when we don't understand.†

**Is suffering always punishment for sin?** No. Job was good, and he suffered anyway. Jesus said the same about a man born blind (John 9:3).

**Did getting new children make up for losing the first ones?** No one can replace a child. The ending shows God's blessing, but Job surely still grieved.†

**Is it okay to be angry with God?** Job cried out honestly, and God said Job spoke rightly of him (42:7). God can handle your honest prayers.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Devastated | He lost his children and everything (1:20) |
| Job's wife | Bitter | She had lost everything too (2:9) |
| The friends | Grieving | They wept when they saw him (2:12) |
| Job | Hopeful | "I know that my Redeemer lives" (19:25) |
| Job | Humbled | He saw God (42:5) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If you're suffering, you must have done something wrong."*

**What's true:** Sometimes our choices bring trouble.

**What the story is mainly about:** Job was a good man and suffered terribly. His friends blamed him, and God said they were wrong. Suffering isn't proof that God is punishing you.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus is the Redeemer Job longed for. He suffered though he was innocent, and he rose. Because he lives, we will see God.

## ✝️ Good News

Job lost everything. In his darkest hour, he held on to one hope.

**"But I know that my Redeemer lives, and in the end He will stand upon the earth"** (19:25).

Jesus is that Redeemer. He stood on the earth again on Easter morning. Because he lives, death and suffering won't have the last word.

**Job lost everything and still said, "I know that my Redeemer lives." God met him in the whirlwind. Jesus is the Redeemer who lives.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said the book of Job shows that God sometimes hides himself in suffering. Faith clings to God even when he seems hidden, like Job did.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Job 38:1-11 in June (Lectionary 12), next to Jesus calming the storm. In Year C, it reads Job 19:23-27a in November (Lectionary 32). In Year B, parts of Job are also read in October (Lectionary 27 to 30).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Job was a good man who lost everything. He cried out to God and held on: "I know that my Redeemer lives." God met him in the whirlwind.**
