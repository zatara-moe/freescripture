# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** The book calls its author "the Teacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem" (1:1). Many readers think of Solomon. Others think a later writer used Solomon's voice.†

**What this book is.** Ecclesiastes is wisdom from someone who tried everything: work, money, pleasure, learning. He asks, "What's the point?"

**What "futility" means.** The Hebrew word is *hevel*. It means breath, vapor, mist. Something that disappears fast and can't be held. Some Bibles say "vanity" or "meaningless."†

**How to read it.** This book is honest about how life can feel. Read to the end of the passage. The Teacher finds something good.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Everything Is Futile | 1:1-3 | The Teacher asks what all our work is for |
| 2. Round and Round | 1:4-11 | Nature repeats, and people are forgotten |
| 3. Chasing the Wind | 1:12-14 | The Teacher tried to understand everything |
| 4. Who Gets It All? | 2:18-23 | You have to leave your work to someone else |
| 5. From the Hand of God | 2:24-26 | Enjoying life is God's gift |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Everything Is Futile

**"Futility of futilities,"** says the Teacher, **"futility of futilities! Everything is futile!"** (1:2). {v: 1:1, 1:2}

Everything is like a breath. Here, then gone. {v: 1:2}

**"What does a man gain from all his labor, at which he toils under the sun?"** (1:3). {v: 1:3}

"Under the sun" means life on earth, seen from a human point of view. {v: 1:3}

### Section 2: Round and Round

Generations come and go. The earth stays. {v: 1:4}

The sun rises and sets. The wind blows round and round. Rivers flow into the sea, but the sea is never full. {v: 1:5, 1:6, 1:7}

**"There is nothing new under the sun"** (1:9). {v: 1:9}

People who came before are forgotten. We will be forgotten too. {v: 1:11}

### Section 3: Chasing the Wind

The Teacher tried to understand everything with wisdom. {v: 1:12, 1:13}

**"I have seen all the things that are done under the sun, and have found them all to be futile, a pursuit of the wind"** (1:14). {v: 1:14}

Trying to hold on to it all is like trying to catch the wind. {v: 1:14}

### Section 4: Who Gets It All?

The Teacher worked hard and built great things. But one day he will die and leave it all to someone else. {v: 2:18}

Will that person be wise or foolish? Who knows? {v: 2:19}

**"So my heart began to despair over all the labor that I had done under the sun"** (2:20). {v: 2:20}

Even at night, his mind wouldn't rest. {v: 2:23}

### Section 5: From the Hand of God

Then the Teacher sees something. {v: 2:24}

**"Nothing is better for a man than to eat and drink and enjoy his work. I have also seen that this is from the hand of God"** (2:24). {v: 2:24}

**"For apart from Him, who can eat and who can find enjoyment?"** (2:25). {v: 2:25}

Life can't be held on to. But it can be received, day by day, as a gift from God. {v: 2:24, 2:25}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Jesus told a parable about a rich man who built bigger barns, then died that night. **"Then who will own what you have accumulated?"** (Luke 12:20).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Ecclesiastes 1:1-14 and 2:18-26 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Everything is futile!"** (1:2) | Life passes quickly, like a breath. |
| **"What does a man gain from all his labor"** (1:3) | Work alone can't give life meaning. |
| **"There is nothing new under the sun"** (1:9) | People keep repeating the same things. |
| **"A pursuit of the wind"** (1:14) | Trying to control everything is like grabbing air. |
| **"I must leave it to the man who comes after me"** (2:18) | We can't keep what we build. |
| **"This is from the hand of God"** (2:24) | Joy in ordinary life is God's gift. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Ecclesiastes means "one who gathers people."** The Hebrew title is *Qoheleth*, "the Teacher" or "the Preacher."†

**Jewish communities read Ecclesiastes** at the Feast of Booths, a harvest festival.†

**The book ends** by saying, **"Fear God and keep His commandments, because this is the whole duty of man"** (12:13).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is the Bible saying life is meaningless?** The Teacher is saying life "under the sun," without God, can't hold meaning. With God, ordinary life becomes a gift.

**Should I stop working hard?** No. The Teacher says enjoying your work is good, a gift from God. Just don't expect work to give your life its meaning.

**What if I feel like this, like nothing matters?** Many people feel that sometimes. Talk to someone you trust. If you ever feel hopeless or have thoughts of not wanting to live, please tell a trusted adult right away. You can also call or text 988 in the United States.

## 💭 How People Feel

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What are you chasing?** Tap one.

- Success :: It's good, but it can't hold your life together.
- Being remembered :: The Teacher worried about that. God never forgets you.
- Nothing matters :: Talk to someone. God says you matter to him.
- Joy :: Enjoy today's food, work, and friends as God's gifts.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If you get enough money and success, you'll be happy."*

**What's true:** Hard work and good things are blessings.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The Teacher had everything and found it was all like a breath. He found joy only when he received life as a gift from God's hand.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus said, **"I have come that they may have life, and have it in all its fullness"** (John 10:10). In him, life isn't futile.

## ✝️ Good News

Life under the sun slips through our fingers. But it comes from God's hand.

**"Nothing is better for a man than to eat and drink and enjoy his work. I have also seen that this is from the hand of God"** (2:24).

In Jesus, our lives are not a breath that vanishes. He gives life that lasts, and he gives joy in ordinary days.

**The Teacher said, "Everything is futile," like chasing the wind. But eating, drinking, and enjoying your work are gifts from God's hand.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved Ecclesiastes. He said it teaches us to enjoy the good things God gives today, with thankful hearts, instead of worrying about tomorrow.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads Ecclesiastes 1:2, 12-14 and 2:18-23 in early August (Lectionary 18), next to Jesus's parable of the rich fool.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The Teacher found that everything under the sun is like a breath, a chasing of the wind. But ordinary joys are gifts from the hand of God.**
