# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul, an apostle of Jesus, wrote this letter to the Christians in Rome around AD 57. He hadn't visited them yet (1:10-13).

**What came before.** In chapters 1 to 7, Paul explains that everyone has sinned, and that God makes people right with him as a gift, through faith in Jesus (3:23-24). In chapter 7, Paul describes the struggle inside him: he wants to do good, but keeps doing wrong. He cries, **"What a wretched man I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death?"** (7:24).

**What this chapter is.** Paul's answer. Many people call Romans 8 the best chapter in the Bible. It starts with "no condemnation" and ends with "nothing can separate us."

**Words to know.** "The flesh" means human nature turned away from God, not just our bodies. "The Spirit" means the Holy Spirit.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: No Condemnation

**"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus"** (8:1). {v: 8:1}

Condemnation means being found guilty and sentenced. For people who belong to Jesus, that sentence is gone. {v: 8:1}

God's law showed what's right, but it couldn't make people good. So God sent his own Son as an offering for sin. {v: 8:2, 8:3}

Jesus did what the law couldn't. {v: 8:4}

### Section 2: Flesh and Spirit

Paul describes two ways to live. One is focused on ourselves, turned away from God. The other is led by God's Spirit. {v: 8:5}

**"The mind of the flesh is death, but the mind of the Spirit is life and peace"** (8:6). {v: 8:6}

If God's Spirit lives in you, the same Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead will give life to your body too. {v: 8:9, 8:10, 8:11}

### Section 3: Abba, Father

**"For all who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God"** (8:14). Daughters too: everyone the Spirit leads. {v: 8:14}

**"For you did not receive a spirit of slavery that returns you to fear, but you received the Spirit of sonship"** (8:15). By the Spirit we cry out to God, **"Abba! Father!"** (8:15). {v: 8:15}

Abba is an Aramaic word a child used for their father. Jesus prayed this way (Mark 14:36). {v: 8:15}

**"The Spirit Himself testifies with our spirit that we are God's children"** (8:16). {v: 8:16}

If we are children, we are heirs, sharing everything with Christ. {v: 8:17}

### Section 4: Creation Groans

**"I consider that our present sufferings are not comparable to the glory that will be revealed in us"** (8:18). {v: 8:18}

The whole creation is waiting. It is stuck in decay, but one day it will be set free. {v: 8:19, 8:20, 8:21}

**"We know that the whole creation has been groaning together in the pains of childbirth until the present time"** (8:22). {v: 8:22}

Birth pains hurt, but they lead to new life. {v: 8:22}

We groan too, waiting for our bodies to be made new. We hope for what we can't see yet, and we wait patiently. {v: 8:23, 8:24, 8:25}

### Section 5: The Spirit Prays for Us

**"In the same way, the Spirit helps us in our weakness. For we do not know how we ought to pray, but the Spirit Himself intercedes for us with groans too deep for words"** (8:26). {v: 8:26}

When you don't know what to pray, God's Spirit prays for you. {v: 8:26, 8:27}

**"And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose"** (8:28). {v: 8:28}

God called his people, made them right with him, and will bring them to glory. {v: 8:29, 8:30}

### Section 6: If God Is for Us

**"What then shall we say in response to these things? If God is for us, who can be against us?"** (8:31). {v: 8:31}

**"He who did not spare His own Son but gave Him up for us all, how will He not also, along with Him, freely give us all things?"** (8:32). {v: 8:32}

Who can accuse us? God declares us right. Who can condemn us? Christ died, rose, and prays for us. {v: 8:33, 8:34}

**"Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall trouble or distress or persecution or famine or nakedness or danger or sword?"** (8:35). {v: 8:35}

**"No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through Him who loved us"** (8:37). {v: 8:37}

**"For I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor principalities, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord"** (8:38-39). {v: 8:38, 8:39}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Many churches read 8:38-39 at funerals. Nothing, not even death, can separate God's people from his love.

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Romans 8 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"No condemnation"** (8:1) | In Christ, the guilty verdict is gone. |
| **"The mind of the Spirit is life and peace"** (8:6) | Life led by God's Spirit is real life. |
| **"Abba! Father!"** (8:15) | God's Spirit makes us God's own children. |
| **"Groaning together in the pains of childbirth"** (8:22) | The world's pain is waiting for new birth. |
| **"Groans too deep for words"** (8:26) | When you can't pray, the Spirit prays for you. |
| **"God works all things together for the good"** (8:28) | God brings good out of everything for his people. |
| **"If God is for us, who can be against us?"** (8:31) | With God on our side, nothing can defeat us. |
| **"Will be able to separate us from the love of God"** (8:39) | God's love in Christ holds on forever. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Roman Christians faced trouble.** Some had been forced out of Rome under Emperor Claudius (Acts 18:2). Later, under Nero, many were killed.†

**Adoption in Rome** gave a child full rights as an heir, the same as a born child.†

**"Principalities"** means spiritual powers and rulers.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does "all things work together for good" mean bad things are good?** No. Bad things are bad. Paul means God can bring good out of anything for his people, even suffering.

**What is "the flesh"? Is my body bad?** No. Your body is God's good creation, and God will raise it (8:11). "Flesh" here means human nature turned away from God.

**What about predestined?** Paul wants to comfort, not confuse. God's love for his people started before they were born, and it will bring them home.†

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If you mess up too badly, God will stop loving you."*

**What's true:** Sin is serious. Paul spends chapters on it.

**What the passage is mainly about:** For people who belong to Christ, there is no condemnation. And nothing, not death, not life, not anything in all creation, can separate them from God's love.

**Where Jesus is:** At the right hand of God, praying for us (8:34). He is the Son God didn't spare, given up for us all.

## ✝️ Good News

Romans 8 begins with "no condemnation" and ends with "nothing can separate us."

**"If God is for us, who can be against us?"** (8:31).

God didn't spare his own Son. He gave him for you. His Spirit makes you his child. And nothing can ever separate you from his love in Christ Jesus.

**There is no condemnation for those in Christ. God is for us, and nothing in all creation can separate us from his love.**

## 📚 From Luther

In his preface to Romans, Luther called this letter "the chief part of the New Testament" and "the purest gospel." He said every Christian should know it by heart and treasure it as daily bread for the soul.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads parts of Romans 8 on the Fifth Sunday in Lent and in July (Lectionary 15, 16, and 17). In Year B, it reads Romans 8:12-17 on Holy Trinity Sunday. Romans 8 is also read at many funerals.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**There is no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. God's Spirit makes us his children, and nothing can separate us from the love of God.**
