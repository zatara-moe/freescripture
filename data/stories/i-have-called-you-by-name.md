# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's for.** God's people in exile in Babylon. Their city and temple were destroyed. They felt forgotten and punished.†

**What comes before.** In chapter 42, God says his people are like prisoners in a dark pit (42:22). They had not listened to him.

**What this passage is.** God answers with some of the most tender words in the Bible. Two passages from the same chapter: a promise of protection (43:1-7) and a promise of something new (43:16-25).

**How to read it.** Notice how many times God says "I" and "Mine." This is God speaking about what *he* will do.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. You Are Mine | 43:1 | God calls his people by name |
| 2. Through the Waters | 43:2-4 | God will be with them in danger |
| 3. Gathered From Everywhere | 43:5-7 | God will bring his scattered people home |
| 4. Something New | 43:16-21 | God will make a way in the wilderness |
| 5. Your Sins No More | 43:22-25 | God forgives for his own sake |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: You Are Mine

God speaks as the one who created and formed his people. {v: 43:1}

**"Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!"** (43:1). {v: 43:1}

To redeem means to buy back, to set free by paying a price. {v: 43:1}

### Section 2: Through the Waters

**"When you pass through the waters, I will be with you; and when you go through the rivers, they will not overwhelm you"** (43:2). {v: 43:2}

**"When you walk through the fire, you will not be scorched"** (43:2). {v: 43:2}

God doesn't promise there will be no deep waters or fire. He promises he will be there in them. {v: 43:2}

**"Because you are precious and honored in My sight, and because I love you"** (43:4). {v: 43:4}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Three young men were thrown into a fiery furnace for staying true to God. They walked out without even smelling like smoke (Daniel 3:27).

### Section 3: Gathered From Everywhere

**"Do not be afraid, for I am with you"** (43:5). {v: 43:5}

God will call to the east, west, north, and south: give back my sons and daughters. {v: 43:5, 43:6}

Everyone called by his name will come home. {v: 43:7}

### Section 4: Something New

God reminds them of the Exodus. He made a path through the sea and defeated Egypt's army. {v: 43:16, 43:17}

But then he says something surprising. **"Do not call to mind the former things; pay no attention to the things of old"** (43:18). {v: 43:18}

**"Behold, I am about to do something new; even now it is coming. Do you not see it?"** (43:19). {v: 43:19}

**"Indeed, I will make a way in the wilderness and streams in the desert"** (43:19). {v: 43:19}

### Section 5: Your Sins No More

God tells the truth. The people hadn't called on him. They had grown tired of him. {v: 43:22}

**"But you have burdened Me with your sins; you have wearied Me with your iniquities"** (43:24). {v: 43:24}

Then comes the surprise. Not punishment, but forgiveness. {v: 43:25}

**"I, yes I, am He who blots out your transgressions for My own sake and remembers your sins no more"** (43:25). {v: 43:25}

Transgressions are sins, times of crossing the line. God forgives because of who *he* is, not because of what *they* did. {v: 43:25}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> In baptism, God calls each of his children by name, through water, and claims them as his own.

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 43:1-7 and 16-25 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"I have called you by your name; you are Mine!"** (43:1) | God knows you personally and claims you. |
| **"When you pass through the waters, I will be with you"** (43:2) | God doesn't promise no troubles, but his presence in them. |
| **"You are precious and honored in My sight"** (43:4) | God treasures his people. |
| **"I am about to do something new"** (43:19) | God isn't finished. He makes new beginnings. |
| **"A way in the wilderness"** (43:19) | God makes a path where there isn't one. |
| **"Remembers your sins no more"** (43:25) | When God forgives, he doesn't hold it against you. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Egypt, Cush, and Seba were powerful lands.** Cush and Seba were south of Egypt. God says he would give up whole nations for his people, to show how much he values them.†

**The Exodus was Israel's great rescue story.** God led them out of Egypt through the sea (Exodus 14). God promises an even greater rescue.†

**Persia would let the exiles go home.** King Cyrus allowed the people to return to Jerusalem (Ezra 1:1-3).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God promise that I'll never get hurt?** No. He promises to be with you in the deep waters and the fire. You won't go through them alone.

**Should we forget the past?** God doesn't want us to forget what he did. He wants us to look for the new things he is doing now, not to stay stuck in the past.

**Did God "give" nations away?** This is a picture of how precious God's people are to him. He would give anything for them.†

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God keeps a list of everything you've done wrong."*

**What's true:** God knows everything. Sin is real.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God says, **"I... remembers your sins no more"** (43:25). God chooses to forget. He forgives for his own sake, because of his love.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus passed through the deep waters of suffering and the fire of the cross. He paid the price to redeem us, and he calls each of his sheep by name (John 10:3).

## ✝️ Good News

God's people felt forgotten. God said, "You are Mine."

**"Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!"** (43:1).

In Jesus, God has redeemed you. In baptism, he called you by name. You belong to him, and he will be with you in every deep water.

**"Do not fear. I have redeemed you. I have called you by name. You are Mine." God is with his people, and he remembers their sins no more.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught people to remember their baptism when they were afraid. In hard times, he would say, "I am baptized." God had called him by name and claimed him.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads Isaiah 43:1-7 on the Baptism of Our Lord and Isaiah 43:16-21 on the Fifth Sunday in Lent. In Year B, it reads Isaiah 43:18-25 on the Seventh Sunday after the Epiphany.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God says, "Do not fear, for I have redeemed you. I have called you by name. You are Mine." He is with us in deep waters, and he forgives our sins.**
