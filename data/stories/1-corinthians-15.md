# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul wrote this letter around AD 54 to the church he started in Corinth, a busy Greek port city.

**What was happening.** Some people in the church were saying there is no resurrection of the dead (15:12). Many Greeks thought the body didn't matter, and that only the soul lived on.†

**What this chapter is.** The longest teaching about the resurrection in the Bible. It begins with the earliest summary of the gospel and ends with a shout of victory over death.

**How to read it.** This chapter is read at many funerals and on Easter. Read it slowly.

## 🗺️ Map of the Letter

| 1. Of First Importance | 15:1-11 | Christ died, was buried, rose, and appeared |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Of First Importance

Paul reminds them of the gospel, the good news that saves them. {v: 15:1, 15:2}

**"For what I received I passed on to you as of first importance: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that He was buried, that He was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures"** (15:3-4). {v: 15:3, 15:4}

Then Paul lists the witnesses. Jesus appeared to Peter, to the Twelve, to more than five hundred people at once, to James, to all the apostles, and last of all to Paul. {v: 15:5, 15:6, 15:7, 15:8}

Most of the five hundred were still alive. You could go ask them. {v: 15:6}

Paul felt unworthy, because he had once attacked the church. **"But by the grace of God I am what I am"** (15:10). {v: 15:9, 15:10}

### Section 2: If Christ Has Not Been Raised

Some people said the dead don't rise. Paul says: then Christ didn't rise either. {v: 15:12, 15:13}

**"And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins"** (15:17). {v: 15:14, 15:17}

**"If our hope in Christ is for this life alone, we are to be pitied more than all men"** (15:19). {v: 15:19}

Everything depends on the resurrection. {v: 15:19}

### Section 3: The Firstfruits

**"But Christ has indeed been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep"** (15:20). {v: 15:20}

Firstfruits were the first part of the harvest, a promise that the rest is coming. {v: 15:20}

**"For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive"** (15:22). {v: 15:21, 15:22}

Christ rose first. When he comes again, those who belong to him will rise. {v: 15:23}

**"The last enemy to be destroyed is death"** (15:26). {v: 15:24, 15:25, 15:26}

### Section 4: How Are the Dead Raised?

Someone asks, "What kind of body will we have?" {v: 15:35}

Paul points to a seed. You plant a seed, it "dies," and something new grows that looks very different. {v: 15:36, 15:37, 15:38}

**"What is sown is perishable; it is raised imperishable"** (15:42). {v: 15:42}

**"It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power"** (15:43). {v: 15:43}

We look like Adam now, made from dust. One day we will look like Christ. {v: 15:47, 15:48, 15:49}

### Section 5: We Will All Be Changed

**"Listen, I tell you a mystery: We will not all sleep, but we will all be changed"** (15:51). {v: 15:50, 15:51}

**"In an instant, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, the dead will be raised imperishable, and we will be changed"** (15:52). {v: 15:52}

**"Death has been swallowed up in victory"** (15:54). {v: 15:53, 15:54}

**"Where, O Death, is your victory? Where, O Death, is your sting?"** (15:55). {v: 15:55}

**"But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ!"** (15:57). {v: 15:56, 15:57}

So keep going. **"Your labor in the Lord is not in vain"** (15:58). {v: 15:58}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Corinthians 15 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"As of first importance"** (15:3) | This is the center of the gospel. |
| **"He appeared to more than five hundred"** (15:6) | Many people saw the risen Jesus. |
| **"If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile"** (15:17) | Christianity stands or falls on the resurrection. |
| **"The firstfruits"** (15:20) | Jesus's resurrection is the first of many. |
| **"The last enemy to be destroyed is death"** (15:26) | Death will not win. |
| **"It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power"** (15:43) | Our new bodies will be strong and lasting. |
| **"Where, O Death, is your sting?"** (15:55) | Death has lost its power over God's people. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Verses 3 to 5 may be the oldest Christian creed.** Paul says he "received" it. Scholars think it goes back to within a few years of the resurrection.†

**Firstfruits** were offered to God at the start of harvest (Leviticus 23:10). Jesus rose on the day after the Sabbath, when firstfruits were offered.†

**Paul quotes Isaiah 25:8 and Hosea 13:14** about death being swallowed up.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Will we be ghosts in heaven?** No. Paul says we'll have bodies, raised and made new, like Jesus's risen body.

**What is "baptized for the dead"?** No one knows for sure what this meant. Paul mentions it but doesn't explain it.†

**What about people who die before Jesus comes back?** Paul says they have "fallen asleep." They will be raised.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"When you die, your soul floats away, and your body doesn't matter."*

**What's true:** The soul goes to be with the Lord.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God cares about bodies too. Jesus rose with a real body. One day, God will raise our bodies, made new and imperishable.

**Where Jesus is:** Risen, the firstfruits, reigning until death itself is destroyed.

## ✝️ Good News

Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose on the third day.

**"But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ!"** (15:57).

Because he lives, death has lost its sting. One day, in the twinkling of an eye, we will be changed.

**Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who sleep. Death is swallowed up in victory, and God gives us the victory through Jesus.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther preached a long series of sermons on 1 Corinthians 15. He said the resurrection of Christ is the foundation of everything we believe, and that a Christian should look at a grave like a farmer looks at a planted field.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads parts of 1 Corinthians 15 on Easter Day and on the Fifth through Eighth Sundays after the Epiphany. In Year B, it reads 1 Corinthians 15:1-11 as an Easter option. It is also read at many funerals.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day. Because he lives, death is swallowed up in victory.**
