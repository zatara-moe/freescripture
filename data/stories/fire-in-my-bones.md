# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Jeremiah was a prophet in Jerusalem for about forty years, from around 627 BC until the city fell to Babylon in 587 BC. God called him when he was young (1:6-7).

**What was happening.** God's people had turned to other gods and ignored the poor. Jeremiah warned that Babylon would conquer them. Almost no one listened. People mocked him, plotted against him, and threw him in prison.

**What this passage is.** Several of Jeremiah's honest prayers and messages from chapters 11 to 28. Jeremiah argues with God, complains, and even says he wants to quit. And God keeps holding on to him.

**How to read it.** Notice how honest Jeremiah's prayers are. The Bible lets him say hard things to God.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Like a Lamb

God showed Jeremiah that people from his own hometown were plotting to kill him. {v: 11:18}

**"For I was like a gentle lamb led to slaughter"** (11:19). {v: 11:19}

Jeremiah leaves his cause with God, who judges fairly. {v: 11:20}

### Section 2: Hope of Israel

During a terrible drought, Jeremiah prays for his people. {v: 14:7}

**"Although our iniquities testify against us, O LORD, act for the sake of Your name"** (14:7). {v: 14:7}

Iniquities means sins. {v: 14:7}

Jeremiah calls God **"O Hope of Israel, its Savior in times of distress"** (14:8). Then he asks why God seems like a stranger just passing through. {v: 14:8}

**"Yet You are among us, O LORD, and we are called by Your name. Do not forsake us!"** (14:9). {v: 14:9, 14:10}

The people admit their sin and remind God of his promise. {v: 14:19, 14:20, 14:21}

**"So we put our hope in You, for You have done all these things"** (14:22). {v: 14:22}

### Section 3: Your Words Were My Joy

Jeremiah is lonely and hurting. {v: 15:15}

**"Your words were found, and I ate them. Your words became my joy and my heart's delight"** (15:16). {v: 15:16}

But he sat alone, because God's message set him apart. {v: 15:17}

**"Why is my pain unending, and my wound incurable, refusing to be healed?"** (15:18). {v: 15:18}

He even says God has been like a mirage, water that isn't there. {v: 15:18}

God answers: **"If you return, I will restore you"** (15:19). {v: 15:19}

**"They will fight against you but will not overcome you, for I am with you to save and deliver you"** (15:20). {v: 15:20, 15:21}

### Section 4: A Tree by the Water

**"Cursed is the man who trusts in mankind"** (17:5). He's like a dry bush in the desert. {v: 17:5, 17:6}

**"But blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD, whose confidence is in Him"** (17:7). {v: 17:7}

**"He is like a tree planted by the waters that sends out its roots toward the stream"** (17:8). {v: 17:8}

When heat and drought come, it isn't afraid. Its leaves stay green and it keeps bearing fruit. {v: 17:8}

**"The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure. Who can understand it?"** (17:9). {v: 17:9}

Only God searches the heart. {v: 17:10}

### Section 5: Fire in My Bones

Jeremiah has been beaten and put in stocks (20:2). Now he lets it all out. {v: 20:7}

**"I am a laughingstock all day long; everyone mocks me"** (20:7). {v: 20:7, 20:8}

He decides to stop speaking for God. But he can't. {v: 20:9}

God's message **"becomes a fire burning in my heart, shut up in my bones, and I become weary of holding it in, and I cannot prevail"** (20:9). {v: 20:9}

Even his friends are waiting for him to fall. {v: 20:10}

**"But the LORD is with me like a fearsome warrior"** (20:11). {v: 20:11, 20:12}

**"Sing to the LORD! Praise the LORD! For He rescues the life of the needy from the hands of evildoers"** (20:13). {v: 20:13}

### Section 6: Like a Hammer

God asks, **"Am I only a God nearby"** (23:23), **"and not a God far away?"** (23:23). {v: 23:23}

No one can hide from God. He fills heaven and earth. {v: 23:24}

False prophets were telling made-up dreams and saying they came from God. {v: 23:25, 23:26, 23:27}

Their dreams are like straw. God's word is like grain. {v: 23:28}

God asks, **"Is not My word like fire"** (23:29), **"and like a hammer that smashes a rock?"** (23:29). {v: 23:29}

### Section 7: A Yoke of Wood, a Yoke of Iron

God had told Jeremiah to wear a wooden yoke, like an ox, as a sign: Babylon would rule over them (27:2). {v: 28:1}

A prophet named Hananiah said God would break Babylon's yoke in two years. {v: 28:2, 28:3, 28:4}

Jeremiah said, **"Amen!"** and **"May the LORD do so!"** (28:6). He wished it were true. {v: 28:5, 28:6}

But a prophet who promises peace is proven true only when it happens. {v: 28:7, 28:8, 28:9}

Hananiah broke Jeremiah's wooden yoke. {v: 28:10, 28:11}

God's answer: Hananiah had broken a yoke of wood, but a yoke of iron would replace it. {v: 28:12, 28:13, 28:14}

**"The LORD did not send you, but you have persuaded this people to trust in a lie"** (28:15). {v: 28:15, 28:16, 28:17}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Jeremiah 11:18-20; 14; 15:15-21; 17:5-10; 20:7-13; 23:23-29; 28 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Like a gentle lamb led to slaughter"** (11:19) | Jeremiah suffered without knowing the danger. |
| **"O Hope of Israel"** (14:8) | Even in disaster, God is the one to trust. |
| **"Your words became my joy"** (15:16) | God's word fed Jeremiah's soul. |
| **"A tree planted by the waters"** (17:8) | People who trust God stay strong in hard times. |
| **"The heart is deceitful above all things"** (17:9) | We can't fully trust our own hearts. |
| **"A fire burning in my heart"** (20:9) | God's word wouldn't let Jeremiah be quiet. |
| **"Like a hammer that smashes a rock"** (23:29) | God's word is powerful. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Jeremiah is called "the weeping prophet."** He cried over his people (9:1), and he may have also written the book of Lamentations.†

**A yoke** was a wooden bar fitted on the necks of oxen so they could pull a plow. It was a picture of being ruled by someone.

**Babylon** conquered Jerusalem in 587 BC, just as Jeremiah warned, and took many people into exile.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Can I really talk to God like Jeremiah did?** Yes. Jeremiah complained, argued, and even accused God. God didn't reject him. Honest prayer is still prayer.

**Did God really "deceive" Jeremiah?** That's how Jeremiah felt. He expected his job to go differently. His words show how hurt he was, and God answered him with a promise.

**How can I tell a true message from a false one?** Jeremiah says God's word is like grain, not straw. Check what people say against God's word.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If you're really faithful, you'll always feel happy and close to God."*

**What's true:** God gives joy in his word.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Jeremiah was faithful, and he was lonely, mocked, and hurting. He even felt like God had tricked him. Yet God held on to him: **"I am with you to save and deliver you"** (15:20).

**Where Jesus is:** Jeremiah, "like a gentle lamb led to slaughter," points to Jesus, who was rejected by his own people and suffered to save them.

## ✝️ Good News

You don't have to be strong to be God's.

**"But the LORD is with me like a fearsome warrior"** (20:11).

Jeremiah was weak, lonely, and ready to quit. God made him a wall of bronze. He promises to be with you too.

**Jeremiah was mocked, lonely, and ready to quit, and he told God so. God held on to him, and his word kept burning like fire in his bones.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther knew times of deep struggle, which he called *Anfechtung*. He said these struggles teach us to cling to God's word, because feelings come and go, but God's promises stand.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads these parts of Jeremiah in the summer: Jeremiah 20:7-13 and 28:5-9 in Year A (Lectionary 12 and 13), 15:15-21 in Year A (Lectionary 22), 17:5-10 on the Sixth Sunday after the Epiphany in Year C, and 23:23-29 in Year C (Lectionary 20).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Jeremiah was honest with God about his pain. God's word burned in him like fire, and God promised, "I am with you to save and deliver you." Blessed is the one who trusts in the LORD.**
