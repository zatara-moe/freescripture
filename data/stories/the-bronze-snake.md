# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Moses:** The leader God chose to bring Israel out of Egypt.
- **The Israelites:** God's people, still wandering in the desert.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- The desert near the land of Edom, south of the Dead Sea.

**When**

- Near the end of Israel's forty years in the desert, more than 3,000 years ago.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**God had fed his people every day.** He gave them manna from heaven and water from a rock (Exodus 16:15, 17:6).

**The people had been in the desert almost forty years.** Most of the first generation had died. Their children were about to enter the promised land (14:33-34).

**They had to take the long way around.** The king of Edom wouldn't let them pass through his land (20:21).

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Tired of it all | 21:4-5 | The people complain about the food |
| 2. Snakes in the camp | 21:6-7 | People are bitten and ask Moses to pray |
| 3. Look and live | 21:8-9 | God tells Moses to make a bronze snake |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Tired of It All

The Israelites set out from Mount Hor. They had to go the long way around Edom. {v: 21:4}

The people became impatient on the journey. {v: 21:4}

They spoke against God and against Moses. **"Why have you led us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness?"** (21:5). {v: 21:5}

They said there was no bread and no water. Then they said they hated the manna. **"We detest this wretched food!"** (21:5). {v: 21:5}

The manna was the bread God himself sent from heaven every morning. {v: 21:5}

### Scene 2: Snakes in the Camp

Then the LORD sent poisonous snakes among the people. Many were bitten, and many died. {v: 21:6}

The people came to Moses. **"We have sinned by speaking against the LORD and against you"** (21:7). {v: 21:7}

They asked Moses to pray that God would take the snakes away. {v: 21:7}

So Moses prayed for the people. {v: 21:7}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture a crowd of frightened people outside Moses's tent. Some are holding bitten arms and legs. They aren't complaining anymore.

### Scene 3: Look and Live

God didn't take the snakes away. He gave a cure instead. {v: 21:8}

The LORD told Moses, **"Make a fiery serpent and mount it on a pole. When anyone who is bitten looks at it, he will live"** (21:8). {v: 21:8}

So Moses made a snake out of bronze and put it up on a pole. {v: 21:9}

**"If anyone who was bitten looked at the bronze snake, he would live"** (21:9). {v: 21:9}

They didn't need medicine or to do something hard. They only needed to look, and trust God's promise. {v: 21:9}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Numbers 21:4-9 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The people grew impatient"** (21:4) | They were tired and lost their trust in God. |
| **"We detest this wretched food!"** (21:5) | They insulted God's daily gift. |
| **"We have sinned"** (21:7) | The people confessed and asked for help. |
| **"Moses interceded for the people"** (21:7) | To intercede means to pray for someone else. |
| **"Mount it on a pole"** (21:8) | The cure was lifted up where everyone could see it. |
| **"When anyone who is bitten looks at it, he will live"** (21:8) | God's promise was attached to the snake. Looking was trusting. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"Fiery serpents" may describe the burning pain of the bite.** The desert of that region has poisonous snakes, including vipers.†

**The bronze snake was kept for hundreds of years.** Later, people started worshiping it. King Hezekiah broke it into pieces (2 Kings 18:4).

**A snake on a pole is still a symbol of healing.** Many medical signs show a snake on a staff, though that symbol may come from Greek stories instead.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did God send the snakes to hurt people?** The Bible says God sent them as judgment for rejecting him. It is a hard part of the story. But God also provided a way to live, right away, for anyone who would look.†

**Wasn't making a snake statue against God's rules?** God himself commanded this one. It wasn't to be worshiped. It was a sign of his promise. When people later worshiped it, it was destroyed.

**Does looking at something make you healthy?** No. Looking at the snake worked because God attached his promise to it. It was trust in God's word.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The people | Impatient | The journey was long (21:4) |
| The people | Afraid | Snakes were biting them (21:6) |
| The people | Sorry | They confessed their sin (21:7) |
| The people | Relieved | Looking at the snake, they lived (21:9) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What would you need to "look up" from today?** Tap one.

- Complaining :: The people complained about God's gifts. You can say sorry, and look up.
- Something that hurts :: God gave a way to live. Look to Jesus.
- I've tried hard to fix it :: The cure wasn't hard. Just look and trust.
- Praying for others :: Moses prayed for people who complained about him.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"You have to earn your way to God."*

**What's true:** God takes sin seriously. It is deadly, like a snake bite.

**What the story is mainly about:** The bitten people couldn't fix themselves. God gave them something to look at, and a promise: look and live. Trusting God's promise saved them.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus pointed to this very story. **"Just as Moses lifted up the snake in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up, that everyone who believes in Him may have eternal life"** (John 3:14-15).

## ✝️ Good News

The people were dying. God lifted up a cure, and everyone who looked lived.

**"If anyone who was bitten looked at the bronze snake, he would live"** (21:9).

Jesus was lifted up on the cross. Everyone who looks to him in faith has life that lasts forever. Right after this, Jesus said, **"For God so loved the world that He gave His one and only Son"** (John 3:16).

**God lifted up a bronze snake so that bitten people could look and live. Jesus was lifted up on the cross so everyone who trusts him will live.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved this picture. He said faith is like those bitten Israelites looking at the bronze snake. We don't look at our own wounds or our own works. We look at Christ on the cross, and live.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Numbers 21:4-9 on the Fourth Sunday in Lent, next to John 3:14-21, where Jesus explains it.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God told Moses to lift up a bronze snake, and everyone who looked at it lived. Jesus was lifted up on the cross, so everyone who trusts him lives.**
