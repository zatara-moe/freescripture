# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Jesus:** The one the Gospels present as the promised Messiah and the Son of God.
- **A Canaanite woman:** A mother who is not Jewish. Her daughter is suffering terribly.
- **The disciples:** Jesus's closest followers. They want her to go away.

**Setting**

- The region of Tyre and Sidon, north of Galilee, along the coast. It was outside the land of Israel.

**When**

- About 2,000 years ago, during Jesus's work in Galilee.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Jesus had just argued with some leaders.** They cared about washing their hands the right way. Jesus said what matters is what comes out of the heart (15:1-20).

**Jesus left for a while.** He went north to Tyre and Sidon, outside Israel (15:21).

**Canaanites were ancient enemies of Israel.** Long before, God's people had fought the Canaanites. For a Jewish person, a Canaanite was a complete outsider.†

**Here's what you know:** Jesus has said that many will come from east and west to share God's feast (8:11).

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A mother cries out | 15:21-22 | A Canaanite woman begs for help |
| 2. Silence | 15:23-24 | Jesus says nothing at first |
| 3. "Lord, help me!" | 15:25-26 | She kneels, and Jesus gives a hard answer |
| 4. Even the crumbs | 15:27-28 | Her answer shows great faith, and her daughter is healed |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: A Mother Cries Out

Jesus went to the area around Tyre and Sidon. {v: 15:21}

A Canaanite woman from there came to him, crying out. {v: 15:22}

**"Lord, Son of David, have mercy on me! My daughter is miserably possessed by a demon"** (15:22). {v: 15:22}

"Son of David" was a name for the King God promised. This outsider called Jesus by that name. {v: 15:22}

### Scene 2: Silence

**"But Jesus did not answer a word"** (15:23). {v: 15:23}

His disciples came and urged him, **"Send her away, for she keeps crying out after us"** (15:23). {v: 15:23}

Jesus said, **"I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel"** (15:24). His work on earth was first to the people of Israel. {v: 15:24}

### Scene 3: "Lord, Help Me!"

But the woman didn't give up. She came and knelt in front of Jesus. {v: 15:25}

**"Lord, help me!"** she said (15:25). {v: 15:25}

Jesus answered, **"It is not right to take the children's bread and toss it to the dogs"** (15:26). {v: 15:26}

This is a picture. The children are the people of Israel. The bread is what Jesus came to give them. In that picture, outsiders were like the little dogs under the table. {v: 15:26}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture the woman kneeling in the dust, not moving, looking up at Jesus. The disciples stand back, waiting to see what happens.

### Scene 4: Even the Crumbs

The woman answered right back. {v: 15:27}

**"Yes, Lord,"** she said, **"even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their master's table"** (15:27). {v: 15:27}

She didn't argue that she deserved the bread. She trusted that Jesus had so much that even a crumb would be enough. {v: 15:27}

**"O woman,"** Jesus answered, **"your faith is great! Let it be done for you as you desire"** (15:28). {v: 15:28}

Her daughter was healed from that very hour. {v: 15:28}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Matthew 15:21-28 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Lord, Son of David, have mercy on me!"** (15:22) | This outsider knew who Jesus was and asked for mercy. |
| **"Jesus did not answer a word"** (15:23) | Sometimes God seems silent. She kept asking. |
| **"Send her away"** (15:23) | The disciples saw her as a bother. |
| **"Lord, help me!"** (15:25) | Her prayer was short and honest. |
| **"Even the dogs eat the crumbs"** (15:27) | She didn't claim to deserve anything. She trusted Jesus's huge kindness. |
| **"Your faith is great!"** (15:28) | Jesus praised an outsider's faith, and gave her what she asked. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Tyre and Sidon were cities on the coast.** They were outside the land of Israel, in what is now Lebanon (Easton's Bible Dictionary).

**Jewish people and their non-Jewish neighbors were often unfriendly.** People sometimes called outsiders "dogs" as an insult.†

**Mark tells this story too.** Mark calls her **"a Greek woman of Syrophoenician origin"** (Mark 7:26).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was Jesus being mean?** His words sound harsh to us. Many readers think Jesus was testing her faith, and drawing it out so everyone could see it. He ended by praising her faith and healing her daughter.†

**Did Jesus call her a dog?** Jesus used a picture of children and little dogs at a family table. The word he used means a small house dog, a pet.† She turned the picture around: even pets get crumbs. Jesus loved her answer.

**Does God sometimes ignore our prayers?** Sometimes God seems silent, like Jesus was at first. But Jesus heard her the whole time. He hears you too. The Bible encourages us to keep praying (Luke 18:1).

**Was her daughter's suffering her fault?** No. The Bible never says so. Her mother loved her and asked Jesus for help.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The woman | Desperate | Her daughter was suffering terribly (15:22) |
| The disciples | Annoyed | She kept crying out after them (15:23) |
| The woman | Determined | She knelt and kept asking (15:25, 15:27) |
| Jesus | Pleased | Her faith was great (15:28) |

# Why It Matters

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God only listens to people who deserve it."*

**What's true:** God is holy, and we don't deserve his gifts.

**What the story is mainly about:** The woman knew she had no right to ask. She asked anyway, trusting Jesus's mercy. Jesus called that great faith. Faith doesn't point to what we deserve. It points to how good Jesus is.

**Where Jesus is:** Outside Israel, hearing a desperate mother, and giving her far more than a crumb.

## ✝️ Good News

The woman said, "Even the dogs eat the crumbs." She trusted that Jesus had more than enough.

**"O woman,"** Jesus answered, **"your faith is great! Let it be done for you as you desire"** (15:28).

Jesus's mercy reached an outsider. It reaches you too. You don't need to deserve it.

**Jesus hears people who cry out for mercy, even outsiders. His mercy is more than enough, and it is for you.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved this story. In a sermon on it, he said the woman held Jesus to his own word and goodness, even when he seemed to say no. He said faith clings to God's yes, hidden under what sounds like a no.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Matthew 15:21-28 in August of Year A (Lectionary 20). Mark's version, Mark 7:24-37, is read in September of Year B (Lectionary 23).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**A Canaanite mother kept asking Jesus for mercy, trusting that even a crumb from him was enough. Jesus praised her great faith.**
