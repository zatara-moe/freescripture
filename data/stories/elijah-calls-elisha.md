# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Elijah was Israel's greatest prophet, who had just stood alone against hundreds of false prophets on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18). Exhausted and afraid, he fled to the wilderness. God met him there and sent him back with a task: appoint a successor.

**What was happening.** Israel's kings had led the nation away from God. Elijah had fought that corruption nearly alone. Now God was telling him his work was not finished — and he would not finish it alone.

**What this passage is.** A calling story. God sends Elijah to anoint three people. The one we follow is Elisha — a farmer who would become the most miracle-working prophet in Israel's history.

**How to read it.** Notice what Elisha gives up, and how quickly he gives it up.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Commission | 19:15-16 | God gives Elijah three people to anoint |
| 2. The Call | 19:19 | Elijah throws his cloak over Elisha |
| 3. The Response | 19:20-21 | Elisha says goodbye, burns his plow, and follows |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Commission

The LORD said to him, **"Go back by the way you came, and go to the Desert of Damascus"** (19:15). {v: 19:15}

God sends the exhausted prophet back to work. The wilderness is not the end of Elijah's story. {v: 19:15}

**"You are also to anoint Jehu son of Nimshi as king over Israel and Elisha son of Shaphat from Abel-meholah to succeed you as prophet"** (19:16). {v: 19:16}

Elisha's name appears last, but he is the one the story follows. He will succeed Elijah. {v: 19:16}

### Section 2: The Call

**"So Elijah departed and found Elisha son of Shaphat. He was plowing with twelve teams of oxen, and he was with the twelfth team. Elijah passed by him and threw his cloak around him"** (19:19). {v: 19:19}

Twelve teams of oxen meant a large, prosperous farm. Elisha was not poor. He had something to give up. Elijah said nothing — he simply threw his cloak around him. That was enough. {v: 19:19}

### Section 3: The Response

Elisha left the oxen, ran after Elijah, and said, **"Please let me kiss my father and mother goodbye, and then I will follow you"** (19:20). {v: 19:20}

Elisha did not debate. He ran. He asked only for a brief goodbye. {v: 19:20}

**"So Elisha turned back from him, took his pair of oxen, and slaughtered them. With the oxen's equipment, he cooked the meat and gave it to the people, and they ate. Then he set out to follow and serve Elijah"** (19:21). {v: 19:21}

He killed his livestock and burned his plow — the tools of the old life. There was no going back. Then he fed the people, said goodbye, and followed. {v: 19:21}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Kings 19:15-16, 19-21 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Succeed you as prophet"** (19:16) | Elisha would continue Elijah's work, not replace it. |
| **"Threw his cloak around him"** (19:19) | The cloak was a symbol of Elijah's prophetic authority passing to Elisha. |
| **"He was plowing with twelve teams of oxen"** (19:19) | Elisha had wealth and a settled life. He left it. |
| **"Then he set out to follow and serve Elijah"** (19:21) | Burning his bridges. No backup plan. |
| **"To follow and serve Elijah"** (19:21) | Elisha began as Elijah's servant before he became the prophet. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**The prophetic cloak** was a distinctive outer garment. Throwing it over someone was a symbolic act of claiming them — or commissioning them. When Elijah later ascended to heaven, Elisha picked up this same cloak (2 Kings 2:13).

**Oxen and plowing** were the tools of a farmer's livelihood. Twelve teams was a significant holding — not a small operation.

**Slaughtering the oxen and burning the equipment** was a visible, public farewell. Everyone watching understood: he is not coming back.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Why did Elijah say, "Go on back — for what have I done to you?"** Elijah's response is puzzling. Some read it as testing Elisha's resolve. Others see it as a kind of release: "I haven't forced you into anything. You can go." Elisha's response shows he understood the call was real.

**Does this mean Christians must leave their jobs when called?** Elisha's act was specific to him. What it illustrates is that the call of God can disrupt settled plans — and that a wholehearted response is possible.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Elijah | Sent | After despair in the wilderness, God gives him a clear task (19:15) |
| Elisha | Decisive | He ran after Elijah; he destroyed his old livelihood (19:20-21) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God calls people to dramatic life changes all at once."*

**What's true:** Elisha did leave his farm in a single day.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"Then he set out to follow and serve Elijah"** (19:21). The call was sudden; the following was a long apprenticeship. Elisha served before he led.

**Where Jesus is:** When Jesus called fishermen — "Come, follow me" — they left their nets immediately (Mark 1:18). The same pattern: a call, a cost, a wholehearted response. Jesus is the one who calls with authority and is worth following at any cost.

## ✝️ Good News

God calls people who are busy with something else.

**"He was plowing with twelve teams of oxen... Elijah passed by him and threw his cloak around him"** (19:19).

God found Elisha at work. He calls people in the middle of ordinary life.

**Elijah found Elisha at his plow. Elisha burned it. The call of God is worth everything.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther preached that God uses human servants to continue his work — that Elisha was not Elijah's replacement but his successor in the same calling. Every pastor and teacher, Luther said, stands in a long line of those called to carry the same message forward.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, 1 Kings 19:15-16, 19-21 is read on the Thirteenth Sunday after Pentecost (Lectionary 13), alongside a Gospel text where Jesus tells a would-be follower that the Son of Man has no place to lay his head.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Elijah threw his cloak. Elisha burned his plow. The old life ended. The new one began with one act of following.**
