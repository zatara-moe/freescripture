# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Isaiah was a prophet in Jerusalem about 740 to 700 BC (1:1). His book is one of the longest in the Bible.

**What was happening.** Jerusalem was busy with worship: sacrifices, feasts, crowds at the temple. But at the same time, the powerful cheated the poor, and widows and orphans had no one to defend them.

**What this passage is.** Three of Isaiah's messages: God's complaint about empty worship, a sad love song about a vineyard, and a promise of a great feast where God swallows up death forever.

**How to read it.** The first two are hard to hear. The third is pure hope. Watch how God moves from warning to invitation.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Empty Worship | 1:10-15 | God is tired of worship without justice |
| 2. White as Snow | 1:16-18 | Learn to do right, and be washed clean |
| 3. The Song of the Vineyard | 5:1-7 | God planted a vineyard, and it grew sour grapes |
| 4. You Have Been a Refuge | 25:1-5 | Praise for God, who shelters the poor |
| 5. A Feast for All Peoples | 25:6-9 | God will swallow up death forever |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Empty Worship

Isaiah calls the leaders of Jerusalem by shocking names: **"you rulers of Sodom"** (1:10). {v: 1:10}

Sodom was a city destroyed for its wickedness (Genesis 19). {v: 1:10}

**"What good to Me is your multitude of sacrifices?"** says the LORD (1:11). {v: 1:11}

The people kept bringing offerings and celebrating feasts. God says he is tired of all of it. {v: 1:12, 1:13, 1:14}

**"When you spread out your hands in prayer, I will hide My eyes from you"** (1:15). {v: 1:15}

Why? **"Your hands are covered with blood"** (1:15). {v: 1:15}

### Section 2: White as Snow

**"Wash and cleanse yourselves. Remove your evil deeds from My sight. Stop doing evil!"** (1:16). {v: 1:16}

**"Learn to do right; seek justice and correct the oppressor. Defend the fatherless and plead the case of the widow"** (1:17). {v: 1:17}

Then God makes an amazing offer. {v: 1:18}

**"Come now, let us reason together,"** says the LORD (1:18). {v: 1:18}

**"Though your sins are like scarlet, they will be as white as snow; though they are as red as crimson, they will become like wool"** (1:18). {v: 1:18}

Scarlet and crimson are deep red dyes that are almost impossible to wash out. {v: 1:18}

### Section 3: The Song of the Vineyard

Isaiah sings a love song about his friend's vineyard. {v: 5:1}

His friend did everything right. He cleared the stones, planted the best vines, and built a watchtower and a winepress. {v: 5:2}

**"He waited for the vineyard to yield good grapes, but the fruit it produced was sour!"** (5:2). {v: 5:2}

**"What more could I have done for My vineyard than I already did for it?"** (5:4). {v: 5:3, 5:4}

So the owner will tear down its walls and let it go wild. {v: 5:5, 5:6}

Then Isaiah explains: **"For the vineyard of the LORD of Hosts is the house of Israel"** (5:7). {v: 5:7}

**"He looked for justice, but saw bloodshed; for righteousness, but heard a cry of distress"** (5:7). {v: 5:7}

### Section 4: You Have Been a Refuge

Later in the book, Isaiah breaks into praise. {v: 25:1}

**"O LORD, You are my God! I will exalt You; I will praise Your name"** (25:1). {v: 25:1, 25:2, 25:3}

**"For You have been a refuge for the poor, a stronghold for the needy in distress, a refuge from the storm, a shade from the heat"** (25:4). {v: 25:4, 25:5}

### Section 5: A Feast for All Peoples

**"On this mountain the LORD of Hosts will prepare a banquet for all the peoples"** (25:6). {v: 25:6}

The best food, the finest wine, for everyone. {v: 25:6}

God will remove the burial cloth that covers all nations. {v: 25:7}

**"He will swallow up death forever. The Lord GOD will wipe away the tears from every face"** (25:8). {v: 25:8}

**"Surely this is our God; we have waited for Him, and He has saved us"** (25:9). {v: 25:9}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 1:10-18; 5:1-7; 25:1-9 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"What good to Me is your multitude of sacrifices?"** (1:11) | God doesn't want worship without love for others. |
| **"Seek justice"** (1:17) | Stand up for people who are treated unfairly. |
| **"Let us reason together"** (1:18) | God invites us to come and talk with him. |
| **"White as snow"** (1:18) | God can wash away even the deepest stains of sin. |
| **"The vineyard of the LORD"** (5:7) | God's people, whom he planted and cared for. |
| **"A refuge for the poor"** (25:4) | God protects people who have no one else. |
| **"He will swallow up death forever"** (25:8) | One day, death will be gone. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Vineyards** needed years of work before they produced good grapes. A sour harvest was heartbreaking.†

**Widows and orphans** had no one to protect them in court or provide for them. God often names them as people his people must defend.

**Paul quotes Isaiah 25:8** when he writes about the resurrection: "Death has been swallowed up in victory" (1 Corinthians 15:54).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God hate worship?** No. He hates worship that pretends everything is fine while people are being hurt. True worship and justice go together.

**Is the feast real?** It's a picture of the joy God has ready for his people. Jesus pointed to it when he spoke of people coming from east and west to feast in God's kingdom (Matthew 8:11).

**Did Jesus use this vineyard song?** Yes. His parable of the tenants starts with the same vineyard (Matthew 21:33).

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| God | Weary | He is tired of empty worship (1:14) |
| God | Heartbroken | His vineyard grew sour grapes (5:4) |
| The poor | Sheltered | God is their refuge (25:4) |
| God's people | Joyful | He has saved us (25:9) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"As long as I go to church, God is happy with me."*

**What's true:** God gathers his people to worship him.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God told busy worshipers, **"Learn to do right; seek justice"** (1:17). Worship and love for neighbor belong together. And God invites us to come, be washed clean, and start again.

**Where Jesus is:** His blood makes sins white as snow, and he is preparing the great feast where death is swallowed up forever.

## ✝️ Good News

God doesn't just point out the stains. He washes them.

**"Though your sins are like scarlet, they will be as white as snow"** (1:18).

And one day he will swallow up death and wipe away every tear.

**God calls his people to seek justice, and he offers to wash their sins white as snow. He will spread a feast for all peoples and swallow up death forever.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said that true worship isn't about how many ceremonies we perform, but about faith in God's promises and love for our neighbor.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Isaiah 1:10-18 in Year C (Lectionary 31), 5:1-7 in Year A (Lectionary 27), and 25:1-9 in Year A (Lectionary 28). Isaiah 25:6-9 is also read on Easter Day in Year B and on All Saints Day.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God is tired of worship without justice, and grieves his sour vineyard. But he says, "Come now, let us reason together": he will wash our sins white as snow and swallow up death forever.**
