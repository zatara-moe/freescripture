# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who is praying.** King Solomon, David's son, around 960 BC. God had given him great wisdom (1 Kings 3:12).

**What was happening.** For seven years, Solomon's workers had built a temple for God in Jerusalem (6:38). David had wanted to build it, but God said his son would (2 Samuel 7:12-13). Now it was finished.

**What this passage is.** The day the temple was opened. The ark of the covenant is carried in, God's glory fills the house like a cloud, and Solomon prays one of the longest prayers in the Bible.

**How to read it.** Notice Solomon's big question: can a building hold God? And notice who he prays for, including foreigners.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Cloud of Glory

Solomon gathered all the leaders of Israel to bring the ark of the covenant into the temple. {v: 8:1, 8:2, 8:3, 8:4}

The ark was a gold-covered chest. Inside were the two stone tablets of the Ten Commandments. {v: 8:9}

The people offered more sacrifices than anyone could count. {v: 8:5}

The priests carried the ark into the Most Holy Place, under the wings of the golden cherubim. {v: 8:6, 8:7, 8:8}

When the priests came out, a cloud filled the temple. {v: 8:10}

**"For the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD"** (8:11). {v: 8:11}

The priests couldn't even stand to do their work. {v: 8:11}

**"I have indeed built You an exalted house, a place for You to dwell forever"** (8:13). {v: 8:12, 8:13}

### Section 2: No God Like You

Solomon stood before the altar and lifted his hands to heaven. {v: 8:22}

**"O LORD, God of Israel, there is no God like You in heaven above or on earth below, keeping Your covenant of loving devotion with Your servants who walk before You with all their hearts"** (8:23). {v: 8:23}

**"What You spoke with Your mouth You have fulfilled with Your hand this day"** (8:24). {v: 8:24}

Solomon asks God to keep his promise that David's family would always have a king. {v: 8:25, 8:26}

### Section 3: Can Heaven Contain You?

Then Solomon asks an amazing question. {v: 8:27}

**"But will God indeed dwell upon the earth? Even heaven, the highest heaven, cannot contain You, much less this temple I have built"** (8:27). {v: 8:27}

God is too big for any building. Yet he chooses to meet his people there. {v: 8:28, 8:29}

**"May You hear from heaven, Your dwelling place. May You hear and forgive"** (8:30). {v: 8:30}

### Section 4: Even the Foreigner

Solomon prays for people who aren't Israelites, who will come from far away because they've heard about God. {v: 8:41, 8:42}

**"Then may You hear from heaven, Your dwelling place, and do according to all for which the foreigner calls to You"** (8:43). {v: 8:43}

**"Then all the peoples of the earth will know Your name"** (8:43). {v: 8:43}

### Section 5: Not One Word Has Failed

When Solomon finished praying, he stood and blessed the people. {v: 8:54, 8:55}

**"Not one word has failed of all the good promises He made through His servant Moses"** (8:56). {v: 8:56}

**"May the LORD our God be with us, as He was with our fathers. May He never leave us nor forsake us"** (8:57). {v: 8:57, 8:58, 8:59}

**"So that all the peoples of the earth may know that the LORD is God. There is no other!"** (8:60). {v: 8:60, 8:61}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Kings 8:1-13, 22-30, 41-43, 54-61 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The glory of the LORD filled the house"** (8:11) | God showed he was present. |
| **"There is no God like You"** (8:23) | The LORD is unique and faithful. |
| **"Fulfilled with Your hand"** (8:24) | God does what he says. |
| **"Heaven... cannot contain You"** (8:27) | God is bigger than any place. |
| **"Hear and forgive"** (8:30) | The temple was a place to find forgiveness. |
| **"The foreigner"** (8:41) | God's house was open to all peoples. |
| **"Not one word has failed"** (8:56) | Every promise of God comes true. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**The ark of the covenant** had traveled with God's people through the desert and into the land. Now it had a permanent home.

**Cherubim** are heavenly beings. Golden figures of them stood over the ark with their wings spread out.

**The cloud of glory** had filled the tabernacle in Moses's time too (Exodus 40:34-35).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did God live in the temple?** Solomon says God is too big for any building. But God promised to be present there for his people in a special way.

**Do we still need a temple?** Jesus called his own body the temple (John 2:19-21). In him, God dwells with us. The church, God's people, is now his temple (1 Corinthians 3:16).

**Is the temple still standing?** No. The Babylonians destroyed it in 587 BC. It was rebuilt, and then the Romans destroyed it in AD 70.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God lives in church buildings."*

**What's true:** God promises to meet us where his word is preached and his sacraments are given.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Solomon himself said, **"Even heaven, the highest heaven, cannot contain You, much less this temple I have built"** (8:27). God is everywhere, and he chooses to come close.

**Where Jesus is:** He is God dwelling with us in the flesh. In him, God's glory came to live among his people (John 1:14).

## ✝️ Good News

The God no building can hold comes close to hear and forgive.

**"May You hear from heaven, Your dwelling place. May You hear and forgive"** (8:30).

Not one of God's promises has ever failed. In Jesus, he has come to dwell with us.

**God's glory filled the temple. Heaven can't contain him, yet he hears his people's prayers and forgives. Not one word of his promises has failed.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther preached at the dedication of the castle church in Torgau in 1544. He said a church is a place where "our dear Lord himself may speak to us through his holy Word and we may speak to him through prayer and praise."†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads 1 Kings 8:22-23, 41-43 in late May or early June (Lectionary 9), alongside the story of the Roman centurion, a foreigner who trusted Jesus.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God's glory filled the new temple. Heaven itself can't contain him, yet he hears and forgives his people and welcomes the foreigner. Not one word of his good promises has failed.**
