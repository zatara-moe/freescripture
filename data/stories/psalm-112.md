# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Anonymous — the companion psalm to Psalm 111, also an acrostic.

**What it is.** A portrait of the person who fears God — what their life looks like, what they receive, and how they face the world.

**What it's about.** Blessed is the one who fears the LORD. Their children are mighty. They are generous. They are not afraid of bad news. The wicked see it and are furious.

**When the church sings it.** On Sundays in Ordinary Time and Epiphany.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Blessed Is the One | 112:1–4 | Fear God, delight in His commands — their descendants are mighty |
| 2. They Give Freely | 112:5–9 | Generous, gracious, steady — they will not be shaken |
| 3. The Wicked See and Are Furious | 112:10 | The wicked gnash their teeth — what they want, the righteous inherit |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Blessed Is the One

**"Hallelujah! Blessed is the man who fears the LORD, who greatly delights in His commandments."** (112:1). {v: 112:1}

Two marks of the blessed person: they fear the LORD, and they delight in His commands. Not duty — delight. {v: 112:1}

**"His descendants will be mighty in the land; the generation of the upright will be blessed"** (112:2). {v: 112:2}

The blessing extends. What a person lives shapes what their children inherit. {v: 112:2}

**"Wealth and riches are in his house, and his righteousness endures forever"** (112:3). {v: 112:3}

Both material abundance and lasting righteousness. The psalm does not spiritualize away the physical. {v: 112:3}

**"Light dawns in the darkness for the upright— for the gracious, compassionate, and righteous."** (112:4). {v: 112:4}

The one who fears God becomes a light. The attributes of God (Psalm 111) are now found in the person who worships Him. {v: 112:4}

### Section 2: They Give Freely

**"It is well with the man who is generous and lends freely, whose affairs are guided by justice."** (112:5). {v: 112:5}

Generosity is not described as sacrifice — it is described as the way a righteous person naturally operates. {v: 112:5}

**"Surely he will never be shaken; the righteous man will be remembered forever."** (112:6). {v: 112:6}

**"He does not fear bad news; his heart is steadfast, trusting in the LORD."** (112:7). {v: 112:7}

This verse is not about optimism — it is about a grounded person. The news may be bad. The heart does not collapse. {v: 112:7}

**"His heart is assured; he does not fear, until he looks in triumph on his foes."** (112:8). {v: 112:8}

**"He has scattered abroad his gifts to the poor; his righteousness endures forever; his horn will be lifted high in honor."** (112:9). {v: 112:9}

Giving to the poor is not separate from righteousness — it is part of it. {v: 112:9}

### Section 3: The Wicked See and Are Furious

**"The wicked man will see and be grieved; he will gnash his teeth and waste away; the desires of the wicked will perish."** (112:10). {v: 112:10}

The final verse: the contrast. The righteous are stable; the wicked seethe. What the wicked desire — what the righteous have — will not be given to them. It will vanish. {v: 112:10}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 112 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"greatly delights in His commandments"** (112:1) | The law is not a burden to this person — it is what they love. |
| **"Light dawns in the darkness"** (112:4) | The one who fears God (Psalm 111) becomes like God — gracious, compassionate, righteous. |
| **"He does not fear bad news"** (112:7) | Steadiness in crisis comes from a heart grounded in trust, not from good circumstances. |
| **"He has scattered abroad his gifts to the poor"** (112:9) | Generosity to the poor is part of what righteousness looks like — not an optional extra. |
| **"the desires of the wicked will perish"** (112:10) | What the wicked wanted — what the righteous have — they will never get. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

Psalm 112 is the companion to Psalm 111: God (111) → the person who fears God (112). Same acrostic structure, same length. The two psalms teach that the character of the God-fearer mirrors the character of God: both are gracious, compassionate, and righteous (111:4; 112:4).

**"Wealth and riches are in his house"** (112:3) — the Old Testament wisdom tradition includes prosperity as part of the blessing on the righteous, while also recognizing that it is not absolute or guaranteed (Job, Psalm 73). The psalm is describing the pattern of blessing, not promising immunity from hardship.

**"his horn will be lifted high in honor"** (112:9) — a horn raised is a symbol of victory and dignity (see also Psalm 75:4–5). The generous person receives honor in return.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"Wealth and riches are in his house"** (112:3) — Does fearing God guarantee financial prosperity? The wisdom tradition presents this as the general pattern, not a formula. Job feared God and lost everything. The psalm is describing what the full life of the righteous looks like over time, not offering a transaction. The NT qualifies this further: godliness with contentment is great gain (1 Timothy 6:6).

**"He does not fear bad news"** (112:7) — This is not denial of bad news. The verse assumes bad news comes. The question is how the heart responds — and a heart steadfast in trust does not collapse when the news is hard.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The righteous | Stable | Heart is secure, not shaken by bad news (112:7–8) |
| The generous | Honored | Giving to the poor produces lasting righteousness (112:9) |
| The wicked | Furious | They see what the righteous have — and cannot get it (112:10) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What does this psalm speak into?** Tap one.

- I'm afraid of bad news :: He does not fear bad news — the heart steadfast in trust stays steady.
- I want my life to matter beyond me :: His descendants will be mighty; his righteousness endures forever.
- I want to be generous but find it hard :: He who gives to the poor — this is what the righteous do, not what they force themselves to do.
- I feel unstable :: Surely he will never be shaken. The steadiness comes from fearing the LORD, not from circumstances.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Generous people lose what they give away."*

**What's true:** Giving costs something in the short term.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The psalm runs the opposite direction — the one who gives freely is the one who is described as never shaken, remembered forever, honored, and secure. The wicked gnash their teeth not because the righteous are poor but because they see what the righteous have. Generosity in God's economy is not loss; it is the shape of a righteous life.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the light in the darkness (John 8:12) — the same description Psalm 112 gives to the one who fears God. He is also the ultimate fulfillment of the pattern: gracious, compassionate, giving freely, not shaken even at death, honored by resurrection. Paul quotes Psalm 112:9 directly in 2 Corinthians 9:9 to describe what Christian generosity looks like.

## ✝️ Good News

**"He does not fear bad news; his heart is steadfast, trusting in the LORD."** (112:7).

Bad news comes. The heart grounded in God does not fall. That is not courage by effort — it is the fruit of a life that fears the LORD and delights in His commands.

**Psalm 112: blessed is the one who fears the LORD and delights in His commands. Gracious, generous, unshaken by bad news — their righteousness endures. The wicked see it and are furious. The desire of the wicked will perish.**

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It appoints Psalm 112 on the Fifth Sunday after Epiphany in Year A and on other Sundays in Ordinary Time.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 112: blessed is the one who fears the LORD. Their descendants are mighty, their righteousness endures, they are a light in darkness. Generous and unafraid of bad news — their heart is steadfast, trusting in the LORD.**
