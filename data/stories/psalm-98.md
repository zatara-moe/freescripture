# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Anonymous — "A Psalm."

**What it is.** A pure praise psalm — short, confident, expanding outward from Israel to all nations to all creation.

**What it's about.** Sing a new song. The LORD has done marvelous things. His salvation is now visible to the nations. The whole earth sings — then the rivers, the mountains, the sea.

**When the church sings it.** At Christmas and on Sundays in Ordinary Time.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Sing to the LORD | 98:1–3 | Sing a new song — He has done marvelous things; all nations have seen |
| 2. Make a Joyful Noise | 98:4–6 | All the earth breaks out in song with harp and horn |
| 3. Let the Creation Sing | 98:7–9 | The sea roars, rivers clap, mountains sing — the LORD is coming to judge |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Sing to the LORD

**"Sing to the LORD a new song, for He has done wonders; His right hand and holy arm have gained Him the victory"** (98:1). {v: 98:1}

A new song for a new act. God's arm — His own power — has accomplished salvation. {v: 98:1}

**"The LORD has proclaimed His salvation and revealed His righteousness to the nations"** (98:2). {v: 98:2}

What God did for Israel is now visible to the whole world. The salvation is not hidden. {v: 98:2}

**"He has remembered His love and faithfulness to the house of Israel; all the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God"** (98:3). {v: 98:3}

The love and faithfulness were promised. Now they are fulfilled — and the ends of the earth are witnesses. {v: 98:3}

### Section 2: Make a Joyful Noise

**"Make a joyful noise to the LORD, all the earth; break forth—let your cry ring out, and sing praises!"** (98:4). {v: 98:4}

The call to Israel becomes a call to all the earth. {v: 98:4}

**"Sing praises to the LORD with the lyre, in melodious song with the harp"** (98:5). {v: 98:5}

**"With trumpets and the blast of the ram's horn shout for joy before the LORD, the King"** (98:6). {v: 98:6}

Harp, voice, trumpets, horn — every instrument, every voice. The worship is total. {v: 98:6}

### Section 3: Let the Creation Sing

**"Let the sea resound, and all that fills it, the world, and all who dwell in it"** (98:7). {v: 98:7}

The invitation goes beyond humans to creation itself. {v: 98:7}

**"Let the rivers clap their hands, let the mountains sing together for joy"** (98:8). {v: 98:8}

Rivers with hands, mountains with voices — the whole created order is personified in its praise. {v: 98:8}

**"before the LORD, for He comes to judge the earth. He will judge the world with righteousness and the peoples with equity"** (98:9). {v: 98:9}

The reason creation rejoices: He is coming to judge with righteousness and equity. A just judge is not feared — He is welcomed. {v: 98:9}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 98 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Sing to the LORD a new song"** (98:1) | A new act of God calls for new praise — the old songs are not enough for what He has done. |
| **"His right hand and holy arm have gained Him the victory"** (98:1) | God saved by His own power, not through human effort or merit. |
| **"all the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God"** (98:3) | What started as Israel's story is now visible to the whole world. |
| **"Let the rivers clap their hands"** (98:8) | Creation is not passive — it participates in praising its Maker. |
| **"He will judge the world with righteousness"** (98:9) | Judgment is not a threat to those who long for justice — it is the hope that everything wrong will be made right. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

Psalm 98 is closely related to Psalm 96 — both call for a new song, both expand worship from Israel to all nations to creation. They may have been used at the same festival occasion.

**"He has remembered His love and faithfulness"** (98:3) — the Hebrew words are *hesed* (steadfast love, covenant faithfulness) and *emet* (truth, reliability). These are the two great attributes of God in the Old Testament, announced to Moses at Sinai (Exodus 34:6).

**"Let the rivers clap their hands"** (98:8) — Isaiah uses similar language in the new creation (Isaiah 55:12). The image of creation joining human praise is not poetry alone — it looks forward to a restored world.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"Rivers clap their hands"** (98:8) — Rivers don't have hands. This is poetic personification — the same move as "the mountains melt like wax before the LORD" (97:5). It is a way of saying that praise overflows language and human capacity. All creation is caught up in it.

**"He comes to judge"** (98:9) — Why does creation rejoice at the prospect of judgment? Because creation has been subject to disorder, decay, and injustice (Romans 8:20–22). A righteous judge coming to set things right is not a threat — it is the answer to creation's long groan. The mountains and rivers are not afraid of this judge.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Israel | Astonished | God's salvation is now visible to the whole world (98:3) |
| All the earth | Joyful | Called to burst into song with every instrument (98:4–6) |
| Creation | Participating | Rivers, mountains, sea — all joining the chorus (98:7–8) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What does this psalm speak into?** Tap one.

- I want to worship :: Sing a new song. Shout for joy. Every instrument, every voice.
- I feel like God's work is invisible :: All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God.
- I'm longing for justice :: He is coming to judge in righteousness and equity. Creation rejoices at that.
- Praise feels small :: Let the sea roar. Let the rivers clap. Let the mountains sing. Your voice is part of something enormous.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Judgment Day is something to dread."*

**What's true:** Judgment is serious, and for those who have lived against God it is sobering.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Psalm 98 presents the coming judgment as the reason for the earth's joy. The mountains sing because He comes to judge. Creation has been waiting for a righteous judge to set things right. This is the hope, not the terror. Those who love justice welcome the one who judges with equity.

**Where Jesus is:** The early church read Psalm 98 as pointing to Christ. His salvation — accomplished by His own right hand and holy arm (the cross and resurrection) — has been revealed to the nations. He is the coming judge who will bring equity to the whole earth. Luke 2:30–31 quotes Isaiah's version of this same psalm: "My eyes have seen Your salvation, which You have prepared in the sight of all peoples."

## ✝️ Good News

**"He has remembered His love and faithfulness to the house of Israel; all the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God"** (98:3).

He remembered. He came through. What He promised — love and faithfulness — He delivered. And the whole world is invited to see.

**Psalm 98: sing a new song — He has done wonders. All the earth sings, harp and horn. The rivers clap, the mountains rejoice — He is coming to judge the world with righteousness and equity.**

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It appoints Psalm 98 on Christmas Day and on the Sixth Sunday of Easter in Year B.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 98: sing a new song — God's right hand has worked salvation, visible to all nations. All the earth, all instruments, all creation — sing. He comes to judge the world in righteousness and equity.**
