# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Moses:** The leader God chose to bring Israel out of Egypt.
- **Aaron:** Moses's older brother and helper.
- **The Israelites:** God's people, recently freed from slavery in Egypt. They are hungry and thirsty in the desert.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- The Desert of Sin, a dry wilderness between Egypt and Mount Sinai. Then a place called Rephidim.

**When**

- About a month after the Israelites left Egypt, more than 3,000 years ago.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**God set his people free.** He brought them out of slavery in Egypt with great signs (12:31).

**God opened a path through the sea.** The Israelites walked through on dry ground, and they sang a song of praise (14:21-22, 15:1).

**Then came the desert.** It was hot and dry, with almost no food or water. Over a million people needed to eat.†

**Here's what you know:** The God who split the sea can feed his people. The Israelites are about to forget that.

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Grumbling in the Desert

The Israelites came into the Desert of Sin. {v: 16:1}

There, the whole community grumbled against Moses and Aaron. To grumble means to complain in a sour, angry way. {v: 16:2}

**"If only we had died by the LORD's hand in the land of Egypt!"** they said (16:3). {v: 16:3}

In Egypt, they said, they had pots of meat and all the bread they wanted. Now they would starve. {v: 16:3}

They forgot that in Egypt they had been slaves. {v: 16:3}

### Scene 2: Bread From Heaven

Then the LORD said to Moses, **"Behold, I will rain down bread from heaven for you"** (16:4). {v: 16:4}

Each day, the people were to go out and gather enough for that day. {v: 16:4}

Moses told them their grumbling was really against the LORD. And the LORD had heard it. {v: 16:7, 16:8}

As Aaron spoke, the people looked toward the desert. The glory of the LORD appeared in a cloud. {v: 16:10}

That evening, quail came and covered the camp. Quail are small birds that are good to eat. {v: 16:13}

In the morning, there was dew all around the camp. When the dew dried up, thin flakes like frost were left on the ground. {v: 16:13, 16:14}

The people asked each other, "What is it?" {v: 16:15}

Moses said, **"It is the bread that the LORD has given you to eat"** (16:15). {v: 16:15}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture a whole camp of people stepping out of their tents at sunrise, kneeling down, touching the white flakes on the sand, and tasting them for the first time.

### Scene 3: Enough for Today

Moses told everyone to gather as much as they needed. {v: 16:16}

Some gathered more, and some less. But when they measured it, everyone had exactly enough. {v: 16:17, 16:18}

Moses told them not to save any for the next morning. {v: 16:19}

Some didn't listen. By morning their extra manna was full of worms and stank. {v: 16:20}

Every morning they gathered what they needed. When the sun got hot, the rest melted away. {v: 16:21}

### Scene 4: A Day of Rest

On the sixth day, they gathered twice as much. {v: 16:22}

Moses said the next day was the Sabbath, a holy day of rest to the LORD. The manna they saved for the Sabbath didn't spoil. {v: 16:23, 16:24}

On the seventh day, some people went out to gather anyway. They found nothing. {v: 16:27}

So the people rested on the seventh day. {v: 16:30}

They called the bread manna. It was white and tasted like wafers made with honey. {v: 16:31}

God fed them with manna for forty years, until they came to the land he promised. {v: 16:35}

### Scene 5: Water From the Rock

Later, the people camped at a place called Rephidim. There was no water to drink. {v: 17:1}

Again they quarreled with Moses. **"Give us water to drink"** (17:2), they said. {v: 17:2}

They grumbled that Moses had brought them out of Egypt to let them and their children die of thirst. {v: 17:3}

Moses cried out to the LORD. **"What should I do with these people? A little more and they will stone me!"** (17:4). {v: 17:4}

God told Moses to take his staff, the one he had used in Egypt, and go ahead with some of the leaders. {v: 17:5}

**"Behold, I will stand there before you by the rock at Horeb. And when you strike the rock, water will come out of it for the people to drink"** (17:6). {v: 17:6}

Moses did it, and water poured out. {v: 17:6}

He named the place Massah and Meribah, which mean "testing" and "quarreling," because the people asked, **"Is the LORD among us or not?"** (17:7). {v: 17:7}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Exodus 16:1 to 17:7 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"If only we had died... in the land of Egypt!"** (16:3) | Fear made them forget how bad slavery was. |
| **"I will rain down bread from heaven"** (16:4) | God answered grumbling with a gift. |
| **"Each day... enough for that day"** (16:4) | God was teaching them to trust him one day at a time. |
| **"He who gathered little had no shortfall"** (16:18) | God made sure everyone had enough. |
| **"Today is a Sabbath to the LORD"** (16:25) | God gave them rest, and food for the day of rest. |
| **"Is the LORD among us or not?"** (17:7) | The people doubted God was with them. He was. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"Manna" sounds like the Hebrew words for "What is it?"** That's what the people asked when they first saw it (16:15).†

**An omer was a measure of grain.** It was about two quarts, enough for one person for a day.†

**A jar of manna was kept as a reminder.** It was placed before the LORD so future generations could see how God fed his people (16:33).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was manna a natural thing?** Some people have suggested natural explanations. But the Bible tells it as God's gift: it came six days a week, enough for everyone, for forty years.†

**Is it wrong to save food?** No. God was teaching his people to trust him one day at a time in the desert. Jesus taught us to pray, "Give us this day our daily bread."

**Was God angry at their grumbling?** God was patient. He answered their complaints with bread, meat, and water. He wanted them to learn to trust him.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Israelites | Hungry | There was no food in the desert (16:3) |
| The Israelites | Confused | They didn't know what manna was (16:15) |
| Moses | Angry | People kept manna overnight against God's word (16:20) |
| Moses | Desperate | The people were ready to stone him (17:4) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If God really cared, I'd never go without."*

**What's true:** God cares about our needs, even our hunger and thirst.

**What the story is mainly about:** God's people were scared and grumbling. God didn't leave them. He fed them every day for forty years. He taught them to trust him one day at a time.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus fed five thousand people in a wilderness. Then he said, **"I am the bread of life"** (John 6:35). And Paul wrote that the rock that gave water in the desert pointed to Christ (1 Corinthians 10:4).†

## ✝️ Good News

The people grumbled. God answered with bread from heaven.

**"It is the bread that the LORD has given you to eat"** (16:15).

Jesus is the true bread from heaven. He gives himself to us in his word and in the Lord's Supper. He gives us what we need, one day at a time.

**God fed his people in the desert, every day, for forty years. Jesus is the bread of life, and he gives us what we need.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Small Catechism, Luther explained "daily bread" in the Lord's Prayer. It means everything we need for this life: food, clothing, home, family, good weather, peace, and more. God gives it even to people who don't ask. We pray so we will recognize it and be thankful.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Exodus 16:2-4 and 9-15 in late July or early August (Lectionary 18), next to Jesus saying, "I am the bread of life." In Year A, it reads Exodus 17:1-7 on the Third Sunday in Lent, next to the woman at the well.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**In the desert, God fed his grumbling people with bread from heaven and water from a rock. Jesus is the bread of life.**
