# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Where it happens.** At Mount Sinai, after God rescued his people from Egypt and gave them the Ten Commandments.

**What this book is.** Leviticus is full of laws about worship, sacrifices, and daily life. Many readers skip it. But tucked in the middle, in chapter 19, is a passage Jesus called one of the two greatest commandments.

**What this passage is.** God tells his people to be holy, because he is holy. Then he shows what holiness looks like: not just in worship, but in fields, workplaces, courtrooms, and hearts.

**How to read it.** Notice the line that keeps repeating: "I am the LORD."

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Be Holy | 19:1-4 | Because God is holy |
| 2. Leave Some for the Poor | 19:9-10 | Don't harvest every last bit |
| 3. Fair and Honest | 19:11-16 | Don't steal, cheat, or play favorites |
| 4. Love Your Neighbor | 19:17-18 | No grudges, no revenge |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Be Holy

God tells Moses to speak to all the people. {v: 19:1}

**"Be holy because I, the LORD your God, am holy"** (19:2). {v: 19:2}

Holy means set apart for God, different from the world around. {v: 19:2}

Respect your mother and father. Keep God's day of rest. Don't turn to idols. {v: 19:3, 19:4}

### Section 2: Leave Some for the Poor

When you harvest your fields, don't cut all the way to the edges. Don't go back for what you missed. {v: 19:9}

Don't pick every last grape. **"Leave them for the poor and the foreigner. I am the LORD your God"** (19:10). {v: 19:10}

Holiness starts in the field: making room for people in need. {v: 19:9, 19:10}

### Section 3: Fair and Honest

**"You must not steal. You must not lie or deceive one another"** (19:11). {v: 19:11, 19:12}

Pay workers on time, the same day. {v: 19:13}

**"You must not curse the deaf or place a stumbling block before the blind"** (19:14). {v: 19:14}

Don't take advantage of people who can't defend themselves. {v: 19:14}

In court, don't favor the poor or the rich. Judge fairly. {v: 19:15}

Don't spread gossip. Don't put your neighbor in danger. {v: 19:16}

### Section 4: Love Your Neighbor

**"You must not harbor hatred against your brother in your heart"** (19:17). {v: 19:17}

If someone wrongs you, talk to them directly. {v: 19:17}

**"Do not seek revenge or bear a grudge against any of your people, but love your neighbor as yourself. I am the LORD"** (19:18). {v: 19:18}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Jesus called this the second great commandment (Matthew 22:39). Later in this chapter, God says to love the foreigner as yourself too (19:34).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Leviticus 19:1-18 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Be holy because I, the LORD your God, am holy"** (19:2) | God's people reflect who he is. |
| **"Leave them for the poor and the foreigner"** (19:10) | Make room for people in need. |
| **"You must not steal"** (19:11) | Respect what belongs to others. |
| **"A stumbling block before the blind"** (19:14) | Don't take advantage of the weak. |
| **"You are to judge your neighbor fairly"** (19:15) | Treat everyone justly. |
| **"Do not seek revenge or bear a grudge"** (19:18) | Don't get even, and don't hold on to anger. |
| **"Love your neighbor as yourself"** (19:18) | Care for others the way you care for yourself. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Gleaning** meant gathering leftover grain after harvest. Ruth gleaned in Boaz's field (Ruth 2:2-3).

**Day workers** were often very poor. Waiting even one night for pay could mean going hungry.†

**"I am the LORD"** appears again and again in this chapter. Every law is rooted in who God is.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Do Christians have to follow all of Leviticus?** Many of its laws about sacrifice and ritual were fulfilled in Jesus. But the heart of chapter 19, holiness and love for neighbor, is repeated in the New Testament (1 Peter 1:16, Romans 13:9).

**Who is my neighbor?** That's exactly what someone asked Jesus. He answered with the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:29-37).

**Can anyone be holy?** Not on our own. God makes us holy through Jesus, and then teaches us to live it.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"The Old Testament is about rules. The New Testament is about love."*

**What's true:** The Old Testament has many laws.

**What the passage is mainly about:** "Love your neighbor as yourself" comes from Leviticus. The Old Testament law was full of care for the poor, the disabled, and the foreigner. Jesus didn't invent love for neighbor. He lived it perfectly.

**Where Jesus is:** He quoted this command and fulfilled it, loving his neighbors all the way to the cross.

## ✝️ Good News

God doesn't just command holiness. He gives it.

**"Be holy because I, the LORD your God, am holy"** (19:2).

We fall short of loving our neighbors. But Jesus loved perfectly, and through him God makes us holy. Then he teaches us to love.

**God calls his people to be holy because he is holy: caring for the poor, dealing honestly, and loving our neighbors as ourselves. In Jesus, he makes us holy.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that God doesn't need our good works, but our neighbor does. Loving our neighbor is how we serve God in daily life.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Leviticus 19:1-2, 9-18 on the Seventh Sunday after the Epiphany and 19:1-2, 15-18 in October (Lectionary 30), alongside Jesus's teaching on the greatest commandments.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**"Be holy because I, the LORD your God, am holy." Holiness looks like leaving food for the poor, dealing honestly, and loving your neighbor as yourself.**
