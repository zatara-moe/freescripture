# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul wrote this letter to the church in Corinth around AD 55. It's one of his most personal letters.

**What was happening.** Some people in Corinth doubted Paul. He wasn't impressive. He suffered a lot. Other teachers looked more powerful (10:10).†

**What this chapter is.** Paul's answer: the power isn't his. It's God's. And it shines through weakness.

**How to read it.** Notice the "but not" lines in verses 8 and 9.

## 🗺️ Map of the Letter

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. We Do Not Lose Heart | 4:1-6 | God's light shines in our hearts |
| 2. Treasure in Jars of Clay | 4:7-12 | God's power shows through weakness |
| 3. We Believe, So We Speak | 4:13-15 | God will raise us too |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: We Do Not Lose Heart

**"Therefore, since God in His mercy has given us this ministry, we do not lose heart"** (4:1). {v: 4:1}

Paul tells the truth openly. He doesn't twist God's word. {v: 4:2}

Some people can't see the good news. The devil has blinded them. {v: 4:3, 4:4}

**"For we do not proclaim ourselves, but Jesus Christ as Lord"** (4:5). {v: 4:5}

At creation God said, **"Let light shine out of darkness."** Now he **"made His light shine in our hearts to give us the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ"** (4:6). {v: 4:6}

The God who made light at creation shines in our hearts. {v: 4:6}

### Section 2: Treasure in Jars of Clay

**"Now we have this treasure in jars of clay to show that this surpassingly great power is from God and not from us"** (4:7). {v: 4:7}

Clay jars were cheap and easy to break. The treasure is the good news of Jesus. We are the jars. {v: 4:7}

**"We are hard pressed on all sides, but not crushed; perplexed, but not in despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed"** (4:8-9). {v: 4:8, 4:9}

Perplexed means confused. Forsaken means abandoned. {v: 4:8, 4:9}

Paul shares in Jesus's suffering, so that Jesus's life can show in him. {v: 4:10, 4:11, 4:12}

### Section 3: We Believe, So We Speak

**"I believed, therefore I have spoken,"** Paul quotes from the Psalms (4:13). {v: 4:13}

He keeps speaking because he knows the God who raised Jesus will raise us too. {v: 4:14}

All of it is so that grace reaches more and more people, and more people thank God. {v: 4:15}

### Section 4: Renewed Day by Day

**"Therefore we do not lose heart. Though our outer self is wasting away, yet our inner self is being renewed day by day"** (4:16). {v: 4:16}

Our bodies get tired, sick, and old. But God makes us new inside, every day. {v: 4:16}

**"For our light and momentary affliction is producing for us an eternal weight of glory that is far beyond comparison"** (4:17). {v: 4:17}

**"So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen. For what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal"** (4:18). {v: 4:18}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

2 Corinthians 4 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"We do not lose heart"** (4:1) | God's mercy keeps us going. |
| **"Let light shine out of darkness"** (4:6) | The God of creation shines in our hearts. |
| **"Treasure in jars of clay"** (4:7) | God's power shows best in ordinary, weak people. |
| **"Hard pressed... but not crushed"** (4:8) | Troubles are real, but they don't win. |
| **"Our inner self is being renewed day by day"** (4:16) | God keeps making us new inside. |
| **"Light and momentary affliction"** (4:17) | Compared to eternity, our troubles are small. |
| **"What is unseen is eternal"** (4:18) | God's promises last forever. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Clay jars** held everything from water to coins. Rich people sometimes hid treasure in plain jars.†

**Paul suffered a lot.** He was beaten, shipwrecked, hungry, and imprisoned (11:23-27).

**Paul quotes Genesis 1:3** ("Let there be light") and **Psalm 116:10** ("I believed, therefore I have spoken").

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is Paul saying suffering is "light"?** Paul suffered terribly. He calls it light only compared to the glory to come. If you're suffering now, it's okay to say it's heavy.

**Does "not crushed" mean I'll never feel crushed?** Paul felt hard pressed and confused. The promise is that troubles won't have the final word.

**What is "the god of this age"?** The devil, who tries to keep people from seeing the light of Jesus.†

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If God is with you, you'll be strong and successful."*

**What's true:** God gives strength.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Paul was beaten down, confused, and wearing out, and that's exactly where God's power showed. God's treasure is in clay jars, so everyone can see the power is his.

**Where Jesus is:** His death and life show in his people. The God who raised him will raise us.

## ✝️ Good News

We are clay jars. Fragile and ordinary.

**"Now we have this treasure in jars of clay to show that this surpassingly great power is from God and not from us"** (4:7).

God's light shines in our hearts. We may be hard pressed, but not crushed. And God renews us every day.

**We have God's treasure in jars of clay, so the power is clearly his. Hard pressed but not crushed, we do not lose heart.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught a "theology of the cross": God shows his power in weakness, in suffering, and in the cross, where the world least expects it.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads 2 Corinthians 4:3-6 on Transfiguration Sunday, 2 Corinthians 4:5-12 on the Ninth Sunday after the Epiphany and in early summer, and 2 Corinthians 4:13 to 5:1 in June (Lectionary 10).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**We have God's treasure in jars of clay. Hard pressed but not crushed, we do not lose heart, because God renews us day by day.**
