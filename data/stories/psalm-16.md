# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** David (16:1). It's called a *Miktam*, a type of psalm whose exact meaning is uncertain — possibly a "golden" psalm.

**What it is.** A prayer of complete trust. David finds in God his only good thing, his inheritance, and his hope beyond death.

**Why it matters.** The apostle Peter quoted this psalm at Pentecost, saying it pointed to Jesus: the Holy One who would not see decay (Acts 2:25-31).

**When the church sings it.** At Easter Vigil, the Second Sunday of Easter, and at other moments of resurrection hope.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. My Only Good | 16:1-4 | Apart from You I have no good thing |
| 2. A Delightful Inheritance | 16:5-8 | The LORD is my portion |
| 3. Joy Beyond Death | 16:9-11 | You will not abandon my soul |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: My Only Good

**"Preserve me, O God, for in You I take refuge"** (16:1). {v: 16:1}

I said to the LORD, **"You are my Lord; apart from You I have no good thing."** (16:2). {v: 16:2}

Everything good comes from God. Apart from him, there is nothing worth having. {v: 16:2}

He delights in those who follow the LORD, and refuses to follow other gods. {v: 16:3, 16:4}

### Section 2: A Delightful Inheritance

**"The LORD is my chosen portion and my cup; You have made my lot secure"** (16:5). {v: 16:5}

Portion and lot refer to inheritance — what is given to you. The LORD himself is David's inheritance. {v: 16:5}

**"The lines of my boundary have fallen in pleasant places; surely my inheritance is delightful"** (16:6). {v: 16:6}

When land was divided in ancient Israel, boundary lines were measured out. David's portion is God himself, and that is more than enough. {v: 16:6}

**"I will bless the LORD who counsels me; even at night my conscience instructs me"** (16:7). {v: 16:7}

**"I have set the LORD always before me. Because He is at my right hand, I will not be shaken"** (16:8). {v: 16:8}

### Section 3: Joy Beyond Death

**"Therefore my heart is glad and my tongue rejoices; my body also will dwell securely"** (16:9). {v: 16:9}

**"For You will not abandon my soul to Sheol, nor will You let Your Holy One see decay"** (16:10). {v: 16:10}

Sheol was the place of the dead. The claim here is extraordinary: God will not leave him there. {v: 16:10}

**"You have made known to me the path of life; You will fill me with joy in Your presence, with eternal pleasures at Your right hand"** (16:11). {v: 16:11}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 16 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"You are my Lord; apart from You I have no good thing"** (16:2) | God is the source of every good in life. |
| **"The LORD is my chosen portion"** (16:5) | God himself is my inheritance. |
| **"I will not be shaken"** (16:8) | God at his right hand makes him steady. |
| **"You will not abandon my soul to Sheol"** (16:10) | Even death cannot separate him from God. |
| **"The path of life"** (16:11) | God shows the way that leads through death to joy. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Sheol** was the Hebrew word for the realm of the dead — a shadowy underworld. The hope expressed here goes beyond what earlier Israelites could fully imagine.

**Boundary lines** were measured with a cord when dividing land among families. David's picture: God is better than any plot of ground.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is "You will not abandon my soul to Sheol" about Jesus or David?** David prayed it about himself. Peter, at Pentecost, argued that since David did die and see decay, the psalm must point forward to someone who would not — Jesus, who rose from the dead (Acts 2:29-31).

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| David | Secure | His lot is in God's hands (16:5-6) |
| David | Joyful | Even his body will rest securely (16:9) |
| David | Hopeful | The path of life leads past death itself (16:11) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Death is the end."*

**What's true:** Death is real and takes everyone.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"You will not abandon my soul to Sheol"** (16:10). The God who is our refuge is also stronger than death. David hoped for it; Jesus proved it.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the Holy One who did not see decay. His resurrection is the fulfillment of this psalm's deepest hope, and the guarantee of ours.

## ✝️ Good News

There is a path of life.

**"You have made known to me the path of life; You will fill me with joy in Your presence"** (16:11).

Jesus walked it, died, and rose. In him, the path is open.

**Psalm 16 is a prayer of complete trust: God is my portion, my refuge, and my hope beyond death. Jesus is the Holy One who fulfilled this hope, and in him, we share it.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said the resurrection of Christ is the foundation of Christian joy. Because Jesus rose, we can face death without terror, trusting the one who holds the path of life.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 16 at the Easter Vigil, the Second Sunday of Easter in Year B, and on the Third Sunday of Easter in Year C.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 16 trusts God as the only true good: my portion, my security, my hope beyond death. Jesus rose from the dead to fulfill this psalm, and in him, the path of life is open.**
