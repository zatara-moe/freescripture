# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Isaiah:** God's prophet in Jerusalem.
- **King Ahaz:** The king of Judah. He is frightened.
- **Shear-jashub:** Isaiah's son. His name means "a remnant will return."
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- Jerusalem, at the end of the water channel that fed the city's upper pool.

**When**

- About 735 BC.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Two kings had joined forces against Judah.** Rezin, king of Aram, and Pekah, king of Israel, marched on Jerusalem. They wanted to put their own king on the throne (7:1, 7:6).

**Ahaz was terrified.** He was thinking of asking the cruel empire of Assyria for help instead of trusting God (2 Kings 16:7).†

**Ahaz was out checking the water supply.** A city under attack needed water to survive.†

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Trembling like trees | 7:1-2 | Two kings attack, and Ahaz is afraid |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Trembling Like Trees

The kings of Aram and Israel marched against Jerusalem. {v: 7:1}

When King Ahaz heard, **"the hearts of Ahaz and his people trembled like trees in the forest shaken by the wind"** (7:2). {v: 7:2}

### Scene 2: "Do Not Be Afraid"

God sent Isaiah and his son to meet Ahaz by the water channel. {v: 7:3}

Isaiah said, **"Calm down and be quiet. Do not be afraid"** (7:4). {v: 7:4}

Isaiah called the two enemy kings **"two smoldering stubs of firewood"** (7:4). Lots of smoke, but almost burnt out. {v: 7:4}

Their plan would fail. **"It will not arise; it will not happen"** (7:7). {v: 7:5, 7:6, 7:7}

Then a warning: **"If you do not stand firm in your faith, then you will not stand at all"** (7:9). {v: 7:9}

### Scene 3: "Ask for a Sign"

God spoke to Ahaz again. **"Ask for a sign from the LORD your God, whether from the depths of Sheol or the heights of heaven"** (7:11). {v: 7:10, 7:11}

Anything at all, from the deepest place to the highest. {v: 7:11}

But Ahaz said, **"I will not ask; I will not test the LORD"** (7:12). {v: 7:12}

It sounded holy. But Ahaz had already made up his mind. He would trust Assyria, not God.† {v: 7:12}

Isaiah said, **"Hear now, O house of David! Is it not enough to try the patience of men? Will you try the patience of my God as well?"** (7:13). {v: 7:13}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture the king standing by the water, arms folded, not looking at the prophet. Isaiah's young son stands quietly beside his father.

### Scene 4: Immanuel

**"Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: Behold, the virgin will be with child and will give birth to a son, and will call Him Immanuel"** (7:14). {v: 7:14}

Immanuel means "God with us." {v: 7:14}

Before that child was old enough to know right from wrong, the two kings Ahaz feared would be gone. {v: 7:15, 7:16}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 7:1-16 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Trembled like trees in the forest"** (7:2) | Ahaz and his people were terrified. |
| **"Calm down and be quiet. Do not be afraid"** (7:4) | God told Ahaz to trust him instead of panicking. |
| **"Two smoldering stubs of firewood"** (7:4) | The enemies looked scary, but they were almost burnt out. |
| **"If you do not stand firm in your faith"** (7:9) | Trusting God was the only safe place to stand. |
| **"I will not test the LORD"** (7:12) | Ahaz used holy-sounding words to refuse God. |
| **"Immanuel"** (7:14) | God with us. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**The two kings were defeated.** Assyria conquered Damascus and much of Israel within a few years (2 Kings 16:9).†

**Ahaz paid Assyria with temple treasure.** He trusted Assyria instead of God, and Judah became Assyria's servant (2 Kings 16:7-8).†

**The Hebrew word for "virgin" can mean "young woman."** The Greek translation used by the first Christians says "virgin." Matthew quotes it about Mary (Matthew 1:23).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was this sign about Jesus, or about Ahaz's time?** Many readers believe the sign had a meaning in Ahaz's day, with a child born then. Matthew says it was completely fulfilled in Jesus, born of the virgin Mary, truly "God with us."†

**Is it wrong to ask God for a sign?** God offered Ahaz a sign. Ahaz refused because he didn't want to trust God. Bringing honest questions to God isn't wrong.

**Why was God impatient with Ahaz?** God kept offering help. Ahaz kept refusing. God still gave the sign, a promise much bigger than Ahaz could imagine.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ahaz and his people | Terrified | Two armies were coming (7:2) |
| Ahaz | Stubborn | He refused to ask God for a sign (7:12) |
| Isaiah | Frustrated | Ahaz was trying God's patience (7:13) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What do you do when you're afraid?** Tap one.

- Panic :: Ahaz did too. God said, "Calm down. Do not be afraid."
- Find my own fix :: Ahaz trusted Assyria. God offers himself.
- Ask God :: God invited Ahaz to ask. You can ask too.
- I need God with me :: That's his name: Immanuel, God with us.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God is far away, up in heaven."*

**What's true:** God is great, high above everything.

**What the story is mainly about:** God's sign was a child named Immanuel, "God with us." God doesn't stay far away. He comes close.

**Where Jesus is:** Matthew says Jesus is Immanuel (Matthew 1:23). And Jesus's last words in Matthew are, **"I am with you always"** (Matthew 28:20).

## ✝️ Good News

Ahaz refused God's help. God gave the sign anyway.

**"Behold, the virgin will be with child and will give birth to a son, and will call Him Immanuel"** (7:14).

Jesus is Immanuel. God came to be with us, as a baby, and he stays with us always.

**When Ahaz was afraid and wouldn't trust God, God promised a sign: a child called Immanuel, God with us. Jesus is that child.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved the name Immanuel. He said if God is with us, nothing can be against us, not sin, death, or the devil.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 7:10-16 on the Fourth Sunday of Advent, next to the angel telling Joseph that Mary's child is Immanuel.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Frightened King Ahaz refused to trust God. God gave a sign anyway: a child named Immanuel, God with us.**
