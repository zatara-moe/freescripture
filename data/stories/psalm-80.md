# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Asaph (80:1), a temple musician. Psalms 73-83 are a collection linked to him.

**What it is.** A psalm of national lament — the people have been defeated, their land ravaged. They cry out to God to restore them.

**The refrain.** Three times the psalm repeats: *Restore us, O God, and cause Your face to shine upon us, that we may be saved.* Each time, the name of God grows: God, God of Hosts, LORD God of Hosts.

**When the church sings it.** In Advent, especially on the First and Second Sundays of Advent in Year B; also in Ordinary Time.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Restore Us | 80:1-3 | First cry: Shepherd of Israel, shine upon us |
| 2. How Long? | 80:4-7 | Tears and mockery — restore us, God of Hosts |
| 3. The Vine | 80:8-16 | God planted Israel; now the vine is cut down |
| 4. Revive Us | 80:17-19 | The son of man at God's right hand; restore us, LORD |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Restore Us

**"Hear us, O Shepherd of Israel, who leads Joseph like a flock; You who sit enthroned between the cherubim, shine forth"** (80:1). {v: 80:1}

God is addressed as a shepherd leading his flock — a picture of tender care. {v: 80:1}

**"Restore us, O God, and cause Your face to shine upon us, that we may be saved"** (80:3). {v: 80:2, 80:3}

The shining face of God is a sign of favor and blessing. The people need it now. {v: 80:3}

### Section 2: How Long?

**"O LORD God of Hosts, how long will Your anger smolder against the prayers of Your people?"** (80:4). {v: 80:4}

The prayers have not stopped. But neither has the trouble. How long? {v: 80:4, 80:5, 80:6}

**"Restore us, O God of Hosts, and cause Your face to shine upon us, that we may be saved"** (80:7). {v: 80:7}

The refrain returns, louder: now God of Hosts — LORD of armies and powers. {v: 80:7}

### Section 3: The Vine

**"You uprooted a vine from Egypt; You drove out the nations and transplanted it"** (80:8). {v: 80:8}

Israel is the vine. God brought it from Egypt, cleared the land, and planted it. {v: 80:8, 80:9, 80:10, 80:11}

But now: **"Why have You broken down its walls, so that all who pass by pick its fruit?"** (80:12). {v: 80:12, 80:13}

**"Return, O God of Hosts, we pray! Look down from heaven and see! Attend to this vine—"** (80:14). {v: 80:14, 80:15, 80:16}

### Section 4: Revive Us

**"Let Your hand be upon the man at Your right hand, on the son of man You have raised up for Yourself"** (80:17). {v: 80:17}

The son of man here was Israel's king. Christians hear Jesus, the Son of Man, in these words. {v: 80:17}

**"Then we will not turn away from You; revive us, and we will call on Your name"** (80:18). {v: 80:18}

**"Restore us, O LORD God of Hosts; cause Your face to shine upon us, that we may be saved"** (80:19). {v: 80:19}

The final refrain is the fullest: LORD God of Hosts. The plea reaches its highest note. {v: 80:19}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 80 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Shepherd of Israel"** (80:1) | God tends his people like a flock. |
| **"Cause Your face to shine upon us"** (80:3) | The shining face of God is favor, blessing, presence. |
| **"You uprooted a vine from Egypt"** (80:8) | The Exodus: God transplanted his people to a new home. |
| **"Why have You broken down its walls, so that all who pass by pick its fruit?"** (80:12) | The walls of the vineyard were its protection. Without God's care, enemies break in. |
| **"Revive us"** (80:18) | Bring us back to life. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"Between the cherubim"** (80:1) — the ark of the covenant had two golden cherubim above it, and God was said to be enthroned there in the tabernacle.

**"Ephraim, Benjamin, and Manasseh"** (80:2) — the northern tribes of Israel. This psalm likely comes from a time when the northern kingdom was under threat or had fallen to Assyria.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Who is the "son of man" in verse 17?** In the original context, the king of Israel. Christians have always read it as pointing to Jesus, the Son of Man, who sits at God's right hand.

**Does God get angry with his people?** In this psalm, the people believe God's anger is why they are suffering. They do not understand why, but they keep praying.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The people | Desperate | Tears for bread, tears for drink (80:5) |
| The people | Confused | They do not understand why God has withdrawn (80:12) |
| The people | Hopeful | They keep returning to the refrain: restore us (80:3, 80:7, 80:19) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**Where are you in this psalm?** Tap one.

- I feel like I'm going backward :: Restore us — it's a prayer you can pray.
- I've been asking God for a long time :: "How long?" is a prayer too (80:4).
- I wonder if God is still with me :: Cause Your face to shine upon us.
- Something I built has been broken down :: He planted the vine; He can restore it.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If God loved you, things wouldn't fall apart."*

**What's true:** Life is hard, and things do fall apart.

**What the passage is mainly about:** When things fall apart, the psalm says: keep praying. Keep coming back with the refrain. **"Restore us, O LORD God of Hosts; cause Your face to shine upon us, that we may be saved"** (80:19). God hears even the long, desperate prayer.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the Son of Man at God's right hand (80:17), raised up by God. He is the true Vine (John 15:1), who does not fail.

## ✝️ Good News

God hears the long prayer.

**"Restore us, O LORD God of Hosts; cause Your face to shine upon us, that we may be saved"** (80:19).

You can pray this even in the middle. Especially in the middle.

**Psalm 80 is the prayer of a people who feel abandoned. Three times they cry: Restore us, shine upon us, save us. Jesus is the Son of Man at God's right hand who makes that restoration possible.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said Advent is the season of waiting and longing — and Psalm 80 captures that longing exactly. The church in Advent prays: come, Lord Jesus; restore us; let your face shine on us.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 80 on the First Sunday of Advent in Year B, the Second Sunday of Advent in Year C, and the Fourth Sunday of Advent in Year C.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 80 is Advent's cry: Restore us, O God — cause Your face to shine upon us, that we may be saved. Three times the prayer returns. Jesus is the Son of Man raised at God's right hand who answers it.**
