# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Moses led Israel out of Egypt and through the wilderness for forty years. He was the only person in the Old Testament who spoke with God "face to face" (Exodus 33:11).

**What was happening.** Israel was traveling through the wilderness and grew tired of manna. They cried for meat, remembering the food of Egypt. Moses, overwhelmed by the weight of leading them, cried out to God: *I cannot carry these people alone.* God gave him a solution — not by making Moses stronger, but by spreading the load.

**What this passage is.** A story about the weight of leadership, the gift of the Spirit, and the greatest wish Moses ever spoke: that all God's people would be prophets.

**How to read it.** Watch two tensions: Israel craving Egypt, and Moses near the breaking point. Then watch how God responds to both.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Israel Craves Egypt

**"Meanwhile, the rabble among them had a strong craving for other food, and again the Israelites wept"** (11:4). {v: 11:4}

The craving starts with "the rabble" — a mixed group at the edge of the community — but spreads to the whole nation. {v: 11:4}

**"We remember the fish we ate freely in Egypt, along with the cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, and garlic. But now our appetite is gone; there is nothing to see but this manna!"** (11:5-6). {v: 11:5, 11:6}

They remember the food but not the slavery. Memory edits out what was hard. What they wanted was the menu of the old life, without the cost of the old life. {v: 11:5, 11:6}

### Section 2: Moses at the Breaking Point

Moses asked the LORD, **"Why have You brought this trouble on Your servant? Why have I not found favor in Your sight, that You have laid upon me the burden of all these people?"** (11:11). {v: 11:11}

Moses is not performing here. He is honest with God about his exhaustion. {v: 11:11}

**"I cannot carry all these people by myself; it is too burdensome for me. If this is how You are going to treat me, please kill me right now—if I have found favor in Your eyes—and let me not see my own wretchedness"** (11:14-15). {v: 11:14, 11:15}

Moses would rather die than continue alone. God does not rebuke him for this prayer. He answers it. {v: 11:14, 11:15}

### Section 3: God's Answer

The LORD said to Moses, **"Bring Me seventy of the elders of Israel known to you as leaders and officers of the people. Bring them to the Tent of Meeting and have them stand there with you"** (11:16). {v: 11:16}

God's solution to Moses's loneliness is community. Share the load. {v: 11:16}

He told Moses he would take some of the Spirit that was on Moses and put it on the seventy elders. {v: 11:16, 11:17}

### Section 4: The Spirit Falls Outside the Tent

**"Then the LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and He took some of the Spirit that was on Moses and placed that Spirit on the seventy elders. As the Spirit rested on them, they prophesied—but they never did so again"** (11:25). {v: 11:25}

The Spirit came, and they spoke God's words — a one-time burst marking their commissioning. {v: 11:25}

**"Two men, however, had remained in the camp—one named Eldad and the other Medad—and the Spirit rested on them... and they prophesied in the camp"** (11:26). {v: 11:26}

Two men who weren't at the tent received the Spirit anyway. Joshua, Moses's right hand, was troubled by this. {v: 11:26}

Joshua spoke up: **"Moses, my lord, stop them!"** (11:28). {v: 11:28}

Joshua wanted to protect Moses's unique authority. Moses's response turned that upside down. {v: 11:28}

Moses replied, **"Are you jealous on my account? I wish that all the LORD's people were prophets and that the LORD would place His Spirit on them!"** (11:29). {v: 11:29}

Moses was not threatened. He wanted *more* of this — not less. His greatest hope was that no one would need to carry the whole load alone because everyone would have God's Spirit. {v: 11:29}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Numbers 11:4-6, 10-16, 24-29 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"There is nothing to see but this manna!"** (11:6) | The miraculous provision became ordinary. Gratitude is harder than craving. |
| **"I cannot carry all these people by myself"** (11:14) | Even Moses had limits. Leadership was not designed to be solitary. |
| **"He took some of the Spirit that was on Moses"** (11:25) | The Spirit does not divide when shared — Moses was not diminished. |
| **"They prophesied in the camp"** (11:26) | The Spirit did not stay within approved boundaries. |
| **"I wish that all the LORD's people were prophets"** (11:29) | The greatest leaders hope to make themselves unnecessary. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Manna** was daily bread from heaven that appeared on the ground each morning (Exodus 16). The Israelites had been eating it for some time and had grown tired of it.

**Prophesying** in this context likely meant speaking under the Spirit's influence — an inspired outpouring of praise or proclamation, not necessarily predicting the future.

**Egypt's food** was real. The list — fish, cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, garlic — reflects actual Egyptian diet. The Israelites were not wrong that they had eaten well there. They were wrong in what they were willing to trade to get it back.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did they actually want to go back to slavery for cucumbers?** The craving was real and the memory was selective. This is a recognizable human experience: idealizing a past that was painful because it was familiar.

**Did the Spirit "leave" Moses when it was placed on the seventy?** Numbers says Moses was not diminished. The Spirit multiplied, not divided.

**Was Eldad and Medad's prophesying unauthorized?** It was unexpected. Moses's response suggests God's Spirit is not bound to organizational structures. Joshua saw a threat; Moses saw a promise.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The people | Ungrateful | They have daily bread from heaven and want something else (11:6) |
| Moses | Overwhelmed | The burden of leadership has become unbearable (11:14-15) |
| Joshua | Protective | He wants to guard Moses's unique role (11:28) |
| Moses | Hopeful | He wishes all of Israel had the Spirit (11:29) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Only certain people are meant to have God's Spirit — pastors, priests, special leaders."*

**What's true:** God does appoint specific people for specific roles.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"I wish that all the LORD's people were prophets and that the LORD would place His Spirit on them!"** (11:29). Moses wanted the Spirit beyond any single person or institution. His wish became the promise of the prophets.

**Where Jesus is:** On the Day of Pentecost, Peter quoted Joel's prophecy: "I will pour out My Spirit on all people. Your sons and daughters will prophesy" (Acts 2:17). Moses's wish came true. The Spirit was poured on everyone — men and women, young and old — in the name of Jesus.

## ✝️ Good News

God heard Moses's cry and answered. He hears yours too.

**"I cannot carry all these people by myself; it is too burdensome for me"** (11:14).

Moses prayed that kind of prayer. God did not rebuke him. He came near, shared the load, and sent the Spirit.

**Moses was overwhelmed. God shared the Spirit. And Moses's greatest wish — that all God's people would have it — came true at Pentecost.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther saw in Pentecost the fulfillment of Moses's prayer: the Spirit poured on all believers, making each one a priest and prophet before God. The "priesthood of all believers" was, for Luther, what Moses longed for — and what Christ gave.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, Numbers 11:4-6, 10-16, 24-29 is read on the Twenty-Sixth Sunday after Pentecost (Lectionary 26). It is also an alternative first reading for Pentecost Sunday in Year B, when the church celebrates the pouring out of the Spirit.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Moses cried out that he could not carry the people alone. God shared the Spirit with seventy elders — and two more outside the tent. Moses's greatest wish: that all God's people would be prophets.**
