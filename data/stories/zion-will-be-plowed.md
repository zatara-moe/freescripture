# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Micah was a prophet from the rural town of Moresheth, a contemporary of Isaiah, who preached in Jerusalem during the reigns of three kings — Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah (Micah 1:1).

**What was happening.** Jerusalem's leaders had turned justice into a commodity. Judges took bribes. Priests sold their teaching. Prophets promised peace to those who paid them. The city called itself God's own and assumed it was safe.

**What this passage is.** A confrontation. Micah names the corruption of Israel's leaders and announces its consequence: the holy city will be reduced to farmland.

**How to read it.** Notice the contrast between the leaders' claim ("Is not the LORD among us?") and what Micah says God actually sees.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. False Prophets | 3:5-7 | They sell their messages; God will silence them |
| 2. True Prophet | 3:8 | Micah stands apart, filled with the Spirit |
| 3. Corrupt Leaders | 3:9-11 | Judges, priests, and prophets all sell what should be free |
| 4. The Verdict | 3:12 | Zion itself will be destroyed |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: False Prophets

This is what the LORD says: **"As for the prophets who lead My people astray, who proclaim peace while they chew with their teeth, but declare war against one who puts nothing in their mouths"** (3:5). {v: 3:5}

The false prophets said what they were paid to say. Put food in their mouths and they called it peace. Give them nothing, and they declared trouble against you. The word of God was for sale. {v: 3:5}

**"Therefore night will come over you without visions, and darkness without divination. The sun will set on these prophets, and the daylight will turn black over them"** (3:6). {v: 3:6}

God will close the channel. No more visions. No more messages to sell. The darkness they have brought will fall on them. {v: 3:6}

**"Then the seers will be ashamed and the diviners will be disgraced. They will all cover their mouths because there is no answer from God"** (3:7). {v: 3:7}

The test of a prophet is simple: does God actually speak? When God goes silent, the false prophets are exposed. {v: 3:7}

### Section 2: True Prophet

**"As for me, however, I am filled with power by the Spirit of the LORD, with justice and courage, to declare to Jacob his transgression and to Israel his sin"** (3:8). {v: 3:8}

Micah names the contrast sharply. He speaks by the Spirit — not for payment, not for popularity. His message is not peace for purchase. It is the truth about what Israel has done. {v: 3:8}

### Section 3: Corrupt Leaders

**"Now hear this, O leaders of the house of Jacob and rulers of the house of Israel, who despise justice and pervert all that is right"** (3:9). {v: 3:9}

The whole leadership class is indicted. Not just the prophets — the rulers too. {v: 3:9}

**"Her leaders judge for a bribe, her priests teach for a price, and her prophets practice divination for money. Yet they lean upon the LORD"** (3:11), saying, "Is not the LORD among us? No disaster can come upon us." {v: 3:11}

The corruption is deep: every pillar of the community — law, religion, prophecy — has been compromised. And yet they assume God protects them. They use God's name as a shield for what they are doing. {v: 3:11}

### Section 4: The Verdict

**"Therefore, because of you, Zion will be plowed like a field, Jerusalem will become a heap of rubble, and the temple mount a wooded ridge"** (3:12). {v: 3:12}

The unthinkable verdict. The holy city — God's city — will be destroyed. Not because God abandoned it, but because its leaders did. A century later, King Hezekiah remembered this prophecy when another prophet said the same thing, and he turned back to God (Jeremiah 26:18-19).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Micah 3:5-12 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Proclaim peace while they chew with their teeth"** (3:5) | The prophets spoke comfort to those who fed them. |
| **"There is no answer from God"** (3:7) | The test of a true prophet: does God actually speak through them? |
| **"Filled with power by the Spirit of the LORD"** (3:8) | Micah's authority was God's Spirit, not money or status. |
| **"Is not the LORD among us? No disaster can come upon us"** (3:11) | False confidence: using God's name to feel secure while ignoring his demands. |
| **"Zion will be plowed like a field"** (3:12) | Sacred places do not protect those who corrupt justice. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Micah's prophecy was remembered.** Over a century later, when Jeremiah said something similar and was threatened with death, the elders cited this verse and said Hezekiah had heard Micah's warning and repented — and the city was spared in his time (Jeremiah 26:17-19).

**Jerusalem and the temple** were considered inviolable by many in Israel. To predict their destruction was either treason or the most courageous thing a prophet could say.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was Zion actually destroyed?** Yes. About a century and a half after Micah, Babylon razed Jerusalem and destroyed the temple in 587 BC. The prophecy came true.

**Does Micah mean all religious leaders are corrupt?** Micah is addressing specific leaders in a specific time who had sold justice for money. He is not condemning all priests or prophets — he draws a sharp contrast with his own ministry (3:8).

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The false prophets | Exposed | God will silence the prophets who sold his word (3:6-7) |
| The leaders | Condemned | They used God's name to shield their corruption (3:11) |
| Micah | Resolute | Filled with the Spirit, he spoke when it was dangerous to do so (3:8) |

# Why It Matters

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"The church is God's protected place — nothing can really go wrong there."*

**What's true:** God is faithful to his people.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"Because of you, Zion will be plowed like a field"** (3:12). God does not protect institutions that have sold justice. His loyalty is to the people and to righteousness — not to buildings or offices.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus stood in the temple and drove out those who had turned God's house into a marketplace (Matthew 21:12-13). He quoted Jeremiah, who quoted Micah. He saw what Micah saw, and paid for speaking with his life.

## ✝️ Good News

God does not stay silent in the face of injustice.

**"I am filled with power by the Spirit of the LORD, with justice and courage, to declare to Jacob his transgression and to Israel his sin"** (3:8).

The Spirit of God still sends truth-tellers. The word that exposes is also the word that can heal.

**Micah spoke when it was dangerous. God filled him with the Spirit to do it. Truth and justice are worth more than safety.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther cited the prophets often as examples of courage — men who spoke God's word against the powerful, not for money or comfort but because the Spirit compelled them. He saw in Micah a predecessor to the Reformers who named what the church had become.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, Micah 3:5-12 is read on the Thirty-First Sunday after Pentecost (Lectionary 31), in the season before Advent, alongside Jesus's warnings about religious leaders who perform for show.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**False prophets sold peace. Corrupt leaders used God's name as a shield. Micah, filled with the Spirit, said the truth: Zion will be plowed like a field.**
