# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's about.** In the second half of Isaiah, God speaks of "My Servant." Sometimes the servant seems to be the people of Israel. In these passages, the servant is one person, who suffers for others.†

**Who it was for.** God's people in exile, about 550 years before Jesus. Many scholars think a later prophet in Isaiah's tradition spoke these words. Others hold that Isaiah wrote them, looking ahead. Either way, the church reads them as God's word.†

**What this passage is.** Two of the "Servant Songs." First, the servant speaks about being beaten and not turning back (50:4-9). Then God and the people speak about the servant who suffers and dies for their sins, and is lifted up (52:13 to 53:12).

**How to read it.** These are some of the most important words in the Old Testament for Christians. Read them slowly. They describe Good Friday, written centuries before.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. I Did Not Turn Back | 50:4-9 | The servant listens to God and endures shame |
| 2. Lifted Up | 52:13-15 | God's servant will be exalted |
| 3. Despised and Rejected | 53:1-3 | No one noticed him |
| 4. Pierced for Our Transgressions | 53:4-6 | He suffered in our place |
| 5. Like a Lamb | 53:7-9 | He went silently to death |
| 6. He Will See the Light | 53:10-12 | After suffering, life and victory |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: I Did Not Turn Back

The servant speaks. God gave him words **"to sustain the weary with a word"** (50:4). Every morning, God wakes him up to listen. {v: 50:4}

He did not rebel or turn back. {v: 50:5}

**"I offered My back to those who struck Me, and My cheeks to those who tore out My beard. I did not hide My face from scorn and spittle"** (50:6). {v: 50:6}

Scorn means mocking and contempt. Spittle is spit. {v: 50:6}

**"Therefore I have set My face like flint"** (50:7). Flint is a very hard stone. He was determined not to give up. {v: 50:7}

He trusted that God would stand up for him. **"Surely the Lord GOD helps Me"** (50:9). {v: 50:8, 50:9}

### Section 2: Lifted Up

Now God speaks about his servant. **"Behold, My Servant will prosper; He will be raised and lifted up and highly exalted"** (52:13). {v: 52:13}

But first, people would be shocked at how badly he was hurt. His appearance would be disfigured beyond that of any man. {v: 52:14}

Kings would be speechless when they understood. {v: 52:15}

### Section 3: Despised and Rejected

Then the people speak, looking back. **"Who has believed our message?"** (53:1). {v: 53:1}

He grew up like a small plant in dry ground. There was nothing special about the way he looked. {v: 53:2}

**"He was despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows, acquainted with grief"** (53:3). {v: 53:3}

People turned away from him. **"We esteemed Him not"** (53:3). To esteem means to value. {v: 53:3}

### Section 4: Pierced for Our Transgressions

**"Surely He took on our infirmities and carried our sorrows"** (53:4). Infirmities are weaknesses and sicknesses. {v: 53:4}

We thought God was punishing him for something he did. {v: 53:4}

**"But He was pierced for our transgressions, He was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed"** (53:5). {v: 53:5}

Stripes are the marks left by a whip. {v: 53:5}

**"We all like sheep have gone astray, each one has turned to his own way; and the LORD has laid upon Him the iniquity of us all"** (53:6). {v: 53:6}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Peter wrote about Jesus: **"He Himself bore our sins in His body on the tree"** (1 Peter 2:24).

### Section 5: Like a Lamb

**"He was led like a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is silent, so He did not open His mouth"** (53:7). {v: 53:7}

He was arrested and condemned unjustly, and cut off from the land of the living. {v: 53:8}

He was buried with the wicked, and with a rich man, though he had done nothing wrong. {v: 53:9}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Before Pilate, Jesus **"gave no answer, not even to a single charge"** (Matthew 27:14). Jesus was buried in the tomb of a rich man, Joseph of Arimathea (Matthew 27:57-60).

### Section 6: He Will See the Light

It was God's will for the servant to suffer, as an offering for sin. But that is not the end. {v: 53:10}

**"He will see His offspring, He will prolong His days"** (53:10). Someone who died will live again. {v: 53:10}

**"After the anguish of His soul, He will see the light of life and be satisfied"** (53:11). {v: 53:11}

**"By His knowledge My righteous Servant will justify many, and He will bear their iniquities"** (53:11). To justify means to declare people right with God. {v: 53:11}

He was counted among sinners. **"Yet He bore the sin of many and made intercession for the transgressors"** (53:12). {v: 53:12}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> On the cross, Jesus prayed for the people who crucified him: **"Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing"** (Luke 23:34).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 50:4-9 and 52:13 to 53:12 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"I have set My face like flint"** (50:7) | The servant was determined to go through suffering. |
| **"Despised and rejected by men"** (53:3) | People turned away from him. |
| **"He was pierced for our transgressions"** (53:5) | He suffered for our sins, not his own. |
| **"By His stripes we are healed"** (53:5) | His wounds bring us healing and peace with God. |
| **"We all like sheep have gone astray"** (53:6) | Everyone has wandered from God. |
| **"Like a lamb to the slaughter"** (53:7) | He went to death quietly, without fighting back. |
| **"My righteous Servant will justify many"** (53:11) | Through him, many are made right with God. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**A guilt offering was a sacrifice for sin.** In God's law, an animal could be offered to make things right after someone did wrong (Leviticus 5:14-19).†

**An Ethiopian official was reading Isaiah 53.** He asked Philip who it was about. Philip told him the good news about Jesus (Acts 8:32-35).

**Jewish readers have often seen the servant as Israel,** suffering among the nations. Christians, starting with the apostles, see these words fulfilled in Jesus.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"It was the LORD's will to crush Him." Was God cruel?** This is hard to understand. The servant suffered willingly, out of love, to bring many people back to God. The Father and the Son were united in this plan of rescue (John 10:17-18).†

**If Jesus took our punishment, does that mean suffering is always deserved?** No. Isaiah says people thought the servant was being punished for his own sins, and they were wrong. Suffering is not a sign that someone deserves it.

**Is it okay to be quiet when someone hurts me, like the lamb?** Jesus chose to suffer to save us. That doesn't mean you should stay silent when someone hurts you. If someone is hurting you, please tell a trusted adult.

# Why It Matters

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Jesus's death was just a sad mistake."*

**What's true:** Jesus's death was unjust. People rejected and killed an innocent man.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Centuries before, God said his servant would suffer for others' sins, willingly, and then be lifted up. It was God's plan to save us. **"The LORD has laid upon Him the iniquity of us all"** (53:6).

**Where Jesus is:** Beaten, silent before Pilate, crucified between two criminals, buried in a rich man's tomb, and raised to life on the third day.

## ✝️ Good News

We all went astray. God laid our sins on his servant.

**"But He was pierced for our transgressions, He was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed"** (53:5).

Jesus took what we deserved. We receive what he deserved: peace with God and healing. And he lives.

**God's servant was despised, pierced, and crushed for our sins. By his wounds we are healed, and he lives.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther called Isaiah 53 a chapter every Christian should know by heart. He said the words "for us" and "our" are the most important. Christ suffered for *our* sins.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Isaiah 50:4-9a on the Sunday of the Passion (Palm Sunday) in all three years, and in Year B in September (Lectionary 24). It reads Isaiah 52:13 to 53:12 on Good Friday in all three years.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God's servant set his face like flint, was despised and pierced for our sins, and was lifted up. By his wounds we are healed.**
