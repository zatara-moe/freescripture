# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's about.** In the second half of Isaiah, God speaks of "My Servant." Here the servant is someone God chooses, fills with his Spirit, and sends to bring justice and light to the whole world.†

**Who it was for.** God's people in exile in Babylon. They felt forgotten, like God had given up on them.

**What this passage is.** Two of the "Servant Songs," which Christians have long read as describing Jesus. In the first, God introduces his servant (42:1-9). In the second, the servant speaks (49:1-7). Then God answers his people's fear that he has forgotten them (49:8-16).

**How to read it.** Notice how gentle the servant is, and how far his light reaches.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Here Is My Servant | 42:1-4 | A gentle servant, full of the Spirit |
| 2. A Light to the Nations | 42:5-9 | God sends him to open blind eyes |
| 3. Called From the Womb | 49:1-6 | The servant's mission reaches the ends of the earth |
| 4. Kings Will Bow | 49:7-12 | God will bring his people home |
| 5. I Will Not Forget You | 49:13-16 | God's people are written on his hands |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Here Is My Servant

**"Here is My Servant, whom I uphold, My Chosen One, in whom My soul delights. I will put My Spirit on Him, and He will bring justice to the nations"** (42:1). {v: 42:1}

He won't shout or force his way. {v: 42:2}

**"A bruised reed He will not break and a smoldering wick He will not extinguish"** (42:3). {v: 42:3}

A bruised reed is a bent plant stem that's almost broken. A smoldering wick is a candle flame that's almost out. He is gentle with people who are hurt and weak. {v: 42:3}

He won't give up until there is justice on the earth. {v: 42:4}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> When Jesus was baptized, a voice from heaven said, **"This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased!"** (Matthew 3:17). It echoes Isaiah 42:1.†

### Section 2: A Light to the Nations

The God who made the heavens and gives every person breath speaks to his servant. {v: 42:5}

**"I will keep you and appoint you to be a covenant for the people and a light to the nations"** (42:6). {v: 42:6}

He will **"open the eyes of the blind"** and bring prisoners out of the darkness (42:7). {v: 42:7}

**"Behold, the former things have happened, and now I declare new things"** (42:9). {v: 42:9}

### Section 3: Called From the Womb

Now the servant speaks. **"The LORD called Me from the womb; from the body of My mother He named Me"** (49:1). {v: 49:1}

God made him like a sharp sword and a polished arrow, ready for his mission. {v: 49:2}

But the servant feels like a failure. **"I have labored in vain"** (49:4). Still, he trusts God. {v: 49:4}

God answers: bringing Israel back isn't enough. **"I will also make You a light for the nations, to bring My salvation to the ends of the earth"** (49:6). {v: 49:6}

### Section 4: Kings Will Bow

The servant was **"despised and abhorred by the nation"** (49:7). But kings will rise and princes will bow because of him. {v: 49:7}

God will say to prisoners, "Come out." His people will not be hungry or thirsty. He will lead them beside springs of water. {v: 49:9, 49:10}

They will come home from far away. {v: 49:12}

### Section 5: I Will Not Forget You

The heavens and earth sing for joy, because God has comforted his people. {v: 49:13}

But Zion, God's people, said, **"The LORD has forsaken me; the Lord has forgotten me!"** (49:14). {v: 49:14}

God answers, **"Can a woman forget her nursing child, or lack compassion for the son of her womb? Even if she could forget, I will not forget you!"** (49:15). {v: 49:15}

**"Behold, I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands"** (49:16). {v: 49:16}

Inscribed means written or carved. God's people are written on his hands, where he can always see them. {v: 49:16}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 42:1-9 and 49:1-16 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"I will put My Spirit on Him"** (42:1) | God's servant is filled with God's Spirit. |
| **"A bruised reed He will not break"** (42:3) | He is gentle with people who are weak and hurting. |
| **"A light to the nations"** (42:6) | His mission is for the whole world. |
| **"I have labored in vain"** (49:4) | Even God's servant felt discouraged. |
| **"To the ends of the earth"** (49:6) | God's salvation is for everyone, everywhere. |
| **"I will not forget you!"** (49:15) | God's love is stronger than a mother's. |
| **"Inscribed you on the palms of My hands"** (49:16) | God never stops thinking of his people. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**The islands and distant peoples** meant faraway nations across the sea. God's light was for them too.†

**Aswan was in the far south of Egypt.** God would bring his people home from every direction.†

**Simeon quoted these words.** Holding baby Jesus in the temple, he called him **"a light for revelation to the Gentiles"** (Luke 2:32).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is the servant Israel or one person?** In 49:3, God calls the servant "Israel." But in 49:5-6, the servant brings Israel back to God. Christians believe Jesus is the true Israel, the one faithful servant.†

**Does God have hands?** God is spirit. This is a comparison to show how close his people are to his heart. Christians also think of Jesus's hands, marked by the nails.†

**Did God's servant fail?** He felt like it. But God promised his work would reach the ends of the earth.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The servant | Discouraged | He felt he had labored in vain (49:4) |
| God's people | Forgotten | They thought God had forgotten them (49:14) |
| Heaven and earth | Joyful | God comforted his people (49:13) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God doesn't notice ordinary people."*

**What's true:** God is great and rules over all nations.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God's chosen servant is gentle with bruised and weak people. God's love is more faithful than a mother's. He writes his people on his hands.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus is God's chosen servant, gentle with the weak, a light for every nation. His hands bear the marks of the nails, for us.

## ✝️ Good News

God's people felt forgotten. God said, "Never."

**"Even if she could forget, I will not forget you! Behold, I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands"** (49:15-16).

Jesus is the servant who brings God's light to the ends of the earth. He doesn't break bruised reeds. He heals them.

**God's gentle servant brings light to every nation. God will never forget his people. They are written on his hands.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved the picture of the bruised reed. He said Christ is gentle with weak and troubled consciences. He doesn't crush them, he comforts them.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 42:1-9 on the Baptism of Our Lord, Isaiah 49:1-7 on the Second Sunday after the Epiphany, and Isaiah 49:8-16a on the Eighth Sunday after the Epiphany.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God's chosen servant is gentle with the weak and a light to every nation. God says, "I will not forget you. I have written you on the palms of My hands."**
