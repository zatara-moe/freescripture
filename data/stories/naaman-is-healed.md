# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Naaman:** A powerful army commander from Aram, a country north of Israel. He has a skin disease called leprosy.
- **A young girl:** An Israelite girl taken captive by Aram's soldiers. She serves Naaman's wife.
- **The king of Aram and the king of Israel:** Rulers of two countries that are often enemies.
- **Elisha:** The LORD's prophet in Israel, who took Elijah's place.
- **Naaman's servants:** His helpers on the journey.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- Aram (today's Syria), the palace of Israel's king, Elisha's house, and the Jordan River.

**When**

- About 2,850 years ago.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Elijah had been taken up to heaven.** His spirit rested on Elisha, who did many miracles (2:11, 2:15).

**Aram and Israel were enemies.** Aram's soldiers sometimes raided Israelite towns and took captives.†

**Leprosy was dreaded.** In the Bible, leprosy means several kinds of serious skin disease. It could spread, and there was no cure.†

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: A Great Man With a Problem

Naaman was the commander of the army of Aram. He was a great man, honored by his king, and a mighty warrior. {v: 5:1}

**"But he was a leper"** (5:1). {v: 5:1}

Soldiers from Aram had captured a young girl from Israel. Now she was a servant for Naaman's wife. {v: 5:2}

She said to her mistress, **"If only my master would go to the prophet who is in Samaria, he would cure him of his leprosy"** (5:3). {v: 5:3}

### Scene 2: A Letter to the King

The king of Aram sent Naaman to Israel with a letter and a huge gift of silver, gold, and fine clothes. {v: 5:5}

The letter said, "Cure my servant Naaman of his leprosy." {v: 5:6}

When Israel's king read it, he tore his clothes in panic. **"Am I God, killing and giving life, that this man expects me to cure a leper?"** (5:7). He thought Aram was trying to start a fight. {v: 5:7}

The prophet Elisha heard about it. He sent the king a message: let Naaman come to me, **"and he will know that there is a prophet in Israel"** (5:8). {v: 5:8}

### Scene 3: "Wash Seven Times"

So Naaman came with his horses and chariots and stopped at Elisha's door. {v: 5:9}

Elisha didn't even come out. He sent a messenger. {v: 5:10}

**"Go and wash yourself seven times in the Jordan, and your flesh will be restored, and you will be clean"** (5:10). {v: 5:10}

Naaman was furious. He expected the prophet to come out, call on God, and wave his hand over the spot. {v: 5:11}

And the Jordan? The rivers back home were better than that muddy river! {v: 5:12}

**"So he turned and went away in a rage"** (5:12). {v: 5:12}

### Scene 4: Like a Little Child

Naaman's servants came to him. {v: 5:13}

**"My father, if the prophet had told you to do some great thing, would you not have done it? How much more, then, when he tells you, 'Wash and be cleansed'?"** (5:13). {v: 5:13}

So Naaman went down to the Jordan. He dipped himself in the water seven times, just as the man of God said. {v: 5:14}

**"And his flesh was restored and became like that of a little child, and he was clean"** (5:14). {v: 5:14}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture a proud general standing waist-deep in a river, going under for the seventh time. He comes up, looks at his arms, and can't stop staring.

### Scene 5: "No God Except in Israel"

Naaman went back to Elisha. {v: 5:15}

**"Now I know for sure that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel"** (5:15). {v: 5:15}

He offered Elisha a gift. But Elisha refused. God's healing was free. {v: 5:15, 5:16}

Naaman promised he would never again worship any god but the LORD. {v: 5:17}

**"Go in peace,"** said Elisha (5:19). {v: 5:19}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

2 Kings 5:1-19 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"But he was a leper"** (5:1) | Even a great man had a problem he couldn't fix. |
| **"If only my master would go to the prophet"** (5:3) | A captive girl cared about her master and knew where help was. |
| **"Am I God, killing and giving life"** (5:7) | Only God can heal. |
| **"He turned and went away in a rage"** (5:12) | Naaman's pride almost stopped him from being healed. |
| **"Wash and be cleansed"** (5:13) | God's cure was simple. Naaman just had to trust it. |
| **"Like that of a little child"** (5:14) | God gave Naaman a brand-new start. |
| **"There is no God in all the earth except in Israel"** (5:15) | A foreign general came to know the true God. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Naaman's gift was enormous.** Ten talents of silver and six thousand shekels of gold was a fortune, enough to buy a city.†

**Naaman took home Israelite soil.** He wanted to worship the LORD on it back in Aram (5:17). People then often thought gods belonged to the land.†

**Jesus mentioned Naaman.** He said there were many lepers in Israel, but only Naaman the Syrian was healed (Luke 4:27).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was the Jordan River magic?** No. It was God's promise, attached to the washing, that healed Naaman. He had to trust God's word.

**Why didn't Elisha come out?** Maybe to show that the healing came from God, not from the prophet. Naaman needed to trust God's word, not a show.†

**What about the girl?** She had been taken from her home. Still, she wanted her master to be healed. Her kindness started everything.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The girl | Hopeful | She knew God's prophet could help (5:3) |
| Israel's king | Panicked | He couldn't heal anyone (5:7) |
| Naaman | Furious | Elisha didn't do it his way (5:11-12) |
| Naaman | Amazed | His skin was like a child's (5:14-15) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What does this story say to you?** Tap one.

- Pride :: Naaman almost walked away. Sometimes we need to let God do it his way.
- Feeling small :: A servant girl and some servants made all the difference.
- A fresh start :: God made Naaman's skin like a child's. He makes people new.
- I'm an outsider :: Naaman was a foreigner and an enemy. God healed him.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Something that simple can't really make a difference."*

**What's true:** Some things in life take hard work.

**What the story is mainly about:** Naaman expected something impressive. God told him to wash in a river. The healing came through a simple washing, joined to God's word. Naaman just had to trust.

**Where Jesus is:** In baptism, God uses simple water joined to his word. Like Naaman, we come out washed clean and made new. Jesus also healed lepers with a touch and a word (Mark 1:41).

## ✝️ Good News

Naaman couldn't heal himself. God healed him for free, through simple water and a promise.

**"His flesh was restored and became like that of a little child, and he was clean"** (5:14).

God still washes people clean. In baptism, water and God's word give forgiveness and a new start, as a free gift.

**God healed proud Naaman through a simple washing in the Jordan. God's gifts are free, and he makes people new.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Small Catechism, Luther wrote that baptism is not just plain water. It is water joined with God's word. He often pointed to Naaman: the Jordan's water healed because God's word was with it.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads 2 Kings 5:1-14 on the Sixth Sunday after the Epiphany, next to Jesus healing a man with leprosy. In Year C, it reads part of 2 Kings 5 in October (Lectionary 28), next to Jesus healing ten lepers.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**A captive girl pointed proud Naaman to God's prophet. He washed in the Jordan, as God said, and was made clean. God's gifts are free.**
