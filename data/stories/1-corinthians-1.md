# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul wrote this letter around AD 54 to the church he started in Corinth (Acts 18:1-11).

**What was happening.** Corinth was a rich, busy Greek city where people loved clever speakers and smart ideas. The church was splitting into groups that followed different leaders. People were proud of their wisdom and gifts.†

**What this chapter is.** Paul starts with thanks. Then he tackles their divisions. Then he says something shocking: God's wisdom looks like foolishness, because it's a cross.

**How to read it.** Notice how many times Paul mentions boasting.

## 🗺️ Map of the Letter

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Grace and Thanks | 1:1-9 | God has given them every gift, and God is faithful |
| 2. Is Christ Divided? | 1:10-17 | Stop splitting into groups |
| 3. The Message of the Cross | 1:18-25 | God's foolishness is wiser than human wisdom |
| 4. God Chose the Weak | 1:26-31 | No one can boast except in the Lord |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Grace and Thanks

Paul greets **"the church of God in Corinth"** (1:2), and all believers everywhere. {v: 1:1, 1:2, 1:3}

**"I always thank my God for you because of the grace He has given you in Christ Jesus"** (1:4). {v: 1:4}

They lack no spiritual gift as they wait for Jesus to return. {v: 1:5, 1:6, 1:7}

God will keep them strong to the end. **"God, who has called you into fellowship with His Son Jesus Christ our Lord, is faithful"** (1:9). {v: 1:8, 1:9}

### Section 2: Is Christ Divided?

**"I appeal to you, brothers, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you agree together, so that there may be no divisions among you"** (1:10). {v: 1:10}

Paul heard they were quarreling. Some said, "I follow Paul." Others said, "I follow Apollos," or "Peter," or even, "I follow Christ," as if Jesus were just another team. {v: 1:11, 1:12}

**"Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Were you baptized into the name of Paul?"** (1:13). {v: 1:13}

No. Only Christ died for them. {v: 1:13, 1:17}

### Section 3: The Message of the Cross

**"For the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God"** (1:18). {v: 1:18}

A crucified Savior sounded ridiculous. Crucifixion was the worst kind of death, for criminals and slaves.† {v: 1:18}

Some people wanted miracles. Others wanted clever ideas. {v: 1:22}

**"But we preach Christ crucified, a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles"** (1:23). {v: 1:23}

But to those God calls, Christ is **"the power of God and the wisdom of God"** (1:24). {v: 1:24}

**"For the foolishness of God is wiser than man's wisdom, and the weakness of God is stronger than man's strength"** (1:25). {v: 1:25}

### Section 4: God Chose the Weak

Paul says, look at yourselves. Not many of you were wise, powerful, or important. {v: 1:26}

**"But God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise; God chose the weak things of the world to shame the strong"** (1:27). {v: 1:27}

He chose the lowly and the people others look down on, **"so that no one may boast in His presence"** (1:29). {v: 1:28, 1:29}

Christ himself is our wisdom, righteousness, holiness, and redemption. {v: 1:30}

**"Let him who boasts boast in the Lord"** (1:31). {v: 1:31}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Corinthians 1 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"God... is faithful"** (1:9) | God will keep his people to the end. |
| **"That there may be no divisions among you"** (1:10) | Christians belong together. |
| **"Is Christ divided?"** (1:13) | Jesus isn't one team among many. |
| **"The message of the cross is foolishness"** (1:18) | A crucified Savior seems silly to the world. |
| **"The power of God"** (1:18) | The cross is how God saves. |
| **"God chose the weak things of the world"** (1:27) | God doesn't need impressive people. |
| **"Boast in the Lord"** (1:31) | Our only pride is in what God has done. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Apollos** was a gifted speaker who taught in Corinth after Paul (Acts 18:24-28).

**Cephas** is Peter's name in Aramaic.

**Greek culture prized wisdom and speaking skill.** Traveling speakers drew big crowds and charged money.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God want us to be foolish?** No. Paul means God's way of saving, through the cross, looks foolish to the world. Learning and thinking are good gifts.

**Is it wrong to like a certain pastor?** No. It's wrong to divide the church over leaders. They all serve the same Lord.

**Did Paul not care about baptism?** He did (see Romans 6:3-4). He was glad no one could claim they were baptized into *his* name.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Christianity is for smart, successful people."*

**What's true:** God gives people minds and gifts to use.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God chose the weak and foolish things of the world on purpose. Salvation comes through the cross, which looks like weakness. That way no one can boast except in God.

**Where Jesus is:** Crucified. The cross is God's power and wisdom.

## ✝️ Good News

The world wanted signs and smarts. God gave a cross.

**"For the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God"** (1:18).

God doesn't need you to be impressive. Christ is your wisdom, righteousness, holiness, and redemption.

**The message of the cross looks foolish, but it is the power of God. God chose the weak and lowly, so we boast only in the Lord.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther built his "theology of the cross" on this chapter. He said the true theologian sees God not in glory and power, but in suffering and the cross.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads 1 Corinthians 1 on the Second, Third, and Fourth Sundays after the Epiphany. In Year B, it reads 1 Corinthians 1:3-9 on the First Sunday of Advent and 1 Corinthians 1:18-25 on the Third Sunday in Lent.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The message of the cross looks like foolishness, but it is the power of God. God chose the weak and lowly, so no one can boast except in the Lord.**
