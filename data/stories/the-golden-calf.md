# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Moses:** The leader God chose to bring Israel out of Egypt. He is up on Mount Sinai with God.
- **Aaron:** Moses's older brother. He was left in charge of the people.
- **The Israelites:** God's people, camped at the foot of the mountain.
- **Joshua:** Moses's young helper.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- The camp at the foot of Mount Sinai, and the mountain itself.

**When**

- A few months after the Israelites left Egypt, more than 3,000 years ago.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**God gave his people the Ten Commandments.** The first two were: no other gods, and no idols (20:3-4).

**The people promised to obey.** **"We will do everything that the LORD has spoken"** (19:8).

**Moses went up the mountain to meet with God.** He was gone for forty days and forty nights (24:18).

**Here's what you know that the people don't:** Up on the mountain, God was giving Moses two stone tablets, written by God himself (31:18).

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: "Make Us Gods"

Moses was gone a long time. The people got tired of waiting. {v: 32:1}

They gathered around Aaron. **"Come, make us gods who will go before us"** (32:1). {v: 32:1}

Aaron told them to bring him their gold earrings. He melted the gold and made a statue of a calf. {v: 32:2, 32:3, 32:4}

The people said, "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you out of Egypt!" {v: 32:4}

Aaron built an altar in front of it and announced a feast. {v: 32:5}

The next day they offered sacrifices, ate and drank, and partied wildly. {v: 32:6}

### Scene 2: On the Mountain

Up on the mountain, the LORD told Moses, "Go down. Your people have turned away from me." {v: 32:7, 32:8}

God said he would destroy them and make a new nation from Moses instead. {v: 32:9, 32:10}

But Moses pleaded with the LORD. {v: 32:11}

He reminded God that he had brought these people out of Egypt with great power. What would the Egyptians say if God destroyed them now? {v: 32:11, 32:12}

He reminded God of his promise to Abraham, Isaac, and Israel: their families would be as many as the stars. {v: 32:13}

**"So the LORD relented from the calamity He had threatened to bring on His people"** (32:14). To relent means to hold back. A calamity is a disaster. {v: 32:14}

### Scene 3: Broken Tablets

Moses went down the mountain carrying the two stone tablets. God himself had written on them. {v: 32:15, 32:16}

Joshua heard noise from the camp. He thought it sounded like war. {v: 32:17}

Moses said, **"I hear the sound of singing!"** (32:18). {v: 32:18}

When Moses came near and saw the calf and the dancing, he burned with anger. {v: 32:19}

He threw the tablets down, and they shattered at the bottom of the mountain. {v: 32:19}

He burned the calf, ground it to powder, scattered it on the water, and made the people drink it. {v: 32:20}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture the music suddenly stopping. Pieces of stone lie scattered across the rocks. The whole camp is silent, staring at Moses.

### Scene 4: "What Did They Do to You?"

**"What did this people do to you,"** Moses asked Aaron, **"that you have led them into so great a sin?"** (32:21). {v: 32:21}

Aaron made excuses. He said the people wanted it. He said he just threw the gold into the fire, and **"out came this calf!"** (32:24). {v: 32:22, 32:23, 32:24}

The people were out of control. Moses called out, **"Whoever is for the LORD, come to me"** (32:26). The Levites, one of Israel's tribes, came to him. {v: 32:25, 32:26}

That day, the Levites carried out God's judgment in the camp, and about three thousand people died. {v: 32:27, 32:28}

### Scene 5: "Blot Me Out"

The next day, Moses told the people, **"You have committed a great sin"** (32:30). He went back up to the LORD. {v: 32:30}

Moses prayed, **"Yet now, if You would only forgive their sin.... But if not, please blot me out of the book that You have written"** (32:32). {v: 32:31, 32:32}

Moses was offering to be lost himself, if only God would forgive the people. {v: 32:32}

God said he would deal with each person's sin himself. But he told Moses to keep leading the people, and promised that his angel would go ahead of them. {v: 32:33, 32:34}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Exodus 32 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Make us gods who will go before us"** (32:1) | The people wanted a god they could see and control. |
| **"Out came this calf!"** (32:24) | Aaron wouldn't take responsibility. |
| **"Stiff-necked"** (32:9) | Stubborn, like an animal that won't turn its neck. |
| **"The LORD relented"** (32:14) | God listened to Moses's prayer and held back. |
| **"Threw the tablets out of his hands"** (32:19) | The people had already broken God's covenant. |
| **"Please blot me out"** (32:32) | Moses was willing to give himself up for his people. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Bulls and calves were common idols.** In Egypt and Canaan, people worshiped gods pictured as bulls, symbols of strength.†

**God made new tablets.** Later, God told Moses to cut two new stone tablets, and he wrote the commandments again (34:1).

**God's name was announced to Moses afterward.** God described himself as **"compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in loving devotion and faithfulness"** (34:6).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did God really change his mind?** The Bible says God relented. God's plan always included listening to his people's prayers. Moses's prayer was part of how God showed mercy.†

**Why did so many people die?** This part of the story is hard. It shows how serious it was to break the covenant right after making it. The Bible doesn't explain every detail.†

**Do people still make golden calves?** Not usually. But Luther said whatever you trust most is your god. Money, popularity, and success can become idols too.

## 💭 How People Feel

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What's hard about waiting on God?** Tap one.

- It takes too long :: The people got tired of waiting. God was working the whole time.
- I want something I can see :: That's normal. God gave us Jesus, God we can see.
- I make excuses :: Like Aaron. You can just be honest with God. He forgives.
- Praying for others :: Moses prayed for his people. You can pray for yours.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"I'm not religious, so I don't have any gods."*

**What's true:** Most people today don't bow down to statues.

**What the story is mainly about:** People made a god they could see and control. Luther said whatever we trust most becomes our god. Everyone trusts something. The true God is the one who brought his people out of slavery and keeps his promises.

**Where Jesus is:** Moses offered to be blotted out so the people could be forgiven. God didn't accept that from Moses. But Jesus did that very thing. He was cut off on the cross so we could be forgiven and written into God's book of life.†

## ✝️ Good News

The people broke God's commandments almost right away. Moses prayed for them, and God showed mercy.

**"Yet now, if You would only forgive their sin"** (32:32).

Moses couldn't take away the people's sin. Jesus can. He gave himself for us, so that our sin is forgiven and our names are written in his book.

**The people turned to a golden calf, but God showed mercy. Jesus gave himself so that our sins are forgiven.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Large Catechism, Luther explained the First Commandment: "That to which your heart clings and entrusts itself is really your God." Only the true God deserves that trust.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Exodus 32:7-14 in September of Year C (Lectionary 24), next to the parables of the lost sheep and lost coin. In Year A, Exodus 32:1-14 is read in October (Lectionary 28).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**While Moses was on the mountain, the people made a golden calf. Moses prayed for them, and God showed mercy. Jesus is the one who gave himself to take away our sin.**
