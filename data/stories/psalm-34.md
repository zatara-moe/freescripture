# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** David — in a dangerous moment. The heading says he pretended to be insane before a Philistine king to escape (1 Samuel 21:10-15). God got him out, and he wrote this psalm.

**What it is.** Like Psalms 25 and 145, each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet. An A-to-Z of praise.

**What it's about.** Seeking the LORD, tasting his goodness, and learning to live with the fear of God — a life that is not afraid.

**When the church sings it.** On several Sundays in Ordinary Time, especially in Year B when John 6 is being read.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: I Will Bless the LORD

**"I will bless the LORD at all times; His praise will always be on my lips"** (34:1). {v: 34:1}

Even when afraid. Even when surrounded. The praise does not stop. {v: 34:1}

**"My soul boasts in the LORD; let the oppressed hear and rejoice"** (34:2). {v: 34:2}

**"Magnify the LORD with me; let us exalt His name together"** (34:3). {v: 34:3}

**"I sought the LORD, and He answered me; He delivered me from all my fears"** (34:4). {v: 34:4}

**"Those who look to Him are radiant with joy; their faces shall never be ashamed"** (34:5). {v: 34:5}

**"This poor man called out, and the LORD heard him; He saved him from all his troubles"** (34:6). {v: 34:6}

**"The angel of the LORD encamps around those who fear Him, and he delivers them"** (34:7). {v: 34:7}

### Section 2: Taste and See

**"Taste and see that the LORD is good; blessed is the man who takes refuge in Him!"** (34:8). {v: 34:8}

This is an invitation to experience God, not just learn about him. {v: 34:8}

**"Fear the LORD, you His saints, for those who fear Him lack nothing"** (34:9). {v: 34:9}

**"Young lions go lacking and hungry, but those who seek the LORD lack no good thing"** (34:10). {v: 34:10}

Even the strong go without. Those who seek God do not. {v: 34:10}

### Section 3: The Fear of the LORD

**"Come, children, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the LORD"** (34:11). {v: 34:11}

The fear of the LORD is not terror. It is reverence — a life shaped by knowing God. {v: 34:11}

He who loves life keeps his tongue from evil and his lips from deceit. {v: 34:12, 34:13}

**"Turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it"** (34:14). {v: 34:14}

**"The eyes of the LORD are on the righteous, and His ears are inclined to their cry"** (34:15). {v: 34:15}

**"The righteous cry out, and the LORD hears; He delivers them from all their troubles"** (34:17). {v: 34:16, 34:17}

**"The LORD is near to the brokenhearted; He saves the contrite in spirit"** (34:18). {v: 34:18}

**"Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the LORD delivers him from them all"** (34:19). {v: 34:19, 34:20}

**"The LORD redeems His servants, and none who take refuge in Him will be condemned"** (34:22). {v: 34:21, 34:22}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 34 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"I will bless the LORD at all times"** (34:1) | Praise is not just for good times. |
| **"Taste and see that the LORD is good"** (34:8) | You cannot just think about God — you experience him. |
| **"Those who fear Him lack nothing"** (34:9) | Reverence for God leads to a full life, not a restricted one. |
| **"The LORD is near to the brokenhearted"** (34:18) | God does not stay away when we are crushed. |
| **"Many are the afflictions of the righteous"** (34:19) | Following God does not remove suffering. |
| **"None who take refuge in Him will be condemned"** (34:22) | Safety is in him, not in our own performance. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"A-to-Z psalm"** — the Hebrew alphabet has 22 letters, and each verse begins with the next letter. It was a way of saying: from beginning to end, everything is covered.

**"Angel of the LORD encamps"** (34:7) — protective language, like an army surrounding a camp to defend it.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"Not one of them will be broken"** (34:20, referring to the righteous person's bones) — John says this was fulfilled when the soldiers did not break Jesus' bones at the crucifixion (John 19:36).

**Do the righteous always get delivered?** Many afflictions come, and God delivers from them all — but not always right away, not always in the way expected. The final deliverance is resurrection.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God helps those who help themselves."*

**What's true:** God calls people to act and live wisely.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"This poor man called out, and the LORD heard him"** (34:6). He was not strong. He had nothing. He called. God answered. The help comes from God, not from our own strength.

**Where Jesus is:** John's Gospel connects verse 20 — "not one of his bones will be broken" — to Jesus on the cross. He is the righteous one who suffered, from whom God delivered, and who redeems those who take refuge in him.

## ✝️ Good News

The LORD is near to the brokenhearted.

**"He saves the contrite in spirit"** (34:18).

You do not have to be strong or put-together to find God. The brokenhearted and the poor man who cries out are exactly who he hears.

**Psalm 34 is an A-to-Z of praise: taste and see that the LORD is good. He hears the brokenhearted, delivers the afflicted, and redeems all who take refuge in him.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved Psalm 34 and cited it often. He saw in it the gospel pattern: the poor sinner who has nothing calls out, and God saves. The righteous are those who trust God, not those who earn his favor.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 34 on several Sundays in Ordinary Time in Year B, including Sundays when John 6 (the bread of life) is read, and at other times in the year.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Taste and see that the LORD is good. He delivers the poor man who calls out, is near to the brokenhearted, and redeems all who take refuge in him. From A to Z, God is faithful.**
