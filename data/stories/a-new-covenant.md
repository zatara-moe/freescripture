# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Jeremiah was a prophet in Jerusalem, about 627 to 586 BC. He is sometimes called "the weeping prophet," because his message was so sad and his life so hard.†

**What was happening.** For years, Jeremiah warned that Babylon would destroy Jerusalem because the people had turned from God. No one listened. Then it happened.

**What this passage is.** Chapters 30 to 33 are sometimes called the "Book of Comfort." In the middle of disaster, God promises to bring his people home and give them a new covenant. A covenant is a binding promise between God and his people.

**How to read it.** Two parts: God's everlasting love and homecoming (31:1-14), and the promise of a new covenant written on hearts (31:31-34).

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. An Everlasting Love | 31:1-6 | God's love never ends |
| 2. Gathered Home | 31:7-9 | God brings his scattered people home |
| 3. Mourning Into Joy | 31:10-14 | God turns sorrow into dancing |
| 4. Not Like the Old | 31:31-32 | God promises a new covenant |
| 5. Written on Hearts | 31:33-34 | God forgives and remembers sin no more |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: An Everlasting Love

**"I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have drawn you with loving devotion"** (31:3). {v: 31:3}

Everlasting means it never ends. Loving devotion means faithful love that doesn't give up. {v: 31:3}

God promises to rebuild his people. They will dance again with tambourines and plant vineyards again on the hills. {v: 31:4, 31:5}

### Section 2: Gathered Home

**"Sing with joy for Jacob"** (31:7). {v: 31:7}

God will bring his people home from the farthest parts of the earth. {v: 31:8}

He will bring everyone, **"including the blind and the lame, expectant mothers and women in labor"** (31:8). No one left behind. {v: 31:8}

**"They will come with weeping"** (31:9). God will lead them by streams, on a smooth path. {v: 31:9}

**"For I am Israel's Father"** (31:9). {v: 31:9}

### Section 3: Mourning Into Joy

**"The One who scattered Israel will gather them and keep them as a shepherd keeps his flock"** (31:10). {v: 31:10}

They will shout for joy in Jerusalem. Their lives will be **"like a well-watered garden"** (31:12). {v: 31:12}

**"I will turn their mourning into joy, and give them comfort and joy for their sorrow"** (31:13). {v: 31:13}

### Section 4: Not Like the Old

Then comes one of the greatest promises in the Old Testament. {v: 31:31}

**"Behold, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah"** (31:31). {v: 31:31}

It won't be like the covenant God made when he led them out of Egypt, written on stone. They broke that covenant, even though God was faithful to them like a husband. {v: 31:32}

### Section 5: Written on Hearts

**"I will put My law in their minds and inscribe it on their hearts. And I will be their God, and they will be My people"** (31:33). {v: 31:33}

Inscribe means write or carve. God's will won't just be on stone tablets. It will be in people's hearts. {v: 31:33}

Everyone will know God, from the least to the greatest. {v: 31:34}

**"For I will forgive their iniquities and will remember their sins no more"** (31:34). {v: 31:34}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> At the Last Supper, Jesus took the cup and said, **"This cup is the new covenant in My blood, which is poured out for you"** (Luke 22:20).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Jeremiah 31:1-14 and 31-34 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"I have loved you with an everlasting love"** (31:3) | God's love never runs out. |
| **"Including the blind and the lame"** (31:8) | God brings everyone home, especially the weak. |
| **"I will turn their mourning into joy"** (31:13) | Sadness won't last forever. |
| **"A new covenant"** (31:31) | God would make a new and better promise. |
| **"Inscribe it on their hearts"** (31:33) | God changes people from the inside. |
| **"I will... remember their sins no more"** (31:34) | God's forgiveness is complete. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**The first covenant was made at Mount Sinai.** God gave the Ten Commandments on stone tablets (Exodus 24:12, 34:1).

**"New covenant" and "new testament" mean the same thing.** The second part of the Bible is called the New Testament because of this promise.†

**Hebrews quotes this whole passage.** It says Jesus brought the new covenant (Hebrews 8:8-12).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did the old covenant fail because of God?** No. God was faithful. The people broke it. The new covenant is new because God himself makes it work, by forgiving and changing hearts.

**Will no one need teachers?** Jeremiah means everyone will know God personally, not just priests and prophets. Christians still learn from teachers and from each other.†

**Is this promise only for Israel?** God made it with Israel and Judah. Through Jesus, people from every nation are brought into it (Ephesians 2:12-13).†

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Being a Christian means following rules."*

**What's true:** God's commandments are good, and Christians want to follow them.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God promised a new covenant, not written on stone but on hearts. God forgives first, then he changes us from the inside, so we know him and love his ways.

**Where Jesus is:** At the Last Supper, Jesus said the cup is the new covenant in his blood. In the Lord's Supper, he still gives that forgiveness.

## ✝️ Good News

The people broke the old covenant. God made a new one.

**"For I will forgive their iniquities and will remember their sins no more"** (31:34).

Jesus made this new covenant with his own blood. God forgives completely and writes his love on our hearts.

**God promised a new covenant written on hearts. Through Jesus's blood, he forgives our sins and remembers them no more.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that the Lord's Supper is the new testament: Christ's last will, giving forgiveness of sins to everyone who believes his words "given for you."†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Jeremiah 31:7-14 on the Second Sunday of Christmas in all three years, and Jeremiah 31:31-34 on the Fifth Sunday in Lent in Year B. Lutheran churches also read Jeremiah 31:31-34 on Reformation Sunday.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God loves his people with an everlasting love. He promised a new covenant, written on hearts, in which he forgives sins and remembers them no more.**
