# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** The sons of Korah, a group of temple musicians (85:1).

**What it is.** A psalm that moves from remembered restoration to present need to promised peace. The people have been forgiven before — they ask again.

**Why it matters.** Verse 10 is one of the most beautiful in the Psalter: lovingkindness and faithfulness meet; righteousness and peace kiss. Christians see the cross there.

**When the church sings it.** In Advent, especially the Second Sunday of Advent in Year C, and on other Sundays in Ordinary Time.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. You Have Restored Before | 85:1-3 | God forgave, covered sin, turned from anger |
| 2. Restore Us Again | 85:4-7 | A prayer for the present moment |
| 3. Righteousness and Peace | 85:8-13 | A promise: loving devotion and faithfulness will meet |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: You Have Restored Before

**"You showed favor to Your land, O LORD; You restored Jacob from captivity"** (85:1). {v: 85:1}

The people remember what God has already done. He brought them home before. {v: 85:1}

**"You forgave the iniquity of Your people; You covered all their sin"** (85:2). {v: 85:2}

Covered sin — not exposed, not punished, but covered. {v: 85:2, 85:3}

### Section 2: Restore Us Again

**"Restore us, O God of our salvation, and put away Your displeasure toward us"** (85:4). {v: 85:4}

They know God has done it before. They need it again. {v: 85:4}

**"Will You not revive us again, that Your people may rejoice in You?"** (85:6). {v: 85:5, 85:6}

**"Show us Your loving devotion, O LORD, and grant us Your salvation"** (85:7). {v: 85:7}

### Section 3: Righteousness and Peace

**"I will listen to what God the LORD will say; for He will surely speak peace to His people and His saints; He will not let them return to folly"** (85:8). {v: 85:8}

A pause. A turning of the ear. What does God say? {v: 85:8}

**"Surely His salvation is near to those who fear Him, that His glory may dwell in our land"** (85:9). {v: 85:9}

**"Loving devotion and faithfulness have joined together; righteousness and peace have kissed"** (85:10). {v: 85:10}

God's character — his love, his truth, his justice, his peace — are not in conflict. At the cross, they all come together. {v: 85:10}

**"Faithfulness sprouts from the earth, and righteousness looks down from heaven"** (85:11). {v: 85:11}

**"The LORD will indeed provide what is good, and our land will yield its increase"** (85:12). {v: 85:12}

**"Righteousness will go before Him to prepare the way for His steps"** (85:13). {v: 85:13}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 85 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"You restored Jacob from captivity"** (85:1) | A reference to the return from exile in Babylon. |
| **"You covered all their sin"** (85:2) | God did not just forget sin; he covered it — atoned for it. |
| **"Will You not revive us again, that Your people may rejoice in You?"** (85:6) | A prayer that remembers the past to ask for the present. |
| **"Loving devotion and faithfulness have joined together"** (85:10) | God's mercy and truth are not opposites. |
| **"Righteousness and peace have kissed"** (85:10) | Justice and peace embrace — only God can make that possible. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

The psalm was likely written after the **exile in Babylon** — the people had returned, but life was still hard. They had experienced restoration before; they needed it again.

**"Righteousness looking down from heaven"** — a picture of God's justice coming to meet the faithfulness rising from the earth.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**How can righteousness and peace "kiss"?** Justice demands that sin be punished; peace means the punishment is settled. At the cross, God's righteousness and his peace come together. Both are satisfied.

**Is "He will not let them return to folly" a warning?** It is a reassurance: the peace God gives is not fragile. He upholds it.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The people | Hopeful about the past | God restored them before (85:1-2) |
| The people | Needy in the present | They ask again: revive us, restore us (85:4-7) |
| The people | Expectant | God will speak peace; righteousness is coming (85:9-13) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God can't forgive and be fair at the same time."*

**What's true:** Sin has real consequences; justice is real.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"Loving devotion and faithfulness have joined together; righteousness and peace have kissed"** (85:10). At the cross, God's justice was fully met and his mercy fully given — they did not cancel each other; they embraced.

**Where Jesus is:** He is where righteousness and peace kiss — his death satisfied God's justice, his resurrection brings God's peace. He is also the salvation that is near.

## ✝️ Good News

God's mercy and truth come together.

**"Loving devotion and faithfulness have joined together; righteousness and peace have kissed"** (85:10).

This is the gospel: what seemed impossible was made possible at the cross.

**Psalm 85 remembers God's past restoration, asks for it again, and promises: righteousness and peace will kiss. At the cross, they do. Jesus is where they meet.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said the gospel is where mercy and truth meet at the cross. God does not overlook sin — he forgives it fully. Justice is satisfied and grace flows freely.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 85 on the Second Sunday of Advent in Year C, the Eighth Sunday after Pentecost in Year B, and on other Sundays in Ordinary Time.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 85 remembers that God restored his people before and asks him to do it again. His answer: loving devotion and faithfulness have joined; righteousness and peace have kissed. At the cross, they do.**
