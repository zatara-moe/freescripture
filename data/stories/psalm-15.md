# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** David (15:1).

**What it is.** A short entrance psalm — five verses, one question, and one answer. People may have sung it as they approached the temple to worship.

**When the church sings it.** On Sundays in Ordinary Time, especially when the readings focus on living justly.

**How to read it.** The question in verse 1 is the question. The rest of the psalm is the answer.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Question | 15:1 | Who may come to God? |
| 2. The Answer | 15:2-5 | A person who lives with integrity |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Question

**"O LORD, who may abide in Your tent? Who may dwell on Your holy mountain?"** (15:1). {v: 15:1}

The tent is the tabernacle, God's dwelling place. The holy mountain is Zion, where the temple stood. The question is: who belongs in God's presence? {v: 15:1}

### Section 2: The Answer

**"He who walks with integrity and practices righteousness, who speaks the truth from his heart"** (15:2). {v: 15:2}

Integrity means being the same inside and outside. Truth comes from the heart, not just the lips. {v: 15:2}

**"who has no slander on his tongue, who does no harm to his neighbor, who casts no scorn on his friend"** (15:3). {v: 15:3}

Words matter. Neighbors matter. A righteous person is careful with both. {v: 15:3}

**"who despises the vile but honors those who fear the LORD, who does not revise a costly oath"** (15:4). {v: 15:4}

Not revising a costly oath means keeping a promise even when it is painful. {v: 15:4}

**"who lends his money without interest and refuses a bribe against the innocent. He who does these things will never be shaken"** (15:5). {v: 15:5}

The person who lives this way stands firm. Not because they earned it, but because they are rooted in God. {v: 15:5}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 15 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Abide in Your tent"** (15:1) | Live in God's presence, not just visit it. |
| **"Walks with integrity"** (15:2) | A whole life that matches what is inside. |
| **"Speaks the truth from his heart"** (15:2) | Honest through and through. |
| **"Does not revise a costly oath"** (15:4) | Keeps promises even when it costs something. |
| **"Will never be shaken"** (15:5) | The firm and stable life God gives. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"Your holy mountain"** is Mount Zion in Jerusalem, where the temple stood.

**Lending without interest** was a matter of justice in ancient Israel. Charging interest on a loan to a poor neighbor was considered exploitation.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is this a list of requirements to earn God's favor?** No. It is a description of a person shaped by God's word. The psalmist is describing what life in God's presence looks like, not a checklist to pass.

**Who can actually do all of this?** No one perfectly. Jesus is the one person who lived this completely. We follow his pattern.

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**Where does this psalm land?** Tap one.

- I want to be more honest :: Speak truth from your heart.
- I've broken a promise :: Ask God for the courage to keep them.
- I feel unstable :: The person who lives this way will never be shaken.
- I wonder if I belong in God's presence :: Jesus kept every oath. In him, you belong.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"It doesn't matter how you live, only what you believe."*

**What's true:** Faith is not about earning your way.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The person who truly believes in God lives differently. **"He who walks with integrity and practices righteousness"** (15:2) — faith has a shape. It shows up in words, neighbors, promises, and money.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the one who kept every promise, never slandered, never took a bribe. In him we are welcome in God's presence, and by his Spirit we grow into the person this psalm describes.

## ✝️ Good News

You are welcome.

**"He who does these things will never be shaken"** (15:5).

Jesus is the one who did these things fully. In him, we are welcomed into God's presence and given a firm place to stand.

**Psalm 15 asks who may draw near to God. The answer: someone shaped by integrity, truth, and care for their neighbor. Jesus lived this fully and welcomes us in.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that genuine faith shows itself in love for the neighbor. The person who truly trusts God cannot remain indifferent to injustice or cruelty.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 15 on several Sundays in Ordinary Time, especially in Years A and B when themes of justice and righteousness appear in the readings.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 15 asks who belongs in God's presence. The answer: the person who walks with integrity, speaks truth, keeps promises, and protects the weak. Jesus lived this and welcomes us in.**
