# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul wrote this letter around AD 57 to the Christians in Rome. He had never visited them yet (1:13).

**What was happening.** The church in Rome had Jewish and non-Jewish believers. Paul hoped to visit on his way to Spain, so he wrote out the good news he preached, carefully and in order (15:23-24).†

**What this passage is.** Paul's opening, where he names the theme of the whole letter (1:16-17). Then we jump ahead to chapter 3, where he explains it: everyone has sinned, and God makes people right with him freely, as a gift.

**How to read it.** Watch for two words: "righteousness" (being right with God) and "faith" (trusting him).

## 🗺️ Map of the Letter

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Called and Loved | 1:1-7 | Paul introduces himself and the good news about Jesus |
| 2. I Long to See You | 1:8-15 | Paul thanks God for them and wants to visit |
| 3. Not Ashamed | 1:16-17 | The good news is God's power to save |
| 4. All Have Sinned | 3:21-26 | Everyone falls short, and God justifies freely |
| 5. No Room to Boast | 3:27-28 | We are made right by faith, not works |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Called and Loved

**"Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus, called to be an apostle, and set apart for the gospel of God"** (1:1). {v: 1:1}

The gospel is the good news. God promised it long ago through the prophets. {v: 1:2}

It's about his Son, who was born from David's family and raised from the dead: **"Jesus Christ our Lord"** (1:4). {v: 1:3, 1:4}

Paul writes **"to all in Rome who are loved by God and called to be saints"** (1:7). Saints here means God's holy people, all believers. {v: 1:5, 1:6, 1:7}

### Section 2: I Long to See You

Paul thanks God because their faith is talked about all over the world. {v: 1:8}

He prays for them all the time and hopes to come. {v: 1:9, 1:10}

He wants them to be **"mutually encouraged by each other's faith"** (1:12). Even the great apostle needs encouragement. {v: 1:11, 1:12}

He has tried to visit before but kept getting stopped. {v: 1:13, 1:14, 1:15}

### Section 3: Not Ashamed

Then Paul states the theme of the whole letter. {v: 1:16}

**"I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, first to the Jew, then to the Greek"** (1:16). {v: 1:16}

The good news shows how God makes people right with him: by faith, from start to finish. {v: 1:17}

As the prophet Habakkuk wrote, **"The righteous will live by faith"** (1:17). {v: 1:17}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Paul is quoting Habakkuk 2:4. This verse changed Martin Luther's life.

### Section 4: All Have Sinned

In the chapters between, Paul shows that everyone, Jew and non-Jew, has turned away from God. Then comes the turning point. {v: 3:21}

**"But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been revealed"** (3:21). {v: 3:21}

It comes through faith in Jesus, to all who believe. **"There is no distinction"** (3:22). {v: 3:22}

**"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus"** (3:23-24). {v: 3:23, 3:24}

Justified means declared right with God. Freely means as a gift. Redemption means being bought back and set free. {v: 3:24}

God gave Jesus as the sacrifice that takes away sin. God is fair, and he makes right everyone who trusts Jesus. {v: 3:25, 3:26}

### Section 5: No Room to Boast

**"Where, then, is boasting? It is excluded"** (3:27). {v: 3:27}

No one can brag, because no one earned it. {v: 3:27}

**"For we maintain that a man is justified by faith apart from works of the law"** (3:28). {v: 3:28}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Romans 1:1-17 and 3:21-28 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Set apart for the gospel of God"** (1:1) | Paul's whole life was for the good news. |
| **"Called to be saints"** (1:7) | All believers are God's holy people. |
| **"I am not ashamed of the gospel"** (1:16) | Paul is proud of the good news, even when others laugh. |
| **"The power of God for salvation"** (1:16) | The good news doesn't just tell about saving. It saves. |
| **"The righteous will live by faith"** (1:17) | People right with God live by trusting him. |
| **"All have sinned and fall short"** (3:23) | No one is good enough on their own. |
| **"Justified freely by His grace"** (3:24) | God declares us right with him as a gift. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Rome** was the capital of the empire, a city of around a million people.†

**Jews and Greeks.** "Greek" here means anyone who isn't Jewish. Paul says the good news is for both.

**Paul finally got to Rome** years later, as a prisoner (Acts 28:16).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Why would anyone be ashamed of the gospel?** A crucified Savior seemed foolish and weak to many people in Rome (1 Corinthians 1:23).

**Does "all have sinned" mean everyone is equally bad?** It means everyone falls short of God's glory. No one can stand before God on their own record.

**Does faith mean good works don't matter?** No. Paul says we are made right by faith, not works. Later in the letter he shows how this new life shows up in how we live (12:1-2).

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Paul | Eager | He longed to see them (1:11, 1:15) |
| Paul | Unashamed | The gospel is God's power (1:16) |
| Everyone | Short of the mark | All have sinned (3:23) |
| Believers | Free | They are justified as a gift (3:24) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God helps those who help themselves."*

**What's true:** God gives us work to do and wants us to use our gifts.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God helps those who can't help themselves. **"All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified freely by His grace"** (3:23-24).

**Where Jesus is:** He is the good news itself, the Son of God raised from the dead, who redeems us.

## ✝️ Good News

Everyone falls short. You, me, everyone.

**"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus"** (3:23-24).

God makes us right with him as a gift, through faith in Jesus.

**The good news is the power of God to save everyone who believes. All have sinned, and all are justified freely by his grace in Christ Jesus.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said that when he finally understood Romans 1:17, that God's righteousness is a gift given through faith, he felt born again, as if the gates of paradise had opened.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Romans 1:1-7 on the Fourth Sunday of Advent, and Romans 1:16-17 with 3:22b-28 in late spring and early summer. Lutheran churches often read Romans 3:19-28 on Reformation Sunday.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The good news is the power of God for salvation. All have sinned and fall short, and God makes us right with him freely, by grace, through faith in Jesus.**
