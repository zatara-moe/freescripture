# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Asaph (81:1).

**What it is.** A festival psalm that turns into a divine speech — God's own voice recounting the Exodus and mourning what Israel chose instead.

**What it's about.** God brought them out of slavery. He told them to turn to Him and He would satisfy them. They did not listen. And He is still calling.

**When the church sings it.** On Sundays in Ordinary Time and at festivals.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Sing Loudly | 81:1–5 | Festival call: sing, blow the horn, remember the day God appointed |
| 2. God Speaks | 81:6–10 | God recalls the Exodus: I freed you, I tested you, I am the LORD |
| 3. If Only | 81:11–16 | Israel did not listen; if they had, God would have satisfied them |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Sing Loudly

**"Sing for joy to God our strength; make a joyful noise to the God of Jacob"** (81:1). {v: 81:1}

The psalm opens at full volume. Festival worship — not quiet reflection. {v: 81:1}

**"Lift up a song, strike the tambourine, play the sweet-sounding harp and lyre"** (81:2). {v: 81:2}

**"Sound the ram's horn at the New Moon, and at the full moon on the day of our Feast"** (81:3). {v: 81:3}

**"For this is a statute for Israel, an ordinance of the God of Jacob"** (81:4). {v: 81:4, 81:5}

This celebration is not optional. God appointed it. {v: 81:4}

### Section 2: God Speaks

**"I relieved his shoulder of the burden; his hands were freed from the basket"** (81:6). {v: 81:6}

The voice shifts. Now it is God speaking. The burden was Egypt — the labor of slavery. {v: 81:6}

**"You called out in distress, and I rescued you; I answered you from the cloud of thunder; I tested you at the waters of Meribah"** (81:7). {v: 81:7}

The rescue is real. And so is the test. Meribah was where Israel demanded water and doubted God (Exodus 17). {v: 81:7}

**"Hear, O My people, and I will warn you: O Israel, if only you would listen to Me!"** (81:8). {v: 81:8}

God breaks into the account with longing. *If only.* {v: 81:8}

**"There must be no strange god among you, nor shall you bow to a foreign god"** (81:9). {v: 81:9}

**"I am the LORD your God, who brought you up out of Egypt. Open wide your mouth, and I will fill it"** (81:10). {v: 81:10}

He identifies Himself by what He has done and makes a staggering offer: open wide and I will fill you. {v: 81:10}

### Section 3: If Only

**"But My people would not listen to Me, and Israel would not obey Me"** (81:11). {v: 81:11}

The offer was refused. Not because God withdrew — because Israel turned away. {v: 81:11}

**"So I gave them up to their stubborn hearts to follow their own devices"** (81:12). {v: 81:12}

This is one of the saddest lines in the Psalms. God's judgment here is not fire — it is release. He let them go the way they chose. {v: 81:12}

**"If only My people would listen to Me, if Israel would follow My ways"** (81:13). {v: 81:13}

Said twice. The longing of God across centuries. {v: 81:13}

**"how soon I would subdue their enemies and turn My hand against their foes!"** (81:14). {v: 81:14, 81:15}

**"But I would feed you the finest wheat; with honey from the rock I would satisfy you"** (81:16). {v: 81:16}

The psalm ends not with condemnation but with the promise still on the table. What He would have given — finest wheat, honey from the rock — He is still willing to give. {v: 81:16}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 81 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"I am the LORD your God, who brought you up out of Egypt"** (81:10) | God identifies Himself by His saving act, the same words as the opening of the Ten Commandments. |
| **"Open wide your mouth, and I will fill it"** (81:10) | An astounding offer: complete satisfaction, if they turn to Him. |
| **"My people would not listen to Me"** (81:11) | The offer was made. It was refused. Not by God's choice but by Israel's. |
| **"I gave them up to their stubborn hearts"** (81:12) | God's judgment was to release them to what they chose. That is itself a kind of horror. |
| **"If only My people would listen to Me"** (81:13) | The longing in God's own voice. He still wants to give what Israel refused. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

Asaph wrote this for a festival — likely the Feast of Tabernacles or Trumpets, which included the blowing of the shofar (ram's horn) at the new moon (81:3).

The psalm quotes the opening of the Ten Commandments almost word for word: "I am the LORD your God, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt" (81:10; cf. Exodus 20:2). This places the festival in direct continuity with the Sinai covenant.

**"The waters of Meribah"** (81:7) — a place of testing in the wilderness where Israel quarreled with God over water (Exodus 17; Numbers 20). God answered even there — but Meribah became a byword for faithlessness.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"I gave them up to their stubborn hearts"** (81:12) — Is God's response to disobedience simply to step aside? Here, yes. This is one of Scripture's most sobering patterns: when people persistently refuse God, He honors that refusal. Paul develops this in Romans 1. The most fearful form of judgment is not a thunderbolt — it is God saying, "All right. Have it your way."

**"Honey from the rock"** (81:16) — an image of abundance in an unlikely place. Rock does not yield sweetness — except when God wills it (Deuteronomy 32:13). The promise is provision from the impossible.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Israel | Celebrating | Festival worship at full volume (81:1–3) |
| God | Longing | *If only My people would listen* — said twice (81:13) |
| Reader | Arrested | The offer is still on the table: open wide and I will fill you (81:10) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What does this psalm speak into?** Tap one.

- I wonder if God is still calling me :: He is. The *if only* is still in His voice.
- I've been going my own way :: He gave them over to what they chose. That is the danger of the path, not a door that can't reopen.
- I feel spiritually empty :: Open your mouth wide and I will fill it. The offer stands.
- I want to come back :: The psalm ends with the promise still available: finest wheat, honey from the rock.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God is finished with people who turn away."*

**What's true:** Israel turned away, and there were real consequences — including being given over to their stubbornness.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The psalm ends not with condemnation but with the promise still extended. "If only my people would listen." The longing is in the present tense, not the past. What God offered — finest wheat, honey from the rock — He is still offering. The door is still open; the voice is still calling.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus quotes Psalm 81's pattern in His own lament over Jerusalem: "How often I have longed to gather your children together... but you were not willing" (Matthew 23:37). The divine longing in Psalm 81 is the same longing in Jesus. And He is the rock from which the honey flows — the one who gives living water to those who come.

## ✝️ Good News

**"I am the LORD your God... Open wide your mouth, and I will fill it"** (81:10).

The offer stands. Turn to Him. He has been calling since the Exodus. He is calling now.

**Psalm 81: God speaks at the festival: I freed you from Egypt, I will fill you. But you did not listen. If only you would — I would satisfy you with the finest wheat and honey from the rock.**

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It appoints Psalm 81 on the Fourteenth Sunday after Pentecost in Year B and on other Sundays in Ordinary Time.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 81: God recalls the Exodus and speaks directly to His people. Open your mouth wide and I will fill you. Israel did not listen — and God gave them over to their own ways. His longing remains: if only you would come back.**
