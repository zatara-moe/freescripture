# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Peter, the fisherman who became one of Jesus's closest followers (1:1). He wrote from "Babylon," likely a code name for Rome (5:13).†

**Who it was for.** Christians scattered across what is now Turkey. Many were new believers, and their neighbors looked down on them, insulted them, and sometimes worse (4:4, 4:14).

**What this letter is.** A letter of hope for people who are suffering. Peter, who once denied Jesus, now tells others how to stand firm. He keeps coming back to Jesus's resurrection, baptism, and the hope it gives.

**How to read it.** This page walks through the whole short letter, picking up the passages read in church, especially in the Easter season.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: A Living Hope

**"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! By His great mercy He has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead"** (1:3). {v: 1:3}

God is keeping an inheritance for us in heaven that can never spoil or fade. {v: 1:4, 1:5}

For now, we may suffer trials. Like gold tested in fire, faith is proven true. {v: 1:6, 1:7}

**"Though you have not seen Him, you love Him; and though you do not see Him now, you believe in Him and rejoice with an inexpressible and glorious joy"** (1:8). {v: 1:8, 1:9}

Inexpressible means too great for words. {v: 1:8}

### Section 2: Bought With Precious Blood

Live with reverence while you're here, like foreigners passing through. {v: 1:17}

**"For you know that it was not with perishable things such as silver or gold that you were redeemed"** (1:18). {v: 1:18}

It was **"with the precious blood of Christ, a lamb without blemish or spot"** (1:19). {v: 1:19, 1:20, 1:21}

So love one another deeply, from the heart. {v: 1:22}

**"For you have been born again, not of perishable seed, but of imperishable, through the living and enduring word of God"** (1:23). {v: 1:23}

### Section 3: Living Stones

**"Like newborn babies, crave pure spiritual milk, so that by it you may grow up in your salvation"** (2:2). {v: 2:2, 2:3}

Jesus is the living stone. People rejected him, but God chose him. {v: 2:4}

**"You also, like living stones, are being built into a spiritual house"** (2:5). {v: 2:5}

Jesus is the cornerstone, the stone the builders rejected. {v: 2:6, 2:7, 2:8}

**"But you are a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God's own possession, to proclaim the virtues of Him who called you out of darkness into His marvelous light"** (2:9). {v: 2:9}

**"Once you were not a people, but now you are the people of God; once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy"** (2:10). {v: 2:10}

### Section 4: The Shepherd of Your Souls

Peter writes to people, including slaves, who suffered unfairly. {v: 2:19, 2:20}

Jesus also suffered unfairly. When people insulted him, he didn't fight back. He trusted God, who judges fairly. {v: 2:21, 2:22, 2:23}

**"He Himself bore our sins in His body on the tree, so that we might die to sin and live to righteousness"** (2:24). {v: 2:24}

**"By His stripes you are healed"** (2:24). Stripes are the wounds from a whip. {v: 2:24}

You were like sheep going astray, but now you have come back **"to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls"** (2:25). {v: 2:25}

### Section 5: The Reason for Your Hope

Even if you suffer for doing what is right, you are blessed. Don't be afraid. {v: 3:13, 3:14}

**"Always be prepared to give a defense to everyone who asks you the reason for the hope that is in you. But respond with gentleness and respect"** (3:15). {v: 3:15, 3:16, 3:17}

**"For Christ also suffered for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring you to God"** (3:18). {v: 3:18}

Peter remembers Noah. In the ark, eight people were saved through water. {v: 3:19, 3:20}

**"And this water symbolizes the baptism that now saves you also"** (3:21). {v: 3:21}

It saves through the resurrection of Jesus, who now reigns at God's right hand. {v: 3:21, 3:22}

### Section 6: Cast Your Cares on Him

**"Beloved, do not be surprised at the fiery trial that has come upon you, as though something strange were happening to you"** (4:12). {v: 4:12}

When you suffer for Jesus, you share in his sufferings, and the Spirit of glory rests on you. {v: 4:13, 4:14}

**"Humble yourselves, therefore, under God's mighty hand, so that in due time He may exalt you"** (5:6). {v: 5:6}

**"Cast all your anxiety on Him, because He cares for you"** (5:7). {v: 5:7}

Stay alert. The devil prowls like a roaring lion. Stand firm. You are not the only one suffering. {v: 5:8, 5:9}

**"And after you have suffered for a little while, the God of all grace, who has called you to His eternal glory in Christ, will Himself restore you, secure you, strengthen you, and establish you"** (5:10). {v: 5:10, 5:11}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Peter 1 to 5 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"A living hope"** (1:3) | Our hope is alive because Jesus is alive. |
| **"The precious blood of Christ"** (1:19) | Jesus paid for us with his own life. |
| **"Like living stones"** (2:5) | Each believer is part of God's house. |
| **"A royal priesthood"** (2:9) | All believers can come to God and pray for others. |
| **"By His stripes you are healed"** (2:24) | Jesus's wounds bring us healing and forgiveness. |
| **"The reason for the hope that is in you"** (3:15) | Be ready to explain why you trust Jesus. |
| **"Baptism that now saves you"** (3:21) | God saves through baptism, because Jesus rose. |
| **"Cast all your anxiety on Him"** (5:7) | Hand your worries to God. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Christians were outsiders.** They didn't join in the worship of local gods or wild parties, so neighbors thought they were strange (4:3-4).

**Many early Christians were slaves.** Peter speaks to them directly and points to Jesus, who also suffered unjustly.

**Peter quotes Isaiah 53** (2:22-25), the song of the Suffering Servant.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does Peter say slaves should accept abuse?** Peter speaks to people who had no power to change their situation and gives them dignity: God sees, Jesus suffered too, and God judges justly. He isn't saying slavery is good.

**Does God want me to suffer?** No. Peter says that when suffering comes, God hasn't left you. Jesus suffered too, and God will restore you.

**What does "preached to the spirits in prison" mean?** It's one of the hardest verses in the Bible. Christians have understood it in different ways. Lutherans have often seen it as Christ announcing his victory.†

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If you're suffering, you must have done something wrong."*

**What's true:** Sometimes our choices do bring trouble.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Peter says believers often suffer for doing good. **"Do not be surprised at the fiery trial"** (4:12). Jesus suffered unfairly, and he is with you in it.

**Where Jesus is:** He bore our sins, he is the Shepherd of our souls, and he rose to give us a living hope.

## ✝️ Good News

Your hope isn't a wish. It's alive.

**"By His great mercy He has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead"** (1:3).

You were bought with Jesus's blood. You belong to God's people. And God cares for you.

**God gave us new birth into a living hope through Jesus's resurrection. We are his chosen people, and after we suffer a little while, the God of all grace will restore us.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther used 1 Peter 2:9 to teach that all baptized Christians are priests. Every believer can come to God directly and pray for others.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads through 1 Peter on the Sundays of Easter, from the Second Sunday of Easter to the Seventh. In Year B, it reads 1 Peter 3:18-22 on the First Sunday in Lent.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God has given us new birth into a living hope through Jesus's resurrection. Bought with his precious blood, we are his chosen people, and we cast all our cares on him, because he cares for us.**
