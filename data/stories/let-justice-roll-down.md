# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Amos was a shepherd and fig farmer from the small town of Tekoa in Judah, the southern kingdom (Amos 1:1, 7:14).

**Who he spoke to.** God sent him north, to Israel, about 760 BC. Israel was rich and at peace. The wealthy lived in fancy houses. But they got rich by cheating the poor.†

**What this passage is.** Several of Amos's strongest messages, from chapters 5 to 8. They are hard words, spoken to comfortable people who had forgotten the poor.

**How to read it.** Amos is blunt. Listen for what God cares about.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Seek the LORD and Live | 5:6-15 | Trampling the poor is turning from God |
| 2. Let Justice Roll Down | 5:18-24 | God doesn't want worship without justice |
| 3. At Ease in Zion | 6:1-7 | Woe to the comfortable who don't care |
| 4. A Plumb Line | 7:7-15 | God measures his people, and sends a farmer to tell them |
| 5. Dishonest Scales | 8:4-7 | God won't forget the cheating of the poor |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Seek the LORD and Live

**"Seek the LORD and live"** (5:6). {v: 5:6}

Some people turned justice into something bitter. They hated anyone who told the truth in court. {v: 5:7, 5:10}

**"Because you trample on the poor and exact from him a tax of grain, you will never live in the stone houses you have built"** (5:11). {v: 5:11}

They took bribes and kept poor people from getting a fair hearing. {v: 5:12}

**"Seek good, not evil, so that you may live"** (5:14). {v: 5:14}

**"Hate evil and love good; establish justice in the gate"** (5:15). The city gate was where court cases were decided. {v: 5:15}

### Section 2: Let Justice Roll Down

The people were looking forward to "the Day of the LORD." They thought it meant God would beat their enemies. {v: 5:18}

Amos said, for you it will be darkness, not light. Like running from a lion only to meet a bear. {v: 5:18, 5:19, 5:20}

Then God speaks. **"I hate, I despise your feasts!"** (5:21). {v: 5:21}

God didn't want their offerings or their songs. {v: 5:22, 5:23}

**"But let justice roll on like a river, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream"** (5:24). {v: 5:24}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. quoted Amos 5:24 in his famous "I Have a Dream" speech in 1963.

### Section 3: At Ease in Zion

**"Woe to those at ease in Zion and those secure on Mount Samaria"** (6:1). {v: 6:1}

They lay on beds decorated with ivory. They ate the best meat, played music, and drank wine by the bowlful. {v: 6:4, 6:5, 6:6}

**"But you fail to grieve over the ruin of Joseph"** (6:6). Joseph means the nation. It was falling apart, and they didn't care. {v: 6:6}

### Section 4: A Plumb Line

God showed Amos a vision: the Lord standing by a wall, with a plumb line in his hand. {v: 7:7}

A plumb line is a string with a weight on the end. Builders use it to check that a wall is perfectly straight. {v: 7:7}

God was measuring his people, and they were crooked. {v: 7:8}

A priest named Amaziah told Amos to go home and stop prophesying in the king's temple. {v: 7:10, 7:12, 7:13}

**"I was not a prophet,"** Amos replied, **"nor was I the son of a prophet; rather, I was a herdsman and a tender of sycamore-fig trees"** (7:14). {v: 7:14}

**"But the LORD took me from following the flock and said to me, 'Go, prophesy to My people Israel.'"** (7:15). {v: 7:15}

### Section 5: Dishonest Scales

**"Hear this, you who trample the needy"** (8:4). {v: 8:4}

Some merchants couldn't wait for the holy days to end so they could get back to selling. {v: 8:5}

They cheated with dishonest scales. They sold even the worthless husks mixed in with the grain. {v: 8:5, 8:6}

They bought poor people as slaves, **"the needy for a pair of sandals"** (8:6). {v: 8:6}

**"I will never forget any of their deeds"** (8:7). {v: 8:7}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Amos 5:6-24, 6:1-7, 7:7-15, and 8:4-7 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Seek the LORD and live"** (5:6) | Turning back to God is the way to life. |
| **"You trample on the poor"** (5:11) | God sees how the powerful treat the weak. |
| **"I hate, I despise your feasts!"** (5:21) | Worship without justice is empty. |
| **"Let justice roll on like a river"** (5:24) | God wants fairness everywhere, never drying up. |
| **"Woe to those at ease in Zion"** (6:1) | Comfort that ignores suffering is dangerous. |
| **"A plumb line"** (7:7) | God measures his people by what is right. |
| **"I will never forget any of their deeds"** (8:7) | Injustice doesn't escape God's notice. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Israel was divided into two kingdoms.** Amos came from Judah in the south and preached in Israel in the north.

**Israel fell to Assyria in 722 BC**, about forty years after Amos. Many people were taken into exile, as he warned.†

**Sycamore-figs** were cheap fruit that had to be cut to ripen. Tending them was humble work.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God hate worship?** No. God hates worship that comes from people who are cruel to the poor. He wants worship and justice together.

**Is it wrong to be rich?** Amos isn't against having things. He is against getting rich by cheating, and not caring about people who suffer.

**Is God just angry in Amos?** Amos is full of warnings. But the book ends with God promising to rebuild and restore his people (9:11-15).

# Why It Matters

- Don't get too comfortable :: Do you care about people who are hurting?

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Faith is private. It has nothing to do with how society works."*

**What's true:** Faith is personal. Each person trusts God for themselves.

**What the passage is mainly about:** For Amos, how we treat the poor is part of how we treat God. Worship that ignores injustice is empty. God wants justice to roll like a river.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus said, **"Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers of Mine, you did for Me"** (Matthew 25:40).

## ✝️ Good News

God sees the poor. He never forgets them.

**"But let justice roll on like a river, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream"** (5:24).

In Jesus, God's righteousness came to us. He forgives our failures and makes us people who care about justice.

**God sent a shepherd named Amos to warn comfortable people who trampled the poor. "Let justice roll on like a river."**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Small Catechism, Luther explained the Seventh Commandment, "You shall not steal." It means not taking our neighbor's money or property by cheating or dishonest dealing, but helping them improve and protect it.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Amos 7:7-15 in July (Lectionary 15) and Amos 5:6-7 and 10-15 in October (Lectionary 28). In Year C, it reads Amos 8:4-7 and 6:1a, 4-7 in September (Lectionary 25 and 26). In Year A, Amos 5:18-24 is an option in November (Lectionary 32).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God sent Amos, a shepherd, to warn rich and comfortable people who cheated the poor. God doesn't want empty worship. "Let justice roll on like a river."**
