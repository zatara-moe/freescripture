# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Anonymous — "A Song of Ascents," one of the pilgrim psalms.

**What it is.** A psalm of protection for the journey — question and answer, first person and second, rising to the promise that God watches over all coming and going, forever.

**What it's about.** I lift my eyes to the hills. Where does my help come from? From the LORD, who made heaven and earth. He does not sleep. He watches over you — now and forever.

**When the church sings it.** At funerals, at sendoffs, and on Sundays in Ordinary Time.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Where Does My Help Come From? | 121:1–2 | I look to the hills — but help comes from the LORD who made them |
| 2. He Will Not Let Your Foot Slip | 121:3–4 | He does not sleep or slumber — He watches over Israel |
| 3. He Watches Over You | 121:5–8 | Shade at your right hand — over your going out and coming in, forever |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Where Does My Help Come From?

**"I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come?"** (121:1). {v: 121:1}

The hills may have been a threat on the ancient road — bandits, enemies, treacherous terrain. Or they may be the hills of Jerusalem, the destination. Either way: the question is asked. {v: 121:1}

**"My help comes from the LORD, the Maker of heaven and earth"** (121:2). {v: 121:2}

The answer: not from the hills themselves, but from the one who made them. The Creator, not the creation. {v: 121:2}

### Section 2: He Will Not Let Your Foot Slip

**"He will not allow your foot to slip; your Protector will not slumber."** (121:3). {v: 121:3}

The voice shifts — now someone is speaking *to* the pilgrim. A reassurance. {v: 121:3}

**"Behold, the Protector of Israel will neither slumber nor sleep."** (121:4). {v: 121:4}

The great contrast with the gods of the nations: they sleep, they are absent, they need waking (1 Kings 18:27). The LORD does not. {v: 121:4}

### Section 3: He Watches Over You

**"The LORD is your keeper; the LORD is the shade on your right hand."** (121:5). {v: 121:5}

Keeper — the word used for a guard or a shepherd. At your right hand — the place of a defender. {v: 121:5}

**"The sun will not strike you by day, nor the moon by night"** (121:6). {v: 121:6}

Ancient dangers: heatstroke, the strange fears of the night. Neither will harm you. {v: 121:6}

**"The LORD will guard you from all evil; He will preserve your soul."** (121:7). {v: 121:7}

Broader now — not just the road, not just the sun, not just the night. *All evil.* And not just the body — the soul. {v: 121:7}

**"The LORD will watch over your coming and going, both now and forevermore"** (121:8). {v: 121:8}

The psalm began with a question. It ends with an answer that covers everything: every coming, every going, now and forever. {v: 121:8}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 121 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"My help comes from the LORD, the Maker of heaven and earth"** (121:2) | The Creator of what you see is also your personal helper. The largest and the closest are the same. |
| **"He will not allow your foot to slip"** (121:3) | The road is dangerous — but the one who guards you is attentive to every step. |
| **"the Protector of Israel will neither slumber nor sleep"** (121:4) | The LORD is never absent, never off-duty, never distracted. |
| **"He will preserve your soul"** (121:7) | The protection goes deeper than physical safety — it reaches the soul. |
| **"Now and forevermore"** (121:8) | The watching does not end at the journey's end or at death. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

The Songs of Ascents (Psalms 120–134) were sung by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem for the great festivals. "Ascents" refers to the ascent to the city — Jerusalem sits on a hill and the road up was literal, long, and sometimes dangerous.

**"He will not allow your foot to slip"** (121:3) — the road to Jerusalem was rough mountain terrain. Slipping on the path was a literal danger. The promise was physical before it was metaphorical.

**"The moon by night"** (121:6) — ancient people believed the moon could cause harm (we still have the word "lunatic" from *luna*). The psalm takes the fears of the ancient world seriously and addresses them: neither sun nor moon will harm you under God's watch.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"The LORD will guard you from all evil"** (121:7) — But bad things happen to believers. Does the promise fail? The psalm is making a claim about ultimate protection — the soul, the whole person in God's hands — not a guarantee against all suffering. "He will preserve your soul" (121:7) points to the deeper thing: even when the foot slips, the soul is kept.

**"I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come?"** (121:1) — Some have read this as though the hills are the answer: "I look to the hills for help." But the psalm immediately answers its own question: help comes from the LORD who *made* the hills. The hills are the horizon, not the source.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The pilgrim | Asking | Long road, real danger — where does help come from? (121:1) |
| The pilgrim | Reassured | Another voice answers: He watches, He does not sleep, He keeps (121:3–5) |
| The reader | Accompanied | The LORD watches your coming and going — you are not traveling alone (121:8) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"You're on your own out there."*

**What's true:** The pilgrim was alone on a real road with real dangers.

**What the passage is mainly about:** You are not alone on the road. The Maker of heaven and earth is your keeper — personally, attentively, without sleeping. The psalm is addressed to the individual (*your* foot, *your* soul, *your* coming and going) — not to Israel as an abstract nation but to the person on the road right now.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the one who watched over His disciples' every coming and going. He is the good shepherd who guards the flock so that none are lost (John 10:28). And His promise to the disciples echoes Psalm 121: "I am with you always, to the end of the age" (Matthew 28:20). The watching that never stops is His.

## ✝️ Good News

**"The LORD will watch over your coming and going, both now and forevermore"** (121:8).

Not just the journey there. Not just this year. Every coming and every going, now and forevermore. You are not unwatched.

**Psalm 121: I lift my eyes to the hills — help comes from the LORD who made them. He does not sleep. He keeps your foot, your soul, your coming and going — now and forevermore.**

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It appoints Psalm 121 on the Second Sunday in Lent in Year A and at funerals and memorial services.†
