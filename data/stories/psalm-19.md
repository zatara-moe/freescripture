# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** David (19:1).

**What it is.** Two poems in one. The first half watches the sky preach without words. The second half reads God's word. Both say the same thing about God.

**When the church sings it.** On the Third Sunday of Lent in Year B and on several other Sundays in Ordinary Time, especially when the readings touch on God's word or wisdom.

**How to read it.** Watch the shift at verse 7: the sky speaks without words, and then the Law speaks in words. Creation and Scripture both reveal the same God.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Sky Preaches | 19:1-6 | The heavens tell of God's glory without a word |
| 2. The Law Is Perfect | 19:7-11 | God's word revives, gives wisdom, gives light |
| 3. Cleanse Me | 19:12-14 | A prayer for hidden and willful faults |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Sky Preaches

**"The heavens declare the glory of God; the skies proclaim the work of His hands"** (19:1). {v: 19:1}

Every day the sky makes its announcement. {v: 19:1}

**"Day after day they pour forth speech; night after night they reveal knowledge"** (19:2). {v: 19:2}

**"Without speech or language, without a sound to be heard,"** (19:3) **"their voice has gone out into all the earth, their words to the ends of the world"** (19:4). {v: 19:3, 19:4}

No words, yet heard everywhere. It is creation's silent sermon. {v: 19:3, 19:4}

In the sky, God pitched a tent for the sun. **"Like a bridegroom emerging from his chamber, like a champion rejoicing to run his course"** (19:5) — the sun rises and runs its full course, and **"nothing is deprived of its warmth"** (19:6). {v: 19:5, 19:6}

### Section 2: The Law Is Perfect

**"The Law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul; the testimony of the LORD is trustworthy, making wise the simple"** (19:7). {v: 19:7}

**"The precepts of the LORD are right, bringing joy to the heart; the commandments of the LORD are radiant, giving light to the eyes"** (19:8). {v: 19:8}

**"The fear of the LORD is pure, enduring forever; the judgments of the LORD are true, being altogether righteous"** (19:9). {v: 19:9}

**"They are more precious than gold, than much pure gold; they are sweeter than honey, than honey from the comb"** (19:10). {v: 19:10}

**"By them indeed Your servant is warned; in keeping them is great reward"** (19:11). {v: 19:11}

### Section 3: Cleanse Me

**"Who can discern his own errors? Cleanse me from my hidden faults"** (19:12). {v: 19:12}

A person cannot even see all their own failures. They need God's light to show them. {v: 19:12}

**"Keep Your servant also from willful sins; may they not rule over me. Then I will be blameless and cleansed of great transgression"** (19:13). {v: 19:13}

**"May the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be pleasing in Your sight, O LORD, my Rock and my Redeemer"** (19:14). {v: 19:14}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 19 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The heavens declare the glory of God"** (19:1) | Creation is a witness to its Creator. |
| **"Without a sound to be heard"** (19:3) | The sky preaches without words — anyone can see it. |
| **"The Law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul"** (19:7) | God's word does not just inform; it restores. |
| **"Sweeter than honey"** (19:10) | God's word is a delight, not a burden. |
| **"Hidden faults"** (19:12) | Sins the person cannot even see in themselves. |
| **"My Rock and my Redeemer"** (19:14) | God is steady and he buys back what was lost. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**"Making wise the simple"** (19:7) — the simple were ordinary, uneducated people. God's law was not reserved for scholars.

**"Honey from the comb"** — wild honey, just found, was the sweetest thing available. God's word beats it.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does the sky literally speak?** No. The point is that all creation silently points to its Maker — anyone who looks can see that something or someone made all this.

**Does this mean creation reveals everything about God?** No. It shows his power and glory. His words and his Son reveal his heart.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| David | Awed | The sky never stops speaking (19:2) |
| David | Delighted | God's word is sweeter than honey (19:10) |
| David | Humble | He can't see all his own errors (19:12) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"You can't know anything about God."*

**What's true:** God is greater than any human idea about him.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"The heavens declare the glory of God"** (19:1) and **"The Law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul"** (19:7). God has made himself known — in the sky above and in the words he gave.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the Word made flesh (John 1:14) — God's final and fullest speech. The Redeemer at the end of the psalm is the one who redeemed us on the cross.

## ✝️ Good News

God speaks.

**"The heavens declare the glory of God"** (19:1). **"The Law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul"** (19:7).

Creation and Scripture both point to him, and he sends his Son as the Word who saves.

**Psalm 19 hears the sky preaching God's glory and reads the Law as sweeter than honey. Both point to the God who speaks, and whose final word is Jesus.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said that creation is God's work, and that in it God reveals his power and wisdom. But to know God's heart — his mercy and grace — we need his word, and finally his Son.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 19 on the Third Sunday of Lent in Year B, the Third Sunday in Ordinary Time in Year C, and other Sundays in Ordinary Time.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The sky preaches without words, and God's word revives the soul. Both declare the glory of the same God — the Rock and Redeemer who sent Jesus as his final word.**
