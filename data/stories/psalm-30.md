# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** David (30:1).

**What it is.** A thank-you song. David was very sick, close to death. He cried to God, and God healed him. Now he can't stop praising.

**What it's about.** How quickly things can change: from feeling safe, to falling apart, to dancing.

**How to read it.** Watch for the opposites: anger and favor, night and morning, mourning and dancing.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. You Lifted Me Up | 30:1-3 | God healed David |
| 2. Joy in the Morning | 30:4-5 | Sorrow lasts a night |
| 3. When You Hid Your Face | 30:6-10 | David remembers how he cried out |
| 4. Mourning Into Dancing | 30:11-12 | God changed everything |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: You Lifted Me Up

**"I will exalt You, O LORD, for You have lifted me up"** (30:1). {v: 30:1}

To exalt means to lift up in praise. God lifted David, so David lifts up God. {v: 30:1}

**"O LORD my God, I cried to You for help, and You healed me"** (30:2). {v: 30:2}

God pulled him back from the edge of death. {v: 30:3}

### Section 2: Joy in the Morning

David invites everyone to sing with him. {v: 30:4}

**"For His anger is fleeting, but His favor lasts a lifetime"** (30:5). {v: 30:5}

Fleeting means it passes quickly. {v: 30:5}

**"Weeping may stay the night, but joy comes in the morning"** (30:5). {v: 30:5}

### Section 3: When You Hid Your Face

When life was good, David felt unshakable. {v: 30:6, 30:7}

**"When You hid Your face, I was dismayed"** (30:7). {v: 30:7}

So he begged God for mercy. If I die, he asked, who will praise you? {v: 30:8, 30:9}

**"Hear me, O LORD, and have mercy; O LORD, be my helper"** (30:10). {v: 30:10}

### Section 4: Mourning Into Dancing

**"You turned my mourning into dancing; You peeled off my sackcloth and clothed me with joy"** (30:11). {v: 30:11}

Sackcloth was rough clothing people wore when they grieved. {v: 30:11}

**"O LORD my God, I will give thanks forever"** (30:12). {v: 30:12}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 30 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"You have lifted me up"** (30:1) | God rescued David from the depths. |
| **"You healed me"** (30:2) | God answered his cry. |
| **"Weeping may stay the night"** (30:5) | Sorrow is real, but it doesn't last forever. |
| **"Joy comes in the morning"** (30:5) | God brings a new day. |
| **"When You hid Your face"** (30:7) | Times when God felt far away. |
| **"Mourning into dancing"** (30:11) | God turns sorrow into joy. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Sheol and the Pit** were names for the place of the dead.

**Sackcloth** was made of rough goat hair. Grieving people wore it and sat in ashes.

**The heading** says this was sung at the dedication of the temple (30:1).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does joy always come the next morning?** Not always the next day. The point is that sorrow is not the end. God's "morning" is coming, and Easter is that morning.

**Was God angry with David?** David felt God's anger in his sickness. But he discovered God's favor lasts far longer.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |

| David | Overconfident | He said, "I will never be shaken" (30:6) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If you're sad, you're not trusting God."*

**What's true:** God gives joy.

**What the passage is mainly about:** David wept. He felt God had hidden his face. He cried and begged. And God turned his mourning into dancing. Tears and faith go together.

**Where Jesus is:** He went down into death and rose on Easter morning. Because of him, joy comes in the morning.

## ✝️ Good News

The night is not forever.

**"Weeping may stay the night, but joy comes in the morning"** (30:5).

God lifts up, heals, and turns mourning into dancing.

**David cried to God from the edge of death, and God healed him. Weeping may stay the night, but joy comes in the morning.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said that God sometimes seems to hide his face, so that we will learn to cling to his promise and not to our feelings.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It sings Psalm 30 on the Third Sunday of Easter in Year C, and on several Sundays after the Epiphany and in summer in Years B and C.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**David cried for help, and God healed him. Weeping may stay the night, but joy comes in the morning. God turns mourning into dancing.**
