# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Abraham:** The man God called to leave his home. God promised to make his family a great nation (12:1-2).
- **Sarah:** Abraham's wife. She is about ninety and has never had a child.
- **Three visitors:** Three men who stop at Abraham's tent. One of them is the LORD.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- Abraham's tent by the great trees of Mamre, near Hebron, in the land of Canaan. Then a hilltop looking toward the city of Sodom.

**When**

- About 4,000 years ago, long before Moses or David.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**God made Abraham a promise.** He said Abraham's family would be as many as the stars (15:5).

**Years went by, and there was no baby.** Abraham was almost one hundred. Sarah was about ninety (17:17).

**God gave them new names.** Abram became Abraham, which means "father of many." Sarai became Sarah. God said Sarah would have a son (17:5, 17:15-16).

**Here's what you know that Sarah doesn't:** God has already promised her a son. She is about to hear it for herself.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Three Men at Noon

It was the hottest part of the day. Abraham was sitting at the entrance of his tent. {v: 18:1}

He looked up and saw three men standing nearby. {v: 18:2}

Abraham ran to meet them and bowed low to the ground. He begged them to stop and rest. {v: 18:2, 18:3}

He brought water to wash their feet. He said, "Rest under the tree, and I'll bring you a bit of bread." {v: 18:4, 18:5}

A "bit of bread" turned into a feast. Abraham hurried to Sarah and told her to bake bread. He ran to his herd and picked out a tender calf to cook. {v: 18:6, 18:7}

He served them meat, milk, and curds, and stood nearby under the tree while they ate. {v: 18:8}

### Scene 2: Sarah Laughs

**"Where is your wife Sarah?"** they asked (18:9). {v: 18:9}

**"There, in the tent,"** Abraham said (18:9). {v: 18:9}

Then the LORD said, **"I will surely return to you at this time next year, and your wife Sarah will have a son!"** (18:10). {v: 18:10}

Sarah was listening behind the tent door. She was far too old to have a baby. {v: 18:10, 18:11}

So she laughed to herself. {v: 18:12}

The LORD asked Abraham why Sarah laughed. Then he asked, **"Is anything too difficult for the LORD?"** (18:14). {v: 18:13, 18:14}

Sarah was afraid. **"I did not laugh,"** she said (18:15). {v: 18:15}

**"No,"** replied the LORD, **"but you did laugh"** (18:15). {v: 18:15}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture Sarah behind the tent flap, hand over her mouth. The visitor didn't see her. But he knew.

### Scene 3: Looking Toward Sodom

When the men got up to leave, they looked down toward the city of Sodom. Abraham walked with them. {v: 18:16}

The LORD said he would not hide his plans from Abraham. Through Abraham, all the nations of the earth would be blessed. {v: 18:17, 18:18}

Then the LORD said there was a great outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah. Their sin was very great. {v: 18:20}

An outcry is a loud cry for help or justice. People in those cities were being hurt.† {v: 18:20}

The LORD would go and see. {v: 18:21}

### Scene 4: Fifty, Forty-Five, Forty...

The two other men went on toward Sodom. But Abraham stayed, standing before the LORD. {v: 18:22}

Abraham came near and asked, **"Will You really sweep away the righteous with the wicked?"** (18:23). Righteous means people who do what is right. {v: 18:23}

What if there were fifty good people in the city? {v: 18:24}

**"Will not the Judge of all the earth do what is right?"** (18:25). {v: 18:25}

The LORD said, "If I find fifty, I will spare the whole city for their sake." {v: 18:26}

Abraham kept going. **"I am but dust and ashes"** (18:27), he said. What about forty-five? Forty? Thirty? Twenty? {v: 18:27, 18:28, 18:29, 18:30, 18:31}

Each time, the LORD said yes. {v: 18:28, 18:29, 18:30, 18:31}

Finally Abraham asked, **"Suppose ten are found there?"** (18:32). {v: 18:32}

**"On account of the ten, I will not destroy it"** (18:32). {v: 18:32}

Then the LORD left, and Abraham went home. {v: 18:33}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Genesis 18 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"He ran from the entrance of his tent to meet them"** (18:2) | Abraham welcomed strangers eagerly, in the heat of the day. |
| **"Your wife Sarah will have a son!"** (18:10) | God repeated his promise, this time with a date. |
| **"She laughed to herself"** (18:12) | It sounded impossible to her. |
| **"Is anything too difficult for the LORD?"** (18:14) | Nothing God promises is impossible for him. |
| **"Will not the Judge of all the earth do what is right?"** (18:25) | Abraham trusted that God is fair. |
| **"I am but dust and ashes"** (18:27) | Abraham knew he was small, and spoke to God anyway. |
| **"On account of the ten, I will not destroy it"** (18:32) | God was willing to spare a whole city for a few good people. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Welcoming travelers was a sacred duty.** In the desert, a traveler without water and shade could die. A host gave the best he had.†

**Three seahs of flour was a lot.** It was enough bread for a big crowd, not three men.†

**Sarah's son was named Isaac.** His name means "he laughs." When he was born, Sarah said God had brought her laughter (21:6).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was one of the visitors really God?** The story says **"the LORD appeared to Abraham"** (18:1). Some Christians believe this was the Son of God, before he was born as Jesus. Others see it as God speaking through his messengers.†

**Did Abraham change God's mind?** Abraham was learning how merciful God is. God invited him to ask. God's mercy was even greater than Abraham's asking.†

**Is it okay to argue with God?** Abraham spoke to God boldly, but humbly. The Bible is full of honest prayers. God wants us to bring him our questions and our concerns for others.

**What happened to Sodom?** Not even ten good people were found. God rescued Abraham's nephew Lot and his daughters before the city was destroyed (19:15-16, 19:29).

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Abraham | Eager | He ran to welcome the strangers (18:2) |
| Sarah | Doubtful | She laughed at the promise (18:12) |
| Sarah | Afraid | She was caught laughing (18:15) |
| Abraham | Concerned | He pleaded for the people of Sodom (18:23) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God is just waiting to punish people."*

**What's true:** God is a fair Judge. He hears the cry of people who are being hurt.

**What the story is mainly about:** God told Abraham his plans, and let him ask for mercy. God was willing to spare a whole city for the sake of ten good people. His heart leans toward mercy.

**Where Jesus is:** Abraham asked God to spare many for the sake of a few righteous people. Jesus is the one righteous man. For his sake, God spares and forgives everyone who trusts in him (Romans 5:19).†

## ✝️ Good News

Sarah laughed, because God's promise sounded impossible. God kept it anyway.

**"Is anything too difficult for the LORD?"** (18:14).

The next year, Isaac was born, and Sarah laughed for joy. Through Abraham's family, Jesus would come, the blessing for all nations. God keeps his promises, even when we doubt.

**Nothing is too difficult for the LORD. He keeps his promises, and his heart leans toward mercy.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved Abraham's prayer for Sodom. He said Abraham shows how believers can pray boldly, holding God to his own goodness and mercy.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads Genesis 18:1-10a in July (Lectionary 16), next to the story of Mary and Martha, and Genesis 18:20-32 the next week (Lectionary 17), next to Jesus teaching about prayer.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Three visitors brought Abraham and Sarah God's promise of a son. Nothing is too difficult for the LORD, and he loves to show mercy.**
