# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul, with his coworkers Silas (also called Silvanus) and Timothy, around AD 50 (1:1). It may be the oldest book in the New Testament.†

**Who it was for.** A young church in Thessalonica, a busy port city in Greece. Paul started it, but he had to leave suddenly when trouble broke out (Acts 17:1-10).

**What this letter is.** A warm, relieved letter. Paul had been worried about them. Then Timothy brought good news: they were standing firm. Paul thanks God, encourages them, and answers a painful question: what happens to believers who die before Jesus comes back?

**How to read it.** This page walks through all five short chapters. Notice how often Paul talks about Jesus's coming.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Faith, Love, and Hope

Paul always thanks God for them. {v: 1:1, 1:2}

He remembers **"your work of faith, your labor of love, and your enduring hope in our Lord Jesus Christ"** (1:3). {v: 1:3}

The good news came to them with power and the Holy Spirit. They received it with joy, even while suffering. {v: 1:4, 1:5, 1:6}

Now people everywhere are talking about their faith. {v: 1:7, 1:8}

They **"turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God"** (1:9). {v: 1:9}

And now they wait for Jesus, God's Son, to come from heaven. {v: 1:10}

### Section 2: Like a Mother and Father

Paul had been treated badly in Philippi, but he still came boldly to preach. {v: 2:1, 2:2}

He didn't use tricks or flattery or try to get money. {v: 2:3, 2:4, 2:5, 2:6}

**"On the contrary, we were gentle among you, like a nursing mother caring for her children"** (2:7). {v: 2:7}

**"We were delighted to share with you not only the gospel of God, but our own lives as well"** (2:8). {v: 2:8}

Paul worked night and day at his trade so he wouldn't be a burden. {v: 2:9, 2:10}

Like a father, he encouraged and comforted each one of them. {v: 2:11, 2:12}

They accepted his message **"not as the word of men, but as the true word of God"** (2:13). {v: 2:13}

### Section 3: Good News From Timothy

Paul had been torn away from them and longed to see them again. {v: 2:17, 2:18}

**"You are indeed our glory and our joy"** (2:20). {v: 2:19, 2:20}

He couldn't stand the worry, so he sent Timothy to check on them. {v: 3:1, 3:2, 3:3, 3:4, 3:5}

Timothy came back with good news about their faith and love. {v: 3:6, 3:7, 3:8}

**"How can we adequately thank God for you in return for our great joy over you in His presence?"** (3:9). {v: 3:9, 3:10, 3:11}

**"And may the Lord cause you to increase and overflow with love for one another and for everyone else"** (3:12). {v: 3:12, 3:13}

### Section 4: A Life That Pleases God

Paul urges them to keep living in a way that pleases God. {v: 4:1, 4:2}

God calls us to be holy, including in how we treat our bodies and other people. {v: 4:3, 4:4, 4:5, 4:6, 4:7, 4:8}

They already love one another. Keep growing in it. {v: 4:9, 4:10}

Live quietly, mind your own business, and work with your hands. {v: 4:11, 4:12}

### Section 5: Those Who Have Died

Some believers in Thessalonica had died. Their friends were afraid they had missed out. {v: 4:13}

**"Brothers, we do not want you to be uninformed about those who sleep in death, so that you will not grieve like the rest, who are without hope"** (4:13). {v: 4:13}

**"For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, we also believe that God will bring with Jesus those who have fallen asleep in Him"** (4:14). {v: 4:14, 4:15}

**"For the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a loud command, with the voice of an archangel, and with the trumpet of God, and the dead in Christ will be the first to rise"** (4:16). {v: 4:16}

Then all believers will be together with the Lord. **"And so we will always be with the Lord"** (4:17). {v: 4:17}

**"Therefore encourage one another with these words"** (4:18). {v: 4:18}

### Section 6: Children of the Day

No one knows when Jesus will come. **"The Day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night"** (5:2). {v: 5:1, 5:2, 5:3}

But believers belong to the light. So stay awake and ready. {v: 5:4, 5:5, 5:6, 5:7}

Put on faith and love like armor, and hope like a helmet. {v: 5:8}

**"For God has not appointed us to suffer wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ"** (5:9). {v: 5:9}

Jesus died for us, so whether we are alive or have died, we live with him. {v: 5:10}

**"Therefore encourage and build one another up, just as you are already doing"** (5:11). {v: 5:11}

### Section 7: Rejoice, Pray, Give Thanks

Respect your church leaders. Live at peace. Help the weak, and be patient with everyone. {v: 5:12, 5:13, 5:14, 5:15}

**"Rejoice at all times. Pray without ceasing. Give thanks in every circumstance, for this is God's will for you in Christ Jesus"** (5:16-18). {v: 5:16, 5:17, 5:18}

Don't put out the Spirit's fire. Test everything, and hold on to what is good. {v: 5:19, 5:20, 5:21, 5:22}

May God make you completely holy. {v: 5:23}

**"The One who calls you is faithful, and He will do it"** (5:24). {v: 5:24, 5:25, 5:26, 5:27, 5:28}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Thessalonians 1 to 5 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Your work of faith, your labor of love, and your enduring hope"** (1:3) | Faith, love, and hope show up in everyday life. |
| **"Turned to God from idols"** (1:9) | They left false gods for the real God. |
| **"Like a nursing mother"** (2:7) | Paul cared for them tenderly. |
| **"Those who sleep in death"** (4:13) | Believers who have died. For them, death is like sleep. |
| **"Not grieve like the rest, who are without hope"** (4:13) | Christians grieve, but with hope. |
| **"Like a thief in the night"** (5:2) | Jesus will come when no one expects. |
| **"Pray without ceasing"** (5:17) | Stay in constant conversation with God. |
| **"He will do it"** (5:24) | God will finish what he started in us. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Thessalonica** was a large city on a major Roman road. It's still a city today, Thessaloniki, in Greece.†

**Paul had to leave quickly** when a mob attacked the house where he was staying (Acts 17:5-10).

**Many people at that time** believed death was the end, with no hope of life after.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Are Christians not supposed to grieve?** Paul says we grieve differently, "not like the rest, who are without hope." Sadness is real. So is hope.

**What does "caught up in the clouds" mean?** Paul pictures Jesus's return, when all believers, living and dead, will be with him. Christians have imagined the details in different ways, but the promise is the same: "we will always be with the Lord."

**Can anyone pray all the time?** "Pray without ceasing" means keeping in touch with God throughout the day, not only at set times.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Strong Christians don't cry at funerals."*

**What's true:** Christians have real hope when someone dies in faith.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Paul doesn't say "don't grieve." He says don't grieve **"like the rest, who are without hope"** (4:13). Because Jesus died and rose, those who die in him will rise too.

**Where Jesus is:** He died and rose, and he will come again to gather all his people.

## ✝️ Good News

Death doesn't get the last word.

**"For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, we also believe that God will bring with Jesus those who have fallen asleep in Him"** (4:14).

Jesus is coming back. The dead in Christ will rise, and we will always be with the Lord. And the God who called you will finish his work.

**Because Jesus died and rose again, we do not grieve without hope. He will come again, and we will always be with the Lord. The One who calls you is faithful.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther often spoke of death for Christians as a sleep. He said that just as we lie down at night and wake in the morning, we will close our eyes in death and wake when Christ calls us.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads through 1 Thessalonians on five Sundays in October and November (Lectionary 29 to 33). In Year B, 1 Thessalonians 5:16-24 is read on the Third Sunday of Advent, and in Year C, 3:9-13 on the First Sunday of Advent. 4:13-18 is often read at funerals.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Faith, love, and hope show up in real life. Because Jesus died and rose again, we do not grieve without hope: he will come again, and we will always be with the Lord.**
