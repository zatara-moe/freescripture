# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Ezekiel was a priest who was taken to Babylon with the first group of exiles in 597 BC. There, by the Kebar River, God called him to be a prophet (1:1-3).

**What was happening.** God's people were far from home. Many blamed their parents for their troubles. Others felt so crushed by their sins that they had given up hope.

**What this passage is.** Four messages from Ezekiel: his call, a promise of a tiny shoot that becomes a great tree, a message about each person's responsibility, and God's job for Ezekiel as a watchman. One cry rings through all of it: turn and live.

**How to read it.** Listen for how often God says he takes no pleasure in anyone's death.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Stand Up, Son of Man | 2:1-7 | God sends Ezekiel to a stubborn people |
| 2. A Tender Shoot | 17:22-24 | God will plant a sprig that becomes a mighty cedar |
| 3. Sour Grapes | 18:1-4, 18:25-32 | Each person answers to God, and God says, "Repent and live!" |
| 4. The Watchman | 33:7-11 | Warn them, because God wants them to live |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Stand Up, Son of Man

**"Son of man,"** God said, **"stand up on your feet and I will speak to you"** (2:1). {v: 2:1}

"Son of man" here means "human being." God is reminding Ezekiel that he is just a man. {v: 2:1}

The Spirit entered Ezekiel and set him on his feet. {v: 2:2}

God sent him to a stubborn, rebellious people. {v: 2:3, 2:4}

**"And whether they listen or refuse to listen—for they are a rebellious house—they will know that a prophet has been among them"** (2:5). {v: 2:5}

**"But you, son of man, do not be afraid of them or their words"** (2:6). {v: 2:6, 2:7}

### Section 2: A Tender Shoot

God gives a picture. He will take a tiny sprig from the top of a cedar tree. {v: 17:22}

He will plant it on a high mountain in Israel. {v: 17:22, 17:23}

It will grow into **"a majestic cedar. Birds of every kind will nest under it"** (17:23). {v: 17:23}

**"I bring the tall tree down and make the low tree tall"** (17:24). {v: 17:24}

God does what no one expects. He lifts up the small and brings down the proud. {v: 17:24}

### Section 3: Sour Grapes

People had a saying: **"The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the teeth of the children are set on edge"** (18:2). {v: 18:1, 18:2}

It meant: "We're suffering for our parents' sins. It's not our fault." {v: 18:2}

God says to stop saying that. {v: 18:3}

**"Behold, every soul belongs to Me; both father and son are Mine"** (18:4). {v: 18:4}

Each person answers to God for their own life. {v: 18:4}

The people complain that God isn't fair. God says, "Is it my way that's unfair, or yours?" {v: 18:25, 18:26}

If a wicked person turns from evil and does what is right, he will live. {v: 18:27, 18:28}

**"Repent and turn from all your transgressions"** (18:30). {v: 18:29, 18:30}

Transgressions means sins. {v: 18:30}

**"Fashion for yourselves a new heart and a new spirit. Why should you die, O house of Israel?"** (18:31). {v: 18:31}

**"For I take no pleasure in anyone's death, declares the Lord GOD. So repent and live!"** (18:32). {v: 18:32}

### Section 4: The Watchman

**"As for you, O son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel"** (33:7). {v: 33:7}

A watchman stood on the city wall and blew a trumpet when he saw danger coming. {v: 33:7}

If Ezekiel doesn't warn people, he is responsible. If he warns them and they don't listen, he has done his job. {v: 33:8, 33:9}

The people cry out: **"Our transgressions and our sins are heavy upon us, and we are wasting away because of them! How can we live?"** (33:10). {v: 33:10}

God answers: **"I take no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but rather that the wicked should turn from their ways and live. Turn! Turn from your evil ways!"** (33:11). {v: 33:11}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Ezekiel 2:1-7; 17:22-24; 18:1-4, 25-32; 33:7-11 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Stand up on your feet"** (2:1) | God lifts up the person he calls. |
| **"Whether they listen or refuse to listen"** (2:5) | Ezekiel's job is to speak, not to make them listen. |
| **"I bring the tall tree down and make the low tree tall"** (17:24) | God lifts up the small and humbles the proud. |
| **"Every soul belongs to Me"** (18:4) | Every person matters to God. |
| **"A new heart and a new spirit"** (18:31) | God wants to change us from the inside. |
| **"I take no pleasure in anyone's death"** (18:32) | God wants everyone to live. |
| **"A watchman"** (33:7) | Someone who warns others of danger. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**The exiles in Babylon** lived far from Jerusalem. Many thought God had abandoned them.

**City walls** had watchtowers. Watchmen kept lookout day and night.†

**Cedars of Lebanon** were the tallest, most valuable trees people knew. They were used to build the temple (1 Kings 5:6).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Can we make ourselves a new heart?** God tells the people to do it, but later he promises, "I will give you a new heart" (36:26). It's God's gift, received when we turn to him.

**Does "the soul who sins will die" mean we're saved by being good?** No. God's point is that no one is stuck because of their parents, and anyone can turn and live. That turning is always toward God's mercy.

**Is the tender shoot about Jesus?** Christians have long seen it as a picture of the Messiah from David's family. Jesus told a similar parable about a mustard seed that grows into a tree where birds nest (Mark 4:30-32).

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ezekiel | Afraid | God tells him not to fear (2:6) |
| The exiles | Blaming | Their parents ate sour grapes (18:2) |
| The exiles | Crushed | Their sins were heavy on them (33:10) |
| God | Pleading | He wants them to turn and live (33:11) |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God is just waiting to punish people."*

**What's true:** God takes sin seriously.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"I take no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but rather that the wicked should turn from their ways and live"** (33:11). God's warnings are an invitation to come home.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the tender shoot from David's family, and he came to call sinners to turn and live.

## ✝️ Good News

God isn't hoping you fail. He's pleading with you to live.

**"For I take no pleasure in anyone's death, declares the Lord GOD. So repent and live!"** (18:32).

However heavy your sins feel, God's invitation stands: turn to him, and live.

**God sends warnings because he loves. He takes no pleasure in anyone's death, and he calls everyone: turn and live.**

## 📚 From Luther

The first of Luther's 95 Theses says that when Jesus said "Repent," he meant the whole life of believers to be one of repentance: turning back to God every day.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Ezekiel 33:7-11 (Lectionary 23) and 18:1-4, 25-32 (Lectionary 26) in September. In Year B, it reads 17:22-24 (Lectionary 11) and 2:1-5 (Lectionary 14) in June and July.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God sent Ezekiel as a watchman to a stubborn people. God takes no pleasure in anyone's death. He makes the low tree tall, and he calls everyone: turn and live.**
