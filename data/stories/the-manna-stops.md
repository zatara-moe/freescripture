# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Joshua led Israel into the promised land after forty years in the wilderness under Moses (Joshua 1:1-2).

**What was happening.** The Israelites had just crossed the Jordan into Canaan. They were camped at Gilgal, just inside the land. For forty years, God had fed them with manna — bread from heaven, day after day. Now, for the first time, they would eat from the land.

**What this passage is.** A moment of transition. The old provision ends. A new life begins. The shame of slavery is lifted. The wandering is over.

**How to read it.** Listen for what God says in verse 9. It sets the tone for everything else.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Reproach Is Rolled Away | 5:9 | God declares the shame of Egypt is gone |
| 2. The First Passover in the Land | 5:10-11 | Israel keeps Passover on Canaan's soil |
| 3. The Manna Stops | 5:12 | God's wilderness provision gives way to the land's abundance |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Reproach Is Rolled Away

The LORD said to Joshua, **"Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you"** (5:9). {v: 5:9}

The name Gilgal sounds like the Hebrew word for "to roll." God named the moment. The shame Egypt had placed on his people — slavery, degradation, powerlessness — is gone. {v: 5:9}

### Section 2: The First Passover in the Land

**"On the evening of the fourteenth day of the month, while the Israelites were camped at Gilgal on the plains of Jericho, they kept the Passover"** (5:10). {v: 5:10}

The first act in the new land was worship. Israel remembered the night God freed them from Egypt. They ate the Passover meal, this time on Canaan's soil. {v: 5:10}

**"The day after the Passover, on that very day, they ate unleavened bread and roasted grain from the produce of the land"** (5:11). {v: 5:11}

Real bread. From real ground. Their own land. {v: 5:11}

### Section 3: The Manna Stops

**"And the day after they had eaten from the produce of the land, the manna ceased. There was no more manna for the Israelites, so that year they began to eat the crops of the land of Canaan"** (5:12). {v: 5:12}

God's provision in the wilderness was complete. The manna had done its work. Now the land would feed his people. The story of the exodus was over. A new chapter had begun. {v: 5:12}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Joshua 5:9-12 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you"** (5:9) | God removes the shame of what was done to his people. |
| **"They kept the Passover"** (5:10) | In a new place, the old memory is honored. Gratitude comes before conquest. |
| **"The manna ceased"** (5:12) | God's provision changes form. The wilderness gift is replaced by the land's abundance. |
| **"They began to eat the crops of the land of Canaan"** (5:12) | The promise made to Abraham was now real enough to eat. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Manna** had sustained Israel for forty years in the desert — a daily miracle of bread on the ground (Exodus 16).

**Gilgal** became a significant place in Israel's early history in the land. Its name, associated with "rolling," became a permanent reminder of God's declaration.

**The Passover** commemorated the last night in Egypt, when God passed over the homes marked with lamb's blood and freed Israel from slavery (Exodus 12).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Why did the manna stop?** God gave exactly what was needed for each season. In the wilderness, the land could not provide. In Canaan, it could. The gift that was sufficient for one season is replaced by the gift sufficient for the next.

**Does "reproach of Egypt" mean only physical slavery?** The word covers shame, disgrace, and the contempt of others. God was declaring that Israel's identity was no longer defined by what Egypt had done to them.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God never changes his methods."*

**What's true:** God's character never changes — he is faithful in every season.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you"** (5:9). God marks the new season with a declaration of freedom. The provision changes because the need changes. His care does not stop.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus called himself the true bread from heaven — greater than the manna in the wilderness (John 6:32-35). He is the provision that does not cease when seasons change. He is what the manna was pointing to all along.

## ✝️ Good News

The shame you carry does not have to define you.

**"Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you"** (5:9).

God says today. He marks the moment. He rolls it away.

**Israel crossed the Jordan and God said: the shame is gone. The manna served its purpose. Now eat from the land I promised.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther saw the Passover in Canaan as a picture of faith: Israel had to enter the land before they could eat its food. Trust must come before the provision becomes visible. The manna that sustained them through the wilderness was God training them to trust his word.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, Joshua 5:9-12 is read on the Fourth Sunday in Lent, alongside Jesus's parable of the Prodigal Son. Both speak of a son returning home, shame lifted, a feast prepared.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God rolled away the shame of Egypt, kept the Passover in the promised land, and stopped the manna. The wilderness was over. A new life had begun.**
