# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **King David:** The shepherd boy God chose to be king of Israel. Now he lives in a palace.
- **Nathan:** A prophet who advises David.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.

**Setting**

- David's palace in Jerusalem.

**When**

- About 3,000 years ago, after David became king.†

## 🧭 Before the Story

**David was anointed as a shepherd boy.** God chose him over his older brothers (1 Samuel 16:12-13).

**David had become king.** He captured Jerusalem and made it his capital (5:6-7).

**David brought the ark of God to Jerusalem.** The ark was a sacred chest that showed God's presence with his people. David danced with joy before it (6:14-15). But the ark was kept in a tent.

## 🗺️ Map of the Story

| Scene | Verses | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A house of cedar | 7:1-3 | David wants to build God a house |
| 2. "Are you the one?" | 7:4-7 | God tells Nathan something surprising |
| 3. "I took you from the pasture" | 7:8-11 | God reminds David where he came from |
| 4. A house forever | 7:11-17 | God promises to build David a house |

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: A House of Cedar

David was settled in his palace. God had given him rest from his enemies. {v: 7:1}

David said to Nathan the prophet, **"Here I am, living in a house of cedar, while the ark of God remains in a tent"** (7:2). {v: 7:2}

Cedar was expensive, beautiful wood. David wanted to build God a house, a temple. {v: 7:2}

**"Go and do all that is in your heart, for the LORD is with you,"** Nathan said (7:3). {v: 7:3}

### Scene 2: "Are You the One?"

But that night, God spoke to Nathan. {v: 7:4}

**"Are you the one to build for Me a house to dwell in?"** (7:5). {v: 7:5}

God reminded David that he had always moved around with his people in a tent. He had never asked anyone to build him a house. {v: 7:6, 7:7}

### Scene 3: "I Took You From the Pasture"

God told Nathan what to say to David. {v: 7:8}

**"I took you from the pasture, from following the flock, to be the ruler over My people Israel"** (7:8). {v: 7:8}

**"I have been with you wherever you have gone"** (7:9). {v: 7:9}

God would make David's name great. He would give his people a safe home. {v: 7:9, 7:10}

### Scene 4: A House Forever

Then the surprise. David wanted to build God a house. Instead, **"He Himself will establish a house for you"** (7:11). {v: 7:11}

This "house" means a family, a royal line of kings. {v: 7:11}

God promised that one of David's sons would build the temple, and that God would be his Father. {v: 7:12, 7:13, 7:14}

**"Your house and kingdom will endure forever before Me, and your throne will be established forever"** (7:16). {v: 7:16}

Nathan told David everything. {v: 7:17}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture David sitting very still as Nathan speaks. He came to give God something. Instead, God is giving him a promise bigger than he can imagine.

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

2 Samuel 7:1-17 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Living in a house of cedar"** (7:2) | David wanted to honor God with a temple. |
| **"Are you the one to build for Me a house"** (7:5) | God didn't need David to do something for him. |
| **"I took you from the pasture"** (7:8) | Everything David had came from God. |
| **"He Himself will establish a house for you"** (7:11) | God would build David a family of kings. |
| **"I will be his Father, and he will be My son"** (7:14) | God would treat David's son as his own child. |
| **"Your throne will be established forever"** (7:16) | God promised an everlasting kingdom. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**David's son Solomon built the temple.** It was finished around 960 BC (1 Kings 6:38).†

**"House" has two meanings.** It can mean a building or a family. God plays on both.

**This promise is called the Davidic covenant.** The prophets kept pointing back to it (Isaiah 9:7, Jeremiah 23:5).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Was it wrong for David to want to build a temple?** No. David's heart was good. God had a different plan: David's son would build it (1 Chronicles 22:8-10).

**Did David's family rule forever?** Kings from David's family ruled for about 400 years. Then Babylon ended their kingdom. But God's promise came true in Jesus, born in David's family, whose kingdom has no end (Luke 1:32-33).

**Does God need anything from us?** No. God gives first. We give back out of thanks.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| David | Grateful | He wanted to honor God (7:2) |
| Nathan | Surprised | God changed the plan (7:4-5) |
| David | Amazed | God promised him a forever kingdom (7:16) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What does this story say to you?** Tap one.

- God gives first :: David wanted to give God a house. God gave David more.
- Where I came from :: God took David from the sheep. He knows where you started too.
- God's plan is different :: Sometimes God says "not that, but something better."
- Forever :: God's promises last forever.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"We need to do great things for God."*

**What's true:** Serving God is good. David's desire to honor God was good.

**What the story is mainly about:** God doesn't need us to build him anything. God built David a house, a family that would last forever. God is the giver.

**Where Jesus is:** The angel told Mary that God would give her son **"the throne of His father David"** (Luke 1:32). Jesus is the King from David's house whose kingdom never ends.

## ✝️ Good News

David wanted to do something for God. God promised to do something greater for David.

**"Your house and kingdom will endure forever before Me, and your throne will be established forever"** (7:16).

Jesus, born from David's family, is that forever King.

**David wanted to build God a house. Instead, God promised to build David a house, a kingdom that would last forever. Jesus is its King.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that God always comes to us first with his promises. We don't build our way up to God. God comes down to us with his gifts.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads 2 Samuel 7:1-11 and 16 on the Fourth Sunday of Advent, next to the angel telling Mary her son will have David's throne.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**David wanted to build God a house. God promised to build David a house, a family of kings whose throne would last forever. Jesus is that King.**
