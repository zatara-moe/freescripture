# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Jesus:** The one the Gospels present as the promised Messiah and the Son of God. People often call him Teacher.
- **An expert in the law:** A teacher of Moses's law. He asks Jesus a question to test him.
- **In Jesus's story:** A hurt traveler, a priest, a Levite, a Samaritan, and an innkeeper.

**Setting**

- Jesus is teaching on his way to Jerusalem. His story takes place on the road from Jerusalem down to Jericho.

**When**

- During Jesus's work, about 2,000 years ago.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**This is a parable.** A parable is a short story Jesus told to teach something. The people in it are made up, but the point is real.

**Jewish people and Samaritans did not get along.** They had distrusted each other for hundreds of years (John 4:9).

**An expert asks a big question.** He wants to know how to have eternal life. Eternal life means life with God that never ends.

**Here's what you know that almost nobody in this story knows:** Jesus's story has a hero. The hero will be the person the listeners least expected.

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: A Test Question

One day an expert in the law stood up to test Jesus. **"Teacher,"** he asked, **"what must I do to inherit eternal life?"** (10:25). {v: 10:25}

Jesus answered with a question. He asked what was written in the Law, and how the expert read it. {v: 10:26}

The expert knew the answer. He quoted two lines from Moses's law. The first line says to love God with all your heart, soul, strength, and mind. This means loving God with everything you are. The second line says, **"Love your neighbor as yourself"** (10:27). {v: 10:27}

Jesus told him he was right. Jesus said that if he did this, he would live. {v: 10:28}

### Scene 2: Who Is My Neighbor?

The expert wanted to justify himself. That means he wanted to show he was in the right. So he asked another question. **"And who is my neighbor?"** (10:29). {v: 10:29}

One likely reason for his question: he wanted to know how far his love had to go. He wanted to know who counted as his neighbor and who did not. {v: 10:29}

Jesus answered with a story. {v: 10:30}

### Scene 3: The Road to Jericho

A man was walking from Jerusalem down to Jericho. {v: 10:30}

Jesus doesn't describe the road. But his listeners knew it. It dropped steeply through rocky desert, and robbers hid along it. {v: 10:30}

Robbers attacked him. They took his clothes and beat him. Then they left him by the road, half dead. That means badly hurt, close to dying. {v: 10:30}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Picture a hot, empty road winding through bare hills. A man lies still in the dust beside it.

### Scene 4: Two Men Walk By

A priest happened to come down the same road. Priests led worship at God's temple in Jerusalem. {v: 10:31}

The priest saw the hurt man. He moved to the other side of the road and kept walking. {v: 10:31}

Then a Levite came to the same spot. Levites helped at the temple. He saw the man too. He also crossed to the other side and kept walking. {v: 10:32}

The Bible doesn't say why they passed by. Jesus does not tell us. The story is not about their reasons. {v: 10:31, 10:32}

### Scene 5: The Samaritan Stops

Then a Samaritan came along the road. Jewish people and Samaritans did not get along. {v: 10:33}

The Samaritan saw the man. He felt compassion. Compassion means caring deeply about someone who hurts. {v: 10:33}

He went to the man. He poured oil and wine on the man's wounds and bandaged them. Back then, oil and wine were used as medicine. {v: 10:34}

The Samaritan had an animal with him. He put the hurt man on it. He took the man to an inn and took care of him there. {v: 10:34}

The next day, the Samaritan gave the innkeeper two silver coins. That was about two days' pay. He told the innkeeper to take care of the man. He promised to pay any extra cost when he came back. {v: 10:35}

### Scene 6: Jesus Turns the Question

Then Jesus asked the expert a question. **"Which of these three do you think was a neighbor to the man who fell into the hands of robbers?"** (10:36). {v: 10:36}

The expert answered, **"The one who showed him mercy"** (10:37). He did not say "the Samaritan." {v: 10:37}

Jesus told him, **"Go and do likewise"** (10:37). Likewise means the same way. {v: 10:37}

The expert had asked, "Who is my neighbor?" Jesus changed the question. He asked who acted like a neighbor. So the question for the expert became: Will I be a neighbor? {v: 10:29, 10:36}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Luke 10:25-37 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Love your neighbor as yourself"** (10:27) | Care about other people as much as you care about yourself. The expert was quoting Moses's law (Leviticus 19:18). |
| **"Wanting to justify himself"** (10:29) | He wanted to look right, or to find a limit on who he had to love. |
| **"He passed by on the other side"** (10:31) | He saw the need and chose not to stop. |
| **"Had compassion"** (10:33) | He cared deeply about the hurt man, and that caring made him help. |
| **"Go and do likewise"** (10:37) | Be a neighbor to anyone who needs help, even someone you were taught to avoid. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Samaritans and Jewish people were enemies.** They disagreed about where to worship God, and they had distrusted each other for centuries (John 4:9).

**Two denarii was about two days' pay.** A denarius was a normal day's wage for a worker (Matthew 20:2).

**The road to Jericho dropped steeply.** It wound through rocky desert, and it was known for robbers. (Easton's Bible Dictionary)

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is this a true event?** It is a parable. Jesus made up the story to teach something true. We don't need to know the traveler's name.

**Is Jesus saying priests are bad?** No. Jesus does not say why they passed by. It's okay to notice things like this. The point is who stopped, and who we will be.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The expert | The Bible doesn't say | He came to test Jesus (10:25). That is what he did, not how he felt. |
| The expert | Wanting to look right | He asked a second question to justify himself (10:29) |
| The Samaritan | Compassion | He saw a man badly hurt (10:33) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**Who are you in this story today?** Tap one.

- The hurt traveler :: Then this story is for you first. Jesus stops for people who can't help themselves.
- Someone walking past :: Everyone walks past sometimes. Jesus doesn't shame the expert. He invites him to be a neighbor.
- The one who stops :: Keep helping. Mercy is one of the ways God's love reaches people. If someone is badly hurt, getting an adult or calling 911 is helping too.
- The expert, looking for a limit :: Jesus doesn't give a list of who counts. Mercy helps without first checking who counts.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"The Good Samaritan means: be nice to strangers."*

**What's true:** Jesus does say, "Go and do likewise." Kindness to strangers matters.

**What the story is mainly about:** Jesus changes the question. The expert asked, "Who counts as my neighbor?" Jesus asks, "Will you be a neighbor?" And the hero is the one person the listeners looked down on.

**Where Jesus is:** From the early church to Martin Luther, many Christians have seen Jesus in the Samaritan. This is a way of reading the story, not something Jesus says in it. In that reading, we are the hurt traveler. Jesus came to us, cared for us, and paid what it cost.†

## ✝️ Good News

Before anyone can "go and do likewise," Jesus comes to them first. Here is the good news in the story's own picture. **Jesus crossed the road to reach us when we could not help ourselves.**†

## 📚 From Luther

Martin Luther preached on this parable (a sermon for the 13th Sunday after Trinity, printed in his *Church Postil*). He taught that Christ is the true Samaritan, who binds up our wounds. This is a comparison. Then we go and do the same for others.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow a reading plan called the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads Luke 10:25-37 in mid-July of Year C (Lectionary 15).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God's love reaches everyone, even enemies, and Jesus comes to rescue us when we cannot help ourselves.**
