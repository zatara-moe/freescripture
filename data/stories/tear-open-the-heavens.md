# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who it's for.** God's people after the exile. They had come home, but Jerusalem and the temple were still in ruins. God seemed far away.†

**What this passage is.** A prayer, one of the most honest in the Bible. The people remember God's love in the past, confess their sin, and beg God to come down.

**How to read it.** Hear it as a cry from people who are waiting for God. That's why the church prays it at the start of Advent, the season of waiting for Jesus.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Remembering God's Love | 63:7-9 | God carried his people in the past |
| 2. Tear Open the Heavens | 64:1-4 | A cry for God to come down |
| 3. Like Filthy Rags | 64:5-7 | An honest confession of sin |
| 4. You Are the Potter | 64:8-9 | We belong to God, our Father |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Remembering God's Love

**"I will make known the LORD's loving devotion and His praiseworthy acts"** (63:7). {v: 63:7}

God said, "They are my people." **"So He became their Savior"** (63:8). {v: 63:8}

**"In all their distress, He too was afflicted"** (63:9). When they suffered, God suffered with them. {v: 63:9}

**"He lifted them up and carried them all the days of old"** (63:9). {v: 63:9}

### Section 2: Tear Open the Heavens

Now the people pray. {v: 64:1}

**"If only You would rend the heavens and come down, so that mountains would quake at Your presence"** (64:1). {v: 64:1}

To rend means to tear open. They want God to rip open the sky and come. {v: 64:1}

They remember times God did amazing things they didn't expect. {v: 64:3}

**"No eye has seen any God besides You, who acts on behalf of those who wait for Him"** (64:4). {v: 64:4}

### Section 3: Like Filthy Rags

Then they confess. **"Surely You were angry, for we sinned"** (64:5). {v: 64:5}

**"All our righteous acts are like filthy rags"** (64:6). Even the good things they did were stained. {v: 64:6}

**"We all wither like a leaf"** (64:6). Like dry leaves, their sins blew them away. {v: 64:6}

No one was calling on God anymore. {v: 64:7}

### Section 4: You Are the Potter

Then comes a "but." {v: 64:8}

**"But now, O LORD, You are our Father; we are the clay, and You are the potter; we are all the work of Your hand"** (64:8). {v: 64:8}

A potter shapes clay with his hands. Clay can't shape itself. {v: 64:8}

**"Do not remember our iniquity forever. Oh, look upon us, we pray; we are all Your people!"** (64:9). {v: 64:9}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> When Jesus was baptized, Mark says he saw **"the heavens breaking open"** (Mark 1:10). The prayer was answered.

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 63:7-9 and 64:1-9 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"In all their distress, He too was afflicted"** (63:9) | God shares the pain of his people. |
| **"He lifted them up and carried them"** (63:9) | God carried his people like a parent. |
| **"Rend the heavens and come down"** (64:1) | A cry for God to come close and act. |
| **"All our righteous acts are like filthy rags"** (64:6) | Even our best isn't clean enough on its own. |
| **"You are our Father"** (64:8) | The people still belong to God. |
| **"We are the clay, and You are the potter"** (64:8) | God can reshape us. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Potters were common.** People used clay pots for cooking, storage, and water. Jeremiah also watched a potter reshape clay (Jeremiah 18:1-6).

**The temple lay in ruins** for many years after the exile, until it was rebuilt around 515 BC (Ezra 6:15).†

**Advent means "coming."** The church prays this prayer as it waits to celebrate Jesus's coming at Christmas, and his coming again.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Are my good works worthless?** Good works help our neighbors, and God loves them. But they can't make us clean before God. Only God's forgiveness can.

**Does God hide his face?** Sometimes God feels far away. The people tell God honestly how it feels. God is still listening.

**Can I pray like this?** Yes. Honest prayer, even "Where are you, God?", is part of the Bible.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |

| The people | Desperate | They wanted God to come down (64:1) |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What would you pray today?** Tap one.

- Come down :: "Tear open the heavens." Tell God you need him.
- I'm sorry :: Like the people, you can confess honestly.
- Shape me :: "We are the clay." Ask God to shape you.
- Thank you :: Remember how God has carried you.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If I try hard enough, I can be good enough for God."*

**What's true:** God wants us to do good.

**What the passage is mainly about:** The people admit that even their best is like filthy rags. They don't promise to try harder. They ask God, their Father and potter, to come down and save them.

**Where Jesus is:** God answered this prayer. He tore open the heavens and came down in Jesus.

## ✝️ Good News

The people cried, "Come down!" God did.

**"But now, O LORD, You are our Father; we are the clay, and You are the potter"** (64:8).

In Jesus, God came down. He shares our pain, forgives our sins, and shapes us into his people.

**The people begged God to tear open the heavens and come down. He did, in Jesus. We are the clay, and he is the potter.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that our righteousness is like filthy rags, but Christ covers us with his own. Isaiah 64:6 was one of the verses he used to show we are saved by grace.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Isaiah 64:1-9 on the First Sunday of Advent. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 63:7-9 on the First Sunday of Christmas.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**"Tear open the heavens and come down!" The people confessed their sin and called on God their Father, the potter. He came down, in Jesus.**
