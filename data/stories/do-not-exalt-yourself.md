# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** The collection in Proverbs 25 is attributed to Solomon but copied by the scribes of King Hezekiah (25:1).

**What it is.** A two-verse proverb about honor, humility, and the danger of reaching for status you have not been given.

**When the church reads it.** On the Twenty-Second Sunday after Pentecost in Year C, alongside Jesus's story about where to sit at a dinner party.

**How to read it.** It is practical wisdom. It is also a pointer to something deeper about how God's kingdom works.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Don't Reach | 25:6 | Do not push yourself into the seat of honor |
| 2. Better to Be Called Up | 25:7 | True honor comes when someone greater invites you |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Don't Reach

**"Do not exalt yourself in the presence of the king, and do not stand in the place of great men"** (25:6). {v: 25:6}

The proverb opens with a picture: someone at court, trying to move closer to the powerful, claiming a place they have not been given. The advice is simple: don't. {v: 25:6}

### Section 2: Better to Be Called Up

**"for it is better that he says to you... than that you should be demoted in the presence of the prince"** (25:7). {v: 25:7}

There are two ways to end up at the head of the table. You can push your way there and be sent back down — publicly. Or you can wait and be invited up. The second is worth more than the first. {v: 25:7}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Jesus told a very similar story. When invited to a feast, he said, take the lowest seat — so that your host might say, "Come up higher" (Luke 14:10). He ended with: "For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted" (Luke 14:11).

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Proverbs 25:6-7 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Do not exalt yourself in the presence of the king"** (25:6) | Status grasped is not the same as status given. |
| **"Do not stand in the place of great men"** (25:6) | Placing yourself among the honored is not the same as being honored. |
| **"It is better... than that you should be demoted in the presence of the prince"** (25:7) | Genuine honor comes from outside you, not from yourself. |
| **"Than that you should be demoted in the presence of the prince"** (25:7) | The cost of overreaching is humiliation. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Royal courts** in ancient Israel were formal places with clear hierarchies. Where you stood or sat signaled your rank. To take a higher place uninvited was not just awkward — it was an insult to the king and could carry consequences.

**Hezekiah's scribes** collected Solomon's proverbs centuries after they were first spoken, suggesting this wisdom was considered valuable enough to preserve and pass on (25:1).

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is this just career advice?** On the surface, yes. But the proverb uses the court as a picture of something larger: God's kingdom also honors those who do not grasp at honor. It is wisdom for how power and status actually work — in any setting.

**Does humility mean never taking initiative?** The proverb is not about passivity. It is about where honor comes from. True status is recognized, not claimed.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The ambitious | Warned | Reaching for the seat can end in public shame (25:7) |
| The humble | Encouraged | The invitation "Come up here!" is real and worth waiting for |

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What does this proverb say to you?** Tap one.

- I work hard for recognition that never comes :: Honor given is worth more than honor claimed.
- I'm tempted to oversell myself :: Wait for the invitation rather than taking the seat.
- I've been publicly embarrassed :: The proverb understands the sting of being sent back down.
- I want to live differently :: Start in the low place. Let someone else lift you.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"You have to promote yourself — no one else will do it for you."*

**What's true:** Taking initiative and advocating for yourself is sometimes necessary.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"It is better... than that you should be demoted in the presence of the prince"** (25:7). Honor that is given carries weight. Honor that is seized can be taken away just as quickly.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus told this proverb as a story (Luke 14:7-11) and added: "For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted." He himself is the one who came to serve, was crucified in the lowest place, and was raised to the highest — not by his own claiming, but by the Father's word.

## ✝️ Good News

You do not have to fight for your place.

**"For it is better that he says to you... than that you should be demoted in the presence of the prince"** (25:7).

There is One who lifts the humble. He did it for his Son. He does it for those who trust him.

**Don't push for the seat of honor. Wait for the invitation. The one who gives it has authority to make it stick.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther saw the proverb as a picture of the gospel itself: sinners who try to earn their place before God are like guests claiming a seat they were not given. But God calls the undeserving to "Come up here" — not because of what they brought, but because of grace.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, Proverbs 25:6-7 is read on the Twenty-Second Sunday after Pentecost (Lectionary 22), alongside Jesus's parable of the dinner guests in Luke 14.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Don't claim the seat of honor. Wait for the invitation. True honor is given, not taken.**
