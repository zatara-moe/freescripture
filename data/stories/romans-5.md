# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul wrote this letter to the Christians in Rome around AD 57.

**What came before.** Paul showed that God makes people right with him through faith, just as he did with Abraham (4:3). Jesus **"was delivered over to death for our trespasses and was raised to life for our justification"** (4:25).

**What this chapter is.** What being made right with God means for our lives: peace, hope, and love. Then Paul compares Adam and Christ.

**A word to know.** "Justified" means declared right with God.

## 🗺️ Map of the Letter

| Section | Verses | What it says |

| 2. Suffering to Hope | 5:3-5 | Suffering can grow hope |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: Peace With God

**"Therefore, since we have been justified through faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ"** (5:1). {v: 5:1}

Peace with God means the war is over. We are no longer God's enemies. {v: 5:1}

Through Jesus, we stand in God's grace, and we rejoice in hope. {v: 5:2}

### Section 2: Suffering to Hope

Paul says something surprising. **"We also rejoice in our sufferings"** (5:3). {v: 5:3}

Not because suffering is good. Because of what God grows through it. {v: 5:3}

**"Suffering produces perseverance; perseverance, character; and character, hope"** (5:3-4). Perseverance means not giving up. {v: 5:3, 5:4}

**"And hope does not disappoint us, because God has poured out His love into our hearts through the Holy Spirit"** (5:5). {v: 5:5}

### Section 3: While We Were Still Sinners

**"For at just the right time, while we were still powerless, Christ died for the ungodly"** (5:6). {v: 5:6}

People rarely die for a good person. {v: 5:7}

**"But God proves His love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us"** (5:8). {v: 5:8}

Jesus didn't wait for us to get better. He died for us at our worst. {v: 5:8}

If God loved us when we were his enemies, how much more will he save us now that we are his friends! {v: 5:9, 5:10, 5:11}

### Section 4: Adam and Christ

Sin entered the world through one man, Adam. Death came with it, and spread to everyone. {v: 5:12}

But Jesus is greater. {v: 5:15}

**"But the gift is not like the trespass"** (5:15). Adam's sin brought death. Christ's gift brings much more: grace and life. {v: 5:15, 5:16, 5:17}

**"For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the one man the many will be made righteous"** (5:19). {v: 5:18, 5:19}

**"But where sin increased, grace increased all the more"** (5:20). {v: 5:20}

Grace is always bigger than sin. {v: 5:20, 5:21}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Romans 5 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"We have peace with God"** (5:1) | Through Jesus, the war between us and God is over. |
| **"Suffering produces perseverance"** (5:3) | God can grow strength in hard times. |
| **"Hope does not disappoint us"** (5:5) | God's love is real. Our hope in him isn't wasted. |
| **"While we were still sinners, Christ died for us"** (5:8) | God loved us before we changed. |
| **"The gift is not like the trespass"** (5:15) | Christ's gift is far greater than Adam's sin. |
| **"Grace increased all the more"** (5:20) | No sin is bigger than God's grace. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Adam's story is in Genesis 3.** He and Eve disobeyed God, and death entered the world.

**Paul calls Adam "a pattern of the One to come"** (5:14). Adam affected everyone. So does Christ, for good.

**Romans was written to a church of Jews and non-Jews.** Paul shows that everyone is in the same place before God: sinners, saved by grace.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Should I be happy when I suffer?** Paul doesn't say suffering feels good. He says we can rejoice because God brings hope out of it. It's okay to be sad. If suffering is too much, talk to someone you trust.

**If grace is bigger than sin, should I sin more?** Paul answers that in the next chapter: **"Certainly not!"** (6:2). Grace sets us free from sin, not free to sin.

**Is everyone guilty because of Adam?** Paul says sin and death spread to everyone. And he says Christ's gift spreads even further.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Clean up your life first, then God will love you."*

**What's true:** God wants to change our lives.

**What the passage is mainly about:** **"While we were still sinners, Christ died for us"** (5:8). God loved us first, at our worst. Change comes after, as his gift.

**Where Jesus is:** Dying for the ungodly at just the right time, the one man whose obedience makes many righteous.

## ✝️ Good News

God didn't wait for us to be good.

**"But God proves His love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us"** (5:8).

Through Jesus, we have peace with God. His love is poured into our hearts. And where sin increased, grace increased even more.

**Justified by faith, we have peace with God. Christ died for us while we were still sinners, and God's grace is greater than all our sin.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that Christians are at the same time saints and sinners. We still sin, but in Christ we are declared righteous, and grace is always greater than our sin.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Romans 5:12-19 on the First Sunday in Lent, Romans 5:1-11 on the Third Sunday in Lent, and Romans 5:1-8 in June (Lectionary 11). In Year C, it reads Romans 5:1-5 on Holy Trinity Sunday.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Justified by faith, we have peace with God. While we were still sinners, Christ died for us, and where sin increased, grace increased all the more.**
