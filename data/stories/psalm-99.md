# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Anonymous.

**What it is.** A coronation psalm for the King of kings — God reigns, and the nations tremble.

**What it's about.** The LORD reigns over Zion, over all nations, over all history. He answered Moses, Aaron, and Samuel. He is holy. Holy. Holy.

**When the church sings it.** On Transfiguration Sunday and Sundays in Ordinary Time.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The LORD Reigns | 99:1–3 | Nations tremble — He is great in Zion, holy |
| 2. He Loves Justice | 99:4–5 | The King loves justice; exalt the LORD |
| 3. He Answered Them | 99:6–9 | He answered Moses, Aaron, Samuel; He is holy |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The LORD Reigns

**"The LORD reigns; let the nations tremble! He is enthroned above the cherubim; let the earth quake!"** (99:1). {v: 99:1}

God enthroned above the cherubim — above the ark of the covenant, the place of divine presence. The nations shake at this. {v: 99:1}

**"Great is the LORD in Zion; He is exalted above all the peoples"** (99:2). {v: 99:2}

**"Let them praise Your great and awesome name— He is holy"** (99:3). {v: 99:3}

The first refrain. Holy — set apart, utterly other than everything else. {v: 99:3}

### Section 2: He Loves Justice

**"The mighty King loves justice. You have established equity; You have exercised justice and righteousness in Jacob"** (99:4). {v: 99:4}

The King's power is not arbitrary. It is directed by love of justice. Equity and righteousness are what His power produces. {v: 99:4}

**"Exalt the LORD our God, and worship at His footstool; He is holy!"** (99:5). {v: 99:5}

The second refrain. Exalt Him — and worship. Both. {v: 99:5}

### Section 3: He Answered Them

**"Moses and Aaron were among His priests; Samuel was among those who called on His name. They called to the LORD and He answered."** (99:6). {v: 99:6}

Three names: Moses, Aaron, Samuel. Three people who called on God — and were answered. {v: 99:6}

**"He spoke to them from the pillar of cloud; they kept His decrees and the statutes He gave them."** (99:7). {v: 99:7}

The cloud — God's veiled presence. He spoke from concealment and they listened. {v: 99:7}

**"O LORD our God, You answered them. You were a forgiving God to them, yet an avenger of their misdeeds."** (99:8). {v: 99:8}

Both: forgiving and just. Moses struck the rock and was barred from the land. Aaron made the golden calf. Samuel's sons were corrupt. God forgave — and consequences remained. {v: 99:8}

**"Exalt the LORD our God and worship at His holy mountain, for the LORD our God is holy."** (99:9). {v: 99:9}

The third and fullest refrain. Exalt and worship — because He is holy. {v: 99:9}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 99 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The LORD reigns"** (99:1) | The first statement is a fact, not a wish. He already reigns — the nations need to catch up to it. |
| **"He is enthroned above the cherubim"** (99:1) | The ark of the covenant was God's throne in the tabernacle and temple. He is present and ruling. |
| **"The mighty King loves justice"** (99:4) | Power without justice is tyranny. God's power is directed by love of what is right. |
| **"yet an avenger of their misdeeds"** (99:8) | Forgiveness and accountability coexist in God's dealing with His people. |
| **"He is holy"** (99:3, 99:5, 99:9) | The refrain said three times — the structure of the psalm itself proclaims His holiness. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

"Holy, holy, holy" was the angelic cry Isaiah heard in his vision (Isaiah 6:3) and what the living creatures cry in John's vision (Revelation 4:8). Psalm 99's threefold "He is holy" (99:3, 99:5, 99:9) catches the same pattern: total, unreserved holiness.

**"enthroned above the cherubim"** (99:1) — the ark of the covenant had two golden cherubim with wings spread over the mercy seat. God was said to be enthroned there (1 Samuel 4:4). When this psalm says He reigns above the cherubim, it is using the specific geography of Israel's worship.

Moses struck the rock twice in anger (Numbers 20) — and was barred from entering Canaan. Aaron made the golden calf (Exodus 32). Samuel's sons "turned aside after dishonest gain and accepted bribes" (1 Samuel 8:3). All three were forgiven, loved, used mightily — and all three faced consequences for their failures.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"He is holy"** — said three times, once at the end of each section. Is this an accident? No — the number three signals completeness in Hebrew poetry. The structure of the psalm is the message: He is wholly holy.

**"yet an avenger of their misdeeds"** (99:8) alongside "You were a forgiving God" — How can both be true? The same way both are true in any serious relationship: forgiveness restores the relationship but does not always eliminate the consequences. Moses and Aaron died before the land. The forgiveness was real; so was the cost.

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What does this psalm speak into?** Tap one.

- I wonder who is really in charge :: The LORD reigns. The nations tremble. He is enthroned above all.
- I'm not sure God hears me :: He answered Moses, Aaron, and Samuel. He hears those who call.
- I've sinned and I'm not sure if I'm forgiven :: He was a forgiving God to them — and they had done serious things.
- I want to worship but feel unworthy :: Bow at His footstool. Exalt Him. His holiness is what draws you near, not your worthiness.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"If God is forgiving, then what we do doesn't really matter."*

**What's true:** God forgave Moses, Aaron, and Samuel.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Forgiveness and consequences coexisted for all three. God's forgiveness is real and complete — and His justice is also real. "A forgiving God... yet an avenger of their misdeeds." He is not indifferent to what His people do. His love is what makes Him just, not what makes Him ignore wrong.

**Where Jesus is:** He is the holy one this psalm proclaims — "the Holy One of God" (John 6:69). He is also the one whose death absorbs the punishment for misdeeds, so that forgiveness can be given without justice being set aside (Romans 3:26). The holiness of God and the mercy of God meet at the cross.

## ✝️ Good News

**"O LORD our God, You answered them"** (99:8).

They called and He answered — from the cloud, through the centuries. He still answers. He is the LORD our God. He is holy.

**Psalm 99: the LORD reigns — let the nations tremble. He loves justice. He answered Moses, Aaron, and Samuel. He forgave and He was just. Exalt Him, for He is holy.**

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It appoints Psalm 99 on Transfiguration Sunday in Year A and on other Sundays in Ordinary Time.†
