# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Anonymous.

**What it is.** A personal thanksgiving — someone who was near death, called on the LORD, and was delivered. Now they are fulfilling their vows in the congregation.

**What it's about.** I love the LORD, because He heard my voice. He saved me from death. How can I repay? I will lift the cup of salvation and call on His name.

**When the church sings it.** On Sundays in Ordinary Time and at communion.

## 🗺️ Map of the Psalm

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. I Love the LORD | 116:1–4 | He heard me — so I will call on Him as long as I live |
| 2. The LORD Delivered Me | 116:5–11 | He is gracious and righteous; He saved my soul |
| 3. What Shall I Render? | 116:12–19 | I will lift the cup of salvation; I will pay my vows |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: I Love the LORD

**"I love the LORD, for He has heard my voice— my appeal for mercy."** (116:1). {v: 116:1}

The psalm opens with love — not duty, not obligation. He heard, so I love Him. {v: 116:1}

**"Because He has inclined His ear to me, I will call on Him as long as I live."** (116:2). {v: 116:2}

The response is permanent: I will call on Him *as long as I live*. One answered prayer shapes a whole life. {v: 116:2}

**"The ropes of death entangled me; the anguish of Sheol overcame me; I was confronted by trouble and sorrow."** (116:3). {v: 116:3}

The danger was real. This is not ordinary trouble — it was the edge of death. {v: 116:3}

Then I called on the name of the LORD: **"O LORD, deliver my soul!"** (116:4). {v: 116:4}

The shortest prayer. And it was heard. {v: 116:4}

### Section 2: The LORD Delivered Me

**"The LORD is gracious and righteous; our God is full of compassion."** (116:5). {v: 116:5}

The testimony leads to a statement of who God is. He saved the psalmist because of His character, not the psalmist's merit. {v: 116:5}

**"The LORD preserves the simplehearted; I was helpless, and He saved me."** (116:6). {v: 116:6}

The simplehearted — those without resources, without power, without sophistication. God's protection runs toward the vulnerable. {v: 116:6}

**"Return to your rest, O my soul, for the LORD has been good to you."** (116:7). {v: 116:7}

The psalmist speaks to their own soul. Rest — because the crisis is over and God was faithful. {v: 116:7}

**"For You have delivered my soul from death, my eyes from tears, my feet from stumbling"** (116:8). {v: 116:8}

Three deliverances: death, tears, stumbling. Complete rescue. {v: 116:8}

**"I will walk before the LORD in the land of the living"** (116:9). {v: 116:9}

Alive. Still here. Walking before God. That is the gift. {v: 116:9}

I believed, therefore I said, **"I am greatly afflicted."** (116:10). {v: 116:10, 116:11}

Paul quotes this verse in 2 Corinthians 4:13. Belief and affliction are not opposites. {v: 116:10}

### Section 3: What Shall I Render?

**"How can I repay the LORD for all His goodness to me?"** (116:12). {v: 116:12}

The question is not rhetorical. He gives an answer. {v: 116:12}

**"I will lift the cup of salvation and call on the name of the LORD."** (116:13). {v: 116:13}

The cup of salvation — lifted as an offering. The same hand that was delivered now holds the cup up in gratitude. {v: 116:13}

**"I will fulfill my vows to the LORD in the presence of all His people."** (116:14). {v: 116:14}

Not in private. In the congregation. Testimony is communal. {v: 116:14}

**"Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints"** (116:15). {v: 116:15}

Even the death of those who love God does not happen carelessly before Him. Each death is seen. Each one matters. {v: 116:15}

**"Truly, O LORD, I am Your servant; I am Your servant, the son of Your maidservant; You have broken my bonds."** (116:16). {v: 116:16}

Servant — three times in one breath. The psalmist belongs to God, and God freed him. {v: 116:16}

**"I will offer to You a sacrifice of thanksgiving and call on the name of the LORD."** (116:17). {v: 116:17}

**"I will fulfill my vows to the LORD in the presence of all His people, in the courts of the LORD's house, in your midst, O Jerusalem. Hallelujah!"** (116:18–19). {v: 116:18, 116:19}

The psalm ends in the temple courts, before the congregation, with praise. The private prayer that was heard becomes public testimony. {v: 116:18, 116:19}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Psalm 116 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"He has inclined His ear to me"** (116:2) | God bends toward those who cry out — He is not a distant deity. |
| **"The LORD preserves the simplehearted"** (116:6) | God's protection runs toward the vulnerable, not the powerful. |
| **"Return to your rest, O my soul"** (116:7) | After a crisis, the soul needs to be told that it is safe to rest. |
| **"Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints"** (116:15) | No death of God's people is incidental or unnoticed by Him. |
| **"I will lift the cup of salvation"** (116:13) | The response to being rescued is to hold the rescue up publicly — to let others see what God has done. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

The Hallel psalms (113–118) were sung at Passover. Psalm 116 would have been part of what Jesus and His disciples sang at the Last Supper — "when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives" (Matthew 26:30). The "cup of salvation" (116:13) takes on new meaning in that context.

**"Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints"** (116:15) — the Hebrew *yaqar* means costly, weighty, of great value. The word is used for precious stones. God does not treat the death of His people as cheap or casual.

I believed, therefore I said, **"I am greatly afflicted."** (116:10) — Paul quotes this in 2 Corinthians 4:13 to describe the early Christians: we speak because we believe, even in suffering. Faith and the acknowledgment of affliction exist together.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**"Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints"** (116:15) — Does God want His saints to die? No — the verse says their death is *precious* (costly, weighty) to Him. It is the opposite of careless: God takes their death very seriously. This is a comfort in the face of mortality, not a fatalistic statement.

**"The cup of salvation"** (116:13) — at the Last Supper, Jesus takes a cup and says it is "the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20). He was singing Psalm 116. The cup He lifted was literally the cup of salvation — and the rescue He was about to accomplish would make the psalmist's survival look like a shadow of the real thing.

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

- I'm in a crisis and need to cry out :: O LORD, deliver my soul. That prayer was heard.
- I'm on the other side of something hard :: Return to your rest, my soul. The LORD has dealt bountifully with you.

- I wonder if my life matters to God :: Precious in His sight is the death of His saints. He is not indifferent to what happens to you.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Faith means you don't admit when things are hard."*

**What's true:** The psalmist openly says "I am greatly afflicted" (116:10).

**What the passage is mainly about:** Faith and honest acknowledgment of suffering are not opposites. "I believed, even when I said, 'I am greatly afflicted.'" The psalm holds both: real danger, real prayer, real deliverance, and then public, grateful testimony. You can tell God how bad it is — and keep believing.

**Where Jesus is:** He sang this psalm at the Last Supper, on the night before His death. The "cup of salvation" He lifted was the Passover cup — and the cup of suffering He was about to drink. He was near death and called on His Father. On the third day, He walked before the LORD "in the land of the living" — the first to do so permanently.

## ✝️ Good News

**"I love the LORD, for He has heard my voice— my appeal for mercy."** (116:1).

He heard. He inclined His ear. He saved. The answer to "What shall I render?" is not a list — it is a lifted cup, a called name, and a testimony in the congregation.

**Psalm 116: I love the LORD — He heard me at the edge of death. He is gracious and merciful. He delivered my soul, my eyes, my feet. What can I give? I will lift the cup of salvation and call on His name.**

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. It appoints Psalm 116 on Maundy Thursday in all years and on other Sundays in Ordinary Time.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**Psalm 116: I love the LORD because He heard my cry at the edge of death. He is gracious, righteous, and merciful. He delivered my soul. Precious in His sight is the death of His saints. I will lift the cup of salvation and pay my vows in the congregation.**
