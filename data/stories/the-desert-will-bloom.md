# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** The book is named for Isaiah, a prophet in Jerusalem (Isaiah 1:1).

**What comes before.** Chapter 34 is a dark picture of God's judgment on nations that opposed him. The land becomes a desert full of thorns and wild animals (34:13).†

**What this passage is.** Chapter 35 turns everything around. The desert bursts into flowers. It is a poem of joy and homecoming.

**How to read it.** Read it out loud. Notice everything that changes: dry to wet, weak to strong, blind to seeing, sad to singing.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Desert Will Rejoice | 35:1-2 | Dry land bursts into bloom |
| 2. Be Strong, Do Not Fear | 35:3-4 | God is coming to save |
| 3. The Blind Will See | 35:5-7 | Bodies and land are healed |
| 4. The Way of Holiness | 35:8-10 | A safe road home, full of singing |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Desert Will Rejoice

**"The wilderness and the land will be glad; the desert will rejoice and blossom like a rose"** (35:1). {v: 35:1}

Imagine a dry, cracked desert suddenly covered in flowers. {v: 35:1}

It will be as green as Lebanon's forests and Carmel's hills. {v: 35:2}

**"They will see the glory of the LORD, the splendor of our God"** (35:2). {v: 35:2}

### Section 2: Be Strong, Do Not Fear

**"Strengthen the limp hands and steady the feeble knees!"** (35:3). {v: 35:3}

Limp hands and shaky knees are what happens when people are scared or tired. {v: 35:3}

Say to those with anxious hearts, **"Be strong, do not fear! Behold, your God will come"** (35:4). {v: 35:4}

God will come to set things right and to save his people. {v: 35:4}

### Section 3: The Blind Will See

**"Then the eyes of the blind will be opened and the ears of the deaf unstopped"** (35:5). {v: 35:5}

**"Then the lame will leap like a deer and the mute tongue will shout for joy"** (35:6). Mute means unable to speak. {v: 35:6}

Water will gush out in the desert. Dry ground will become pools and springs. {v: 35:6, 35:7}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> When John the Baptist asked if Jesus was the one, Jesus pointed to these very signs: **"The blind receive sight, the lame walk... the deaf hear"** (Matthew 11:5).

### Section 4: The Way of Holiness

A highway will run through the desert. **"And there will be a highway called the Way of Holiness"** (35:8). {v: 35:8}

No lions or wild animals will be on it. It will be safe. {v: 35:9}

**"But the redeemed will walk upon it"** (35:9). The redeemed are people God has set free. {v: 35:9}

**"So the redeemed of the LORD will return and enter Zion with singing, crowned with everlasting joy. Gladness and joy will overtake them, and sorrow and sighing will flee"** (35:10). {v: 35:10}

Zion means Jerusalem, God's city. {v: 35:10}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 35 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"The desert will rejoice and blossom"** (35:1) | God can make life where there was none. |
| **"Strengthen the limp hands"** (35:3) | Encourage people who are tired and scared. |
| **"Be strong, do not fear!"** (35:4) | God is coming, so there is hope. |
| **"The eyes of the blind will be opened"** (35:5) | God will heal what is broken. |
| **"The Way of Holiness"** (35:8) | God makes a safe road home for his people. |
| **"Sorrow and sighing will flee"** (35:10) | One day, sadness will run away for good. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Israel is mostly dry.** In the desert to the south, rain is rare. A blooming desert was a picture of a miracle.†

**Lebanon, Carmel, and Sharon were famous for being green.** Lebanon had great cedar forests. Carmel and Sharon had rich pastures and flowers.†

**Exile meant a long journey through the desert.** People taken to Babylon would have to cross harsh land to come home.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does God heal every disability now?** Jesus healed many people as signs of his kingdom. Not everyone is healed in this life. Isaiah's promise will be fully true when God makes all things new.†

**Does a disability mean something is wrong with a person?** No. Every person is made in God's image and loved by him. This promise is about God ending all suffering and pain.

**"God will come with vengeance." Is that scary?** For people who are hurt and afraid, it is good news. God will come to set things right and to save them.†

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What part of you feels like a desert?** Tap one.

- Tired :: "Strengthen the limp hands." God gives strength.
- Anxious :: "Be strong, do not fear! Your God will come."
- Sad :: One day, sorrow and sighing will flee.
- I need healing :: Jesus brought these signs. He cares for your body and heart.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Some places, and some people, are just dry and dead."*

**What's true:** Some seasons of life feel like a desert.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God makes deserts bloom. He strengthens the weak, heals the broken, and brings his people home singing. When God comes, nothing stays the same.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus opened blind eyes and made the lame walk. Those were the signs Isaiah promised. He is the God who came to save.

## ✝️ Good News

The desert was dry. God made it bloom.

**"Be strong, do not fear! Behold, your God will come"** (35:4).

God came in Jesus. He healed people and opened a way home to God. One day he will make everything new, and sorrow will run away.

**God turns deserts into gardens and sorrow into singing. In Jesus, our God has come to save.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther taught that God's promises are for people who feel weak and afraid, not for the strong. The gospel is God's word to anxious hearts: "Be strong, do not fear."†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 35:1-10 on the Third Sunday of Advent, next to Jesus's answer to John the Baptist. In Year B, it reads Isaiah 35:4-7a in September (Lectionary 23).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**The desert will bloom. Be strong, do not fear: your God will come to save, heal, and bring his people home singing.**
