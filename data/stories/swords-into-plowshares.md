# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Isaiah was a prophet in Jerusalem, about 740 to 700 BC (Isaiah 1:1). A prophet is a messenger who speaks for God.

**What was happening.** Chapter 1 is a hard message. God's people had turned away from him. Their worship was empty, and they didn't care for the poor (1:16-17).†

**What this passage is.** A bright vision right after that hard message. Isaiah looks ahead to "the last days," when God will gather every nation and end war.

**How to read it.** The same promise, almost word for word, is also in Micah 4:1-3.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Mountain of the LORD | 2:1-2 | God's house will be the highest mountain |
| 2. Nations Streaming In | 2:3 | People of every nation come to learn God's ways |
| 3. Swords Into Plowshares | 2:4 | War will end |
| 4. Walk in the Light | 2:5 | God's people are invited to start now |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: The Mountain of the LORD

**"In the last days the mountain of the house of the LORD will be established as the chief of the mountains"** (2:2). {v: 2:1, 2:2}

The house of the LORD was the temple in Jerusalem, on a hill called Mount Zion. It wasn't the tallest hill around. {v: 2:2}

But one day, Isaiah says, it will be the highest of all. **"All nations will stream to it"** (2:2). {v: 2:2}

This is a comparison. God's house will be the most important place in the world. {v: 2:2}

### Section 2: Nations Streaming In

People from many nations will say, **"Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD"** (2:3). {v: 2:3}

Why? **"He will teach us His ways so that we may walk in His paths"** (2:3). {v: 2:3}

Rivers usually flow down. Here, people flow *up* the mountain, drawn to God. {v: 2:3}

### Section 3: Swords Into Plowshares

God will settle arguments between nations. {v: 2:4}

**"They will beat their swords into plowshares and their spears into pruning hooks"** (2:4). {v: 2:4}

A plowshare is the metal blade of a plow, used for farming. A pruning hook is a curved knife for trimming vines. Weapons will become farm tools. {v: 2:4}

**"Nation will no longer take up the sword against nation, nor train anymore for war"** (2:4). {v: 2:4}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Near the United Nations building in New York, there's a statue of a man beating a sword into a plowshare. These words are carved on the wall across the street.†

### Section 4: Walk in the Light

Isaiah ends with an invitation. **"Come, O house of Jacob, let us walk in the light of the LORD"** (2:5). {v: 2:5}

The house of Jacob means God's people. They don't have to wait for the last days. They can start walking in God's light now. {v: 2:5}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Isaiah 2:1-5 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"In the last days"** (2:2) | The time when God finishes what he started. |
| **"All nations will stream to it"** (2:2) | God's welcome is for every people. |
| **"He will teach us His ways"** (2:3) | People will come wanting to learn from God. |
| **"Swords into plowshares"** (2:4) | Weapons become tools that grow food. |
| **"Nor train anymore for war"** (2:4) | Peace will be so complete, no one needs armies. |
| **"Let us walk in the light of the LORD"** (2:5) | God's people can live in his peace today. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Isaiah lived in a time of war.** Assyria, a powerful empire, was attacking nations all around Israel.†

**Swords and plows were made of the same metal.** Reshaping one into the other took heat and hammering, real work.†

**The prophet Joel turned it around.** In a call to battle, Joel said, "Beat your plowshares into swords" (Joel 3:10). Isaiah's promise is the opposite.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Does this mean Christians can't be soldiers?** Christians have answered this differently. Isaiah is describing God's final peace, which only God can bring.†

**When are "the last days"?** The New Testament says the last days began when Jesus came (Hebrews 1:2). They will be complete when he returns.†

**Will all nations really come?** Jesus sent his followers to make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19). People of every nation are coming to God now.

# Why It Matters

## 🙋 Your Turn

**What would you turn into a plowshare?** Tap one.

- Angry words :: Words can hurt or help. You can choose to build up.
- A fight :: Is there a fight you could end today?
- Fear :: God's peace is coming. You can trust him now.
- Walk in light :: Isaiah invites you to start living God's peace today.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"There will always be war. Peace is impossible."*

**What's true:** The world has always had war. People can't make perfect peace on their own.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God himself promises to end war. He will teach the nations, and they will turn weapons into tools. His peace is coming.

**Where Jesus is:** Jesus is the Prince of Peace. He told Peter to put away his sword (Matthew 26:52), and he made peace through the cross.

## ✝️ Good News

People can't end war on their own. God promises he will.

**"They will beat their swords into plowshares and their spears into pruning hooks"** (2:4).

In Jesus, God's peace has begun. One day it will fill the whole earth.

**God will gather every nation, teach them his ways, and end war. Let us walk in the light of the LORD.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther said that Christ's kingdom is a kingdom of peace, spread by the word, not by the sword.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year A, it reads Isaiah 2:1-5 on the First Sunday of Advent, the start of the church year.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**One day, all nations will stream to God and beat their swords into plowshares. Come, let us walk in the light of the LORD.**
