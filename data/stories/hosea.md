# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who he was.** Hosea was a prophet in Israel, the northern kingdom, around 750 to 720 BC (1:1).

**What was happening.** Israel was worshiping other gods, especially Baal, a god people thought brought rain and good harvests. They still said they belonged to the LORD, but their hearts were somewhere else.

**What this book is.** God asked Hosea to marry a woman named Gomer, who would be unfaithful to him (1:2-3). Hosea's broken marriage became a living picture of God's broken heart over his people. And Hosea's faithful love became a picture of God's.

**How to read it.** This is a love story about a God who won't give up. Listen for his tender words.

## 🗺️ Map of the Message

| Section | Verses | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A Marriage as a Message | 1:2-3 | Hosea's marriage pictures God and his people |
| 2. I Will Speak Tenderly | 2:14-23 | God woos his people back |
| 3. Come, Let Us Return | 5:15 to 6:6 | God wants mercy, not sacrifice |
| 4. How Could I Give You Up? | 11:1-9 | God's love for his child |

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: A Marriage as a Message

God tells Hosea to marry a woman who will be unfaithful. Hosea's marriage will show how God's people have been unfaithful to him. {v: 1:2}

**"So Hosea went and married Gomer daughter of Diblaim"** (1:3). {v: 1:3}

They had children, and God gave them names that were sad warnings. {v: 1:3}

### Section 2: I Will Speak Tenderly

God's people had run after other gods. But instead of giving up, God makes a plan to win them back. {v: 2:14}

**"Therefore, behold, I will allure her and lead her to the wilderness, and speak to her tenderly"** (2:14). {v: 2:14}

Allure means to attract or woo. {v: 2:14}

God will turn the Valley of Achor, a valley of trouble, **"into a gateway of hope"** (2:15). {v: 2:15}

They will call God "my Husband," not "my Master." {v: 2:16, 2:17}

God will end war and let them live in safety. {v: 2:18}

**"So I will betroth you to Me forever; I will betroth you in righteousness and justice, in loving devotion and compassion"** (2:19). {v: 2:19}

Betroth means to promise to marry. {v: 2:19}

**"And I will betroth you in faithfulness, and you will know the LORD"** (2:20). {v: 2:20, 2:21, 2:22}

Hosea's children had been named "No Compassion" and "Not My People." God will change it. {v: 2:23}

He will say to "Not My People," **"You are My people,"** and they will say, **"You are my God"** (2:23). {v: 2:23}

### Section 3: Come, Let Us Return

God says he will wait until the people admit their guilt and look for him. {v: 5:15}

**"Come, let us return to the LORD. For He has torn us to pieces, but He will heal us; He has wounded us, but He will bind up our wounds"** (6:1). {v: 6:1}

**"On the third day He will raise us up, that we may live in His presence"** (6:2). {v: 6:2}

**"Let us press on to know the LORD"** (6:3). {v: 6:3}

But God says their love is like morning mist. It disappears. {v: 6:4, 6:5}

**"For I desire mercy, not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings"** (6:6). {v: 6:6}

> 🧵 **Follow the thread**
> Jesus quoted Hosea 6:6 twice: when he ate with sinners (Matthew 9:13) and when he defended his hungry disciples (Matthew 12:7).

### Section 4: How Could I Give You Up?

Now God speaks like a parent. {v: 11:1}

**"When Israel was a child, I loved him, and out of Egypt I called My son"** (11:1). {v: 11:1}

But the more God called, the farther they went. {v: 11:2}

**"It was I who taught Ephraim to walk, taking them in My arms"** (11:3). {v: 11:3}

Ephraim is another name for Israel. {v: 11:3}

**"I led them with cords of kindness, with ropes of love"** (11:4). God bent down to feed them. {v: 11:4}

They refused to turn back, and trouble was coming. {v: 11:5, 11:6, 11:7}

Then God's heart breaks open. {v: 11:8}

**"How could I give you up, O Ephraim? How could I surrender you, O Israel?"** (11:8). {v: 11:8}

**"My heart is turned within Me; My compassion is stirred!"** (11:8). {v: 11:8}

**"For I am God and not man"** (11:9), God says. He is **"the Holy One among you"** (11:9), **"and I will not come in wrath"** (11:9). {v: 11:9}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Hosea 1:2-3; 2:14-23; 5:15 to 6:6; 11:1-9 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Speak to her tenderly"** (2:14) | God wins his people back with love, not force. |
| **"A gateway of hope"** (2:15) | God turns places of trouble into new beginnings. |
| **"I will betroth you to Me forever"** (2:19) | God's love is a lifelong promise. |
| **"You are My people"** (2:23) | God claims people who had no claim on him. |
| **"I desire mercy, not sacrifice"** (6:6) | God wants real love more than religious acts. |
| **"Cords of kindness"** (11:4) | God leads with gentleness. |
| **"How could I give you up, O Ephraim?"** (11:8) | God can't bear to abandon his people. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Baal** was a Canaanite god. People believed he controlled rain and crops.

**Marriage** in the Bible is often a picture of God's covenant with his people. Unfaithfulness to God is called adultery.

**Assyria** destroyed the northern kingdom of Israel in 722 BC, not long after Hosea spoke.

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Did God really tell Hosea to marry an unfaithful woman?** That's what the book says. Christians have understood it in different ways. Either way, Hosea's marriage was meant to show God's pain, and his faithful love.

**Does "on the third day He will raise us up" mean Jesus?** Hosea was speaking about Israel. Christians have long heard an echo of Jesus, who rose on the third day.

**Does God change his mind?** God's anger is real. But Hosea shows that God's deepest heart is compassion.

## 💭 How People Feel

| Who | Feeling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hosea | Betrayed | His wife was unfaithful |
| God | Heartbroken | His people ran from him (11:2) |
| God | Tender | He speaks tenderly to them (2:14) |
| God | Compassionate | His heart is turned within him (11:8) |

# Why It Matters

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"God puts up with us, but he's mostly angry."*

**What's true:** God is grieved and angry when his people turn away.

**What the passage is mainly about:** God's heart overflows with love. **"How could I give you up, O Ephraim?"** (11:8). He woos his people back like a faithful husband and carries them like a loving parent.

**Where Jesus is:** He is God's faithful love in person. He ate with sinners and quoted Hosea: "I desire mercy, not sacrifice."

## ✝️ Good News

God won't give you up.

**"So I will betroth you to Me forever; I will betroth you in righteousness and justice, in loving devotion and compassion"** (2:19).

Even when we wander, God keeps loving, keeps calling, and keeps his promise.

**God's people were unfaithful, but God stayed faithful. He speaks tenderly, calls them back, and says, "How could I give you up?"**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther described faith as a marriage between Christ and the believer. Christ, the bridegroom, takes the bride's sin as his own and gives her his righteousness. He called it a "joyful exchange."†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year B, it reads Hosea 2:14-20 on the Eighth Sunday after the Epiphany. In Year A, it reads 5:15 to 6:6 in June (Lectionary 10), alongside Jesus's call of Matthew. In Year C, it reads passages from Hosea 1 and 11 in July.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God's people were unfaithful, but God stayed faithful like a loving husband and parent. He desires mercy, not sacrifice, and his heart cries out, "How could I give you up?"**
