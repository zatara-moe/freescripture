# The Story

## 🧭 Before You Read

**Who wrote it.** Paul wrote this letter to churches he had started in Galatia, a region in what is now central Turkey. It may be one of his earliest letters.†

**What was happening.** After Paul left, other teachers arrived. They said that non-Jewish believers had to be circumcised and keep the law of Moses to really belong to God. Paul was alarmed. If that were true, Jesus wasn't enough.

**What this letter is.** Paul's most urgent letter. He skips the usual thank-you and goes straight to the point: we are made right with God by faith in Christ, not by keeping the law. And people set free should use their freedom to love.

**How to read it.** This page walks through the whole letter, six short chapters, picking up the key passages in each.

## 📖 Section by Section

### Section 1: No Other Gospel

Paul begins by saying Jesus **"gave Himself for our sins to rescue us from the present evil age"** (1:4). {v: 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5}

Then, no thank-you. Just alarm. {v: 1:6}

**"I am amazed how quickly you are deserting the One who called you by the grace of Christ and are turning to a different gospel"** (1:6). {v: 1:6}

It isn't even a gospel. Someone is twisting the good news. {v: 1:7}

Paul says that even if an angel preached a different gospel, don't listen. {v: 1:8, 1:9}

He isn't trying to please people. He got this message from Jesus himself. {v: 1:10, 1:11, 1:12}

### Section 2: From Persecutor to Preacher

Paul reminds them who he used to be. He attacked the church and tried to destroy it. {v: 1:13, 1:14}

**"But when God, who set me apart from my mother's womb and called me by His grace, was pleased to reveal His Son in me so that I might preach Him among the Gentiles"** (1:15-16). {v: 1:15, 1:16}

He went to Arabia, then Damascus, and only after three years did he meet Peter in Jerusalem. {v: 1:17, 1:18, 1:19, 1:20, 1:21, 1:22}

People said, **"The man who formerly persecuted us is now preaching the faith he once tried to destroy."** And they praised God (1:23-24). {v: 1:23, 1:24}

### Section 3: Christ Lives in Me

Paul says that even Jewish believers like him and Peter know this. {v: 2:15}

**"A man is not justified by works of the law, but by faith in Jesus Christ"** (2:16). {v: 2:16}

Then come some of Paul's most personal words. {v: 2:19}

**"I have been crucified with Christ, and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself up for me"** (2:20). {v: 2:20}

If we could be right with God by keeping the law, Jesus died for nothing. {v: 2:21}

### Section 4: All One in Christ

The law was like a guardian that watched over children until they grew up. It led us to Christ. {v: 3:23, 3:24, 3:25}

**"You are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus. For all of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ"** (3:26-27). {v: 3:26, 3:27}

**"There is neither Jew nor Greek, slave nor free, male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus"** (3:28). {v: 3:28}

Everyone who belongs to Christ is part of Abraham's family and shares God's promise. {v: 3:29}

### Section 5: When the Time Had Fully Come

**"But when the time had fully come, God sent His Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those under the law, that we might receive our adoption as sons"** (4:4-5). {v: 4:4, 4:5}

God sent his Spirit into our hearts, so we call God **"Abba, Father!"** (4:6). {v: 4:6}

**"So you are no longer a slave, but a son; and since you are a son, you are also an heir through God"** (4:7). {v: 4:7}

An heir is someone who will receive everything the family owns. {v: 4:7}

### Section 6: Free to Love

**"It is for freedom that Christ has set us free. Stand firm, then, and do not be encumbered once more by a yoke of slavery"** (5:1). {v: 5:1}

A yoke was a wooden bar put on animals to make them pull loads. {v: 5:1}

Freedom isn't a chance to do whatever we want. **"Rather, serve one another in love"** (5:13). {v: 5:13}

The whole law comes down to this: **"Love your neighbor as yourself"** (5:14). {v: 5:14, 5:15}

**"So I say, walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh"** (5:16). {v: 5:16, 5:17, 5:18}

"The flesh" means our selfish nature. Paul lists what it produces: hatred, jealousy, rage, drunkenness, and more. {v: 5:19, 5:20, 5:21}

**"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control"** (5:22-23). {v: 5:22, 5:23}

Fruit isn't forced. It grows. {v: 5:22}

**"Since we live by the Spirit, let us walk in step with the Spirit"** (5:25). {v: 5:24, 5:25, 5:26}

### Section 7: Carry Each Other's Burdens

If someone falls into sin, help them back gently. {v: 6:1}

**"Carry one another's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ"** (6:2). {v: 6:2, 6:3, 6:4, 6:5}

Whatever you plant, you will harvest. {v: 6:6, 6:7, 6:8}

**"Let us not grow weary in well-doing, for in due time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up"** (6:9). {v: 6:9, 6:10}

Paul writes the ending in his own big handwriting. {v: 6:11, 6:12, 6:13}

**"But as for me, may I never boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ"** (6:14). {v: 6:14}

**"What counts is a new creation"** (6:15). {v: 6:15, 6:16}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

Galatians 1 to 6 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"A different gospel"** (1:6) | Any message that says Jesus isn't enough. |
| **"Christ lives in me"** (2:20) | Jesus's life fills the believer's life. |
| **"Clothed yourselves with Christ"** (3:27) | In baptism, we are wrapped in Jesus. |
| **"You are all one in Christ Jesus"** (3:28) | No one is a second-class Christian. |
| **"When the time had fully come"** (4:4) | Jesus came at exactly the right time. |
| **"It is for freedom that Christ has set us free"** (5:1) | Don't trade freedom for rule-keeping. |
| **"The fruit of the Spirit"** (5:22) | What the Spirit grows in us. |
| **"Carry one another's burdens"** (6:2) | Help each other with what's heavy. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Circumcision** was the sign of God's covenant with Abraham's family. Some teachers wanted non-Jewish believers to be circumcised to become fully part of God's people.

**Paul and Peter** were both apostles. Paul's story of correcting Peter (2:11-14) shows that even leaders can get it wrong.

**Paul's large handwriting** (6:11) may mean he usually dictated his letters and signed the end himself.†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Is Paul against the Old Testament law?** No. He says the law was good and led us to Christ. But keeping it can't make us right with God. Only Christ can.

**Does "neither male nor female" mean those differences disappear?** Paul means all are equally God's children and heirs in Christ. No one is worth less before God.

**Can I make the fruit of the Spirit grow by trying hard?** It's the Spirit's fruit. We "walk in step" with the Spirit through God's word, prayer, and the church, and he grows it.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Jesus got you in, but now it's up to you to stay in."*

**What's true:** Christians are called to live new lives.

**What the passage is mainly about:** Paul says that kind of thinking is "a different gospel." **"I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself up for me"** (2:20). The new life is fruit the Spirit grows, not a fee we pay.

**Where Jesus is:** He loved us, gave himself for us, and now lives in us.

## ✝️ Good News

You don't have to earn your place in God's family.

**"You are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus"** (3:26).

Christ set you free. The Spirit lives in you and grows good fruit. You are God's child and heir.

**We are made right with God by faith in Christ, not by keeping the law. Christ set us free, and the Spirit grows his fruit in us as we serve one another in love.**

## 📚 From Luther

Luther loved Galatians so much that he called it "my Katie," after his wife, Katharina von Bora. His lectures on Galatians are among his most famous works.†

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow the Revised Common Lectionary. In Year C, it reads through Galatians on six Sundays in early summer (Lectionary 9 to 14). Galatians 4:4-7 is read on the First Sunday of Christmas in Year B and on the Name of Jesus.†

## 💡 Big Idea

**We are made right with God by faith in Christ, not by keeping the law. It is for freedom that Christ set us free: free to serve one another in love as the Spirit grows his fruit in us.**
