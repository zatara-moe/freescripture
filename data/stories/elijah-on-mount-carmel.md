# The Story

## 🎬 Scene Card

**Cast**

- **Elijah:** A prophet known as Elijah the Tishbite, from the land of Gilead (17:1). A prophet is someone God sends to speak God's message.
- **The LORD:** God. LORD in capital letters stands for God's personal name. Jewish readers traditionally don't say it aloud, out of respect.
- **Ahab:** The king of Israel, the northern kingdom. He worshiped Baal.
- **Jezebel:** Ahab's wife, the queen. She was a princess from Sidon, a city on the coast north of Israel (16:31).
- **The prophets of Baal:** Four hundred fifty men who served the god Baal (18:19).
- **The people of Israel:** A huge crowd gathered on the mountain.
- **Elijah's servant:** A helper who traveled with Elijah.
- **An angel:** A messenger from God.

**Setting**

- Mount Carmel, a long ridge of hills near the Mediterranean Sea. Then the desert far to the south, and Horeb, the mountain of God.

**When**

- About 2,900 years ago, in a terrible drought.

## 🧭 Before the Story

**Ahab and Jezebel ruled Israel.** King Ahab married Jezebel, a princess from Sidon. Ahab built a temple for Baal in Samaria, his capital city, and worshiped there (16:31-32). Jezebel had killed many of the LORD's prophets (18:4).

**Who was Baal?** The name Baal means "lord" or "master." Baal was the main god of the Canaanites and Phoenicians, the peoples who lived around Israel (Easton's Bible Dictionary). Many scholars today say people prayed to Baal for rain and good crops.†

**Elijah announced a drought.** A drought is a long time with no rain. Elijah told Ahab, **"there will be neither dew nor rain in these years except at my word"** (17:1). Now it was the third year. Crops failed, and people went hungry (18:1-2). Ahab had searched every nation for Elijah (18:10).

**Here's what you know that almost nobody in this story knows:** God had already told Elijah, **"I will send rain upon the face of the earth"** (18:1).

## 📝 Plain Retelling

### Scene 1: Troubler of Israel

King Ahab went out to meet a man he had searched for in every nation. It was Elijah. {v: 18:10, 18:16, 18:17}

Ahab said, **"Is that you, O troubler of Israel?"** (18:17). One likely reason: Ahab blamed Elijah for the drought. {v: 18:17}

Elijah did not back down. He said Ahab was the one who had troubled Israel. Ahab and his family had left God's commands. They followed the Baals instead. {v: 18:18}

Then Elijah gave the king an order. Call all Israel to Mount Carmel. Bring the 450 prophets of Baal. Bring the 400 prophets of Asherah, the goddess worshiped with Baal. These prophets all ate at Jezebel's table. {v: 18:19}

### Scene 2: Two Opinions

Ahab sent for everyone. A great crowd gathered on the mountain. {v: 18:20}

Elijah came close to the people. He asked, **"How long will you waver between two opinions? If the LORD is God, follow Him. But if Baal is God, follow him"** (18:21). To waver means to go back and forth and never decide. {v: 18:21}

The people did not say a word. {v: 18:21}

Elijah said he was the only prophet of the LORD left. Baal had 450. {v: 18:22}

So Elijah set up a test. Bring two bulls. Baal's prophets would cut up one bull and lay it on wood. Elijah would do the same with the other. Nobody would light a fire. {v: 18:23}

Then each side would pray. **"The God who answers by fire, He is God"** (18:24). {v: 18:24}

The people finally spoke. They said this was a good plan. {v: 18:24}

### Scene 3: No Answer

Elijah told Baal's prophets to go first, because there were so many of them. They got their bull ready. From morning until noon they shouted, **"O Baal, answer us!"** (18:26). They leaped around their altar. {v: 18:25, 18:26}

Nothing happened. No sound. No answer. {v: 18:26}

At noon, Elijah began to mock them. To mock means to make fun of someone. He told them to shout louder. Maybe Baal was deep in thought, or busy. Maybe he was on a trip. Maybe he was asleep and needed to be woken up. {v: 18:27}

Elijah did not believe any of this. He was pointing out that Baal could not answer. {v: 18:27}

The prophets shouted louder. As their custom was, they cut themselves with knives and spears until they bled. {v: 18:28}

They kept shouting wildly all afternoon, until the time of the evening sacrifice. Still no one answered. {v: 18:29}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Hot sun beats down on a rocky hilltop. Hoarse voices shout the same words again and again, and dry wood sits on the altar, cold.

### Scene 4: Water on the Altar

Late in the day, Elijah called, "Come near to me." The people came close. {v: 18:30}

The LORD's old altar on the mountain had been torn down. Elijah repaired it. {v: 18:30}

He took twelve stones, one for each tribe of Israel. The tribes came from the twelve sons of Jacob, whom God had renamed Israel. {v: 18:31}

Elijah built the altar with the stones. He dug a ditch around it. He set the wood in place and laid the bull on it. {v: 18:32, 18:33}

Then he told the people to fill four big jars with water. "Pour it on the offering and the wood," he said. {v: 18:33, 18:34}

They did. "Do it again," Elijah said. They did. "Do it a third time." They did. {v: 18:34}

Water ran down the altar and filled the ditch. One likely reason for the water: now nobody could say the fire was a trick. {v: 18:35}

### Scene 5: Fire From the LORD

It was time for the evening sacrifice. Elijah went up to the altar. He prayed to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel. He asked the LORD to show that he is God in Israel. He asked the LORD to show that Elijah was his servant. Elijah had done all this because God told him to. {v: 18:36}

**"Answer me, O LORD! Answer me, so that this people will know that You, the LORD, are God, and that You have turned their hearts back again"** (18:37). {v: 18:37}

Then the fire of the LORD fell. {v: 18:38}

It burned up the bull. It burned the wood. It burned the stones and the dirt. It even burned up all the water in the ditch. {v: 18:38}

The people fell facedown. They cried out, **"The LORD, He is God! The LORD, He is God!"** (18:39). {v: 18:39}

### Scene 6: At the Kishon Valley

Then Elijah gave an order. **"Seize the prophets of Baal! Do not let a single one escape"** (18:40). {v: 18:40}

The people grabbed them. Elijah took them down to the Kishon Valley, below the mountain. He killed them there. {v: 18:40}

This is a hard part of the story. Christians have struggled with this passage for a long time. There is more about it below, under "Wait, what?" {v: 18:40}

### Scene 7: Rain

Elijah told Ahab to go eat and drink. He said there was the sound of heavy rain. {v: 18:41}

Ahab went to eat and drink. But Elijah climbed to the top of Carmel. He bent down to the ground and put his face between his knees. {v: 18:42}

He sent his servant to look out toward the sea. The servant came back. "There is nothing there." {v: 18:43}

Seven times Elijah said, "Go back." {v: 18:43}

The seventh time, the servant saw a cloud **"as small as a man's hand"** (18:44). This is a comparison. It means a very small cloud. {v: 18:44}

Elijah sent word to Ahab. Get your chariot ready and go down now, before the rain stops you. {v: 18:44}

The sky grew dark. The wind rose. Heavy rain began to fall. The drought was over. {v: 18:45}

Ahab rode toward the city of Jezreel. **"And the hand of the LORD came upon Elijah"** (18:46). These words mean God gave Elijah strength. Elijah tucked his long coat into his belt. He ran ahead of Ahab all the way to Jezreel. {v: 18:45, 18:46}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Rain drums on dusty ground for the first time in years. Water runs in muddy streams down the side of the mountain.

### Scene 8: Under the Broom Tree

Ahab told Jezebel everything Elijah had done. He told her Elijah had killed the prophets. {v: 19:1}

Jezebel sent Elijah a message. By this time tomorrow, she would kill him too. {v: 19:2}

Elijah was afraid. He ran for his life. He went south to Beersheba, at the far edge of Judah. He left his servant there. {v: 19:3}

Then Elijah went on alone, a whole day into the desert. He sat down under a broom tree. A broom tree is a desert bush with thin branches. {v: 19:4}

Elijah was worn out. He felt alone. He prayed that he might die. **"I have had enough, LORD,"** he said. **"Take my life, for I am no better than my fathers"** (19:4). His fathers means his ancestors, the people in his family long ago. {v: 19:4}

Not long before, Elijah had watched fire fall from heaven. Now he could not go on. {v: 18:38, 19:4}

If this part of the story brings up big feelings, talk with a parent, pastor, or trusted adult. There is more help below. {v: 19:4}

### Scene 9: Bread and Water

Elijah lay down under the tree and fell asleep. {v: 19:5}

Then an angel touched him and said, "Get up and eat." {v: 19:5}

Elijah looked around. By his head was a loaf of bread, baked on hot coals. There was a jar of water too. Elijah ate and drank. Then he lay down again. {v: 19:6}

The angel of the LORD came a second time. He touched Elijah again. **"Get up and eat, or the journey will be too much for you"** (19:7). {v: 19:7}

The angel did not scold Elijah. He did not give him a speech. He gave him food, water, and sleep. {v: 19:5, 19:6, 19:7}

Elijah ate and drank. The food gave him strength. He walked forty days and forty nights. At last he came to Horeb, the mountain of God. {v: 19:8}

### Scene 10: The Cave

At Horeb, Elijah went into a cave and spent the night. {v: 19:9}

The LORD spoke to him. **"What are you doing here, Elijah?"** (19:9). {v: 19:9}

Elijah told God his story. He had been very zealous for the LORD. Zealous means eager and completely loyal. But the Israelites had turned away from God's covenant. A covenant is God's lasting promise with his people. They had torn down God's altars and killed his prophets. **"I am the only one left, and they are seeking my life as well"** (19:10). {v: 19:10}

God told him to go out and stand on the mountain. The LORD was about to pass by. {v: 19:11}

A great wind tore at the mountains and broke the rocks. But the LORD was not in the wind. {v: 19:11}

Then came an earthquake. But the LORD was not in the earthquake. {v: 19:11}

Then came a fire. But the LORD was not in the fire. {v: 19:12}

And after the fire came **"a still, small voice"** (19:12). {v: 19:12}

When Elijah heard it, he covered his face with his coat. He went out and stood at the mouth of the cave. {v: 19:13}

A voice asked him again, **"What are you doing here, Elijah?"** (19:13). Elijah gave the same answer. He still felt he was the only one left. {v: 19:13, 19:14}

> 🎥 **Imagine the Scene** *(imagination, not Scripture)*
> Broken rocks lie scattered on the mountainside, and smoke drifts in the air. Then everything is very quiet.

### Scene 11: Not Alone

The LORD gave Elijah work to do. Go back the way you came, to the desert near Damascus. {v: 19:15}

God told Elijah to anoint new kings. To anoint someone is to pour oil on them to show God has chosen them. Hazael would be king of Aram. Jehu would be king of Israel. {v: 19:15, 19:16}

Elijah was also to anoint Elisha to be the next prophet after him. {v: 19:16}

God said that Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha would each put people to death with the sword. This is a hard verse too. {v: 19:17}

Then God answered Elijah's loneliness. Elijah was not the only one left. God had kept seven thousand people in Israel who had never bowed down to Baal. {v: 19:18}

Earlier, a palace official named Obadiah had hidden a hundred of the LORD's prophets in caves (18:13). Elijah had never been as alone as he felt. {v: 18:13, 19:18}

Elijah did not have to do God's work by himself. {v: 19:18}

# Understand It

## 📜 Read It

1 Kings 18:17 to 19:18 (BSB)

## 🔍 What Does This Mean?

| The text says | It means |
|---|---|
| **"Waver between two opinions"** (18:21) | The Hebrew is a picture of limping back and forth.† The people wanted both the LORD and Baal. Elijah said they had to choose. |
| **"The God who answers by fire, He is God"** (18:24) | Many scholars say Baal was believed to bring rain and storms.† The test showed who really had power over the sky. |
| **"Answer me, O LORD!"** (18:37) | Elijah did not do anything to light the fire. He prayed. God acted. The prayer asks that God turn the people's hearts back. |
| **"The LORD, He is God!"** (18:39) | The people finally answered Elijah's question from the morning. The name Elijah means "whose God is the LORD" (Easton's Bible Dictionary). |
| **"I have had enough, LORD"** (19:4) | Elijah was honest with God about how he felt. God answered with care, not anger. |
| **"A still, small voice"** (19:12) | God did not come in the loud, scary things. God came quietly. Other Bibles say "a gentle whisper" or "a sound of sheer silence." The Hebrew words are hard to put into English. |
| **"What are you doing here, Elijah?"** (19:9) | God asked a question and let Elijah tell his story. The Bible does not say what tone of voice God used. |
| **"I have reserved seven thousand"** (19:18) | Elijah thought he was alone. God had kept many more faithful people. |

## 🌍 Back Then...

**Mount Carmel is a long ridge of hills.** It runs about 12 miles, from the plain inland to the sea (Easton's Bible Dictionary). The Kishon Valley lies below it (18:40).

**The evening sacrifice was offered every afternoon.** God's law told Israel's priests to offer a lamb in the morning and at twilight each day (Exodus 29:38-39).

**The broom tree is a desert bush.** It grows in many parts of the land. Older Bibles call it a juniper (Easton's Bible Dictionary).

**Horeb is the name of the mountains where Mount Sinai stands** (Easton's Bible Dictionary). This is where God spoke to Moses from the burning bush (Exodus 3:1). It is where God made his covenant with Israel (Deuteronomy 5:2). Moses stayed on the mountain **"forty days and forty nights"** (Exodus 24:18). God also told Moses, **"when My glory passes by, I will put you in a cleft of the rock"** (Exodus 33:22). A cleft is a crack in a rock.

**How long was the drought?** First Kings says the rain came in the third year (18:1). Jesus and the letter of James both say **"three and a half years"** (Luke 4:25, James 5:17).

**Elijah is still remembered at Passover.** Many Jewish families set out a cup for Elijah at the Passover meal. It looks forward to the day God promised to send Elijah again (Malachi 4:5).†

## 🚨 Literal Reader Alert: "Wait, what?"

**Why did Elijah make fun of Baal's prophets?** Elijah was using mockery. He said the opposite of what he meant. He did not believe Baal was thinking, busy, traveling, or asleep. He was showing that Baal was not there to answer. This is not a lesson on how to treat people who believe differently. Christians are told to speak about their faith **"with gentleness and respect"** (1 Peter 3:15).

**Why did the prophets of Baal cut themselves?** The Bible says it was their custom (18:28). They hoped it would make Baal answer. It did not. God never asks anyone to hurt themselves. If you ever feel like hurting yourself, please tell someone you trust today. There are help lines below.

**Why were the prophets of Baal killed?** The Bible says it plainly. After the fire fell, Elijah had the prophets seized. He took them to the Kishon Valley and killed them (18:40). Moses's law said a prophet who led Israel to other gods must be put to death (Deuteronomy 13:5). But this story does not record God commanding this killing. It also does not stop to say if it was right or wrong. Christians have struggled with this passage for a long time. It is okay to find it troubling. When God seems frightening in a story like this, Christians look to Jesus, the clearest picture of who God is. Once, some villages refused to welcome Jesus. His disciples James and John asked, **"Lord, do You want us to call down fire from heaven to consume them?"** (Luke 9:54). **"But Jesus turned and rebuked them"** (Luke 9:55). To rebuke means to correct someone sharply. No one should ever hurt others in God's name.

**Was Elijah depressed?** The Bible does not use that word, and we cannot diagnose someone from long ago. The Bible says Elijah was afraid, alone, and so worn out that he wanted to die (19:3-4). Many readers today recognize feelings like his. God did not tell Elijah to feel happier or try harder. God did not call it a lack of faith. First, God gave him food, water, and sleep. Then God listened. Then God told him he was not alone.

**What if I feel like Elijah did?** You are not alone, and you are not a bad Christian. You can tell God exactly how you feel, like Elijah did. Please also tell someone you trust. If you ever wish you could die, like Elijah did, tell someone today. You do not have to wait until it is an emergency. If sadness or worry is making it hard to sleep, eat, or get through the day, a doctor or counselor can help. Getting help is not a lack of faith. God often cares for us through other people. If this story brings up big feelings, talk with a parent, pastor, or trusted adult. If you are in danger right now, call 911 or your local emergency number. In the United States, you can also call or text 988 any time, day or night. Outside the United States, findahelpline.com lists free helplines.

## 🤔 You Might Have Heard...

*"Elijah was a hero of faith. If your faith is strong, you won't get discouraged."*

**What's true:** God did great things through Elijah. Elijah prayed with courage on Mount Carmel.

**What the story is mainly about:** What God does. God sent the fire. God sent the rain. God sent bread when Elijah wanted to die. God spoke quietly and told Elijah he was not alone. The same Elijah who called down fire sat under a bush and asked to die. The Bible says, **"Elijah was a man just like us"** (James 5:17). Strong faith does not mean never feeling low. Faith is a gift from God. God keeps us as his own, even when we feel too weak to trust him.

**Where Jesus is:** Centuries later, Elijah appeared on a mountain again. He and Moses talked with Jesus about **"His departure, which He was about to accomplish at Jerusalem"** (Luke 9:31). That meant Jesus's death on the cross. The night before he died, Jesus said, **"My soul is consumed with sorrow to the point of death"** (Mark 14:34). Then **"an angel from heaven appeared to Him and strengthened Him"** (Luke 22:43). Some early copies of Luke leave out this verse. Jesus knows how deep sorrow like this feels.†

## ✝️ Good News

Elijah said, **"I am the only one left"** (19:10). Many years later, Paul wrote about this story. Paul was writing about his own Jewish people. He said, **"God did not reject His people"** (Romans 11:2). He said God's answer to Elijah was about grace: **"there is a remnant chosen by grace"** (Romans 11:5). A remnant is a group that is kept safe. God keeps his people. They are kept by his grace, not by how strong they feel.

When Elijah was at his lowest, God fed him bread baked over coals. After Jesus rose from the dead, he did the same for Peter and the other disciples. Peter had said three times that he did not know Jesus. Jesus made breakfast on **"a charcoal fire there with fish on it, and some bread"** (John 21:9). Jesus still feeds his worn-out people. He gives us his own body and blood in the Lord's Supper.

Jesus says, **"Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest"** (Matthew 11:28).

**When you have had enough, God does not give up on you. He feeds you, stays near, and tells you that you are not alone.**

## 📚 From Luther

In the Small Catechism, Luther explains the First Commandment, "You shall have no other gods." He writes, "We should fear, love, and trust in God above all things." Luther explains more in the Large Catechism (1529). He says your god is whatever you expect every good thing from. It is whatever you run to in every trouble.† The people on Carmel wanted to trust both Baal and the LORD for rain. Luther teaches that only the true God gives us all good things, including rain and daily bread.

## 📅 In Church

Many churches follow a reading plan called the Revised Common Lectionary. It reads parts of this story on several Sundays in the summer. In Year A, it reads the still, small voice (19:9-18) in early to mid-August (Lectionary 19). In Year B, it reads the bread and water (19:4-8) in early to mid-August (Lectionary 19). Some churches follow a second set of Old Testament readings that goes through the stories in order. In Year C, that set reads the contest on Carmel in late May or early June (Lectionary 9). It reads Elijah under the broom tree and at the mountain in late June (Lectionary 12).†

## 💡 Big Idea

**God answers with fire, then quietly feeds his worn-out prophet and tells him he is not alone.**
